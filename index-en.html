Our Vision

Torah accessible at every level

Daat Torah is a halakha study platform designed to accompany every student — from the curious beginner to the seasoned Talmid Chacham — in a serious and structured study of the Torah.

Each text is presented at three levels: an accessible and pedagogical version (Base), a scholarly version with full pilpul (Lamdan), and a magisterial synthesis for review and memorization.

Initiated by Rav Yossef Haim Samama, the platform begins with Hilkhot Shabbat (Orach Chaim) and will progressively expand to all of Halakha.

"כִּי תוֹרַת ה' תְּמִימָה, מְשִׁיבַת נָפֶשׁ"

Pedagogical system

Three study levels

Each topic is treated at three progressive depths, enabling everyone to study at their own level.

01
מתחילים
Base

Hebrew text with fluent translation. Pedagogical explanation of the essential concepts, accessible to beginners as well as intermediate students.

02

03

Magisterial Synthesis

Structured summary for review and memorization. Comparative tables, golden rules, practical cases, classic pitfalls.

Artificial Intelligence

Multilingual

Answers in French with Hebrew citations. Eventually: Hebrew, English and Spanish. Torah study without a language barrier.

Sample dialogue — Siman רמ״ו, Seif Alef
"Can one rent a tool to a non-Jew before Shabbat if he will use it on Shabbat day?"
According to Halakha (O.C. 246:1), the Shulchan Aruch and the Rama agree on the prohibition. The Rama clarifies: "וכן עיקר כסברא האחרונה" — he rules according to the opinion of Rabbeinu Yona, the Rosh, and Tosafot, who forbid on account of שכר שבת (Shabbat wages) and נראה כשלוחו (appearance of agency). This prohibition applies even if the Jew is not present on Shabbat day.

About

An initiative for the community

Daat Torah was born from a simple observation: halakhic literature in French, accessible at every level, is still too rare. Thousands of Francophones — in France, in Israel, in Canada, in Morocco — deserve access to serious study in their own language.

Each text is carefully transcribed, translated, and annotated by Rav Yossef Haim Samama, with the concern of remaining faithful to the primary sources while making the concepts accessible.

The project starts modestly — a community platform — with the ambition of eventually becoming an international reference for halakha study in French.
