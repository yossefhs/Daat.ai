1. The Central Axiom

Universal principle of Siman כ״א:

The siman treats the respect of severed tsitsit and worn-out talits: what is to be done with ציציות שנפסקו and with טליתות ישנים? Severed tsitsit threads may be thrown away (יכול לזורקן), for the tsitsit is a מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה — a תשמיש מצוה and not a תשמיש קדושה. But as long as they are קבועים בטלית, it is forbidden to use them (to tie anything with them) — ביזוי מצוה. Rama: וי״א not to throw them in a degrading place, and יש מדקדקין put them in geniza — והמחמיר ומדקדק במצות תע״ב. The worn-out talits of mitsva: one keeps apart from them — no קינוח and no תשמיש מגונה. On the other hand, מותר ליכנס בציצית לבית הכסא — וכ״ש to sleep in them (the custom is lenient). And one takes care not to drag one's tsitsit on the ground (שלא יגרור ציציותיו).
💡 The universal key: the tsitsit is a תשמיש מצוה — no intrinsic kedusha, hence no obligatory geniza; but as long as the mitsva is attached to it (or the object recalls it), the ביזוי מצוה commands respect.
  • חוטים שנפסקו → detached: one may throw them away; hiddur: geniza (והמחמיר תע״ב)
  • קבועים בטלית → no profane use (tying…) → ביזוי מצוה
  • טליתות שבלו → keep apart: no קינוח and no תשמיש מגונה; one throws them away (כלים)
  • בית הכסא / sleep → permitted; גרירה → take care not to drag the tsitsit

2. Master table of the ד׳ seifim

The ד׳ סעיפים of the Mechaber group into four axes: (A) the חוטי ציצית שנפסקו — one may throw them away (מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה), but fixed to the talit, any use is ביזוי מצוה; (B) the worn-out טליתות של מצוה — keep apart from any degrading use; (C) the בית הכסא — permitted to enter in tsitsit, and all the more to sleep in them; (D) the גרירה — not to drag one's tsitsit.

⚖ The structure of the siman at a glance

The ד׳ סעיפים of the Mechaber decide the respect of severed tsitsit and worn-out talits: (A) the חוטים שנפסקו — one may throw them away (מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה), but fixed to the talit, any use is ביזוי מצוה; Rama: not in a degrading place, and geniza as a hiddur (והמחמיר תע״ב); (B) the worn-out טליתות — keep apart: no קינוח and no תשמיש מגונה, but one throws them away (כלים); (C) the בית הכסא — permitted, וכ״ש to sleep in them; the custom is lenient; (D) the גרירה — not to drag one's tsitsit. The Shulchan Aruch HaRav (the Alter Rebbe) unfolds this siman into ה׳ סעיפים: the severed tsitsit (תשמישי מצוה), the hiddur of geniza, the worn-out talits, the בית הכסא and the גרירה.

3. חוטי ציצית שנפסקו — throw away, without contempt

Q: What is to be done with tsitsit threads that snapped or were replaced?
↓
🟢 יכול לזורקן לאשפה — One may throw them away, for it is a מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה (seif 1).
🔴 קבועים בטלית — As long as they are fixed to the talit: no use (tying something…) → ביזוי מצוה (seif 1).
🟡 Rama — וי״א — Even once detached, do not throw them in a degrading place; though they require no geniza (seif 1).
🟢 יש מדקדקין — Some put them in geniza: והמחמיר ומדקדק במצות תע״ב (seif 1).

The law of the חוטים שנפסקו

The siman opens on the founding distinction: the tsitsit is a תשמיש מצוה, a מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה — unlike the תשמישי קדושה (tefillin, mezuzot), which require geniza. Snapped or replaced threads may therefore be thrown away (יכול לזורקן לאשפה). But two limits frame this permission: first, as long as the threads are קבועים בטלית, it is forbidden to use them — to tie an object or any similar use — משום ביזוי מצוה; second, the Rama cites the י״א: even once detached, one does not treat them with contempt by throwing them in a degrading place (מקום מגונה), though they require no geniza; and יש מדקדקין put them in geniza — והמחמיר ומדקדק במצות תע״ב (he who is strict, a blessing will come upon him). The Rama adds the reference וע״ל סי׳ תרס״ד סעיף ח׳ ט׳. The foundation (תשמישי מצוה נזרקין, Megila 26b) is developed in Level 2; the psak, in Level 4 (refer to the Rav).

4. טליתות של מצוה שבלו — the worn-out talits

Q: What is to be done with an old, worn-out talit of mitsva?
↓
🟡 אדם בודל עצמו מהם — One keeps apart from them: no familiar or debasing use (seif 2).
🔴 לא לקנח ולא תשמיש מגונה — Forbidden to wipe oneself with them or to assign them to a degrading use (seif 2).
🟢 זורקן והם כלים — But one may throw them away: they are utensils, without intrinsic kedusha (seif 2).
📚 The yesod of seif 2: the worn-out talit keeps the status of תשמיש מצוה: it requires no geniza — one may throw it away, והם כלים (they are utensils). But the trace of the mitsva imposes a distance of respect: אדם בודל עצמו מהם — one does not wipe oneself with it (קינוח), one does not assign it to a תשמיש מגונה (a rag, a debasing use). The line of the siman is constant: no intrinsic kedusha, but no contempt. The detail (throwing away with dignity, geniza as a hiddur) is treated in Level 4 (refer to the Rav).

5. בית הכסא ושינה — lavatory and sleep in tsitsit

Q: May one enter the lavatory or sleep in a garment bearing tsitsit?
↓
🟢 מותר ליכנס בציצית לבית הכסא — Permitted to enter the lavatory in tsitsit (seif 3).
🟢 Rama — וכ״ש לשכב בהן — All the more, sleeping in them is permitted (דשרי) (seif 3).
🟡 ויש שכתבו — Some avoid sleeping in a talit bearing tsitsit and giving it to wash to a non-Jewish launderer — so that the mitsvot should not be despised (seif 3).
🟢 נוהגין להקל — But the custom is lenient about sleeping in them (seif 3).

The yesod of the בית הכסא

Since the tsitsit has no intrinsic kedusha, it is permitted to enter the lavatory while wearing it — מותר ליכנס בציצית לבית הכסא — unlike the tefillin. The Rama draws the kal vachomer: וכ״ש לשכב בהן דשרי — all the more may one sleep in them. He records, however, that some wrote of the custom not to sleep in a talit bearing tsitsit, and not to give it to a non-Jewish launderer to wash — all for the same motive: that the mitsvot should not be despised (שלא יהיו מצות בזויות עליו). And he concludes: the custom is lenient about sleeping in them. The difference from the tefillin and the limits of the minhag are developed in Level 2; the psak, in Level 4.

6. שלא יגרור ציציותיו — not dragging the tsitsit

Q: How does one wear the talit daily without disrespecting the mitsva?
↓
⚠️ יש ליזהר — When one wears a talit, one must take care (seif 4).
🔴 שלא יגרור ציציותיו — Not to drag one's tsitsit on the ground (seif 4).
📚 הטעם — The same guiding thread: the mitsva carried upon oneself must not be בזויה (despised) (seif 4).
📚 The yesod of seif 4: the last seif brings the whole siman back to daily life: he who wears a talit must take care that his tsitsit do not drag on the ground. It is the same logic as the preceding seifim — the tsitsit has no intrinsic kedusha, but as long as it serves the mitsva, letting it sweep the floor would be a ביזוי מצוה. The continuation (the respect due to the mitsvot, the לועג לרש) is found in siman 23; the detailed psak, in Level 4 (refer to the Rav).

7. Golden rules — Memory Aid

The 4 golden rules of Siman כ״א
  • חוטים שנפסקו — detached: one may throw them away (מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה); hiddur: geniza (והמחמיר תע״ב); never in a degrading place.
  • קבועים בטלית — no profane use (tying…) → ביזוי מצוה.
  • טליתות שבלו — keep apart: no קינוח and no תשמיש מגונה; but one throws them away (כלים).
  • בית הכסא / sleep / גרירה — permitted to enter the lavatory and to sleep in them (the custom is lenient); but שלא יגרור ציציותיו.

8. Mnemonic — Memory Aid

Acrostic: T-B-B-G — the four markers of Siman כ״א
  • Tashmish mitsva (מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה) — the severed threads may be thrown away; geniza = hiddur.
  • Bizuy mitsva (ביזוי מצוה) — fixed to the talit: no use.
  • Beit hakisse (בית הכסא) — permitted to enter, וכ״ש to sleep in them; the custom is lenient.
  • Gerira (גרירה) — never drag one's tsitsit on the ground.
3 foundational formulas to memorize:
  • מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה — the severed threads may be thrown away
  • כל זמן שהם קבועים בטלית אסור להשתמש בהם משום ביזוי מצוה
  • מותר ליכנס בציצית לבית הכסא

9. Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ Pitfall 1: Believing that severed tsitsit threads require geniza like tefillin.
→ The seif: מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה — one may throw them away; geniza is only a hiddur (והמחמיר תע״ב) (seif 1).
❌ Pitfall 2: Using the threads still fixed to the talit (tying a parcel, fastening an object).
→ The seif: קבועים בטלית → אסור להשתמש בהם — ביזוי מצוה (seif 1).
❌ Pitfall 3: Throwing the detached threads anywhere, even in a degrading place.
→ The Rama: וי״א not to throw them במקום מגונה; the best — geniza (seif 1).
❌ Pitfall 4: Using an old talit as a rag or for a debasing use.
→ The seif: אדם בודל עצמו מהם — no קינוח and no תשמיש מגונה; one throws it away with dignity (seif 2).
❌ Pitfall 5: Removing one's talit katan to enter the lavatory or to sleep, thinking it forbidden.
→ The seif: מותר ליכנס בציצית לבית הכסא — וכ״ש to sleep in them; the custom is lenient; but do not drag the tsitsit (seifim 3–4).

✦ ❖ ✦ The 3 Practical Commandments of Siman כ״א in Brief

  1. Throw away the severed threads with dignity — מצוה שאין בגופה קדושה; the best — geniza (והמחמיר תע״ב); but fixed to the talit: no use (ביזוי מצוה).
  2. Keep apart from the worn-out talit — אדם בודל עצמו מהם: no rag and no debasing use; one throws it away with dignity.
  3. Wear the tsitsit without despising it — permitted in the lavatory and for sleep; but שלא יגרור ציציותיו (SA HaRav, ה׳ סעיפים).
