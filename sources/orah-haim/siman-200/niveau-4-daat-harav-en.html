Level 4 — Daat HaRav (Alter Rebbe) — Siman ר׳ · דִּין הַמַּפְסִיק כְּדֵי לְבָרֵךְ

The Alter Rebbe’s way on דִּין הַמַּפְסִיק כְּדֵי לְבָרֵךְ : הַמַּפְסִיק עַל כָּרְחוֹ לְהַשְּׁנַיִם ; שְׁמִיעָה עַד "הַזָּן אֶת הַכֹּל" ; שְׁנַיִם אֵינָן חַיָּבִים לְהַפְסִיק לְאֶחָד ; פָּרַח זִמּוּן ; נַעֲשָׂה טָפֵל לִשְׁנַיִם ; חֲזָרָה לִסְעוּדָתוֹ בְּלֹא "הַמּוֹצִיא" — in the Habad sensibility.

Why a Level 4 devoted to the Alter Rebbe ? The Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch is not a commentary on the Mehaber (מחבר) — it is a complete, self-standing Shulchan Aruch, written by the Alter Rebbe (Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, founder of Habad, disciple of the Maggid (מגיד) of Mezeritch). Its singularity : it combines halakha (הלכה) + טעמי המצוות + inner dimension in one work, and rules with a unique Talmudic rigor.

For Habad, the Alter Rebbe is הפוסק האחרון — his psak is the deciding authority. This page gathers the Rav’s way on siman ר׳ — דִּין הַמַּפְסִיק כְּדֵי לְבָרֵךְ (One who interrupts his meal in order to bless), which unfolds in the Rav over ג׳ סְעִיפִים (three seifim) : from the one who has not finished his meal and interrupts against his will for the two who have — distinguishing what suffices for zimun to be made over him from what he himself needs in order to be discharged — to the refusal of the converse, where two do not interrupt for one and the obligation arises only with their consent, up to the laws of “parah zimun” and of the diner who makes himself subordinate to each new pair; and finally to the return to the interrupted meal, which the mere intention to continue preserves from any closure — with no new blessing of “hamotzi.”

1 · טקסט אדמו״ר הזקן

שולחן ערוך הרב — סימן ר׳ — דִּין הַמַּפְסִיק כְּדֵי לְבָרֵךְ

The full text of the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch

שולחן ערוך הרב, סימן ר׳ — דִּין הַמַּפְסִיק כְּדֵי לְבָרֵךְ — וּבוֹ ג׳ סְעִיפִים
The Alter Rebbe’s own text (Sefaria source Shulchan_Arukh_HaRav,_Orach_Chayim.200), followed by our English translation. The siman comprises three seifim (ג׳ סְעִיפִים ; seif k → .200.k).

סעיף א שְׁלֹשָׁה שֶׁאָכְלוּ כְּאַחַת — "הָאֶחָד מַפְסִיק עַל כָּרְחוֹ לְהַשְּׁנַיִם".

דִּין הַמַּפְסִיק כְּדֵי לְבָרֵךְ וּבוֹ ג׳ סְעִיפִים:
שְׁלֹשָׁה שֶׁאָכְלוּ כְּאַחַת וְגָמְרוּ [שְׁנַיִם] סְעוּדָתָם וְאֶחָד לֹא גָּמַר — הָאֶחָד מַפְסִיק עַל כָּרְחוֹ לְהַשְּׁנַיִם וְעוֹנֶה עִמָּהֶם בִּרְכַּת הַזִּמּוּן, וְשׁוֹמֵעַ עַד "הַזָּן אֶת הַכֹּל", כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קצ״ג. וְאִם לָאו — הוּא לֹא יָצָא יְדֵי חוֹבַת זִמּוּן.
אֲבָל הֵם מְזַמְּנִין עָלָיו בֵּין עוֹנֶה בֵּין אֵינוֹ עוֹנֶה, כָּל שֶׁהוּא עוֹמֵד שָׁם וְשׁוֹמֵעַ וְיָכוֹל לַעֲנוֹת. וְאִם אֵינוֹ עוֹמֵד עִמָּהֶם — קוֹרִין לוֹ עַד שֶׁיִּתְקָרֵב אֲלֵיהֶם וְיוּכַל לִשְׁמֹעַ וְלַעֲנוֹת עִמָּהֶם, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קצ״ד (מַה שֶּׁאֵין כֵּן בְּחֵרֵשׁ שֶׁאֵינוֹ יָכוֹל לִשְׁמֹעַ וְאִלֵּם שֶׁאֵינוֹ יָכוֹל לַעֲנוֹת — אֵין מְזַמְּנִין עֲלֵיהֶם אֶלָּא בַּעֲשָׂרָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קצ״ט):

One who interrupts his meal in order to bless — and it comprises three seifim. Three who ate together, and [two] finished their meal while one did not finish — that one interrupts against his will for the two, answers the zimun blessing together with them, and listens until “hazan et hakol” (Who feeds all), as was explained in siman קצ״ג. And if not — he himself has not fulfilled the obligation of zimun.
But they make zimun over him whether he answers or does not answer, so long as he stands there, hears, and is able to answer. And if he does not stand with them — they call him until he draws near to them and can hear and answer together with them, as was explained in siman קצ״ד (not so with a deaf man who cannot hear and a mute who cannot answer — zimun is made over them only with ten, as was explained in siman קצ״ט).
Explanation. Two distinct things are held together here without being confused. The first: once the obligation of zimun has arisen from the shared table, it does not bend to anyone’s convenience — the one who has not finished interrupts against his will, because the zimun belongs to the company and not to the individual. The second: being counted and being discharged are not the same. For them to make zimun over him, it is enough that he be present and able to answer; for him to have fulfilled the zimun, he must actually have listened until “hazan et hakol.” Hence the consequence: he is called back within earshot — for it is the possibility of hearing and answering that makes the bond — whereas the deaf and the mute, who lack that possibility by nature, are referred by the Rav to the regime of ten in siman קצ״ט.

סעיף ב "שְׁנַיִם אֵינָן חַיָּבִים לְהַפְסִיק לְאֶחָד" — וְדִינֵי "פָּרַח זִמּוּן".

אֲבָל שְׁנַיִם אֵינָן חַיָּבִים לְהַפְסִיק לְאֶחָד. לְפִיכָךְ, אֵין חִיּוּב זִמּוּן חָל עֲלֵיהֶם כְּלָל עַד שֶׁיִּתְרַצּוּ לְהַפְסִיק לָאֶחָד וּלְבָרֵךְ. וְאִם לֹא רָצוּ לְהַפְסִיק וְזִמֵּן הוּא עֲלֵיהֶם וְלֹא עָנוּ אַחֲרָיו, אוֹ שֶׁלֹּא שָׁמְעוּ עַד "הַזָּן אֶת הַכֹּל" — לֹא עָשָׂה כְּלוּם וְלֹא יָצָא יְדֵי זִמּוּן. לְפִיכָךְ, אַף הוּא אֵינוֹ רַשַּׁאי לְבָרֵךְ עַד שֶׁיִּגְמְרוּ הַשְּׁנַיִם סְעוּדָתָם וִיזַמֵּן עֲלֵיהֶם, שֶׁהֲרֵי כְּבָר נִתְחַיֵּב בְּזִמּוּן מִתְּחִלַּת הַסְּעוּדָה אִם הִתְחִילוּ שְׁלָשְׁתָּם יַחַד, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קצ״ג.
וְאִם נִתְרַצּוּ לְהַפְסִיק לוֹ וְזִמֵּן עֲלֵיהֶם וְאַחַר כָּךְ בָּא אֶחָד מִן הַשּׁוּק וְקָבַע עִמָּהֶם וְגָמְרוּ סְעוּדָתָם עִמּוֹ — אֵינָן חוֹזְרִין וּמְזַמְּנִין, שֶׁכְּבָר פָּרַח מֵהֶם זִמּוּן כְּשֶׁזִּמְּנוּ פַּעַם רִאשׁוֹנָה. אֲבָל אֶחָד הַמַּפְסִיק לִשְׁנַיִם וְאַחַר כָּךְ בָּאוּ שְׁנַיִם מִן הַשּׁוּק וְקָבְעוּ אֶצְלוֹ וְגָמַר סְעוּדָתוֹ עִמָּהֶם — חוֹזֵר וּמְזַמֵּן עִמָּהֶם, שֶׁהוּא נַעֲשָׂה טָפֵל לִשְׁנַיִם אֵלּוּ שֶׁלֹּא זִמְּנוּ עֲדַיִן, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קצ״ג. וְהוּא הַדִּין פַּעַם ב׳ וְג׳ אוֹ יוֹתֵר, יָכוֹל לְהַפְסִיק וּלְזַמֵּן עִם כָּל שְׁנַיִם שֶׁבָּאוּ וְקָבְעוּ אֶצְלוֹ וְאָכַל עִמָּהֶם כְּזַיִת.
וְהוּא הַדִּין אִם הָיוּ תִּשְׁעָה קְבוּעִים יַחַד מִתְּחִלָּה וְהִפְסִיק אֶחָד לִשְׁנַיִם מֵהֶם — יָכוֹל [הָ]אֶחָד לַחֲזֹר וּלְהַפְסִיק לִשְׁנַיִם אֲחֵרִים מֵהֶם, וְלַחֲזֹר וּלְהַפְסִיק לִשְׁנַיִם, אֲבָל שְׁנַיִם הָאַחֲרוֹנִים פָּרַח זִמּוּן מֵהֶם כְּשֶׁזִּמְּנוּ הָרִאשׁוֹנִים, וְלֹא נִשְׁאַר זִמּוּן בַּחֲבוּרָה אִם לֹא שֶׁאָכְלוּ עוֹד אַחַר זִמּוּן הָרִאשׁוֹנִים, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר שָׁם בְּסִימָן קצ״ג, וְשָׁם נִתְבָּאֲרוּ כָּל פְּרָטֵי דִּינֵי "פָּרַח זִמּוּן":

But two are not obliged to interrupt for one. Therefore the obligation of zimun does not fall upon them at all until they consent to interrupt for the one and to bless. And if they did not wish to interrupt, and he made zimun over them and they did not answer after him, or they did not listen until “hazan et hakol” — he has done nothing and has not fulfilled the zimun. Therefore he too may not bless until the two have finished their meal and he makes zimun over them, for he already became liable for zimun from the beginning of the meal if the three of them began together, as was explained in siman קצ״ג.
And if they consented to interrupt for him and he made zimun over them, and afterward one came from the market and settled with them and they finished their meal with him — they do not repeat the zimun, for the zimun already flew away from them when they made zimun the first time. But one who, being alone, interrupts for two, and afterward two came from the market and settled with him and he finished his meal with them — he repeats and makes zimun with them, for he becomes subordinate (tafel) to these two who have not yet made zimun, as was explained in siman קצ״ג. And the same holds a second and a third time, or more: he may interrupt and make zimun with every pair that has come and settled with him, provided he ate a kezayit with them.
And the same holds if nine were settled together from the beginning and one interrupted for two of them — [the] one may return and interrupt for another two of them, and return again and interrupt for two; but as for the last two, the zimun flew away from them when the first ones made zimun, and no zimun remained in the company — unless they ate again after the zimun of the first ones — as was explained there in siman קצ״ג; and there all the details of the laws of “parah zimun” (“the zimun has flown away”) were explained.
Explanation. The previous seif compelled the one to interrupt for the two; this one refuses the converse, and the Rav draws from it a consequence one would not have expected. Since the two owe nothing, the obligation itself does not yet arise: until they consent, there is no failed zimun — there is no zimun at all. A zimun recited without their agreement is therefore not a defective zimun; he has done nothing. And because the obligation did arise in him from the beginning of the shared meal, neither may he bless alone: he must wait. The second half of the seif applies the rule of the “zimun that has flown away.” Those who have already made zimun have exhausted their obligation, and a newcomer does not revive it; but one who has not finished his meal has exhausted nothing — he may therefore make himself tafel, as often as needed, to each new pair, provided he ate a kezayit with it. The case of the nine adds no new rule: it carries the same mechanism to its end, to the moment when no obligation is left in the company to be awakened.

סעיף ג הַמַּפְסִיק לְזִמּוּן — "אֵין כָּאן הֶפְסֵק וְהֶסַּח הַדַּעַת כְּלָל".

הַמַּפְסִיק לְזִמּוּן אֵין צָרִיךְ לַחֲזֹר וּלְבָרֵךְ "הַמּוֹצִיא" כְּשֶׁחוֹזֵר לִסְעוּדָתוֹ, שֶׁאֵין כָּאן הֶפְסֵק וְהֶסַּח הַדַּעַת כְּלָל, כִּי גַּם בִּשְׁמִיעָתוֹ בִּרְכַּת "הַזָּן" הָיְתָה כַּוָּנָתוֹ לֶאֱכֹל עוֹד וְשֶׁלֹּא לָצֵאת בִּבְרָכָה זוֹ. וּלְפִיכָךְ, אִם אַחַר כָּךְ נִמְלַךְ שֶׁלֹּא לֶאֱכֹל עוֹד — צָרִיךְ לַחֲזֹר וּלְבָרֵךְ בִּרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן.
אֲבָל אִם לֹא הָיָה בְּדַעְתּוֹ לֶאֱכֹל עוֹד פַּת רַק פַּרְפֶּרֶת — יָצָא יְדֵי חוֹבַת בִּרְכַּת "הַזָּן", וּמַתְחִיל בִּרְכוֹת הַמָּזוֹן מִבִּרְכַּת הָאָרֶץ. וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, יִזָּהֵר שֶׁלֹּא יָ[שִׂ]יחַ בֵּינְתַיִם, וְאַחַר בִּרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן יְבָרֵךְ מֵעֵין ג׳, שֶׁהִיא בְּרָכָה אַחֲרוֹנָה עַל הַפַּרְפֶּרֶת, שֶׁהִיא מֵחֲמֵשֶׁת הַמִּינִין, כִּי לֹא נִפְטְרָה בְּבִרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן, הוֹאִיל וְלֹא בֵּרַךְ בָּהּ בִּרְכַּת "הַזָּן".
וְאִם אַחַר שֶׁיָּצָא יְדֵי חוֹבַת בִּרְכַּת "הַזָּן" נִמְלַךְ לַחֲזֹר וְלֶאֱכֹל פַּת — צָרִיךְ לִטֹּל יָדָיו וּלְבָרֵךְ "הַמּוֹצִיא" תְּחִלָּה, שֶׁכְּבָר עָשָׂה סִלּוּק וְהֶסַּח הַדַּעַת בְּבָרְכוֹ בְּרָכָה אַחַת מִבִּרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן (אֲבָל הֶסַּח הַדַּעַת לְבַדּוֹ אֵינוֹ זָקוּק לְבָרֵךְ "הַמּוֹצִיא", כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קע״ט):

One who interrupts for the zimun need not go back and bless “hamotzi” when he returns to his meal, for there is here neither interruption nor diversion of mind (hesech hadaat) at all, since even while hearing the blessing of “hazan” his intention was to eat further and not to be discharged by this blessing. Therefore, if afterward he changed his mind and no longer wishes to eat — he must go back and recite birkat hamazon.
But if it was not in his mind to eat further bread, but only a parperet (an accompanying dish, of the five species) — he has fulfilled the obligation of the blessing of “hazan,” and he begins the blessings of the mazon from the blessing of the land (birkat haaretz). Nevertheless, let him take care not to converse in the meantime; and after birkat hamazon he shall recite me’ein shalosh, which is the final blessing over the parperet, which is of the five species, for it was not discharged by birkat hamazon, since he did not recite in it the blessing of “hazan.”
And if, after fulfilling the obligation of the blessing of “hazan,” he changed his mind and wishes to go back and eat bread — he must wash his hands and bless “hamotzi” first, for he has already effected a closure (siluk) and a diversion of mind by reciting one of the blessings of birkat hamazon (but diversion of mind alone does not require him to bless “hamotzi,” as was explained in siman קע״ט).
Explanation. This whole seif turns on intention. Listening to “hazan” in order to make up the count is not reciting it for oneself: the thought of continuing the meal, and of not being discharged by that blessing, prevents the listening from counting as closure — the meal was therefore not interrupted, and “hamotzi” need not be repeated. But the same intention, reversed, produces the opposite effect: one who listened while no longer wanting bread has genuinely received the blessing of “hazan,” so that he need only resume at birkat haaretz — hence the warning not to speak in between, a blessing once begun not tolerating a break. The last case measures the difference: when he has actually recited a blessing of birkat hamazon, the closure is made in deed and no longer in thought alone; that is why netilat yadayim and “hamotzi” are required — whereas mere hesech hadaat, without an act, would not require it (siman קע״ט).

2 · למעשה

הלכה למעשה — מנהג חב״ד

The practical Habad conduct

Points of conduct that follow directly from siman ר׳ in the sensibility of the Alter Rebbe and Habad — presented at the level of principle, referring to the Rav for the detail of cases.

For the Habad Hassid — Siman ר׳ (דִּין הַמַּפְסִיק כְּדֵי לְבָרֵךְ)

  • ① Seif א. Three who ate together, two of whom have finished: the one who has not finished interrupts against his will, answers the zimun with them and listens until “hazan et hakol” — failing which he himself has not fulfilled the zimun. They, on the other hand, make zimun over him whether he answers or not, so long as he is present and able to answer; if he stands apart, they call him until he can hear and answer (siman קצ״ד).
  • ② Seif ב. The converse does not oblige: two do not interrupt for one, and until they consent the obligation does not even arise — a zimun recited without them has produced nothing. He, however, may not bless alone: liable from the beginning of the shared meal, he waits for them to finish. After a zimun, the zimun has “flown away” from those who said it, and a newcomer does not revive it; but one who has not finished his meal may make himself tafel and make zimun again with each pair that settles with him — provided he ate a kezayit with it.
  • ③ Seif ג. One who interrupted for the zimun does not repeat “hamotzi” on resuming his meal: his intention to continue prevents any closure — and that is why, if he changes his mind and stops eating, he will have to recite birkat hamazon. If he no longer intended to eat bread but only a parperet, he has fulfilled “hazan,” resumes at birkat haaretz, takes care not to converse in the meantime, and afterward recites me’ein shalosh over the parperet. And if he then changes his mind and takes bread again: netilat yadayim and “hamotzi”, for a blessing of birkat hamazon already recited has effected closure in deed (unlike mere hesech hadaat — siman קע״ט).

⚠ This section presents the Habad conduct grounded in the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch, siman ר׳ (the real text reproduced in §1). It does not replace a rabbinic decision for a particular case. For any concrete question — whether to interrupt in the middle of one’s meal, a repeated zimun with a new pair, resuming the meal and the need for “hamotzi” — consult your Rav, and for Habad, a Habad Rav.
