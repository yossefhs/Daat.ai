Siman 4 — Morning Netilat Yadayim (Netilat Yadayim Shacharit)

Choulhan Aroukh, Orach Chaim 4 — 23 seifim

Opening of the Siman — Choulhan Aroukh with the glosses of the Rama (Seif Alef)
דִּינֵי נְטִילַת יָדַיִם. וּבוֹ כ״ג סְעִיפִים: יִרְחַץ יָדָיו וִיבָרֵךְ עַל נְטִילַת יָדָיִם. הגה: וְיֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים גַּם אֲשֶׁר יָצַר, וַאֲפִילּוּ לֹא עָשָׂה צְרָכָיו (וְכֵן נָהֲגוּ) (אֲבוּדַרְהַם).
The laws of netilat yadayim — and it contains 23 seifim: One washes one's hands upon waking and recites the berachah al netilat yadayim. Gloss (Rama): some also say asher yatzar, even if one has not relieved oneself (and so is the custom) (Abudraham).

The main themes of the Siman

עַל נְטִילַת יָדָיִם
The morning berachah
Upon waking, one washes one's hands and recites al netilat yadayim; the Rama adds asher yatzar, even without having relieved oneself (Abudraham).
רוּחַ רָעָה
The three pourings
One pours water three times to remove the rouach raah that rests on the hands during sleep. Into a vessel (keli), from the right to the left, over the right first.
כְּבְרִיָּה חֲדָשָׁה
The renewed soul
Each morning a person is ke-beriyah chadashah: he sanctifies his hands like the Kohen sanctifying his hands at the kiyor before the avodah. The netilah is a kedushah of awakening.
סְפֵק בְּרָכוֹת
The doubt over the berachah
Awake all night, having risen before amud ha-shachar, or having slept during the day: one washes the hands but without a berachah (sfek berachos le-hakel).
דְּבָרִים הַצְּרִיכִים נְטִילָה
What requires a netilah
Rising, leaving the bathroom or the bathhouse, cutting nails, removing shoes, touching one's feet, washing one's head, touching covered parts… require a new netilah (seif 18).
עַל נְקִיּוּת יָדָיִם
When lacking water
Without water, one cleans one's hands with a stone or earth and recites al nekiyut yadayim. This suffices for tefillah, but does not remove the rouach raah (seif 22).

Structure of the Siman — the 23 seifim

Seif Subject Theme Detail
א׳–ב׳ הברכה ורוח רעה The morning netilah Washing the hands and reciting al netilat yadayim (Rama: also asher yatzar); pouring three times to remove the rouach raah.
ג׳–ו׳ הזהירות והשיעור Care and measure Not touching the mouth/nose/ears/eyes before the netilah; not massaging certain places; no revi'it needed for the netilah of tefillah.
ז׳–י״א הכלי וההנהגה The vessel The things that are mekavev (vessel, into a vessel, water forbidden for benefit); taking the vessel in the right hand and giving it to the left to pour over the right first; not taking from one who has not done netilah.
י״ב–ט״ז שכשוך וספקות Doubts Shichshuch (counts for keriat shema/tefillah, not for the rouach raah); awake all night / before dawn / daytime sleep → doubts, without berachah; David and the sixty breaths.
י״ז–כ״א נקיות הגוף Cleanliness Rinsing the mouth; the things that require a netilah (seif 18); the dangers (bloodletting, shaving, nails without netilah); drying the face well; the covered places before tefillah/a meal (cf. siman 92).
כ״ב–כ״ג בלא מים ועיקר התקנה ✦ Le-maaseh Without water: cleaning (tzeror/afar) and reciting al nekiyut yadayim (suffices for tefillah, not for the rouach raah); the netilah was instituted only for keriat shema/tefillah — the birkot ha-shachar may precede the netilah, unless one slept unclothed.

Taam ha-Netilah — the twofold reason of the morning netilah

Source Register Contribution Central idea
ברכות ס׳ ע״ב · הזוהר Talmud · Kabbalah The source The morning netilah: removing the rouach raah / bat melech that rests on the hands during sleep (Zohar), and sanctifying the hands upon waking.
השולחן ערוך Halacha — seifim א׳–כ״א The conduct Reciting al netilat yadayim, pouring three times into a vessel, over the right first; the doubts without a berachah; the things that require a new netilah.

Frequently asked questions — Siman ד׳

Which berachah does one say over the morning netilat yadayim, according to Siman 4?

According to the Choulhan Aroukh, Orach Chaim 4:1, one washes one's hands upon waking and recites the berachah al netilat yadayim. The Rama adds that one also says asher yatzar, even if one has not relieved oneself (and so is the custom reported by the Abudraham).

Why does one pour water three times over the hands upon waking, according to Siman 4?

The Choulhan Aroukh (Orach Chaim 4:2) teaches that one is careful to pour water three times over the hands in order to remove the rouach raah that rests upon them during sleep. One pours into a vessel (4:8), from the right hand to the left, so as to pour over the right hand first (4:10).

What does one do if one stayed awake all night or slept during the day, according to Siman 4?

The Choulhan Aroukh (Orach Chaim 4:13–15) treats these cases as sfekos (doubts): one who stayed awake all night, who rose before amud ha-shachar, or who slept during the day, washes the hands but without reciting the berachah (sfek berachos le-hakel). For the precise case, one studies the Rav's position and follows the ruling of a competent Rav.

How does one perform the morning netilat yadayim when there is no water, according to Siman 4?

According to the Choulhan Aroukh (Orach Chaim 4:22), in the absence of water one cleans one's hands with a stone (tzeror), with earth (afar) or with any cleansing material, and recites al nekiyut yadayim. This suffices for tefillah, but it does not remove the rouach raah resting upon the hands.

Which actions require a new netilat yadayim during the day, according to Siman 4?

The Choulhan Aroukh (Orach Chaim 4:18) lists the things that require a new netilah: rising from bed, leaving the bathroom or the bathhouse, cutting one's nails, removing one's shoes, touching one's feet, washing one's head, touching the covered parts of the body… One must also be careful before tefillah or a meal not to touch the mekomot mechusim (4:21, cf. siman 92).
