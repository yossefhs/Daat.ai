דעת הרב

Level 4 — Daat HaRav (Alter Rebbe) — Siman רט״ו · עֲנִיַּת אָמֵן אַחַר הַבְּרָכוֹת

The Alter Rebbe’s way on עֲנִיַּת אָמֵן אַחַר הַבְּרָכוֹת : אָמֵן אַחַר בִּרְכוֹתָיו ; אֲחִיזָה לָזוֹ בָּזוֹ ; חוֹבָה לַעֲנוֹת אָמֵן ; אָמֵן יְתוֹמָה ; נָכְרִי, כּוּתִי וּמִין ; מְשַׁנֶּה מַטְבֵּעַ הַבְּרָכָה ; תִּינוֹק הַלּוֹמֵד ; בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה ; מֵאָה בְּרָכוֹת — in the sensibility of Habad.

Why a Level 4 devoted to the Alter Rebbe ? The Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch is not a commentary on the Mehaber (מחבר) — it is a complete, self-standing Shulchan Aruch, written by the Alter Rebbe (Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, founder of Habad, disciple of the Maggid (מגיד) of Mezeritch). Its singularity: it combines halakhah (הלכה) + טעמי המצוות + the inner dimension in a single work, and it decides with a unique talmudic rigour.

For Habad, the Alter Rebbe is הפוסק האחרון — his psak is the deciding authority. This page gathers the Rav’s way on siman רט״ו — עֲנִיַּת אָמֵן אַחַר הַבְּרָכוֹת (answering amen after the blessings), which in the Rav runs to ה׳ סְעִיפִים (five seifim) where the Mehaber has only four. One continuous movement runs through them, from amen toward what forbids it: one does not answer amen after one’s own blessing — except where two blessings hold to one another, as Barukh she-amar and Yishtabach; after another’s blessing, by contrast, answering is an obligation, even without having heard it, provided one knows which blessing it was; yet there are mouths from which no amen is received — the gentile whose blessing one has not heard in full, the min, one who has altered the formula, the child rehearsing his blessings emptily before his teacher. And from there the Rav slides toward the root of the matter: the unnecessary blessing, the Name of Heaven borne in vain, after which it is forbidden to answer amen; then the wider prohibition of causing one, even to complete the hundred blessings of the day; and he closes his Hilkhot Berakhot on the practical case in which one gains required blessings while causing none: the fruits of the meal, set aside for after the Birkat Hamazon.

1 · טקסט אדמו״ר הזקן

שולחן ערוך הרב — סימן רט״ו — עֲנִיַּת אָמֵן אַחַר הַבְּרָכוֹת

The full text of the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch

שולחן ערוך הרב, סימן רט״ו — עֲנִיַּת אָמֵן אַחַר הַבְּרָכוֹת — וּבוֹ ה׳ סְעִיפִים
The Alter Rebbe’s own text (Sefaria source Shulchan_Arukh_HaRav,_Orach_Chayim.215), followed by our English translation. The siman has five seifim (ה׳ סְעִיפִים ; seif k → .215.k).

סעיף א הָעוֹנֶה אָמֵן אַחַר בִּרְכוֹתָיו — מְגֻנֶּה, אֶלָּא בִּשְׁתֵּי בְּרָכוֹת שֶׁיֵּשׁ אֲחִיזָה לָזוֹ בָּזוֹ.

עֲנִיַּת אָמֵן אַחַר הַבְּרָכוֹת, וּבוֹ ה׳ סְעִיפִים:
הָעוֹנֶה אָמֵן אַחַר בִּרְכוֹתָיו — הֲרֵי זֶה מְגֻנֶּה, אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן בֵּרַךְ שְׁתֵּי בְּרָכוֹת אוֹ יוֹתֵר, שֶׁיֵּשׁ אֲחִיזָה לָזוֹ בָּזוֹ — עוֹנֶה בְּסוֹף כֻּלָּן "אָמֵן", כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן נ״ד. וְיֵשׁ חוֹלְקִין גַּם בָּזֶה כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר שָׁם. וְכֵן מִנְהַג מְדִינוֹת אֵלּוּ, וְאֵין לְשַׁנּוֹת הַמִּנְהָג.
וְאַחַר בְּרָכָה אַחֲרוֹנָה שֶׁל פֵּרוֹת וְכַיּוֹצֵא בָּהֶם — לְדִבְרֵי הַכֹּל אֵינוֹ עוֹנֶה "אָמֵן" אַחַר בִּרְכַּת עַצְמוֹ, כִּי אֵין לָהּ אֲחִיזָה עִם בְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה, שֶׁהֲרֵי רַשַּׁאי לְהַפְסִיק וּלְדַבֵּר בֵּינְתַיִם. וְאֵינוֹ דּוֹמֶה לְ"יִשְׁתַּבַּח" שֶׁהִיא בְּרָכָה אַחֲרוֹנָה אַחַר פְּסוּקֵי דְּזִמְרָה, וְיֵשׁ לָהּ אֲחִיזָה עִם בְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה שֶׁהִיא "בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר", לְפִי שֶׁאָסוּר לְהָשִׂיחַ בְּאֶמְצַע פְּסוּקֵי דְּזִמְרָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר שָׁם:

Answering amen after the blessings, and it contains five seifim. One who answers amen after his own blessings — this is unseemly, unless he has said two blessings or more that hold to one another: he then answers “amen” at the end of them all, as was explained in siman נ״ד. And there are those who dispute even this, as was explained there. But such is the custom of these countries, and the custom is not to be changed.
And after the final blessing over fruits and the like — by all opinions he does not answer “amen” after his own blessing, for it has no hold upon the first blessing, since he is permitted to interrupt and to speak in between. And it is not comparable to “Yishtabach,” which is a final blessing after Pesukei deZimra and does have a hold upon the first blessing, namely “Barukh she-amar,” because it is forbidden to converse in the middle of Pesukei deZimra, as was explained there.

סעיף ב אַחַר בִּרְכַּת יִשְׂרָאֵל — חוֹבָה לַעֲנוֹת אָמֵן ; נָכְרִי, כּוּתִי, מִין, מְשַׁנֶּה מַטְבֵּעַ, וְתִינוֹק הַלּוֹמֵד.

אֲבָל אַחַר בִּרְכַּת אָדָם אַחֵר מִיִּשְׂרָאֵל חוֹבָה לַעֲנוֹת "אָמֵן", אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהוּא אֵינוֹ מְחֻיָּב בִּבְרָכָה זוֹ כְּלָל, וַאֲפִלּוּ לֹא שְׁמָעָהּ מִתְּחִלָּתָהּ וְעַד סוֹפָהּ אֶלָּא סוֹפָהּ בִּלְבַד, אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ לֹא שְׁמָעָהּ כְּלָל, רַק שֶׁהוּא יוֹדֵעַ אֵיזוֹ בְּרָכָה הִיא שֶׁלֹּא תְּהֵא "אָמֵן" יְתוֹמָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קכ״ד.
אֲבָל נָכְרִי הַמְבָרֵךְ בְּרָכָה מִן הַבְּרָכוֹת שֶׁתִּקְּנוּ חֲכָמִים — אֵין עוֹנִים אַחֲרָיו "אָמֵן" אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן שָׁמַע כָּל הַבְּרָכָה מִפִּיו. וְכוּתִים עֲשָׂאוּם כְּנָכְרִים לְכָל דִּבְרֵיהֶם. אֲבָל אַחַר מִין, דְּהַיְנוּ הָאָדוּק לַעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה — אָסוּר לַעֲנוֹת "אָמֵן" אֲפִלּוּ שָׁמְעוּ כָּל הַבְּרָכָה מִפִּיו (לְפִי שֶׁסְּתָם מַחֲשֶׁבֶת מִין בְּהַזְכָּרַת הַשֵּׁם הִיא לַעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה).
וְכֵן מִי שֶׁשִּׁנָּה מַטְבֵּעַ הַבְּרָכָה — אֵין עוֹנִין אַחֲרָי[ו] "אָמֵן".
וְכֵן אֵין עוֹנִין "אָמֵן" אַחַר תִּינוֹק שֶׁלּוֹמֵד הַבְּרָכוֹת לִפְנֵי רַבּוֹ, שֶׁמֻּתָּר לְלַמֵּד לְתִינוֹק הַבְּרָכוֹת כְּתִקּוּנָן אֲפִלּוּ שֶׁלֹּא בִּזְמַנָּן, וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהֵם לְבַטָּלָה בִּשְׁעַת הַלִּמּוּד, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאָסוּר לְגָדוֹל הַמִּתְלַמֵּד. אֲבָל כְּשֶׁאֵינָן מְבָרְכִין לְבַטָּלָה — עוֹנִין אַחֲרֵיהֶם "אָמֵן", כְּגוֹן שֶׁמְּבָרְכִין אוֹתָן בִּזְמַנָּן כְּדֵי לִפְטֹר מֵחִיּוּבָן שֶׁהֵם חַיָּבִים בָּהֶן כְּדֵי לְחַנְּכָם בְּמִצְוֹת. וְכֵן בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁקּוֹרִין הַהַפְטָרָה בְּצִבּוּר, וּמְבָרְכִין לְפָנֶיהָ וּלְאַחֲרֶיהָ:

But after the blessing of another person of Israel it is an obligation to answer “amen” — even though he himself is not obligated in that blessing at all, and even if he did not hear it from its beginning to its end but only its end, or even if he did not hear it at all, provided that he knows which blessing it is, so that it not be an “orphaned amen,” as was explained in siman קכ״ד.
But a gentile who says one of the blessings instituted by the Sages — one does not answer “amen” after him unless one heard the entire blessing from his mouth. And the Kutim were made like gentiles in all their matters. But after a min, that is, one attached to idolatry — it is forbidden to answer “amen” even if they heard the entire blessing from his mouth (because the thought of a min, when he mentions the Name, is ordinarily toward idolatry).
And likewise one who altered the formula of the blessing — one does not answer “amen” after him.
And likewise one does not answer “amen” after a child who is learning the blessings before his teacher: for it is permitted to teach a child the blessings in their proper formula even not in their proper time, and although they are in vain at the time of learning — even though this is forbidden to an adult who is learning. But when they are not blessing in vain — one answers “amen” after them: for instance when they bless in their proper time, so as to discharge the obligation that rests upon them by way of their training in the mitzvot. And likewise at the time when the haftarah is read in public, and blessings are said before it and after it.

סעיף ג כָּל הַמְבָרֵךְ בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה — נוֹשֵׂא שֵׁם שָׁמַיִם לַשָּׁוְא, וְאָסוּר לַעֲנוֹת אַחֲרָיו.

כָּל הַמְבָרֵךְ בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה — הֲרֵי זֶה נוֹשֵׂא שֵׁם שָׁמַיִם לַשָּׁוְא, וַהֲרֵי הוּא כְּאִלּוּ נִשְׁבַּע לַשָּׁוְא, וְאָסוּר לַעֲנוֹת אָמֵן אַחֲרָיו. וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמִּן הַתּוֹרָה אֵין אִסּוּר בְּהַזְכָּרַת שֵׁם שָׁמַיִם לְבַטָּלָה אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁמַּזְכִּירוֹ בְּדִבְרֵי הֲבַאי שֶׁאֵין בָּהֶם צֹרֶךְ, וְלֹא בְּדֶרֶךְ בְּרָכָה אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה, מִכָּל מָקוֹם, מִדִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים אָסוּר אֲפִלּוּ בְּדֶרֶךְ בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה (אֲבָל לְצֹרֶךְ מֻתָּר לְהַזְכִּיר אֲפִלּוּ בִּדְבַר הָרְשׁוּת):

Whoever says a blessing that is not required — behold, he bears the Name of Heaven in vain, and he is as one who swore in vain, and it is forbidden to answer amen after him. And although from the Torah there is no prohibition in mentioning the Name of Heaven in vain except when one mentions it in idle matters in which there is no need, and not by way of a blessing, even one that is not required — nevertheless, from the words of the Scribes it is forbidden even by way of a blessing that is not required (but for a need it is permitted to mention it, even in a matter of one’s own choosing).

סעיף ד אָסוּר לִגְרֹם בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה — אַף בְּשַׁבָּת לְהַשְׁלִים מֵאָה בְּרָכוֹת.

אָסוּר לִגְרֹם בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה, כְּגוֹן כָּל שְׁנֵי דְּבָרִים שֶׁיָּכוֹל לְפָטְרָם בִּבְרָכָה אַחַת — אָסוּר לִגְרֹם שֶׁיִּצְטָרֵךְ לַחֲזֹר וּלְבָרֵךְ, כְּגוֹן שֶׁיַּפְסִיק בֵּינְתַיִם בְּשִׂיחָה, אוֹ בְּדָבָר אַחֵר הַגּוֹרֵם שֶׁיִּצְטָרֵךְ לַחֲזֹר וּלְבָרֵךְ.
וַאֲפִלּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת שֶׁחָסְרוּ הַרְבֵּה בְּרָכוֹת מִמִּנְיַן מֵאָה בְּרָכוֹת שֶׁחַיָּב אָדָם לְבָרֵךְ בְּכָל יוֹם — אָסוּר לִגְרֹם בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה כְּדֵי לְהַשְׁלִים לְמֵאָה.
בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁיָּכוֹל לִפְטֹר שְׁנֵי דְּבָרִים בִּבְרָכָה שֶׁהִיא מְיֻחֶדֶת לִשְׁנֵיהֶם, אֲבָל אִם הִיא מְיֻחֶדֶת לְדָבָר אֶחָד אֶלָּא שֶׁיָּכוֹל לִפְטֹר בָּהּ גַּם הַשֵּׁנִי בְּתוֹרַת טְפֵלָה — טוֹב יוֹתֵר לְהַרְבּוֹת בִּבְרָכוֹת, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן רי״ב:

It is forbidden to cause a blessing that is not required: thus, for any two things that one can discharge with a single blessing — it is forbidden to cause oneself to have to bless again, such as by interrupting in between with conversation, or with anything else that causes one to have to bless again.
And even on Shabbat, when many blessings are lacking from the count of the hundred blessings a person must say each day — it is forbidden to cause a blessing that is not required in order to complete the hundred.
In what case is this said? When one can discharge two things with a blessing that is specific to them both; but if it is specific to one thing, and one can discharge the second by it only as a subordinate item — it is better to multiply blessings, as was explained in siman רי״ב.

סעיף ה פֵּרוֹת בְּתוֹךְ הַסְּעוּדָה — טוֹב יוֹתֵר לְהָנִיחָם לְאַחַר בִּרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן.

לְפִיכָךְ, אִם הֵבִיאוּ לוֹ פֵּרוֹת בְּתוֹךְ הַסְּעוּדָה — טוֹב יוֹתֵר לְהָנִיחָם לְאַחַר בִּרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן, כְּדֵי שֶׁיְּבָרֵךְ אַחֲרֵיהֶם בִּרְכָתָם הָאַחֲרוֹנָה הַמְיֻחֶדֶת לָהֶם, וְלֹא יִפְטְרֵם בְּבִרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן שֶׁאֵינָהּ מְיֻחֶדֶת לָהֶם, וְאֵינָהּ פּוֹטַרְתָּם אֶלָּא מִשּׁוּם שֶׁהֵם נֶחְשָׁבִים מִכְּלַל הַסְּעוּדָה, הוֹאִיל וּבָאוּ קֹדֶם שֶׁמָּשְׁכוּ יְדֵיהֶם מִן הַפַּת. וְאִם בָּאוּ אַחַר כָּךְ — טְעוּנִים בְּרָכָה לִפְנֵיהֶם וּלְאַחֲרֵיהֶם וְאֵין נִפְטָרִים בְּבִרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן, שֶׁאֵין בִּרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן פּוֹטֶרֶת מֵעֵין ג׳ וְלֹא "בּוֹרֵא נְפָשׁוֹת רַבּוֹת", כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קע״ז וְר״ח. וְיֵשׁ חוֹלְקִים.
וְיֵשׁ לְהַחֲמִיר כַּסְּבָרָא הָרִאשׁוֹנָה. וּבִפְרָט בְּשַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב כְּדֵי לְהַשְׁלִים מֵאָה בְּרָכוֹת — יֵשׁ לוֹ לְהָנִיחָם לְאַחַר בִּרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן לְהַשְׁלִים בָּהֶם, וְלֹא לִסְמֹךְ עַל שְׁמִיעַת חֲזָרַת הַשְּׁלִיחַ צִבּוּר וּבִרְכַּת הַתּוֹרָה וְהַהַפְטָרָה:
סְלִיק הִלְכוֹת בְּרָכוֹת.
תַּם וְנִשְׁלַם שֶׁבַח לָאֵל בּוֹרֵא עוֹלָם.

Therefore, if fruits were brought to him during the meal — it is better to set them aside until after the Birkat Hamazon, so that he may say after them their own specific final blessing, and not discharge them with the Birkat Hamazon, which is not specific to them and discharges them only because they are reckoned as part of the meal, having come before the hands were withdrawn from the bread. And if they came afterwards — they require a blessing before them and after them, and are not discharged by the Birkat Hamazon, for the Birkat Hamazon does not discharge me’ein shalosh nor “Borei nefashot rabbot,” as was explained in siman קע״ז and ר״ח. And there are those who dispute this.
And one should be stringent like the first opinion. And especially on Shabbat and Yom Tov, in order to complete the hundred blessings — he should set them aside until after the Birkat Hamazon so as to complete the count with them, and not rely on hearing the repetition of the prayer leader, nor the blessings of the Torah and of the haftarah.
Here end the laws of blessings.
Finished and complete — praise to God, Creator of the world.

2 · למעשה

הלכה למעשה — מנהג חב״ד

Habad practice

Points of practice that follow directly from siman רט״ו in the sensibility of the Alter Rebbe and of Habad — set out at the level of principle, referring to the Rav for the detail of cases.

For the Habad Hassid — Siman רט״ו (עֲנִיַּת אָמֵן אַחַר הַבְּרָכוֹת)

  • ① Seif א. The opening principle: one does not answer amen after one’s own blessing — it is מְגֻנֶּה, unseemly. There is a single exception: when one has said two blessings or more that “hold to one another” (אֲחִיזָה לָזוֹ בָּזוֹ); one then answers “amen” at the end of them all, as in siman נ״ד · 54 of the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch — the case of Yishtabach, the final blessing of Pesukei deZimra, which holds to Barukh she-amar precisely because it is forbidden to converse between them. The Rav notes that some dispute even this case, and then rules without hesitation: such is the custom of these countries, and the custom is not to be changed. By contrast, after the final blessing over fruits and the like, all agree that one does not answer amen: that blessing has no hold upon the first, since one is permitted to interrupt and speak in between.
  • ② Seif ב. The reverse side: after another Jew’s blessing, answering amen is an obligation — even if one is not obligated in that blessing at all, even if one heard only its end, and even if one did not hear it at all, provided one knows which blessing it is: otherwise it would be an “orphaned amen” (siman קכ״ד · 124). Four cases follow in which one does not answer: the gentile who says a blessing instituted by the Sages — one answers only on condition of having heard the entire blessing from his mouth (the Kutim having, in every respect, the status of gentiles); the min, one attached to idolatry — there it is forbidden even after hearing it all, because his thought, when he mentions the Name, is ordinarily toward idolatry; one who has altered the formula of the blessing; and the child learning the blessings before his teacher, whose blessings are then in vain — the Rav notes in passing that it is permitted to teach him the blessings in their exact formula even out of their proper time, which remains forbidden to an adult who is learning. But as soon as the child blesses in its proper time, to discharge what his training obliges him to, one answers amen after him — and likewise for the blessings of the haftarah read in public.
  • ③ Seif ג. Here the siman pivots from amen to what makes amen impossible: the unnecessary blessing. One who says a בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ צְרִיכָה bears the Name of Heaven in vain and is as one who swore in vain; it is therefore forbidden to answer amen after him. The Rav then sets out the distinction of level that carries the whole difficulty: from the Torah, the prohibition of mentioning the Name in vain covers only a mention made in idle talk, with no need at all — and not a mention made “by way of a blessing,” even an unnecessary one; from the words of the Scribes, by contrast, it is forbidden even in the form of an unnecessary blessing. And he immediately adds the counterpart, in parentheses: for a need, mentioning the Name is permitted, even in an optional matter.
  • ④ Seif ד. One degree further: it is forbidden not only to say, but to cause (לִגְרֹם) an unnecessary blessing. Two things that could be covered by a single blessing: it is forbidden to arrange matters so as to have to bless again — by interrupting with conversation, or with anything else that forces one to begin again. The Rav applies the rule to the very case that would tempt one most: Shabbat, when many blessings are lacking from the count of the hundred daily ones — even there, it is forbidden to cause an unnecessary blessing in order to complete the hundred. He then bounds his own rule: it holds only where the single blessing is specific to both things; if it is specific to one and covers the second only as a subordinate item (טְפֵלָה), it is better, on the contrary, to multiply blessings (siman רי״ב · 212).
  • ⑤ Seif ה. The concrete application, and the Rav’s last word on the laws of blessings: fruits brought during the meal. Better to set them aside until after the Birkat Hamazon, so as to say over them their own specific final blessing, rather than let them be discharged by a Birkat Hamazon that is not specific to them and covers them only because they count as part of the meal, having arrived before the hands were withdrawn from the bread. If they arrive afterwards — a blessing before and after, since the Birkat Hamazon discharges neither me’ein shalosh nor Borei nefashot rabbot (simanim קע״ז · 177 and ר״ח · 208); the Rav notes that some dispute this, and then rules: one is stringent like the first opinion. And here the tension of seif ד resolves: on Shabbat and Yom Tov, to reach the hundred blessings, one sets aside precisely these fruits for after the Birkat Hamazon — blessings that are required, not contrived — rather than relying on hearing the prayer leader’s repetition and the blessings of the Torah and the haftarah. With that the Rav closes Hilkhot Berakhot: סְלִיק הִלְכוֹת בְּרָכוֹת.

⚠ This section sets out Habad practice grounded in the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch, siman רט״ו (the real text is reproduced in §1). It does not replace a rabbinic ruling for a particular case. For any concrete question — whether to answer amen in a given case, what to do after a doubtful blessing, how to complete the hundred blessings of Shabbat without contriving unnecessary ones — consult your Rav, and for Habad, a Habad Rav.
