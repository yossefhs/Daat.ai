Level 4 — Daat HaRav (Alter Rebbe) — Siman רי״ב · שֶׁהָעִקָּר פּוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל

The Alter Rebbe’s way on שֶׁהָעִקָּר פּוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל : עִקָּר וְטָפֵל ; תַּעֲרֹבֶת דָּגָן ; דָּג מָלוּחַ וּפַת ; יֵין שָׂרָף ; נְטִילַת יָדַיִם ; לְמַתֵּק הַשְּׁתִיָּה ; חָבִיב ; דַּעְתּוֹ עָלָיו ; שִׁנּוּי מָקוֹם — in the sensibility of Habad.

Why a Level 4 devoted to the Alter Rebbe ? The Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch is not a commentary on the Mehaber (מחבר) — it is a complete, self-standing Shulchan Aruch, written by the Alter Rebbe (Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, founder of Habad, disciple of the Maggid (מגיד) of Mezeritch). Its singularity: it combines halakhah (הלכה) + טעמי המצוות + the inner dimension in a single work, and it decides with a unique talmudic rigour.

For Habad, the Alter Rebbe is הפוסק האחרון — his psak is the deciding authority. This page gathers the Rav’s way on siman רי״ב — שֶׁהָעִקָּר פּוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל (that the primary exempts the secondary). The Mehaber gives this siman only two seifim; the Rav writes י״א סְעִיפִים (eleven seifim), and that gap is the whole subject: what the Shulchan Aruch states as a rule, the Alter Rebbe takes apart into its conditions. He begins by grading the degrees of subordination — from a mixture where the second ingredient merely seasons the first, down to two foods not mixed at all where bread itself becomes secondary to salted fish — and then lays down the rule that governs the siman: in any mixture containing one of the five grains, the grain is always primary, so long as one also means to eat it. Then come the borderline cases that really interest him: bread soaked in brandy before and after the meal, mezonot pastry soaked in drink or dipped in honey, bread crumbled into hot beer where no substance of grain remains. And then the conditions: subordination lasts only while the two are eaten together; it exempts from netilat yadayim as well; it can never cover what is eaten before drinking, since one may not enjoy this world without a prior blessing; it yields to the food that is always the most beloved; it requires that the secondary was before him or clearly in mind; and it lapses if one has moved from room to room between the primary and the secondary.

1 · טקסט אדמו״ר הזקן

שולחן ערוך הרב — סימן רי״ב — שֶׁהָעִקָּר פּוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל

The full text of the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch

שולחן ערוך הרב, סימן רי״ב — שֶׁהָעִקָּר פּוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל — וּבוֹ י״א סְעִיפִים
The Alter Rebbe’s own text (Sefaria source Shulchan_Arukh_HaRav,_Orach_Chayim.212), followed by our English translation. The siman comprises eleven seifim (י״א סְעִיפִים ; seif k → .212.k).

סעיף א כָּל שֶׁהוּא עִקָּר וְעִמּוֹ טְפֵלָה — מְבָרֵךְ עַל הָעִקָּר וּפוֹטֵר אֶת הַטְּפֵלָה.

שֶׁהָעִקָּר פּוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל, וּבוֹ י״א סְעִיפִים:
כָּל שֶׁהוּא עִקָּר וְעִמּוֹ טְפֵלָה — מְבָרֵךְ עַל הָעִקָּר וּפוֹטֵר אֶת הַטְּפֵלָה, בֵּין מִבְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה בֵּין מִבְּרָכָה אַחֲרוֹנָה.
אֵין צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר אִם הַטְּפֵלָה מְעֹרֶבֶת עִם הָעִקָּר, כְּגוֹן כָּל תַּעֲרֹבֶת שְׁנֵי מִינִים שֶׁהָאֶחָד הוּא עִקָּר וְהַשֵּׁנִי אֵינוֹ אֶלָּא לְתַקְּנוֹ וּלְהַכְשִׁירוֹ, אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם עִקָּר אֶלָּא שֶׁהָאֶחָד מְרֻבֶּה וְהָאֶחָד מוּעָט, שֶׁהָרֹב נֶחְשָׁב עִקָּר וְהַמּוּעָט טָפֵל, אוֹ בְּתַעֲרֹבֶת מַמָּשׁוּת דָּגָן, שֶׁאֲפִלּוּ הוּא מוּעָט נֶחְשָׁב עִקָּר.
וַאֲפִלּוּ אֵינוֹ מְעֹרָב מַמָּשׁ עִם הַטָּפֵל, אֶלָּא שֶׁנַּעֲשָׂה עַל דַּעַת לֶאֱכֹל שְׁנֵיהֶם יַחַד, כְּגוֹן עִסָּה מְמֻלֵּאת בְּפֵרוֹת, וְאָז אֲפִלּוּ אוֹכֵל הַטָּפֵל לְבַדּוֹ אַחַר שֶׁבֵּרַךְ עַל הָעִקָּר — אֵין צָרִיךְ לַחֲזֹר וּלְבָרֵךְ.
אֶלָּא אֲפִלּוּ אֵינָן מְעֹרָבִין כְּלָל זֶה עִם זֶה, אֶלָּא שֶׁעִקַּר הָאֲכִילָה הוּא מִמִּין אֶחָד וְהַשֵּׁנִי אֵינוֹ בָּא אֶלָּא מֵחֲמַת הָרִאשׁוֹן, שֶׁאִם לֹא הָיָה אוֹכֵל הָרִאשׁוֹן לֹא הָיָה אוֹכְלוֹ כְּלָל עַתָּה, הֲרֵי הוּא טָפֵל אֵלָיו וְנִפְטָר בְּבִרְכָתוֹ, אֲפִלּוּ הוּא לֶחֶם, שֶׁהוּא חָשׁוּב מִכָּל הַמִּינִים, כְּגוֹן מִי שֶׁחָפֵץ לֶאֱכֹל דָּג מָלוּחַ וּכְדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יַזִּיקֶנּוּ בִּגְרוֹנוֹ אוֹכֵל עִמּוֹ פַּת — הֲרֵי הוּא טְפֵלָה לַדָּג וְנִפְטֶרֶת בְּבִרְכָתוֹ.
וְהוּא שֶׁאֵינוֹ רָעֵב וְאֵינוֹ תָּאֵב כְּלָל לַאֲכִילַת הַפַּת, וְלֹא הָיָה אוֹכְלוֹ כְּלָל עַכְשָׁו אִם לֹא הַמָּלִיחַ, אֲבָל אִם הוּא תָּאֵב גַּם לַאֲכִילַת פַּת, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאוֹכְלוֹ עִם הַמָּלִיחַ — אֵינָהּ טְפֵלָה אֵלָיו, אֲפִלּוּ אִם תָּאֵב לוֹ יוֹתֵר, וְצָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ "הַמּוֹצִיא" וּפוֹטֵר אֶת הַמָּלִיחַ, הוֹאִיל וְהוּא מִדְּבָרִים שֶׁדַּרְכָּן לָבוֹא תּוֹךְ הַסְּעוּדָה, שֶׁהֵם נִפְטָרִים לְעוֹלָם בְּבִרְכַּת הַפַּת, אַף שֶׁאֵינָם בָּאִים מֵחֲמָתָהּ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קע״ז:

That the primary exempts the secondary, and it comprises eleven seifim. Whatever is primary (עִקָּר) and has a secondary (טְפֵלָה) with it — one blesses over the primary and thereby exempts the secondary, both as to the first blessing and as to the final blessing.
It goes without saying that this is so if the secondary is mixed with the primary: for instance, any mixture of two kinds of which one is primary and the second is there only to season it and make it fit; or even where both are primary, save that one is in large quantity and the other in small — for the majority is reckoned primary and the minority secondary; or in a mixture that has substance (מַמָּשׁוּת) of grain in it, which, even in small quantity, is reckoned primary.
And even if it is not literally mixed with the secondary, but was made with the intent of eating both together — such as a dough filled with fruit — then, even if he eats the secondary by itself after having blessed over the primary, he need not bless again.
More than that: even if they are not mixed with one another at all, but the main eating is of one kind and the second comes only on account of the first — such that, were he not eating the first, he would not be eating the second at all now — it is secondary to it and exempted by its blessing; and this even where it is bread, which is the most important of all the kinds: thus one who wishes to eat salted fish and, so that it should not harm his throat, eats bread with it — the bread is secondary to the fish and exempted by its blessing.
And that is provided he is not hungry and has no appetite at all for eating bread, and would not have eaten it now at all were it not for the salted fish; but if he does have an appetite for eating bread as well, even though he eats it with the salted fish — it is not secondary to it, even if he has a greater appetite for the salted fish; and he must bless « הַמּוֹצִיא » over the bread, which then exempts the salted fish, since it is of the things that customarily come within a meal, which are always exempted by the blessing over bread even when they do not come on its account, as explained in siman קע״ז (177).

סעיף ב פַּת שְׁרוּיָה בְּיֵין שָׂרָף לִפְנֵי הַסְּעוּדָה — הַפַּת עִקָּר וְהַיַּיִן טָפֵל.

וְכֵן מִי שֶׁאוֹכֵל לִפְנֵי סְעוּדָה פַּת שְׁרוּיָה בְּיֵין שָׂרָף, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁעִקַּר כַּוָּנָתוֹ בָּזֶה עַל הַיֵּין שָׂרָף, אֶלָּא מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהוּא חָזָק וְקָשֶׁה לוֹ לִשְׁתּוֹתוֹ לְבַדּוֹ שׁוֹרֶה בּוֹ פַּת וְאוֹכְלוֹ, מִכָּל מָקוֹם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהוּא לִפְנֵי הַסְּעוּדָה וַדַּאי כַּוָּנָתוֹ גַּם כֵּן לִסְעֹד הַלֵּב, וּלְפִיכָךְ אֵין הַפַּת טְפֵלָה לְהַיֵּין שָׂרָף, וְצָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ "הַמּוֹצִיא", וּפוֹטֵר אֶת הַיֵּין שָׂרָף אֲפִלּוּ אֵין כַּוָּנָתוֹ בּוֹ כְּדֵי לְעוֹרֵר תַּאֲוַת הַמַּאֲכָל, שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה נִפְטַר בְּבִרְכַּת הַפַּת אִם הָיָה שׁוֹתֵהוּ בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קע״ד.
וְכֵן אֲפִלּוּ אִם שׁוֹרֶה פַּת בְּיַיִן שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלָיו "בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגָּפֶן" אַף בְּתוֹךְ הַסְּעוּדָה — אֵין צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עַל הַבָּלוּעַ בַּפַּת הַשְּׁרוּיָה בּוֹ, שֶׁכֵּיוָן שֶׁכַּוָּנָתוֹ גַּם כֵּן לִסְעֹד הַלֵּב — הַפַּת עִקָּר וְהַיַּיִן טָפֵל, שֶׁכָּל תַּעֲרֹבֶת מִין דָּגָן עִם מִין אַחֵר — הַדָּגָן עִקָּר לְעוֹלָם, כָּל שֶׁמִּתְכַּוְּנִין גַּם כֵּן לַאֲכִילָתוֹ, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁעִקַּר הַכַּוָּנָה הוּא בִּשְׁבִיל מִין הָאַחֵר, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן ר״ח:

Likewise one who, before the meal, eats bread soaked in brandy (יֵין שָׂרָף): although his main intention in this is upon the brandy — only that, since it is strong and hard for him to drink by itself, he soaks bread in it and eats it — nevertheless, since this is before the meal, his intention is certainly also to sustain the heart (לִסְעֹד הַלֵּב); therefore the bread is not secondary to the brandy, and he must bless « הַמּוֹצִיא » over it, which exempts the brandy even if his intention in it is not to arouse the appetite — which would not have been exempted by the blessing over bread had he drunk it by itself, as explained in siman קע״ד (174).
And likewise, even if he soaks bread in wine over which one must bless « בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגָּפֶן » even within the meal — he need not bless over what is absorbed in the bread soaked in it; for since his intention is also to sustain the heart, the bread is primary and the wine secondary: for in any mixture of a grain-kind with another kind, the grain is always primary, so long as one also intends to eat it, even if the main intention is for the other kind, as explained in siman ר״ח (208).

סעיף ג פַּת הַבָּאָה בְּכִיסָנִין הַשְּׁרוּיָה בְּמַשְׁקֶה — וּמָתַי נַעֲשֵׂית טְפֵלָה לוֹ.

וּלְפִיכָךְ, אֲפִלּוּ פַּת הַבָּאָה בְּכִיסָנִין שֶׁמְּבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ "בּוֹרֵא מִינֵי מְזוֹנוֹת", אִם שׁוֹרֶה אוֹתָהּ בְּיַיִן אוֹ יֵין שָׂרָף וּשְׁאָר מַשְׁקִין — אֵין צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עַל מַשְׁקֶה הַבָּלוּעַ בָּהּ, וְלֹא עַל מַשְׁקֶה שֶׁעַל גַּבָּהּ, כְּגוֹן שֶׁמְּטַבֵּל בִּדְבַשׁ וְאוֹכֵל, וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם עִקַּר כַּוָּנָתוֹ עַל הַמַּשְׁקֶה (שֶׁכֵּיוָן שֶׁהִיא מֵה׳ מִינִים הִיא עִקָּר).
אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן אֵינוֹ מִתְכַּוֵּן לַאֲכִילָתָם כְּלָל, אֶלָּא כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יְטַנְּפוּ אֶת הַיָּדַיִם מֵהַדְּבַשׁ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קס״ח, אוֹ שׁוֹרֶה בְּיֵין שָׂרָף וְכַוָּנָתוֹ עַל הַיֵּין שָׂרָף בִּלְבַד, אֶלָּא שֶׁמִּפְּנֵי שֶׁהוּא חָזָק וְקָשֶׁה לוֹ לִשְׁתּוֹתוֹ לְבַדּוֹ הוּא שׁוֹרֶה בּוֹ פַּת וְאוֹכְלָהּ, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵינוֹ מִתְאַוֶּה לָהּ כְּלָל, כְּגוֹן מִי שֶׁשּׁוֹרֶה אַחַר הַסְּעוּדָה מְעַט פַּת בְּיֵין שָׂרָף וְאוֹכְלָהּ לְעַכֵּל הַמַּאֲכָל, שֶׁאָז נַעֲשֵׂית הַפַּת טְפֵלָה לַיֵּין שָׂרָף, וְצָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ "שֶׁהַכֹּל", כְּדִין יֵין שָׂרָף הַבָּא לְעַכֵּל הַמַּאֲכָל, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קע״ד.
(וְאִם שׁוֹרֶה בּוֹ מַאֲכָל שֶׁמִּתְאַוֶּה לוֹ וְאֵינוֹ מֵחֲמֵשֶׁת הַמִּינִים — בָּאנוּ לַמַּחֲלֹקֶת שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן ר״ב, שֶׁיֵּשׁ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁכָּל תַּעֲרֹבֶת ב׳ מִינִים שֶׁאֶחָד מֵהֶם הָיָה אוֹכְלוֹ גַּם אִלּוּ הָיָה לְבַדּוֹ וְהַשֵּׁנִי לֹא הָיָה אוֹכְלוֹ לְבַדּוֹ — נַעֲשָׂה זֶה טָפֵל לָזֶה הוֹאִיל וְאֵינוֹ אוֹכְלוֹ אֶלָּא עַל יְדֵי שֶׁמְּעֹרָב [בָּ]זֶה, וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁכַּוָּנָתוֹ עַל שְׁנֵיהֶם בְּשָׁוֶה. וְיֵשׁ חוֹלְקִים בָּזֶה. וּסְפֵק בְּרָכוֹת לְהָקֵל, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר שָׁם):

Therefore, even pat haba’ah bekhisnin (פַּת הַבָּאָה בְּכִיסָנִין, pastry) over which one blesses « בּוֹרֵא מִינֵי מְזוֹנוֹת »: if he soaks it in wine, or brandy, or other drinks — he need not bless over the drink absorbed in it, nor over the drink upon it, as when he dips it in honey and eats; and this even if his main intention is upon the drink (for since it is of the five grain species, it is the primary).
Unless he does not intend to eat them at all, but only so that his hands should not be soiled by the honey, as explained in siman קס״ח (168); or he soaks it in brandy and his intention is upon the brandy alone, only that, since it is strong and hard for him to drink by itself, he soaks bread in it and eats it, though he has no appetite for it at all — as one who, after the meal, soaks a little bread in brandy and eats it to digest the food: then the bread becomes secondary to the brandy, and he must bless « שֶׁהַכֹּל » over it, according to the law of brandy that comes to digest the food, as explained in siman קע״ד (174).
(And if he soaks in it a food for which he has an appetite and which is not of the five species — we have come to the mahloket set out in siman ר״ב (202): some say that in any mixture of two kinds, one of which he would have eaten even by itself and the second of which he would not have eaten by itself — the latter becomes secondary to the former, since he eats it only because it is mixed with it, and this even though his intention is equally upon both; and others dispute this. And a doubt in matters of blessings is resolved leniently, as explained there.)

סעיף ד מְפָרֵר לֶחֶם דַּק דַּק לְתוֹךְ שֵׁכָר — הַשֵּׁכָר עִקָּר.

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם מִתְכַּוֵּן וּמִתְאַוֶּה לִטְעֹם הַפַּת הַשְּׁרוּיָה בַּמַּשְׁקֶה, אֶלָּא שֶׁאֵינוֹ עוֹשֶׂה לַאֲכִילָה אֶלָּא לִשְׁתִיָּה, כְּגוֹן שֶׁמְּפָרֵר לֶחֶם דַּק דַּק וְנוֹתֵן לְתוֹךְ שֵׁכָר חַם כְּדֵי שֶׁיִּתֵּן בּוֹ טַעַם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁאֵין בַּלֶּחֶם מַמָּשׁוּת וְדָבָר חָשׁוּב — הַשֵּׁכָר עִקָּר (שֶׁלֹּא אָמְרוּ שֶׁכָּל תַּעֲרֹבֶת שֶׁיֵּשׁ בּוֹ דָּגָן הַדָּגָן עִקָּר, אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁיֵּשׁ בּוֹ מַמָּשׁוּת הַדָּגָן, וְלֹא רִבָּה בּוֹ מַשְׁקֶה הַרְבֵּה עַד שֶׁנַּעֲשָׂה רַךְ וְרָאוּי לִשְׁתִיָּה אֶלָּא הוּא עָב וְרָאוּי לַאֲכִילָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן ר״ח):

And even if he intends and desires to taste the bread soaked in the drink, only that he does so not for eating but for drinking — as when he crumbles bread very fine and puts it into hot beer to give it flavour — since there is in the bread neither substance nor anything of importance, the beer is primary. (For they said that in any mixture containing grain the grain is primary only where there is substance of grain in it, and one has not added so much liquid that it becomes thin and fit for drinking, but it is thick and fit for eating, as explained in siman ר״ח (208).)

סעיף ה מַה שֶּׁאוֹכֵל אַחַר כָּךְ לְבַדּוֹ — צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלָיו תְּחִלָּה וָסוֹף.

וְכָל זֶה בְּפַת אוֹ מַאֲכָל אַחֵר שֶׁאוֹכֵל עִם הַמָּלִיחַ אוֹ עִם הַיֵּין שָׂרָף בְּיַחַד, אֲבָל מַה שֶּׁאוֹכֵל אַחַר כָּךְ לְבַדּוֹ — צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלָיו תְּחִלָּה וָסוֹף, כֵּיוָן שֶׁבַּאֲכִילָה זוֹ אֵינֶנּוּ טָפֵל כְּלָל, וְגַם מִתְּחִלָּה לֹא נַעֲשָׂה טָפֵל אֶלָּא בִּשְׁעַת אֲכִילָתוֹ אוֹתָם יַחַד, וְלֹא מִתְּחִלַּת עֲשִׂיָּתָם יַחַד בַּאֲפִיָּה אוֹ בִּשּׁוּל, שֶׁעַל יְדֵי זֶה נַעֲשׂוּ כְּדָבָר אֶחָד מַמָּשׁ לִבְרָכָה, לְעִנְיָן שֶׁהָעִקָּר פּוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל אַף אִם אוֹכְלוֹ אַחַר כָּךְ לְבַד (אֲבָל לֹא לְעִנְיָן שֶׁיְּבָרֵךְ עַל הַטָּפֵל בִּרְכַּת הָעִקָּר אִם בָּא לְאָכְלוֹ לְבַדּוֹ מִתְּחִלָּה, מִטַּעַם שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר).
וְאִם גַּם בַּאֲכִילָה זוֹ הוּא טָפֵל — אֵין צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלָיו אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאוֹכְלוֹ לְבַדּוֹ, כְּגוֹן מִי שֶׁאוֹכֵל פַּת לְמַתֵּק הַשְּׁתִיָּה שֶׁשָּׁתָה כְּבָר — אֵין צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ, שֶׁהִיא טְפֵלָה לַמַּשְׁקֶה וְנִפְטֶרֶת בְּבִרְכָתוֹ:

And all this applies to bread, or another food, that he eats together with the salted fish or with the brandy; but what he eats afterwards by itself — he must bless over it before and after, since in that eating it is not secondary at all; and because from the outset it became secondary only at the time he ate them together, and not from the time they were made together by baking or by cooking — for through that they become literally one thing as regards the blessing, such that the primary exempts the secondary even if he eats it afterwards by itself. (But not to the extent that he would bless the primary’s blessing over the secondary if he came to eat it by itself from the outset, for a reason that will be explained.)
And if in this eating too it is secondary — he need not bless over it, even though he eats it by itself: thus one who eats bread to sweeten the drink he has already drunk — he need not bless over it, for it is secondary to the drink and exempted by its blessing.

סעיף ו כָּל פַּת שֶׁהִיא טְפֵלָה לְעִנְיַן בְּרָכָה — טְפֵלָה גַּם לִנְטִילַת יָדַיִם.

וְכָל פַּת שֶׁהִיא טְפֵלָה לְעִנְיַן בְּרָכָה — הִיא טְפֵלָה גַּם כֵּן לְעִנְיַן נְטִילַת יָדַיִם, וְאֵין צָרִיךְ לִטּוֹל יָדָיו, אֲפִלּוּ אוֹכֵל כְּבֵיצָה.
וְטוֹב לִמְנֹעַ מִלֶּאֱכֹל פַּת לְמַתֵּק הַשְּׁתִיָּה, כִּי מִי יוּכַל לְהַבְחִין אִם אוֹכְלָהּ לְמַתֵּק הַשְּׁתִיָּה אוֹ לְמִלּוּי כֶּרֶס, שֶׁאָז צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ "הַמּוֹצִיא":

And any bread that is secondary as regards the blessing is secondary also as regards netilat yadayim: he need not wash his hands, even if he eats an egg’s volume (כְּבֵיצָה) of it.
And it is good to refrain from eating bread to sweeten a drink, for who can discern whether he eats it to sweeten the drink or to fill his belly — in which case he must bless « הַמּוֹצִיא » over it.

סעיף ז הָאוֹכֵל קֹדֶם הַשְּׁתִיָּה — צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ בְּרָכָה הַמְיֻחֶדֶת לָהּ.

וְכָל זֶה בְּמִתּוּק הַשְּׁתִיָּה שֶׁשָּׁתָה כְּבָר, אֲבָל הָאוֹכֵל גַּרְעִינֵי גֻּדְגְּ[דָ]נִיּוֹת וְכַיּוֹצֵא בָּהֶם לְמַתֵּק הַשְּׁתִיָּה שֶׁיִּשְׁתֶּה, שֶׁאֲכִילָתָם גּוֹרֶרֶת לוֹ תַּאֲוָה לִשְׁתִיָּה, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֲכִילָתָם טְפֵלָה לְהַשְּׁתִיָּה וְהָיְתָה נִפְטֶרֶת בְּבִרְכָתָהּ אִם הָיָה אוֹכֵל אַחַר שֶׁבֵּרַךְ עַל הַשְּׁתִיָּה, מִכָּל מָקוֹם, כְּשֶׁאוֹכֵל קֹדֶם הַשְּׁתִיָּה אִי אֶפְשָׁר שֶׁיִּפָּטֵר בְּבִרְכַּת הַשְּׁתִיָּה שֶׁיְּבָרֵךְ אַחַר אֲכִילָתוֹ, כִּי אָסוּר לֵהָנוֹת מֵהָעוֹלָם הַזֶּה בְּלֹא בְּרָכָה בַּתְּחִלָּה, לָכֵן צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עֲלֵיהֶם בְּרָכָה הַמְיֻחֶדֶת לָהֶם.
וְכֵן הָרוֹצֶה לִשְׁתּוֹת בַּבֹּקֶר אוֹ אַחַר שֵׁנַת צָהֳרַיִם, וְרוֹצֶה לֶאֱכֹל מְעַט תְּחִלָּה כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יִשְׁתֶּה עַל לֵב רֵיקָן, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֲכִילָה זוֹ הִיא צֹרֶךְ הַשְּׁתִיָּה וּטְפֵלָה אֵלֶיהָ — צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ בְּרָכָה הַמְיֻחֶדֶת לָהּ, כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יֵהָנֶה מֵהָעוֹלָם הַזֶּה בְּלֹא בְּרָכָה:

And all this concerns the sweetening of a drink he has already drunk; but one who eats gudganiyot pits (גַּרְעִינֵי גֻּדְגְּ[דָ]נִיּוֹת) and the like to sweeten the drink he is about to drink — for eating them draws on his desire to drink — although their eating is secondary to the drink and would have been exempted by its blessing had he eaten them after blessing over the drink, nevertheless, when he eats them before the drinking it is impossible for them to be exempted by the blessing over the drink which he will say after eating them, for it is forbidden to derive benefit from this world without a blessing beforehand; therefore he must bless over them the blessing particular to them.
And likewise one who wishes to drink in the morning, or after the midday sleep, and wishes to eat a little first so as not to drink on an empty heart (עַל לֵב רֵיקָן) — although this eating is a need of the drinking and secondary to it — he must bless over it the blessing particular to it, so as not to derive benefit from this world without a blessing.

סעיף ח בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים — וּלְעוֹלָם יַרְבֶּה הָאָדָם בַּבְּרָכוֹת הַצְּרִיכוֹת.

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁשּׁוֹתֶה יַיִן שֶׁבִּרְכָתוֹ "בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגָּפֶן", שֶׁאִי אֶפְשָׁר לְבָרֵךְ בְּרָכָה זוֹ עַל הַמַּאֲכָל, אֲבָל הַשּׁוֹתֶה מַשְׁקֶה שֶׁבִּרְכָתוֹ "שֶׁהַכֹּל" — יֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁיְּבָרֵךְ עַל הַמַּאֲכָל שֶׁאוֹכֵל תְּחִלָּה "שֶׁהַכֹּל" וְלֹא בִּרְכָתוֹ הַמְיֻחֶדֶת לוֹ, שֶׁכֵּיוָן שֶׁאֲכִילָתוֹ אֵינָהּ אֶלָּא לְצֹרֶךְ הַשְּׁתִיָּה וּטְפֵלָה אֵלֶיהָ — מְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ בִּרְכַּת הַשְּׁתִיָּה שֶׁהִיא עִקָּר.
אֲבָל כָּל טָפֵל שֶׁאֵין אֲכִילָתוֹ טְפֵלָה אֶל הָעִקָּר אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁאוֹכֵל שְׁנֵיהֶם יַחַד אוֹ הַטָּפֵל אַחַר הָעִקָּר, וּכְשֶׁאוֹכְלוֹ קֹדֶם הָעִקָּר אֵין אֲכִילָתוֹ טְפֵלָה אֵלָיו כְּלָל, כְּגוֹן שֶׁהֵבִיאוּ אֵלָיו פַּת אוֹ מַאֲכָל אַחֵר לֶאֱכֹל עִם הַמָּלִיחַ שֶׁלֹּא יַזִּיקֶנּוּ בִּגְרוֹנוֹ אוֹ לְמַתֵּק הַשְּׁתִיָּה אַחַר שֶׁיִּשְׁתֶּה, וּכְשֶׁבָּא לֶאֱכֹל מִמֶּנּוּ לִפְנֵי הַמָּלִיחַ אוֹ לִפְנֵי הַשְּׁתִיָּה אֵין אֲכִילָה זוֹ טְפֵלָה אֵלֶיהָ כְּלָל — צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ בְּרָכָה הַמְיֻחֶדֶת לָהּ וְלֹא בִּרְכַּת הַמָּלִיחַ אוֹ הַמַּשְׁקֶה, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהִיא "שֶׁהַכֹּל".
לְפִיכָךְ, טוֹב שֶׁיֹּאכַל מִמֶּנּוּ מְעַט קֹדֶם בִּרְכַּת הַמָּלִיחַ אוֹ הַמַּשְׁקֶה, כְּדֵי שֶׁיְּבָרֵךְ עָלָיו בְּרָכָה הַמְיֻחֶדֶת לוֹ, וְלֹא יִפְטְרֶנּוּ בִּבְרָכָה אַחֶרֶת בְּתוֹרַת טְפֵלָה.
וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמִּן הַדִּין אֵין לָחֹשׁ לְכָךְ, שֶׁאַף שֶׁבִּרְכַּת הָעִקָּר הִיא "שֶׁהַכֹּל" שֶׁהִיא בְּרָכָה כּוֹלֶלֶת וּבִרְכַּת הַטְּפֵלָה הִיא בְּרָכָה מְבֹרֶרֶת וּפוֹרֶטֶת — אֵין לָהּ דִּין קְדִימָה עַל הָעִקָּר, כֵּיוָן שֶׁאֵינוֹ אוֹכֵל עַתָּה הַטָּפֵל אֶלָּא בִּשְׁבִיל אֲכִילַת הָעִקָּר, אִם כֵּן קֹדֶם שֶׁהִתְחִיל בַּאֲכִילַת הָעִקָּר שֶׁעֲדַיִן אֵינוֹ חָפֵץ לֶאֱכֹל הַטָּפֵל — אֵינוֹ מְחֻיָּב לְאָכְלוֹ בִּשְׁבִיל קְדִימַת בִּרְכָתוֹ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן רי״א, מִכָּל מָקוֹם כֵּיוָן שֶׁהָעִקָּר וְהַטָּפֵל אֵין בִּרְכוֹתֵיהֶם שָׁווֹת, וְאִם יְבָרֵךְ עַל הָעִקָּר תְּחִלָּה לֹא יְבָרֵךְ כְּלָל עַל הַטָּפֵל, וְאִם יְבָרֵךְ עַל הַטָּפֵל תְּחִלָּה יַחֲזֹר וִיבָרֵךְ עַל הָעִקָּר — טוֹב יוֹתֵר לַעֲשׂוֹת כֵּן, לְהַרְבּוֹת בַּבְּרָכוֹת, כִּי לְעוֹלָם יַרְבֶּה הָאָדָם בַּבְּרָכוֹת הַצְּרִיכוֹת וִימַעֵט בְּשֶׁאֵינָן צְרִיכוֹת, וְזוֹ הִיא בְּרָכָה הַצְּרִיכָה, שֶׁהֲרֵי הִיא מְיֻחֶדֶת לְהַטָּפֵל וְלֹא לְהָעִקָּר, וְגַם (בְּ)בִרְכַּת הָעִקָּר אֵינָהּ מְיֻחֶדֶת אֶל הַטָּפֵל אֶלָּא כּוֹלֶלֶת גַּם אֶת הַטָּפֵל, וְלֹא צִוּוּ לְמַעֵט אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁיָּכוֹל לִפְטֹר שְׁנֵי דְּבָרִים בִּבְרָכָה אַחַת הַמְיֻחֶדֶת לִשְׁנֵיהֶם:

In what case is this said? When he drinks wine, whose blessing is « בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגָּפֶן », for it is impossible to say that blessing over food. But one who drinks a beverage whose blessing is « שֶׁהַכֹּל » — some say that he should bless « שֶׁהַכֹּל » over the food he eats first, and not the blessing particular to it: since his eating is only for the need of the drinking and secondary to it, he blesses over it the blessing of the drink, which is primary.
But any secondary whose eating is secondary to the primary only when he eats both together or the secondary after the primary, and which, when he eats it before the primary, is not secondary to it at all — for instance where bread or another food was brought to him to eat with the salted fish so that it should not harm his throat, or to sweeten the drink after he drinks, and he comes to eat of it before the salted fish or before the drink, this eating not being secondary to it at all — he must bless over it the blessing particular to it, and not the blessing of the salted fish or of the drink, even though the latter is « שֶׁהַכֹּל ».
Therefore it is good that he eat a little of it before the blessing over the salted fish or the drink, so that he may bless over it the blessing particular to it, and not have it exempted by another blessing under the rubric of a secondary.
And although by strict law there is no need to be concerned for this — for even though the primary’s blessing is « שֶׁהַכֹּל », which is an inclusive blessing, and the secondary’s blessing is a distinct and particular one, it has no right of precedence over the primary, since he eats the secondary now only for the sake of eating the primary; consequently, before he has begun eating the primary, when he does not yet wish to eat the secondary, he is not obliged to eat it for the sake of the precedence of its blessing, as explained in siman רי״א (211) — nevertheless, since the primary and the secondary do not have the same blessings, and if he blesses over the primary first he will not bless over the secondary at all, whereas if he blesses over the secondary first he will then bless again over the primary — it is better to do so, in order to increase blessings; for a person should always increase the necessary blessings and reduce those that are not necessary, and this is a necessary blessing, since it is particular to the secondary and not to the primary, and the primary’s blessing, for its part, is not particular to the secondary but merely includes it; and they enjoined reducing only where one can exempt two things with a single blessing particular to both.

סעיף ט עִקָּר הֶחָבִיב לְעוֹלָם יוֹתֵר מִן הַטָּפֵל — מְבָרֵךְ עָלָיו תְּחִלָּה.

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁאֵין הָעִקָּר חָבִיב לוֹ יוֹתֵר מֵהַטָּפֵל, אֶלָּא שֶׁהַשָּׁעָה צְרִיכָה לְכָךְ לַעֲשׂוֹת מִמֶּנּוּ עִקָּר, כְּגוֹן שֶׁאָכַל כְּבָר פֵּרוֹת מְתוּקִין בְּיוֹתֵר וְהֻצְרַךְ לֶאֱכֹל אַחֲרֵיהֶם מָלִיחַ לְחַתֵּךְ הַלֵּחוֹת הַנִּקְבָּצוֹת בָּאִצְטוֹמְכָא, וְאוֹכֵל עִמּוֹ אֵיזֶה מַאֲכָל שֶׁלֹּא יַזִּיקֶנּוּ בִּגְרוֹנוֹ, אֲבָל אֵין הַמָּלִיחַ חָבִיב עָלָיו וְאֵינוֹ מִתְאַוֶּה לוֹ כְּלָל, כְּמוֹ שֶׁאֵינוֹ מִתְאַוֶּה לְהַמַּאֲכָל עַכְשָׁו אִם לֹא הַמָּלִיחַ.
אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ אִם הָעִקָּר חָבִיב עָלָיו עַכְשָׁו, אֶלָּא שֶׁבְּרֹב הַפְּעָמִים הַטָּפֵל חָבִיב עָלָיו יוֹתֵר, וְאִם כֵּן אֵין קְדִימָה לְהָעִקָּר, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן רי״א.
וְאֵין צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר אִם גַּם עַכְשָׁו הַטָּפֵל חָבִיב עָלָיו יוֹתֵר, אֶלָּא שֶׁאַף עַל פִּי כֵן לֹא הָיָה אוֹכְלוֹ אִם לֹא שֶׁהֻצְרַךְ לֶאֱכֹל הַמָּלִיחַ, וּמִפְּנֵי זֶה הוּא טָפֵל אֵלָיו. וְאַף שֶׁאַף עַל פִּי כֵן אֵין צָרִיךְ לְהַקְדִּים הַטָּפֵל מִן הַדִּין אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהוּא חָבִיב, כֵּיוָן שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה אוֹכְלוֹ כְּלָל אִם לֹא הָעִקָּר, מִכָּל מָקוֹם, כְּדֵי לְהַרְבּוֹת בַּבְּרָכוֹת — טוֹב לְהַקְדִּימוֹ, כֵּיוָן שֶׁאֵין לְהָעִקָּר דִּין קְדִימָה מִצַּד עַצְמוֹ.
וְאַף אִם מִצַּד בִּרְכָתוֹ יֵשׁ לוֹ דִּין קְדִימָה, כְּגוֹן שֶׁבִּרְכָתוֹ "בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה" אוֹ "בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָעֵץ" וּבִרְכַּת הַטָּפֵל הִיא "שֶׁהַכֹּל", מִכָּל מָקוֹם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁאִם יְבָרֵךְ עַל הָעִקָּר תְּחִלָּה "בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָעֵץ" אוֹ "בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה" לֹא יְבָרֵךְ כְּלָל עַל הַטָּפֵל שֶׁבִּרְכָתוֹ "שֶׁהַכֹּל נִהְיָה בִּדְבָרוֹ" — לֹא שַׁיָּךְ כְּלָל בָּזֶה קְדִימָה, וְיוֹתֵר טוֹב לְהַרְבּוֹת בַּבְּרָכוֹת, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר.
אֲבָל אִם הָעִקָּר חָבִיב עָלָיו לְעוֹלָם יוֹתֵר מִן הַטָּפֵל — צָרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלָיו תְּחִלָּה, שֶׁכֵּיוָן שֶׁהוּא חָשׁוּב מִמֶּנּוּ וּמֻקְדָּם לִבְרָכָה מֵחֲמַת חֲבִיבוּת עַצְמוֹ וְלֹא מֵחֲמַת בִּרְכָתוֹ — רָאוּי הוּא לְבָרֵךְ עָלָיו וְלִפְטֹר הַטָּפֵל בְּבִרְכָתוֹ מֵחֲמַת טְפֵלָתוֹ, שֶׁזֶּהוּ עִקַּר מַעֲלַת חֲשִׁיבוּתוֹ כְּשֶׁפּוֹטֵר דָּבָר אַחֵר בְּבִרְכָתוֹ, וְלֹא קְדִימָה לְבַדָּהּ, שֶׁאֵין בָּהּ מַעֲלָה כָּל כָּךְ לַדְּבָרִים הַחֲשׁוּבִים מִצַּד עַצְמָן, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן רי״א.
וְאַף אִם בִּרְכַּת הָעִקָּר הִיא "שֶׁהַכֹּל" וּבִרְכַּת הַטָּפֵל הִיא מְבֹרֶרֶת וּפְרָטִית — אֵין זֶה מוֹעִיל לְהַקְדִּימוֹ, כֵּיוָן שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה אוֹכְלוֹ כְּלָל אִם לֹא הָעִקָּר.
וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם עַכְשָׁו כְּשֶׁאוֹכֵל הַטָּפֵל עִם הָעִקָּר הַטָּפֵל חָבִיב עָלָיו יוֹתֵר אֶלָּא שֶׁכְּשֶׁאוֹכְלוֹ לְבַדּוֹ אֵינוֹ חָבִיב לוֹ כְּמוֹ הָעִקָּר — אֵין לוֹ לְבָרֵךְ עָלָיו כְּלָל, כֵּיוָן שֶׁכְּשֶׁבָּא לְאָכְלוֹ לְבַדּוֹ הֲרֵי אֵינוֹ חָבִיב עָלָיו כְּמוֹ הָעִקָּר, וּכְשֶׁבָּא לְאָכְלוֹ עִם הָעִקָּר הֲרֵי הוּא טָפֵל אֵלָיו, וְכָל עִקָּר וְטָפֵל אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהַטָּפֵל חָבִיב — מְבָרֵךְ עַל הָעִקָּר וּפוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל הַנֶּאֱכָל עִמּוֹ אוֹ אַחֲרָיו:

In what case is this said? When the primary is not more beloved to him (חָבִיב) than the secondary, but the hour requires that it be made the primary — as where he has already eaten overly sweet fruits and needed to eat something salted after them to cut the humours gathered in the stomach (אִצְטוֹמְכָא), and he eats some food with it so that it should not harm his throat, while the salted food is not beloved to him and he has no appetite for it at all, just as he has no appetite for that food now were it not for the salted food.
Or even if the primary is beloved to him now, only that most of the time the secondary is more beloved to him — so that the primary has no precedence, as explained in siman רי״א (211).
And needless to say where now too the secondary is more beloved to him, yet even so he would not have eaten it had he not needed to eat the salted food, and for that reason it is secondary to it. And although even so he need not put the secondary first by strict law, even though it is beloved, since he would not have eaten it at all were it not for the primary — nevertheless, in order to increase blessings, it is good to put it first, since the primary has no right of precedence in its own regard.
And even if from the side of its blessing it has a right of precedence — for instance where its blessing is « בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה » or « בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָעֵץ » and the secondary’s is « שֶׁהַכֹּל » — nevertheless, since if he blesses « בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָעֵץ » or « בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה » over the primary first he will not bless at all over the secondary, whose blessing is « שֶׁהַכֹּל נִהְיָה בִּדְבָרוֹ » — precedence has no bearing here at all, and it is better to increase blessings, as explained.
But if the primary is always more beloved to him than the secondary — he must bless over it first; for since it is more important than it and precedes it for the blessing on account of its own belovedness and not on account of its blessing — it is fitting to bless over it and to exempt the secondary by its blessing by virtue of its subordination: for that is the chief excellence of its importance, when it exempts another thing by its blessing, and not precedence alone, in which there is not so much excellence for things important in themselves, as explained in siman רי״א (211).
And even if the primary’s blessing is « שֶׁהַכֹּל » and the secondary’s is distinct and particular — that does not avail to put it first, since he would not have eaten it at all were it not for the primary.
And even if now, when he eats the secondary with the primary, the secondary is more beloved to him, but when he eats it by itself it is not as beloved to him as the primary — he should not bless over it at all: since when he comes to eat it by itself it is not as beloved to him as the primary, and when he comes to eat it with the primary it is secondary to it; and in every case of primary and secondary, even though the secondary is beloved — one blesses over the primary and exempts the secondary eaten with it or after it.

סעיף י אֵין הַטָּפֵל נִפְטָר אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁהָיָה לְפָנָיו אוֹ שֶׁהָיְתָה דַּעְתּוֹ עָלָיו.

לְעוֹלָם אֵין הַטָּפֵל נִפְטָר בְּבִרְכַּת הָעִקָּר, וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם הֵם שְׁנֵי מִינִים שֶׁבִּרְכוֹתֵיהֶם שָׁווֹת, אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁהָיָה הַטָּפֵל לְפָנָיו, אוֹ שֶׁהָיְתָה דַּעְתּוֹ עָלָיו בְּבֵרוּר בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁבֵּרַךְ עַל הָעִקָּר, אֲבָל אִם אַחַר הַבְּרָכָה הֱבִיאוּהוּ לְפָנָיו וְלֹא הָיְתָה דַּעְתּוֹ עָלָיו בְּבֵרוּר, בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁאִם לֹא הָיָה טָפֵל הָיָה צָרִיךְ לַחֲזֹר וּלְבָרֵךְ — אֵין טְפֵלָתוֹ מוֹעֶלֶת כְּלוּם לְפָטְרוֹ בְּבִרְכַּת הָעִקָּר, לֹא מִבְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁבִּרְכוֹתֵיהֶם שָׁווֹת, וְעַל דֶּרֶךְ מַה שֶּׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן ר״ו, וְלֹא מִבְּרָכָה אַחֲרוֹנָה כְּשֶׁאֵין בִּרְכוֹתֵיהֶם [הָאַחֲרוֹנוֹת] שָׁווֹת, אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן קָבַע עַצְמוֹ עַל הָעִקָּר, וְהוּא דָּבָר שֶׁקְּבִיעוּת מוֹעֶלֶת בּוֹ מִפְּנֵי שֶׁדַּרְכּוֹ בְּכָךְ, כְּגוֹן פַּת, וְכֵן יַיִן, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן ר״ו, וְכֵן שֵׁכָר וּדְבַשׁ בִּמְדִינוֹת אֵלּוּ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן רי״ג.
וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, מִי שֶׁאוֹכֵל פַּת אוֹ מַאֲכָל אַחֵר לְמַתֵּק הַשְּׁתִיָּה וְדַרְכּוֹ בְּכָךְ בְּרֹב הַפְּעָמִים — הֲרֵי זֶה כְּאִלּוּ הָיְתָה דַּעְתּוֹ עָלָיו.
וְאִם אֵין דַּרְכּוֹ בְּכָךְ — מְבָרֵךְ עַל הַפַּת "שֶׁהַכֹּל" כְּבִרְכַּת הַמַּשְׁקֶה שֶׁהִיא טְפֵלָה אֵלָיו.
(אֲבָל אֵין צָרִיךְ לִטּוֹל יָדָיו תְּחִלָּה, אַף אִם הוּא בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְבָרֵךְ עָלֶיהָ "הַמּוֹצִיא", כְּגוֹן שֶׁהִיא טְפֵלָה לְדָבָר שֶׁאֵין בִּרְכָתוֹ "שֶׁהַכֹּל", וּלְעִנְיַן נְטִילַת יָדַיִם לֹא שַׁיָּךְ לְחַלֵּק בֵּין אִם הָיְתָה דַּעְתּוֹ עַל הַטָּפֵל בִּשְׁעַת בִּרְכַּת הָעִקָּר אוֹ שֶׁנִּמְלַךְ עָלָיו אַחַר הַבְּרָכָה, אֶלָּא כֵּיוָן שֶׁאוֹכְלָהּ בְּתוֹרַת טְפֵלָה לְדָבָר אַחֵר — פָּטוּר מִנְּטִילַת יָדַיִם, שֶׁלֹּא תִּקְּנוּ לַפַּת אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁאוֹכְלָהּ לִסְעֹד הַלֵּב, שֶׁסְּעוּדָתָהּ רְגִילָה וּמְצוּיָה תָּמִיד, וְיֵשׁ לָחֹשׁ לְסֶרֶךְ תְּרוּמָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קנ״ח):

The secondary is never exempted by the blessing over the primary — and this even where they are two kinds whose blessings are the same — unless the secondary was before him, or he had it clearly in mind at the time he blessed over the primary. But if after the blessing they brought it before him and he did not have it clearly in mind, in such a way that, had it not been secondary, he would have had to bless again — its being secondary avails nothing to exempt it by the blessing over the primary: not as to the first blessing, even though their blessings are the same, along the lines of what was explained in siman ר״ו (206); and not as to the final blessing, where their [final] blessings are not the same — unless he settled himself (קָבַע) upon the primary, and it is a thing for which settling avails because such is its way, as bread, and likewise wine, as explained in siman ר״ו (206), and likewise beer and honey in these lands, as will be explained in siman רי״ג (213).
And nonetheless, one who eats bread or another food to sweeten the drink and whose way this is most of the time — it is as though he had it in mind.
And if this is not his way — he blesses « שֶׁהַכֹּל » over the bread, like the blessing of the drink to which it is secondary.
(But he need not wash his hands first, even where the case is such that he must bless « הַמּוֹצִיא » over it, for instance where it is secondary to something whose blessing is not « שֶׁהַכֹּל »; for as regards netilat yadayim there is no room to distinguish between his having had the secondary in mind at the time of the blessing over the primary and his having changed his mind about it after the blessing: since he eats it under the rubric of a secondary to another thing — he is exempt from netilat yadayim; for they instituted it for bread only when one eats it to sustain the heart, whose meal is regular and constantly frequent, and where there is cause to be concerned for accustoming to terumah (סֶרֶךְ תְּרוּמָה), as explained in siman קנ״ח (158).)

סעיף י״א טְפֵלָה הַנֶּאֱכֶלֶת אַחַר הָעִקָּר — בַּמָּקוֹם שֶׁאָכַל הָעִקָּר וְלֹא שִׁנָּה מְקוֹמוֹ.

וְכָל טְפֵלָה הַנֶּאֱכֶלֶת אַחַר אֲכִילַת הָעִקָּר אֵינָהּ נִפְטֶרֶת בְּבִרְכָתוֹ אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן אוֹכְלָהּ בַּמָּקוֹם שֶׁאָכַל הָעִקָּר וְלֹא שִׁנָּה מְקוֹמוֹ בֵּינְתַיִם מֵחֶדֶר לְחֶדֶר, כְּמוֹ שֶׁלֹּא הָיְתָה נִפְטֶרֶת אִם לֹא הָיְתָה טְפֵלָה, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁעֲמִידָתוֹ מִמְּקוֹמוֹ הוּא גְּמַר סְעוּדָתוֹ, וּמַה שֶּׁאוֹכֵל אַחַר כָּךְ — סְעוּדָה אַחֶרֶת הִיא, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן קע״ח.
אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן הִיא טְפֵלָה לְדָבָר הַטָּעוּן בְּרָכָה לְאַחֲרָיו בִּמְקוֹמוֹ, שֶׁאָז אֵין עֲמִידָתוֹ גְּמַר סְעוּדָתוֹ כָּל שֶׁלֹּא בֵּרַךְ בְּרָכָה הָאַחֲרוֹנָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר:

And any secondary eaten after the eating of the primary is not exempted by its blessing unless he eats it in the place where he ate the primary and did not change his place in the meantime, from one room to another — just as it would not have been exempted had it not been secondary; because his rising from his place is the completion of his meal, and what he eats afterwards is another meal, as explained in siman קע״ח (178).
Unless it is secondary to something that requires a blessing after it in its place, in which case his rising is not the completion of his meal so long as he has not said the final blessing, as explained.

2 · למעשה

הלכה למעשה — מנהג חב״ד

The practical Habad conduct

Points of conduct that follow directly from siman רי״ב in the sensibility of the Alter Rebbe and Habad — presented at the level of principle, referring to the Rav for the detail of cases.

For the Habad Hassid — Siman רי״ב (שֶׁהָעִקָּר פּוֹטֵר אֶת הַטָּפֵל)

  • ① Seif א. The mother-rule of the siman: עִקָּר וְטָפֵל. Where one thing is primary and the other comes only on its account, one blessing is said — that of the primary — and it exempts the secondary, before and after. The Rav grades four levels from weakest to strongest: a mixture where the second is only there to season the first; a mixture where both count but the majority prevails; a mixture containing substance of grain, which is primary even in small quantity; and finally the extreme case — two foods not mixed at all, where the second comes only on account of the first. It is there that bread, the most important of foods, can become secondary: the classic case of salted fish eaten with bread so that it should not harm the throat. But the Rav at once sets the condition that decides everything in practice: this holds only if he has no appetite at all for the bread. If he wants it too, bread is bread again — one says “hamotzi,” and it is the bread that exempts the fish.
  • ② Seif ב. Bread soaked in brandy before the meal: his intention may be upon the alcohol, but before a meal one also eats to sustain the heart — so the bread is not secondary: “hamotzi,” and the brandy is exempted along with it. And here is the general principle that governs the whole siman: in a mixture containing one of the five grains, the grain is always primary, so long as one also means to eat it — even if the first desire was for the other ingredient. That is why one does not bless separately over the wine absorbed in the soaked bread.
  • ③ Seif ג. The same principle extends to mezonot pastry (pat haba’ah bekhisnin): soaked in wine, brandy or honey, it remains the primary — being of the five grain species — and one does not bless over the drink. Two exceptions worth remembering: when one does not wish to eat the pastry at all (it serves as a utensil so as not to soil the hands), and when one wants only the alcohol and the bread is there merely to make it drinkable — the typical case of a piece of bread soaked after the meal to aid digestion: there the bread becomes secondary and one says “shehakol.” The Rav finally refers to the mahloket of siman ר״ב (202) and decides by safek berakhot lehakel.
  • ④ Seif ד. A fine and very contemporary boundary: bread crumbled into a hot drink to give it flavour. Here there is indeed a desire to taste, but the act aims at drinking, not eating, and no substance of grain remains in the drink. The rule “grain is always primary” therefore does not apply: the drink is primary. The criterion the Rav lays bare: grain dominates only if it keeps its consistency — thick and fit to eat, not thinned out and fit to drink.
  • ⑤ Seif ה. Subordination has a duration. It holds only for what is eaten together with the primary; what is eaten afterwards by itself requires a blessing again, before and after. With one exception: when the two were made together by cooking — there they have become one thing, and the primary continues to exempt the secondary even eaten separately. And if the subordination persists in the very act of eating (bread taken to sweeten a drink already drunk), no blessing is required, even eaten alone.
  • ⑥ Seif ו. A practical consequence often forgotten: bread that is secondary as to the blessing is secondary as to netilat yadayim as well — even an egg’s volume does not oblige washing. But the Rav adds a counsel of caution very typical of his manner: better to refrain from eating bread to sweeten a drink, for no one can truly know whether he eats it for that or to fill himself — and in the latter case “hamotzi” would be required.
  • ⑦ Seif ז. A reversal of order: sweetening a drink already drunk is not the same as eating before drinking. What is eaten first cannot be exempted by a blessing that will come afterwards — for it is forbidden to enjoy this world without a blessing beforehand. So, despite its subordination, one must bless over it its own blessing. Two everyday applications: pits eaten to whet thirst, and the morsel taken in the morning or after a nap so as not to drink on an empty stomach.
  • ⑧ Seif ח. The Rav distinguishes two situations. If the drink is wine, one obviously cannot say “hagafen” over food. If its blessing is “shehakol,” some say one will say “shehakol” over the food eaten first. But since, eaten before, the food is not secondary at all, it recovers its own blessing. Hence the Rav’s practical counsel: eat a little of it beforehand, so as to say its own blessing over it. The reasoning deserves reflection: this is not a question of precedence (the primary has none here), but of not losing a necessary blessing — “one increases the necessary blessings and reduces those that are not.”
  • ⑨ Seif ט. All this counsel presupposes that the primary is not חָבִיב — the most beloved. The Rav considers the cases where the status of primary is imposed by circumstance (the salted food taken after overly sweet fruits) and not by affection. There, putting the secondary first in order to increase blessings is good. But if the primary is always the most beloved, one blesses over it first and the secondary is exempted: for the true dignity of an important food is not to come first, it is to exempt something else by its blessing. Neither a more specific blessing on the secondary’s side, nor its being momentarily more beloved, overturns this rule.
  • ⑩ Seif י. A substantive condition, and doubtless the most operative day to day: subordination works only if the secondary was before him or clearly in mind at the moment of the blessing over the primary. Brought afterwards and not contemplated, it is exempted neither by the first blessing nor by the final one — save through kevi’ut on a food for which settling avails (bread, wine; beer and honey “in these lands”). Two practical details: one who is in the habit of eating to sweeten his drink is deemed to have had it in mind; and failing such a habit, he will say “shehakol” over the bread, like the drink. As for the hands — never netilat yadayim for bread eaten as a secondary.
  • ⑪ Seif י״א. A last condition: place. A secondary eaten after the primary is exempted only if eaten where the primary was eaten, without moving from room to room — for rising is the completion of one’s meal, and what follows is another meal. Exception: if the primary is of those things requiring a final blessing on the spot, then so long as it has not been said the meal is not complete and the subordination holds.

⚠ This section sets out the Habad conduct grounded in the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch, siman רי״ב (the real text is reproduced in §1). It does not replace a rabbinic ruling in a particular case. For any concrete question — which food is primary and which secondary, whether a separate blessing is required, whether the hands must be washed — ask your Rav, and for Habad, a Habad Rav.
