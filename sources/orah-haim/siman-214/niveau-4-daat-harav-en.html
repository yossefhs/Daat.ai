דעת הרב

Level 4 — Daat HaRav (Alter Rebbe) — Siman רי״ד · בְּכָל בְּרָכָה צָרִיךְ שֶׁיִּהְיֶה שֵׁם וּמַלְכוּת

The Alter Rebbe’s way on בְּכָל בְּרָכָה צָרִיךְ שֶׁיִּהְיֶה שֵׁם וּמַלְכוּת : הַזְכָּרַת הַשֵּׁם ; מַלְכוּת בִּפְתִיחָתָן ; בְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה שֶׁל תְּפִלַּת י״ח ; מֵעֵין שֶׁבַע ; תֵּבַת הָעוֹלָם ; מֶלֶךְ לְבַדּוֹ ; מוּסָב עַל בָּרוּךְ ; הַמֶּלֶךְ — in the sensibility of Habad.

Why a Level 4 devoted to the Alter Rebbe ? The Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch is not a commentary on the Mehaber (מחבר) — it is a complete, self-standing Shulchan Aruch, written by the Alter Rebbe (Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, founder of Habad, disciple of the Maggid (מגיד) of Mezeritch). Its singularity: it combines halakhah (הלכה) + טעמי המצוות + the inner dimension in a single work, and it decides with a unique talmudic rigour.

For Habad, the Alter Rebbe is הפוסק האחרון — his psak is the deciding authority. This page gathers the Rav’s way on siman רי״ד — בְּכָל בְּרָכָה צָרִיךְ שֶׁיִּהְיֶה שֵׁם וּמַלְכוּת (every blessing must contain the Name and Kingship), which in the Rav runs to ב׳ סְעִיפִים (two seifim) where the Mehaber has only one. Here the Rav sets out the very frame of a blessing. First the threshold: a formula in which the Name is not mentioned is not a defective blessing — it is not a blessing at all. Then Kingship, required of every blessing that opens with “Baroukh”, and required at the opening itself: failing which one has not discharged the obligation. Two formulas escape this second requirement, and for one and the same reason: the first blessing of the Amidah is neither a thanksgiving over an enjoyment nor a blessing over a mitzvah, but the opening of a request — one first sets out the praises of the Omnipresent and only then prays; therefore the Sages did not institute Kingship in it. And the Me’ein Sheva of Friday night follows it, its opening being patterned on that same blessing. The second seif descends to the level of a single word: omitting “ha’olam” alone requires beginning again, since “melekh” left standing on its own says nothing — and there, in one long parenthesis, the Rav unfolds the grammar of the formula: what “melekh” refers back to, what the remainder of the blessing refers back to, and why only “Baroukh melekh ha’olam” answers the intent of the Sages.

1 · טקסט אדמו״ר הזקן

שולחן ערוך הרב — סימן רי״ד — בְּכָל בְּרָכָה צָרִיךְ שֶׁיִּהְיֶה שֵׁם וּמַלְכוּת

The full text of the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch

שולחן ערוך הרב, סימן רי״ד — בְּכָל בְּרָכָה צָרִיךְ שֶׁיִּהְיֶה שֵׁם וּמַלְכוּת — וּבוֹ ב׳ סְעִיפִים
The Alter Rebbe’s own text (Sefaria source Shulchan_Arukh_HaRav,_Orach_Chayim.214), followed by our English translation. The siman has two seifim (ב׳ סְעִיפִים ; seif k → .214.k).

סעיף א שֵׁם וּמַלְכוּת בְּכָל בְּרָכָה — חוּץ מִבְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה שֶׁל תְּפִלַּת י״ח וּמֵעֵין שֶׁבַע.

בְּכָל בְּרָכָה צָרִיךְ שֶׁיִּהְיֶה שֵׁם וּמַלְכוּת, וּבוֹ ב׳ סְעִיפִים:
כָּל בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵין בָּהּ הַזְכָּרַת הַשֵּׁם — אֵינָהּ בְּרָכָה. וְכָל בְּרָכוֹת הַפּוֹתְחוֹת בְּ"בָרוּךְ" — צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר גַּם מַלְכוּת בִּפְתִיחָתָן, וְאִם לָאו — לֹא יָצָא. חוּץ מִבְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה שֶׁל תְּפִלַּת י״ח, שֶׁאֵינָהּ הוֹדָאָה עַל הֲנָאָה, אוֹ מִצְוָה, אֶלָּא עַל תְּבִיעַת צְרָכָיו שֶׁל אָדָם, כְּדֵי שֶׁיְּסַדֵּר שְׁבָחָיו שֶׁל מָקוֹם וְאַחַר כָּךְ יִתְפַּלֵּל, לְפִיכָךְ לֹא תִּקְּנוּ בָּהּ מַלְכוּת. וְכֵן בְּבִרְכוֹת מֵעֵין שֶׁבַע שֶׁבְּלֵיל שַׁבָּת, הוֹאִיל וּפְתִיחָתָהּ הִיא מֵעֵין בְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה שֶׁל תְּפִלַּת י״ח:

סעיף ב דִּלֵּג תֵּבַת הָעוֹלָם — חוֹזֵר וּמְבָרֵךְ, שֶׁמֶּלֶךְ לְבַדּוֹ אֵינוֹ מַלְכוּת.

אֲפִלּוּ אִם לֹא דִּלֵּג אֶלָּא תֵּבַת "הָעוֹלָם" לְבַד — צָרִיךְ לַחֲזֹר וּלְבָרֵךְ, שֶׁ"מֶּלֶךְ" לְבַדּוֹ אֵינוֹ חָשׁוּב מַלְכוּת (כִּי אֵין לוֹ בֵּאוּר כְּלָל בְּלֹא תֵּבַת "הָעוֹלָם" שֶׁהוּא סָמוּךְ אֲלֵיהֶם, שֶׁאִם תִּמְצֵי לוֹמַר שֶׁהוּא מוּסָב עַל תֵּבַת "אֱלֹהֵינוּ", כְּלוֹמַר שֶׁ"אֱלֹהֵינוּ" הוּא "מֶלֶךְ" — אֵין זוֹ כַּוָּנַת חֲכָמִים שֶׁתִּקְּנוּ מַלְכוּת בִּבְרָכוֹת, אֶלָּא שֶׁיִּהְיֶה מוּסָב עַל "בָּרוּךְ" שֶׁבִּתְחִלַּת הַבְּרָכָה, כְּלוֹמַר "בָּרוּךְ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם", שֶׁהֲרֵי מְסַיֵּם "שֶׁהַכֹּל כוּ׳", אוֹ "אֲשֶׁר כוּ׳", אוֹ "שֶׁעָשָׂה כוּ׳", שֶׁכָּל זֶה מוּסָב עַל "בָּרוּךְ", וּכְשֶׁאֵינוֹ אוֹמֵר "הָעוֹלָם" — אֵינוֹ מוּסָב עַל "בָּרוּךְ" אֶלָּא תֵּבַת "ה׳ אֱלֹהֵינוּ", אֲבָל "מֶלֶךְ" נִשְׁאַר לְבַדּוֹ, אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן הָיָה אוֹמֵר "הַמֶּלֶךְ". וְאִם תִּמְצֵי לוֹמַר שֶׁ"מֶּלֶךְ" מוּסָב לְמַטָּה, "מֶלֶךְ שֶׁעָשָׂה כוּ׳", אוֹ "מֶלֶךְ שֶׁהַכֹּל כוּ׳" — גַּם זוֹ אֵינָהּ כַּוָּנַת חֲכָמִים בְּמַטְבֵּעַ הַבְּרָכוֹת, אֶלָּא שֶׁשְּׁאָר הַבְּרָכָה יְהֵא מוּסָב עַל "הַשֵּׁם" שֶׁהוּא עִקַּר הַבְּרָכָה, וְלֹא עַל "מֶלֶךְ" לְבַד):

2 · למעשה

הלכה למעשה — מנהג חב״ד

Habad practice

Points of practice that follow directly from siman רי״ד in the sensibility of the Alter Rebbe and of Habad — set out at the level of principle, referring to the Rav for the detail of cases.

For the Habad Hassid — Siman רי״ד (בְּכָל בְּרָכָה צָרִיךְ שֶׁיִּהְיֶה שֵׁם וּמַלְכוּת)

  • ① Seif א. Two requirements, not one. First, mention of the Name: a formula in which the Name is not uttered is not an imperfect blessing — it is not a blessing. Then Kingship, for every blessing that opens with “Baroukh”: it must be said within the opening itself, and if it was not said, one has not discharged the obligation. The Rav then sets out two exceptions, both resting on a single underlying reason. The first blessing of the Amidah (בְּרָכָה רִאשׁוֹנָה שֶׁל תְּפִלַּת י״ח) is neither a thanksgiving over an enjoyment nor a blessing over a mitzvah: it opens a request, in the fitting order — first setting out the praises of the Omnipresent, and only then praying; therefore the Sages did not institute Kingship in it. And the blessing of Me’ein Sheva (מֵעֵין שֶׁבַע) on Friday night follows it: its opening is patterned on that first blessing. The practical upshot to retain: these two are not abridged formulas awaiting completion — they were instituted so from the outset, and one does not repeat them for want of Kingship.
  • ② Seif ב. Here the Rav descends to the level of a single word: omitting “ha’olam” alone, everything else having been said, requires repeating the blessing, since “melekh” on its own is not Kingship. There follows, held entirely within a parenthesis, one of the finest grammatical analyses in the Rav’s Shulchan Aruch: three ways of attaching the word “melekh”, two of which are rejected. (a) Attaching it backwards to “Elokenu” — “Elokenu is melekh” — is not what the Sages instituted when they established Kingship in the blessings. (b) Attaching it forwards to what follows — “melekh she’asa”, “melekh shehakol” — is no more the shape of the formula, for the remainder of the blessing must refer back to the Name, which is its essence, and not to “melekh” alone. (c) One reading remains: “melekh” attaches to “Baroukh” at the beginning — “Baroukh melekh ha’olam” — exactly as the conclusions “shehakol…”, “asher…”, “she’asa…” attach to it. And that attachment holds only by the adjacent word “ha’olam”: without it the sentence reorganizes itself around “Hashem Elokenu”, and “melekh” is left orphaned. The Rav closes with the one variant that would have saved the formula: had he said “hamelekh”, with the definite article — for then the word would stand on its own.

⚠ This section presents Habad practice founded on the Alter Rebbe’s Shulchan Aruch, siman רי״ד (the real text is reproduced in §1). It does not replace a rabbinic ruling on a particular case. For any concrete question — a blessing said without the Name or without Kingship, a word dropped in the middle of the opening, or the doubt whether one must repeat — ask your Rav, and for Habad, a Habad Rav.
