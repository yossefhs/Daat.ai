Siman 35 — The Number of Lines in the Tefillin (Minyan ha-Shitin)

Choulhan Aroukh, Orach Chaim 35 — סעיף אחד (1 seif)

The Siman — Choulhan Aroukh, Orach Chaim siman ל״ה (1 seif)
דִּין מִנְיַן הַשִּׁיטִין, וּבוֹ סָעִיף אֶחָד. נָהֲגוּ בְּמִנְיַן הַשִּׁיטִין לִכְתּוֹב בְּשֶׁל יָד שִׁבְעָה שִׁיטִין בְּכָל פָּרָשָׁה, וּבְשֶׁל רֹאשׁ אַרְבָּעָה שִׁיטִין ; וְאִם שִׁנָּה לֹא פָּסַל.
The law of the number of lines; and it contains one seif (סעיף אחד): it is customary (נהגו), received from the scribes' tradition, to write in the של יד (the arm-tefillin) שבעה שיטין — seven lines — in each parasha, and in the של ראש (the head-tefillin) ארבעה שיטין — four lines; ואם שינה לא פסל — and if he changed, he did not invalidate. To this number of lines is added a קבלה about the ראשי שיטין (the line-heads), and the limit of the כתב מנומר — not to distort the letters when adjusting the writing.

The main themes of the Siman

מִנְיַן הַשִּׁיטִין
the number of lines
A מנהג received from the scribes' tradition, transmitted איש מפי איש: a fixed number of lines (שיטין) per parasha, a mark of the scribe's careful work.
שֶׁל יָד — ז׳ שִׁיטִין
seven lines on the arm
In the של יד, whose four parshiyot are written on a single hide, the custom is שבעה שיטין — seven lines — in each parasha.
שֶׁל רֹאשׁ — ד׳ שִׁיטִין
four lines on the head
In the של ראש, whose each parasha occupies a separate slip in its בית, the custom is ארבעה שיטין — four lines — per parasha.
שִׁנָּה — לֹא פָּסַל
b'dieved valid
ואם שינה לא פסל: this number is a matter of מנהג and הידור, not a דין that would invalidate. B'dieved, tefillin of another count remain valid.
רָאשֵׁי שִׁיטִין
the line-heads
A further קבלה concerns the words placed at the head of each line. Scribes today are precise about the number, less about the ראשי שיטין.
כְּתָב מְנֻמָּר
spotted writing
One neither lengthens nor shortens the letters (להאריך/לקצר) to the point that the writing looks מנומר — spotted. Referral to the rules of the ס״ת in יורה דעה.

Frequently asked questions — Siman ל״ה

How many lines are written in each parasha of the tefillin, according to Siman 35?

According to the Choulhan Aroukh, Orach Chaim 35:1, it is customary (מנהג), received from the scribes' tradition, to write שבעה שיטין — seven lines — in each parasha of the של יד (the arm-tefillin, whose four parshiyot are on a single hide), and ארבעה שיטין — four lines — in each parasha of the של ראש (the head-tefillin, whose each parasha is on a separate slip).

What happens if the scribe wrote a different number of lines (שינה), according to Siman 35?

The Mechaber rules: ואם שינה לא פסל — if he changed the number of lines, he did not invalidate. This number is a matter of מנהג and הידור, not a דין that would invalidate; b'dieved the tefillin remain valid. It is the careful work of the scribe that aims for this number le-chatchila.

What are the ראשי שיטין and must one be precise about them, according to Siman 35?

Beyond the number of lines, there is a קבלה (transmitted איש מפי איש) about the ראשי שיטין — the words placed at the head of each line. Scribes today are precise about the number of lines, but less so about the line-heads. It is a scribal הידור, not a condition of validity.

May one lengthen or shorten the letters to align the lines, according to Siman 35?

One must not lengthen (להאריך) nor shorten (לקצר) the letters to the point that the writing looks מנומר — spotted, uneven. The scribe adjusts with measure, without distorting the letters. The Choulhan Aroukh HaRav refers on this point to the rules of the ס״ת (in יורה דעה). For practical conduct, one refers to one's Rav and one's scribe.
