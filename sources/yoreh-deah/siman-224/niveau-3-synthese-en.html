Siman 224 — synthesis and revision (יורה דעה רכ״ד)

מי שאמר נכסך עלי חרם
One seif, three formulas, and a dispute that stays open

1. The three formulas

עליך — “upon you”: my property is closed to you, you are the one bound. עלי — “upon me”: your property is closed to me, I am the one bound. Both — each is closed to the other. The preposition, and it alone, designates the נאסר.

2. Shared property

The two men have forbidden each other
▼
A thing jointly owned by all Israel (Temple Mount, courtyards) → permitted
▼
A thing jointly owned by the townspeople (synagogue, ark, books) → forbidden
▼
Remedy: convey to a third party the share they hold in it
▼
יש אומרים: where there is no דין חלוקה, nothing was forbidden
The criterion is not “public / private” but “do I have a share of my own in it?”. In the Temple Mount, no; in my town’s synagogue, yes.

3. What the apparatus adds

Shakh ס״ק א: מדינא yes, but by the Ra’avad’s takana one cannot forbid one’s share of the synagogue or of the books. Shakh ס״ק ב: the יש אומרים is the main view in the Shas. Taz ס״ק א–ב: four thousand characters to save the first opinion, and the יש אומרים reduced to the Ramban and the Ran alone. Ba’er Hetev ס״ק ב: sets the two face to face. Pitḥei Teshuva: see both, and the Mishkenot Yaakov.
« כמו ב״ה כו׳. היינו מדינא אבל מצד התקנה כב״י בשם הא״ח שהורו הראב״ד וכל חכמי דורו שאין אדם יכול לאסור חלקו מב״ה ולא מספרים ואם אסר אין איסורו איסור וכן כתב הב״ח וכן כתב המחבר בא״ח סי׳ קנ״ג סט״ו: » (ש״ך יו״ד רכ״ד ס״ק א)
« וי״א כו׳. בכל דבר שאין בו דין חלוקה כלומר בב״ה וכה״ג אין בהם דין חלוקה ומותר כדלקמן סימן רכ״ו וכ״פ מהרש״ל פ׳ שור שנגח את הפרה סי׳ ל״ב וכן נראה עיקר בש״ס: » (ש״ך יו״ד רכ״ד ס״ק ב)
« חלוקה. כלומר בבהכ״נ וכה״ג אין בהם דין חלוקה דמותר וכן נראה עיקר בש״ס ש״ך אבל הט״ז כתב דבש״ס משמע דבבהכ״נ יש בו דין חלוקה ואסור כדעת ראשונה שכתב המחבר ע״ש ועיין בא״ח סי׳ קנ״ג סי״ז: » (באר היטב יו״ד רכ״ד ס״ק ב)

Ten clauses — nine of the Meḥaber, one of the Rama.

5. The open disputes

Three disagreements run through this siman, and none is closed by the Shulḥan Arukh itself.

The synagogue: forbidden or not? The Meḥaber’s first opinion, upheld by the Taz: forbidden. The יש אומרים, upheld by the Shakh and the Maharshal: permitted, for want of דין חלוקה. The Ba’er Hetev lays out both, the Pitḥei Teshuva refers to both and adds the Mishkenot Yaakov siman 48.
The lashes. The Rambam, reported by the Tur, distinguishes according to who spoke. The Beit Yosef warns that the Ran differs on this — see what he wrote at the end of siman 220.
The Rambam’s contradiction. The Ramban and the Ran leave it open (אסקוה בקושיא). The Ba’ḥ answers it, the Taz refutes him and proposes his own reading in three degrees.

6. Vocabulary

TermMeaning
חרם“set apart” — a vow formula that withdraws a thing from use
הנאהbenefit, in the widest sense: any advantage drawn from a thing or a man
הנודר / המודרthe one who utters the vow / the one about whom it is uttered — the mishna’s terms, which the Shulḥan Arukh replaces by Reuven and Shimon
דבר של עולי בבלwhat belongs to all Israel: Temple Mount, courtyards, the cistern on the road
דין חלוקהthe right to demand partition of jointly held property — the pivot of the whole debate
התיבהthe desk on which the scroll is laid for reading — listed by the mishna alongside the books
תקנת רבנו גרשוםthe ordinances of Rabbenu Gershom; the Rama invokes one for a house lent to a synagogue

7. Recapitulation

ע for עלי / עליך: the preposition designates the נאסר. — ח for חלק: do I have a share in this thing? — ק for קנין: the remedy is an act of acquisition, not a formula.
Golden rules. (1) Always establish the exact formula before anything else. (2) For shared property, ask: do I have a share of my own in it? (3) The seif’s remedy is a real transfer of the share, not a declaration. (4) For the synagogue and the books, the takana reported by the Shakh overrides the debate: the power to forbid does not exist.
Pitfalls. (1) Thinking the vow is reciprocal: it is so only if both formulas were uttered. (2) Confusing “public” with “no share of one’s own” — the synagogue is public and yet each has a share in it. (3) Taking the Taz’s position for the Shakh’s: they are opposed, and the Ba’er Hetev says so. (4) Forgetting that the Shakh’s ס״ק א changes plane and moves from the law to the takana. (5) Looking for the lashes in the Shulḥan Arukh: it does not mention them here; it is the Tur who reports them from the Rambam.

The line of ruling

Establish the formula: עליך binds the addressee, עלי binds the speaker, both together bind both. Property of all Israel stays open. For the town’s property the Shakh–Taz dispute remains open, and the seif’s remedy — transferring one’s share to a third party — works in every case. But for the synagogue and the books in particular, the takana reported by the Shakh in the Ra’avad’s name, and adopted by the Shulḥan Arukh in או״ח קנ״ג:ט״ו, removes outright the power to forbid. Any real situation goes to a Rav.
