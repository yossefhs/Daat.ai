Siman 207 — Deformed words that bind (כינויי נדרים)

דין כנויי נדרים
A single seif, reduced to one question: does this word, here, say “vow”?

1. The axiom: usage makes the word

The axiom. A vow is made by speech, and speech is worth what it means where it is uttered. A word therefore has no force of its own: it has force because, in that place and at that time, it says “offering”. From this the whole siman follows — both its rule and its exception.
« כנויי נדרים כנדרים » (שו״ע יו״ד ר״ז:א)
« וְכֵן כָּל כַּיּוֹצֵא בָּזֶה הוֹלְכִין אַחַר לְשׁוֹן כְּלַל הָעָם בְּאוֹתוֹ מָקוֹם וּבְאוֹתוֹ זְמַן: » (רמב״ם הלכות נדרים פרק א׳ הלכה ט״ז)
Three consequences. (1) A deformed word binds, because usage has adopted it. (2) A word usage has not adopted does not bind, even a word of the Mishnah. (3) The list of three words is an example, not a closure: wherever a community has given itself a vow-word, that word binds.

2. The three reflex questions

1. Is the word uttered the proper word (קרבן, נדר…)? Yes — it is a vow, and siman 207 is not needed. No — on to the next question.
▼
2. Does this word say “vow” in this place and at this time? Yes — כינויי נדרים כנדרים: it binds. No — on to the next question.
▼
3. Is it so far from the expression that nobody recognises it? Yes — כינויי כינויים: it does not bind. In between: a question for a Rav.

4. The three degrees of speech

The caseVerdictSource
The proper word — קרבן, נדרBindsשו״ע יו״ד ר״ז:א

A second-degree corruption — מקנמנא מקנחנא מקנסנאDoes not bindשו״ע יו״ד ר״ז:א · ש״ך ס״ק ב
A הקדש pronounced by a כינויBinds — it is a הקדשרמב״ם הלכות נדרים פרק א׳ הלכה י״ז
The dividing line does not run between Hebrew and other languages — it runs between what is spoken and what is not. That is why an entire foreign language binds (and all the more so, says the Shach), while a doubly deformed Hebrew word does not.

5. The apparatus of the siman in five lines

Five entries, and five only. Here they are, each with what it contributes.

Shach, ס״ק א — the reason of the ruling. He opens with a declared transcription of the Rambam: there are places where the language is spoiled, and there one follows the spoiled word.
« ל׳ רמב״ם פ״א מהלכות נדרים דין ט״ז יש מקומות שאנשיהם עלגים ומפסידין את הלשון ומכנין על דבר בדבר אחר הולכים שם אחר הכינוי » (ש״ך יו״ד ר״ז ס״ק א)
Shach, ס״ק א — the Ran’s answer. The Mishnah does not give a list: it gives the weakest case, corrupted Hebrew, and thereby teaches a fortiori about whole languages.
« דרבותא אשמועינן דלא מבעיא בלשונות עובדי כוכבים שהן לשונות גמורים הנודר בהן נדרו נדר » (ש״ך יו״ד ר״ז ס״ק א)
Shach, ס״ק ב — the content of the limit. One line, and without it one would not know what the seif excludes.
« כגון דאמר מקנמנא מקנחנא מקנסנא. » (ש״ך יו״ד ר״ז ס״ק ב)
Taz, ס״ק א — the reading of כל נדרי. He uses the ruling to explain why the formula names certain כינויים and covers the rest with a general word.
« ומה שזכר בנוסח כל נדרי וקונמי וקנסי וכינויי רצונו לומר קונמי הוא הכינוי היותר פשוע שכן הוזכר במשנה תחילה לכינויים » (ט״ז יו״ד ר״ז ס״ק א)
Baer Hetev, ס״ק א — the reason, in two lines. He keeps from the Rambam only the linguistic fact.
« הטעם כתב הרמב״ם דיש מקומות שאנשיהם עלגים ומפסידין את הלשון ומכנין על דבר בדבר אחר הולכים שם אחר הכינוי: » (באר היטב יו״ד ר״ז ס״ק א)
What the apparatus does not contain. On this siman neither the Pitchei Teshuva nor the Nekudot HaKesef give an entry. Attribute none to them.

TermMeaning

כינויי כינוייםSubstitutes of substitutes — מקנמנא, מקנחנא, מקנסנא; they do not bind

7. Golden rules, mnemonic and classic pitfalls

Three words, two rules, one question. Three words: קונם · קונח · קונס. Two rules: the substitute binds, the substitute of the substitute does not. One question: here, does this word say “vow”?
The golden rules
  • It is not the word that makes usage, it is usage that makes the word.
  • The Mishnah’s list is an example, never a closure.
  • The criterion of the limit is distance: שרחוק הרבה מהלשון.
  • A vow is made in any language; the כינויים are the hard case, not the general one.
  • The rule holds also for שבועה, נזירות, חרם and הקדש — so say the Mishnah and the Rambam.
The classic pitfalls
  • Thinking קונם has a virtue of its own: usage, and nothing else, is what makes it work.
  • Thinking the gloss “if he does not know” contradicts the rule: it is its exact consequence.
  • Confusing נדר and שבועה: the first bears on the thing, the second on the person.
  • Reading the כינויי כינויים as a stringency: it is a leniency.
  • Attributing an entry to the Pitchei Teshuva or the Nekudot HaKesef on this siman: they do not comment on it.
  • Writing קונז after the Rambam in the belief of correcting the Shulchan Arukh: they are two nusḥa’ot, and one does not decide between them.
