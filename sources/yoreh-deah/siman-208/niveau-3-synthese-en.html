Siman 208 — The unspecified vow, and the חרם

דיני סתם נדרים והנודר בחרם
Five seifim, reduced to two questions: what does this word mean, and what does transgressing it cost?

1. The axiom: one attaches, and the term of the attachment decides

The axiom. A vow by comparison is worth only what the thing it compares to is worth. Attaching to a vowed thing (דבר הנדור) produces a vow; attaching to a merely forbidden thing (דבר האסור) produces nothing. Hence the whole siman: since some expressions denote both, a rule of construction is needed — and that is סתם נדרים להחמיר.
« שהרי הוא מתפיס בדבר הנדור » (שו״ע יו״ד ר״ח:א)
« שהרי הוא מתפיס בדבר האסור אנו תולין אותו להחמיר » (שו״ע יו״ד ר״ח:א)
Three consequences. (1) With no specification, we take the sense that makes the vow hold. (2) With a specification, we follow the man — unless the usage of his place has already fixed the sense. (3) One who takes refuge afterwards in a far-fetched sense is treated more severely: this is no longer an ambiguity but a way out.

2. The three reflex questions

1. To what did he attach? To a vowed thing — it is a vow. To a forbidden thing — it is nothing. To both at once — next question.
▼
2. Did he specify, and does the usage of the place allow it? He specified and nothing contradicts him — he is believed. He said nothing, or the majority of the place says the opposite — stringently.
▼
3. Did he transgress? A vow of this chapter — no penalty. A Torah vow, deliberately — the penalty of days. An unkeepable vow — immediate release.

6. The open maḥlokot of the apparatus

Five points on which the apparatus does not speak with one voice. To know them is to know what must be asked of a Rav.

Does the עם הארץ need a שאלה at seif 1? The Mehaber says no. The Bach says yes, after the Rambam and after siman 205. The Shach defends the Mehaber by distinguishing the two situations. Shach ס״ק ב · Baer Hetev ס״ק ב.
Is the ת״ח believed against majority usage? The Hagahat Prisha says yes, a fortiori from seif 2. The Shach says no. The Chavot Yair, reported by the Pitchei Teshuva, holds the Hagahat Prisha to be principal. Shach ס״ק ה · Pitchei Teshuva ס״ק ב.
Does regret suffice, or is a door required? The seif says regret suffices. The Bach requires a door from another place. The Rambam, the source of the ruling, opens a door. Shach ס״ק ט · Baer Hetev ס״ק ה · Taz ס״ק ב.
Thirty days, or the whole count? Seif 3 and the parenthesis of seif 5 say thirty. The Shach cites Tosafot, the Rosh, Rabbenu Yeroḥam, the Raavad and the Rivash, and writes אין להקל. Shach ס״ק י״ג · Baer Hetev ס״ק ח and ס״ק י.
Is the חרם of the Torah? The Shach lays it down as a premise, and the Baer Hetev takes it up. The Noda BiYehuda, in the second collection, changes his mind: it is מדברי קבלה, and a doubt about it is decided leniently. Shach ס״ק י״ד · Baer Hetev ס״ק ט · Pitchei Teshuva ס״ק ד.

8. Golden rules, mnemonic and classic pitfalls

Two questions, three answers, one cap. Two questions: to what did he attach, and did he specify? Three answers: stringently if he kept silent, by his word if he spoke, by usage if the place has settled it. One cap: thirty days — which the Shach contests.
The golden rules
  • A vow by comparison is worth what the term of the comparison is worth.
  • The stringency of seif 1 is not a presumption against the man: it is a rule for construing silence.
  • The usage of the place prevails over the individual’s declaration — the same logic as in siman 207.
  • Seif 2 is harsher than seif 1 because it is no longer an ambiguity but a way out.
  • The penalty of days is owed only for what is of the Torah — save the Taz’s reservation about an oath.
The classic pitfalls
  • Thinking that סתם נדרים להחמיר means “he is never believed”: he is believed as soon as he specifies.
  • Confusing דבר הנדור and דבר האסור, and so reversing the outcome of the vow.
  • Reading seif 2 as a continuation of seif 1: they are two régimes, and the second is harsher.
  • Taking the thirty days of seif 3 as settled: the Shach writes אין להקל.
  • Counting seventeen ס״ק for the Shach: Sefaria addresses sixteen, the first holding two paragraphs.
  • Ascribing to the Shach the Beit Yosef’s position on the Rambam: that is precisely what he contests, and he writes ותימה.
