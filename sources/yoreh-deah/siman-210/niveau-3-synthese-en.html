Siman 210 — Mouth and heart (שצריך לפרוט הנדר כשפתיו)

שצריך לפרוט הנדר כשפתיו
Three seifim reduced to one question: what does it take for a word to bind?

1. The axiom: two conditions, not one

The axiom. A vow is born neither of speech alone nor of intention alone, but of their meeting. The seif says it in one sentence: he must have articulated it with his lips, and his mouth and heart must be alike. The whole siman consists in examining what happens when the two do not coincide exactly.
« אין הנדר חל עד שיוציא בשפתיו ויהיו פיו ולבו שוים » (שו״ע יו״ד ר״י:א)
« שיוציא בשפתיו. שנאמר לבטא בשפתים » (ט״ז יו״ד ר״י ס״ק א)
Three consequences. (1) Speech without matching intention: nothing. (2) Speech wider than the intention: the vow holds on the intersection. (3) Neither speech nor intention — the dream: nothing by the letter, but the Mehaber requires it to be reckoned with, and the Shach writes that such is the practice.

2. The three reflex questions

1. Did the word come out? No — nothing, whatever the intention was. Yes — next question.
▼
2. Does the word said contain the word thought? No — nothing, on either. Yes — the vow holds on what the two share. And if the wide word was chosen deliberately, see the dispute.
▼
3. Did he retract? Aloud, and within the interval counted from the vow — the vow is undone. Inwardly, or too late — it stands.

5. The open disputes among the commentators

Three, and they are named.
  • The wide word said deliberately — the Ba’h, the Shach and the Maharshal forbid all bread; the Beit Yosef rejects that reading; the Chavot Yair doubts in the other direction without ruling.
  • A vow in a dream — the Rosh says: nothing at all, and the Maharal of Prague answered so in practice; the Rashba requires strictness as a precaution, and the Shach writes that such is the practice.
  • A woman who vowed in a dream — the Ba’h denies the husband the power to annul; the Taz writes that a stringency cannot outweigh an oath made awake.
« אבל אם הוציא בשפתיו פת סתם בכוונה ומתכוין שלא להוציא פת חטין בשפתיו אלא לחשוב בלבו פת חטים אסור בכל פת דדברים שבלב אינן דברים » (ש״ך יו״ד ר״י ס״ק ב)
« ודעתו דאפי׳ אם כוון להוציא פת סתם מותר בשל שעורים אלא דמסתפי לחלוק על כמה גדולים קמאי ובתראי שדעתם כדברי הב״ח » (פתחי תשובה יו״ד ר״י ס״ק א)
« ומצאתי למהר״ל מפראג שהשיב בתשוב׳ הלכה למעשה כהרא״ש דאין צריך התרה כלל » (ט״ז יו״ד ר״י ס״ק ד)
« ויש לחוש לדבריהם. והכי נהוג ונראה דה״ה באשה שנדרה בחלום שצריכה י׳ כמו האיש ואין הבעל יכול להפר לה ביום שמעו דדין נדר זה כמו נדוי עכ״ל ב״ח וכן נוהגין » (ש״ך יו״ד ר״י ס״ק ה)
« אלא שמו״ח ז״ל כתב דאין הבעל יכול להפר כיון דמן השמים השביעוה » (ט״ז יו״ד ר״י ס״ק ד)

7. Rules of thumb, a mnemonic, and standard pitfalls

Two threads, one intersection, one interval. Two threads: speech and intention. One intersection: what the word said and the word thought share. One interval: the time of a greeting, counted from the vow.
Rules of thumb
  • Thought never creates a vow; it can only narrow a word that covers it.
  • The other word produces nothing at all — not even on what he actually said.
  • Retraction is of the same order as the vow: it goes through the mouth.
  • A dream is not a case of doubtful vow: it is a case where neither mouth nor heart operated.
  • The interval for retraction is counted from the vow, never from another man’s protest.
Standard pitfalls
  • Thinking the first clause of seif 1 is lenient: it is neutral, and nothing takes hold either way.
  • Treating פת סתם as an exception: it is on the contrary the application of the second verse.
  • Forgetting that the rule of seif 1 is, for the Ba’h and the Shach, limited to a word that slipped out.
  • Taking the Rashba’s position for law: the Taz says expressly that it is a חשש.
  • Counting the interval from another man’s protest: the Rama, the Taz and the Shach say the opposite, against the Beit Yosef.
  • Counting more than twenty-one entries in the apparatus: Sefaria addresses exactly twenty-one on this siman.
