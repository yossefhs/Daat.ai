Siman 146 — Which idol can be nullified and which cannot

איזו עבודת כוכבים יש לה ביטול ואיזו אין לה ביטול

Fifteen seifim. The previous siman asked what remains permitted although worshipped; this one asks how the prohibition can be lifted. The answer is ביטול — and the siman fixes its author, its act and its term

1. The axiom: nullification is not damage, it is renunciation

What nullifies the object is not what happens to it, but what its owner declares of it. A landslide that crushes it does not nullify (seif ט); a spontaneous break does not nullify (seif יא); spittle does not nullify (seif ז). Yet a leaf torn off for one’s own use nullifies the whole tree (seif ו), and a departure with no thought of return nullifies without anything being touched (seif י).

The act of seif ז — cutting, crushing the face — nullifies not because it damages but because it can mean only one thing. The proof stands right beside it: spitting humiliates just as much and nullifies nothing, because the Talmud knows that a man returns to a god he has insulted.

« אָמַר חִזְקִיָּה, דְּאָמַר קְרָא: ״וְהָיָה כִי יִרְעַב וְהִתְקַצַּף וְקִלֵּל בְּמַלְכּוֹ וּבֵאלֹהָיו וּפָנָה לְמַעְלָה » (עבודה זרה נ״ג ע״א)

Hence the three exclusions of persons in seif א and seif ה: one needs someone who believed, who still believes enough for his renunciation to mean something, and who is not a Jew — for a Jew never conferred on the object a status he could withdraw.

2. The 3 reflex questions

  1. Whose was it, and where is it now? A Jew’s idol: never nullified. A non-Jew’s idol already acquired by a Jew: no longer nullifiable. Otherwise, the next question arises.
  2. Who acted? A non-Jew who knows the nature of idols is required. Not an undiscerning minor, not an imbecile, not a ger toshav, not an Ishmaelite.
  3. What did he do, and could it have meant anything else? Mutilating, crushing the face, taking a leaf for himself, leaving in peacetime: yes. Spitting, dragging, selling, being driven off by war: no.

3. Master table — the fifteen seifim, one per line

SeifWhat it says
אA non-Jew nullifies; a Jew never — neither the non-Jew’s nor his own, which “never has nullification.” And a non-Jewish partner nullifies only his share: the Jew’s share stays forbidden forever.
בOnce in Jewish hands and acquired, the idol has no nullification — a rabbinic decree, lest it be confused with a Jew’s own idol. Accessories and ornaments keep theirs.
גScrap bought from a non-Jew in which an idol is found: paid without taking, or taken without paying — return it. Both — take it to the Dead Sea.
דThe convert inheriting with his non-Jewish brother may say “take the idols and I the money” — until they enter his domain.
הA non-Jew who knows the nature of idols nullifies, even another’s, even without worshipping that one, even under compulsion. Those who do not nullify: an undiscerning minor, an imbecile, a ger toshav — and, says the Rama, an Ishmaelite.
וA leaf taken from the ashera for his own use nullifies the tree; pruning to beautify it does not. In both cases the chips are permitted.
זCutting off the tip of the ear, nose or finger, or crushing the face: nullifies, even removing nothing. Spitting, urinating, dragging, defiling: does not. Rama: speech alone nullifies, but not under duress.
חSelling or pledging does not nullify, even to a Jewish goldsmith; and some permit with him. Rama: the accessories, sold or pledged, are nullified.
טA landslide left uncleared, a theft left unpursued: this is no nullification.
יWorshippers who left in peacetime: permitted. Who left in wartime: forbidden — unless they could have returned and did not.
יאBroken of itself: the fragments stay forbidden until nullified, and those one finds are forbidden by doubt. Jointed, so that a layman can reassemble it: each piece must be nullified; otherwise one limb suffices for all.
יבA damaged altar: still forbidden, until most of it is torn down by a non-Jew. A damaged bimos: permitted. The bimos is one stone, the altar many.
יגNullifying the idol nullifies its accessories — even if already with a Jew, adds the Rama. Nullifying the accessories does not nullify the idol.
ידA mitzvah, for whoever finds an idol, to destroy it: grind and scatter to the wind, or cast into the sea. Rama: likewise its accessories and all made for it.
טוOne must root out the idol and give it a name of contempt.

4. The three axes of the siman: who, by what act, until when

מי — who. Seifim א · ב · ד · ה. The power rests on belief, not ownership: a non-Jew nullifies even another’s idol, even under compulsion; a Jew nullifies nothing, not even his own. And there is a term — acquisition by a Jew.
▼
במה — by what act. Seifim ו · ז · ח · ט · י. What strikes the figure nullifies; what humiliates without striking it does not; what moves it or monetises it does not either, except on the Rambam’s reading about the Jewish goldsmith. And abandonment nullifies when it is free.
▼
ומה נגרר — and what follows. Seifim יא · יב · יג. What was not nullified stays forbidden — the fragments, the damaged altar. And nullifying the idol carries its accessories, but not the reverse.

The term, and why it is measured

The bolt of seif ב is not biblical: it is a decree, and the Taz draws his whole reading from that.

« ונ״ל דכיון שאין הטעם אלא משום גזירה לא גזרו אלא אם באה לידו אחר הזכייה דוקא » (ט״ז יו״ד קמ״ו ס״ק ג)
« והב״ח כתב דאפילו לא זכה בה כגון שמצאה לפני יאוש או גזלה אע״ג דמדינא עבודת כוכבים של עובד כוכבים היא ויש לה ביטול מ״מ מדרבנן אין לה ביטול » (ש״ך יו״ד קמ״ו ס״ק א)

⚖ What to retain

Three questions in order: who acted? What did he do? And was there still time?

5. The three degrees of abandonment: the act, the word, the silence

מעשה — the act. Seif ז. Cutting off the tip of the ear, nose or finger; crushing the face. It always nullifies, even under compulsion — the object is struck, and an idolater does not restore rank to what he himself damaged.
▼
אמירה — the word. The Rama’s gloss, seif ז. It nullifies on the ויש אומרים view — but not under duress, and the Shach specifies that a word which announces the act is meant, not a formula.
▼
שתיקה — the silence. Seifim ט · י. It nullifies only when unambiguous: leaving in peacetime, or being able to return and not returning. Not clearing rubble, not pursuing thieves, fleeing a war: ambiguous silences, hence without effect.

What the Shach means by “mere speech”

« ר״ל דהכא מיירי שאומר שרוצה לבטל לקטוע אזנה או חוטמה וכה״ג אבל התם אינו מבטלו וגם סובר שהקונה לא יבטל ודלא כב״י » (ש״ך יו״ד קמ״ו ס״ק ח)
« כתב ב״י על זה ותמיהני מדין מכרה או משכנה בסמוך דמשמע אפילו מכרה ברצונו לא בטלה וכתב ד״מ שאני התם דלא ידעינן בודאי שבטלה » (ט״ז יו״ד קמ״ו ס״ק ז)

And why flight in wartime does not nullify

« אִי (בָּעֲיָא) [בְּעוֹ] לְמִיהְדָּר הֲדוּר, מִדְּלָא הֲדוּר בַּטּוֹלֵי בַּטְּלֻהָ » (עבודה זרה נ״ג ע״ב)

6. The six disputes of the siman

Six places where the Rishonim, and then the Acharonim, part ways. The table gives each position, and where the question is settled.

7. The 5 governing rules

  1. Nullification belongs to the believer. Not to the owner — a non-Jew nullifies another’s idol; not to a Jew — he never conferred anything; not to one who never believed — minor, imbecile, ger toshav, Ishmaelite.
  2. It has a term, and the term is rabbinic. Once the Jew has acquired the object and received it, nothing nullifies it. Since the reason is a decree, it covers only the idol itself: the accessories keep their nullification.
  3. The act must be unambiguous. What strikes the figure nullifies; what humiliates without striking does not. Sale and pledge do not nullify — except, on the second view, with a Jewish goldsmith known to melt the statue down.
  4. What happens to the object does not nullify it. Landslide, theft, spontaneous breakage, a chipped altar: a hand is needed, and an intention.
  5. Nullification descends, it does not ascend. Nullifying the idol nullifies its accessories; nullifying the accessories does not nullify it.

8. Mnemonic — the “ביטול” aide-mémoire

ב בַּעַל הָאֱמוּנָה — only one who believed can withdraw; not a Jew, not a minor, not an Ishmaelite (seifim א · ה).
י יַד יִשְׂרָאֵל — once the Jew has acquired and received, the door is shut (seif ב).
ט טִיב אֱלִילִים — he must know the nature of idols; otherwise the act says nothing (seif ה).
ו וִתּוּר שֶׁמַּשְׁמָעוֹ אֶחָד — mutilating, yes; spitting, no; selling, no; leaving in peace, yes (seifim ו · ז · ח · י).
ל לֹא נֶזֶק וְלֹא חֲרָטָה — neither landslide nor breakage nullifies: a hand is needed (seifim ט · יא · יב).

9. The 4 classic pitfalls

  1. Thinking that damage nullifies. This is the error three seifim correct one after another: the landslide (ט), the spontaneous break (יא), the chipped altar (יב). A statue found broken remains forbidden.
  2. Forgetting the second condition of seif ב. “Came into his hand” and “acquired it.” For the Taz an object paid for but still at the seller’s is still nullifiable; the Bach disagrees, and the Ba’er Hetev reports both.
  3. Reading “he sold it” as abandonment. Seif ח says the opposite, and the Beit Yosef uses it to object to nullification by speech. Selling is transferring — not renouncing.
  4. Assuming the name of contempt applies everywhere. The Shach and the Nekudot HaKesef limit it to the Land of Israel, from the phrase מן המקום ההוא; the Taz alone sees a copyist’s error there. The duty to destroy what comes to hand knows no border.
« ול״נ דאין כאן ט״ס אלא כיון דבש״ס פ׳ כל הצלמים קאמר דהא דצריך לשרש אחריה נפקא לן מואבדתם את שמם מן המקום ההוא » (נקודות הכסף יו״ד קמ״ו)
« בלא שחיקה אבל לשאר נהרות בעי שחיקה ב״י וד״מ ושאר אחרונים » (ש״ך יו״ד קמ״ו ס״ק י״ג)
