1. The axiom: נותן טעם לפגם מותר

The starting point:

Siman 122 opens with four words and nothing else: נותן טעם לפגם מותר — “a taste that spoils is permitted” (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:א). All the rest of the siman is the small print. The principle itself comes from the baraita's machloket between רבי מאיר and רבי שמעון, and the halacha follows רבי שמעון: « לְשֶׁבַח — אָסוּר, וְלִפְגָם — מוּתָּר » (עבודה זרה ס״ז ע״ב). רב כהנא derives it from the whole body of sources: « מִדִּבְרֵי כּוּלָּם נִלְמַד נוֹתֵן טַעַם לִפְגָם מוּתָּר » (עבודה זרה ס״ז ע״ב).

But note the siman's title: בכלים. A spoiling taste from one food fallen into another is Siman 103 — and the Shach opens our siman by sending the reader there: summary — up to the end of seif 4 everything has already been properly set out in Siman 103 (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק א). Here the taste is the one sleeping in the wall of a vessel.
The sourceWhat it establishesIts consequence in the siman

💡 The signpost: the whole siman is read on two storeys. Below, Torah law: a spoiled taste does not forbid — hence בדיעבד, what was cooked is permitted. Above, rabbinic law: a pot in which a prohibited thing was cooked remains forbidden to use — hence לכתחלה, one does not cook in it. Whenever a seif seems to contradict itself, simply ask which storey it is speaking on.

⚖ Two ways a taste can be פגום

  1. By its nature — the prohibited substance is of the kind that ruins the dish it falls into, the classic case of גיעולי גויים. The רמב״ם lays down the decisive condition: summary — it must spoil from beginning to end; what spoils at first but ends by improving, or improves at first even though it ends by spoiling, remains forbidden (רמב״ם הלכות מאכלות אסורות פרק ט״ו הלכה כ״ח).
  2. By time — the taste was good, and waiting spoiled it. This is the mechanism proper to our siman: קדירה שאינה בת יומא (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ב).
Keep the practical difference in mind: spoiling by nature cannot be undone; spoiling by time can be cancelled — by prohibited matter left בעין or by a sharp food (seif 3), and it can even be reset to zero (seif 4).

2. The 3 reflex questions

■ 1. TIME — how many hours since the prohibited thing was cooked in it? Less than twenty-four hours: the pot is בת יומא, its taste is still לשבח, nothing to hope for. Twenty-four hours or more: הויא נותן טעם לפגם — yet still forbidden לכתחלה (seif 2). And for a pot of בשר בחלב the count may start over (seif 4).
↓ if time has done its work, what can undo it?
■ 2. NATURE — is the prohibited matter still בעין, and is what you are cooking חריף? A badly rinsed pot, where forbidden fat is still stuck on, does not spoil with waiting; and a sharp dish — mainly vinegar, spices, or other sharp things — is not governed by the rule of נותן טעם לפגם (seif 3).
↓ if the taste really is spoiled and nothing cancels it, where do you stand?
■ 3. THE STOREY — לכתחלה or בדיעבד? This is the question that settles everything. לכתחלה, the rabbinic prohibition stands. בדיעבד — the food is already cooked, or the vessel is one whose history is unknown — the presumption סתם כלי עובד כוכבים הם בחזקת שאינם בני יומן tips the case to the permitted side (seifim 6 and 7).

3. Master grid A — time: בן יומו and אינו בן יומו

Basis: seifim 2 and 4 of the Mehaber, with the ט״ז and the פתחי תשובה. The twenty-four hours are counted from the moment the prohibited thing was cooked in the vessel, not from the day it was acquired nor from the day it was washed.

Other prohibitions, in the same situation The count does not restart מה שאין כן בשאר איסורים שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ד
📌 Key reading: seif 4 is the only place in the siman where time moves backwards. Everywhere else the clock runs one way only: the longer the vessel waits, the more its taste spoils. Here, heating water in a בשר בחלב pot that is still בת יומא recreates the prohibition — because in בשר בחלב alone we say that what was absorbed itself becomes נבילה, so that this water is fresh prohibited matter now entering the walls (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ב, referring to Siman 103).

⚖ Two discussions of the פתחי תשובה about this threshold

  • Does a merely rabbinic prohibition fall under the decree? summary — the נודע ביהודה writes that strictly it would be permitted לכתחלה, since that would be a decree upon a decree; but that one should not rely on this, as the poskim have not written such a ruling (פתחי תשובה יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ב).
  • And after twelve months? summary — the חכם צבי permits cooking a sharp food on Pesach in a chametz pot over which twelve months have passed, since after twelve months no taste at all remains, as with jars of יין נסך; yet he did not bring himself to permit cooking in it לכתחלה, and both the פנים מאירות and the רשב״א in his responsa disagree (פתחי תשובה יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ג).

4. Master grid B — what cancels the spoiling: matter בעין and the דבר חריף

Seif 3 is the lamdanic heart of the siman. It brings two corrections to seif 2: time spoils only what has been absorbed, and it spoils only with respect to a food that is not sharp.

CaseDoes the spoiling hold?The decisive wordsSource

It was the original prohibited food that was sharp; 24 h later a non-sharp permitted food is cooked in it 🟢 Yes — permitted דאין החריפות הראשון משוי ליה לשבח רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ב:ג, in the name of the ארוך כלל ל״ח
⚠ The sharpness one looks at is not the one people assume: what blocks the spoiling is the sharp character of what you are cooking now — sharpness rouses the wall and draws the taste out as though it were לשבח. The sharp character of the past prohibited food blocks nothing: the end of seif 3 says so expressly.
💡 The Shach extends the rule: summary — if a sharp food was cooked in a meat pot that is not בת יומא and then milk was cooked in it within twenty-four hours, the dish is permitted, and we do not say that the sharpness in the pot now makes it emit its taste לשבח; likewise if a sharp food was cooked in a prohibited pot that is not בת יומא and twenty-four hours then passed from that cooking (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ב, in the name of the דרכי משה and the תורת חטאת כלל ס״א).

5. Master grid C — the two presumptions: their vessels and ours

Seifim 6 and 7 are what make everything else workable: without them no vessel whose history is unknown would ever be usable.

PointThe ruleSource
A non-Jew's vessels, when nothing is known about them סתם כלי עובד כוכבים הם בחזקת שאינם בני יומן שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ו
The consequence when one transgressed and used them before kashering לפיכך אם עבר ונשתמש בהם קודם הכשר התבשיל מותר שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ו

The same water, had it been heated for drinking summary — the Shach: it would be permitted to drink, since for that it already counts as בדיעבד ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ה, in the name of the תורת חטאת כלל נ״ט
Something the non-Jew made for his own sake, which the Jew then buys Permitted לכתחלה, since it was already done by his hand — מקרי דיעבד רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ב:ו ; ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ו
Telling him “cook me vegetables in your pot”, or “make me a preserve” Forbidden — שכל האומר בשל לי הרי הוא כאלו בישל בידיו שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ו
Going through an apothecary, or another craftsman אפשר that this is permitted: craftsmen keep clean vessels for their trade so as not to spoil their work שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ו
The Mehaber's moral conclusion ובעל נפש יחוש שדברים אלו מביאים לידי טהרה ונקיות שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ו
Our own vessels, when nothing is known about them כך סתם כלים שלנו בחזקת שאינן בני יומן שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ז ; ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ו
A difference between the two presumptions summary — the באר שבע: for our own vessels the presumption applies only when the owner is not before us to be asked; whereas a non-Jew is not asked even when present, since his word neither forbids nor permits פתחי תשובה יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ו
📌 Key reading: the presumption of seif 6 is not a leniency about the vessel, it is a count of doubts. Two doubts suffice to permit after the fact; one does not suffice to permit in advance. That is why the same seif says in one breath that the non-Jew's vessel still needs kashering — and that if one transgressed, the food remains permitted.

7. Two side rules: a small prohibition in a large vessel, and a vessel lost in a mixture

Seif 5 — when disproportion permits

A small amount of prohibited matter absorbed into a vessel that is never used with so little permitted food that the prohibited matter could give taste to it — a large pot, a barrel: one may use it לכתחלה, even if it is בן יומו, לפי שאי אפשר לבא לידי נתינת טעם. But a vessel used with a small quantity — a bowl — remains forbidden, שאין מבטלין איסור לכתחלה אפילו איסור מועט ואפילו איסור הבלוע (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ה).

⚖ A seif the nossei kelim do not accept

The Shach: « והרא״ה והטור השיגו ע״ז », and so the ב״ח ruled (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ג). The Taz is sharper still: summary — as regards halacha we do not hold this way, as he established in Siman 99 (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ג). So remember seif 5 as the Mehaber's position, contested by both great commentators — and do not treat it as an everyday permission.

Seif 8 — the vessel lost in the pile

כלי שנאסר בבליעת איסור ונתערב באחרים ואינו ניכר בטל ברוב (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ח, in the name of the טור בשם רשב״א). A vessel is not a food: it is not measured in sixty, it is nullified by a simple majority. The Shach notes that the rule is given elsewhere: summary — it is a repetition of Siman 102, where it was explained (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ז).

8. The 6 golden rules

  1. A taste that spoils does not forbid. That is the whole sentence of seif 1, and the entire siman is only its application to the walls of a vessel (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:א).
  2. Twenty-four hours from the cooking of the prohibited food — and from nothing else: that is the threshold from בת יומא to אינה בת יומא (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ב).
  3. Permitted בדיעבד never means permitted לכתחלה. Spoiling by time was re-forbidden by the Sages, גזירה אטו בת יומא (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ב).
  4. Time spoils only what is absorbed. Forbidden fat left בעין on the wall does not spoil by waiting: the pot must have been well rinsed (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ג).
  5. Sharpness cancels the spoiling. A dish mainly of vinegar, spices or other sharp things does not benefit from the rule; an ordinary dish with a little spice does (רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ב:ג).
  6. In ignorance, presume the vessel is not בן יומו — one's own as much as a non-Jew's. It is a ספק ספיקא: enough בדיעבד, never לכתחלה (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ו-ז ; ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ד).

9. Mnemonic — the « לפגם » aide-mémoire

ל-פ-ג-ם — the four letters of the word לפגם
  • ל — לכתחלה ובדיעבד: the two storeys of the siman. A spoiled taste permits after the fact; it never authorises cooking in advance (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ב).
  • פ — פגום ולא בעין: time spoils only the absorbed taste. What remains visible on the wall is not spoiled by waiting (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ג).
  • ג — גזירה: a pot that is אינה בת יומא stays forbidden לכתחלה, גזירה אטו בת יומא (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ב ; עבודה זרה ע״ו ע״א).
  • ם — מעת לעת: it is time that spoils — twenty-four hours from the cooking, and for בשר בחלב the count may start over (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ב:ב, ד).
The ladder of the pot — from the most forbidden to the most permitted
  • בת יומא (less than 24 h) → forbidden, and the dish is forbidden (seif 2, a contrario)
  • אינה בת יומא + מאכל חריף → forbidden: sharpness cancels the spoiling (seif 3)
  • אינה בת יומא + איסור בעין → forbidden: nothing was spoiled (seif 3)
  • אינה בת יומא, well rinsed → forbidden לכתחלה, permitted בדיעבד (seif 2)
  • כלי שאין ידוע (their vessels or ours) → presumed not בן יומו: permitted בדיעבד (seifim 6-7)
  • איסור מועט בכלי גדול → the Mehaber permits even לכתחלה — but the Shach and the Taz object (seif 5)

10. The 5 classic pitfalls

❌ Pitfall 1 — Reading « נותן טעם לפגם מותר » as a permission to use: it is not a permission to use the vessel, it is a permission to eat what was made in it. Seif 2 says so in the same sentence: the pot really is נותן טעם לפגם, ואפילו הכי אסרו חכמים לבשל בה לכתחלה. Never turn a בדיעבד into a practice.
❌ Pitfall 2 — Believing that twenty-four hours always suffice: seif 3 overturns that twice. First when the pot was not well washed and the prohibited matter remained בעין — waiting spoils nothing that was not absorbed. Then when what is cooked in it is חריף — sharpness draws the taste out as though it were לשבח. Both exceptions sit in the same seif: never recite seif 2 without seif 3.
❌ Pitfall 3 — Counting the twenty-four hours from the wrong moment: the starting point is the cooking of the prohibited food — משנתבשלו בה האיסור — not the purchase, not the washing, not just any last use. And seif 4 adds a case where the clock is reset: in a בשר בחלב pot, heating water before one night has passed recreates the prohibition and the count starts from that hour — which is not so for other prohibitions.
❌ Pitfall 4 — Taking seif 5 for a general rule: the idea that a small prohibited amount absorbed into a very large vessel would permit using it לכתחלה even when בן יומו is the Mehaber's position, and both great commentators reject it: the Shach cites the objection of the רא״ה and the טור and the ruling of the ב״ח, and the Taz writes that as regards halacha we do not hold this way (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ג ; ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ג). A seif of the Mehaber is not always the accepted halacha.
❌ Pitfall 5 — Confusing the two outcomes of seif 9: vessels taken back from the non-Jew on the same day carry only one doubt and require הגעלה; those taken back only after a delay, or which then wait twenty-four hours with the Jew, carry two and we incline to leniency. And the Rama's final leniency, ובמקום צורך יש להקל בדיעבד, is rejected by the Taz: « כבר נתבאר שלענין הלכה לא קיימא לן כן » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ב ס״ק ט).

For halacha le-ma'aseh, consult your Rav.

11. Recap of the 12 seifim — one line each

SeifSubjectThe essentials
1The principleנותן טעם לפגם מותר — four words, and the siman's title: דין נותן טעם לפגם בכלים, in twelve seifim.
2The pot that is not בת יומאA pot in which twenty-four hours have passed since the prohibited food cooked in it is נותן טעם לפגם; and even so the Sages forbade cooking in it לכתחלה, גזירה אטו בת יומא — whether the wall absorbed prohibited food and one wishes to cook permitted food in it, or it absorbed milk and one wishes to cook meat in it, or the reverse.
3The two limits of the spoilingי״א: the pot is deemed spoiled only if it was well rinsed and all the forbidden fat stuck to it is gone; if the prohibited matter remained בעין, waiting does not spoil it. Rama: (a) if there is sixty against what is stuck alone, everything is permitted, for what is in the wall is already spoiled and needs no nullification; (b) י״א that with sharp foods this rule is not said — a dish mainly of vinegar, spices or other sharp things cooked in a prohibited pot that is not בת יומא is forbidden, but a dish is not called sharp for a little spice in it, וכן נוהגין; (c) conversely, if it was the prohibited food that was sharp, and twenty-four hours later a non-sharp permitted food was cooked in it — that is permitted.
4בשר בחלב and the count resetA pot absorbed with meat and milk in which water was heated before one night had passed: it counts as though the prohibited food had cooked in it afresh, and the twenty-four hours are counted from the hour the water was heated — which is not so for other prohibitions.
5A small prohibition in a large vesselA small amount of prohibited matter absorbed into a vessel not used with so little permitted food that it could give taste to it — a large pot, a barrel: it may be used לכתחלה even if it is בן יומו, since it is impossible to come to any giving of taste. But a vessel used with a small quantity, like a bowl, is forbidden: one does not nullify a prohibition לכתחלה, even a small one, even an absorbed one.
6The presumption about their vesselsסתם כלי עובד כוכבים are presumed not בני יומן; therefore if one transgressed and used them before kashering, the dish is permitted. Rama: but water heated in their vessel in order to knead bread may not be used for kneading — for that it is still לכתחלה, the benefit not having begun; whereas something the non-Jew made for his own purposes is permitted לכתחלה, מקרי דיעבד. Even so it is forbidden to tell him cook me vegetables in your pot, for whoever says “cook for me” is as if he cooked with his own hands; through apothecaries or other craftsmen אפשר that it is permitted, since they keep clean vessels for their trade; and a בעל נפש will take care, for these things lead to purity and cleanliness.
7The presumption about oursJust as their vessels are presumed not בני יומן, so too our own vessels are presumed not בני יומן.
8A vessel mixed among othersA vessel forbidden through absorption that became mixed among others and is no longer recognisable is nullified by the majority.
9Vessels left at a non-Jew'sOne should avoid leaving meal vessels in a non-Jew's house, lest he use them. Rama: even when given to a craftsman for repair one must put a mark on them; if one failed to, they require הגעלה when taken back — and only if they stayed a while, that is about half a day, for a short while is no concern. All this when one wishes to use them the same day they were given, where there is only one doubt; but if they were not taken back until after a delay, or waited twenty-four hours with the Jew, we incline to leniency. Taken back the same day and used without הגעלה, some forbid the dish as with a vessel that is certainly בן יומו; and where there is need one may be lenient בדיעבד. לכתחלה one should be careful in every case, even with the non-Jewish servants of a Jewish household.
10Bowls sent out with foodBowls a Jew sent to a non-Jew with food and which stayed there: if the food he sent is still recognisable in them, they are permitted; if not, they are forbidden — where dishes are customarily washed in hot water, lest they were washed together with a prohibited vessel.
11Buying vessels said to be newMeal vessels in a non-Jew's hand, who מסיח לפי תומו says they are new, and it appears to be as he says: it is permitted to buy them from him. Rama: some are stricter and hold him not to be believed — one should take vessels from a non-Jew only when he sells many vessels and one buys a vessel among the others, and such is the custom לכתחלה; but in a pressing situation, such as a guest at a non-Jew's who has no vessels, we follow the first opinion, and that is the main ruling.
12Bowls from overseasיש מי שאומר: bowls that come from overseas, hollowed, greenish or reddish, may never be used with hot food, because they are not new — non-Jews use them and it does not show.

⚖ The reflex in 3 questions

  1. Time? Less than twenty-four hours since the prohibited food cooked → nothing to be done. More than twenty-four hours → the taste is פגום.
  2. What could cancel it? Prohibited matter left בעין on the wall, or a sharp food in the pot. Either one, and the spoiling no longer counts.
  3. Which storey are you on? לכתחלה, the decree stands and one kashers. בדיעבד — the dish is already cooked, the vessel's history unknown — the spoiling and the presumption suffice.
For halacha le-ma'aseh, consult your Rav.
