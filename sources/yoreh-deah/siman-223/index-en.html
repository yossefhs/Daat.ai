Siman 223 — the father who forbids himself his son’s service so as not to tear him from study (יורה דעה רכ״ג)

A father forbids himself his son’s benefit — for his son, so that he not be torn from study by the thousand small services a son renders his father. And yet three services remain permitted: filling a jar, lighting the lamp, roasting a small fish. Why? Because we presume he did not aim at what costs almost no study time. Then the siman turns to property: the vow that outlives the dead, the estate suspended on a grandson becoming a man of learning, and what a forbidden heir may do with property he has no right to touch. (Shulḥan Arukh, Yoreh De’ah 223 — 4 seifim)

המדיר עצמו מהנאת בנו כדי שלא יתבטל הבן מלימודו בשבילו מותר הבן למלאות לו חבית של מים ולהדליק לו את הנר ולצלות לו דג קטן

One who forbids himself the benefit of his son, so that the son not be diverted from his study on his account — the son is permitted to fill him a jar of water, to light his lamp and to roast him a small fish. The vow is here an instrument for protecting study; and the permission that follows says what such a vow cannot have meant to aim at.

Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De'ah 223:1

Frequently asked questions — Siman 223

Is this siman about a father punishing his son?

No, and that is the commonest misreading of this text. The Shulḥan Arukh writes « המדיר עצמו מהנאת בנו כדי שלא יתבטל הבן מלימודו בשבילו » (שו״ע יו״ד רכ״ג:א): it is the father who deprives himself, and he does it so that his son may study. The Rambam reads the case the other way, and the Tur reports it; but the Meḥaber chose the reading of the Ramban and the Rosh.

Why do three services remain permitted?

Because a vow is read according to what we presume the man meant. The Shakh gives the measure: « דמסתמא ממילי זוטרי כי האי דליכא בהו ביטול תורה כל כך לא אדריה » (ש״ך יו״ד רכ״ג ס״ק א). The criterion is therefore not the size of the service but what it costs in study time.

And if the vow were of the other kind — the father forbidding his son?

Then, according to the Shakh, the Taz and the Ba’ḥ, the reason for the permission falls away and even these small services are forbidden. This is a divergence from the Rambam, who permits them in that case too. Since the Meḥaber reported only the Ramban’s reading, the two commentators draw that consequence.

Does a vow forbidding the inheritance outlive the father?

Only if he said so. “You are forbidden my benefit” lapses at his death, because the property is no longer “his”. If he added “in my lifetime and after my death”, the son does not inherit. The gemara derives a general principle from this: « אדם אוסר דבר שברשותו לכשיצא מרשותו » (נדרים מ״ז ע״א).

An heir who cannot touch his inheritance — what does he do?

Three positions, and they must be known before acting. The Ran: he does not give, he shows the place and withdraws. The Beit Yosef at siman 216: an actual gift to the sons and brothers is permitted because he informs them. The Maharshal gives a complete order — and expressly excludes the heir’s own children. A real case goes to a Rav before any step is taken.

Why does the Pitḥei Teshuva write nothing here?

The Pitḥei Teshuva surveys later responsa; his absence means that modern questions have not fallen on this matter, where practice is rare. He is read at simanim 224 and 225, and abundantly at siman 228, on the dissolution of vows. No entry has been manufactured here to complete a table.
