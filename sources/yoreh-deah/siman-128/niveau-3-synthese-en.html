Siman 128 — A non-Jew left alone with the wine — yiḥud

דין מקום שיש בו יין ונתיחד עובד כוכבים שמה

Nobody saw anything, nobody accuses, and no contact has been established. The wine was merely left where a non-Jew could reach it unseen — and that is enough. When unwatched presence alone forbids, what removes the fear, and how far the prohibition reaches: drinking, or benefit as well

1. The axiom: presence alone, before any contact

The starting point:

Simanim 123 to 126 asked what makes wine forbidden — contact, shaking, mixture, the connecting stream. Siman 127 asked who is believed to say that it happened. Siman 128 raises a third question, and it is the most unsettling of them: what if nobody saw anything, and nobody accuses? The wine was merely left where a non-Jew could reach it with no witness at all.

The Mehaber answers in a single line, and the line is severe: the bare situation of יחוד — having been left alone with the wine — forbids, with no contact established whatsoever. The four seifim that follow are the opposite catalogue, and that is where the substance of the siman lies: everything that removes the fear.

« עובד כוכבים שנתייחד עם היין אפילו ברשותנו אפילו שעה מועטת אסור בהנאה » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א).

Two words carry the whole seif, and both are concessions: אפילו ברשותנו — even in our own domain, where one thought oneself safe — and אפילו שעה מועטת — even for a moment.

The simanWhat it asksWhat must be established
123 to 126 — the physical regime of wine Was there contact, shaking, mixture? An event
127 — the word of one man Who is believed to say that the event took place? Credibility
128 — yiḥud Could the non-Jew have acted unseen? Nothing: the situation suffices
💡 The shift to grasp: this siman never judges a contact. It judges an opportunity. That is why its five seifim do not read as cases of mounting severity, but as one question asked five times over: was there anything holding him back? The bolt, the hole in the door, the loan secured on the wine, the city judge — each is an answer to that single question.

2. The 3 reflex questions

■ 1. WHO IS THE NON-JEW? An עובד עבודת כוכבים — yiḥud alone forbids, and forbids benefit as well. Someone known to us not to serve idols: yiḥud becomes permitted, in our domain and for a short time.
↓ the person settled, place and duration remain
■ 2. WHERE, AND FOR HOW LONG? With us and briefly, that is יחוד. In his own house, or for a long time even in ours, that is הפקדה — a deposit, and a deposit stays forbidden even with one who does not serve idols.
↓ and if he is found on the spot, with nothing seen
■ 3. COULD HE HAVE ACTED UNSEEN? All the rest of the siman turns on that: does he hold a loan secured on this very wine, which frees him from the fear of loss? Did he bolt the door? Could the Jew look through a hole? Would he be seized as a thief? Every answer moves the verdict by one step.

3. Who the non-Jew is — the categories of seif 1

Seif 1 does not lay down one rule but a rule and its exception, and the exception is defined by knowledge, not by a favourable guess.

Who is meantThe law of yiḥudVerdictSource
One who serves idols « עובד כוכבים שנתייחד עם היין אפילו ברשותנו אפילו שעה מועטת אסור בהנאה » Forbidden — benefit included שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א
One known to us not to serve idols « אבל מי שנודע לנו שאינו עובד עבודת כוכבים מותר לייחד יין אצלו ברשותנו לזמן מועט » Yiḥud is permitted שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א
Who that is in practice summary: the גר תושב and the ישמעאלים, as the Tur, the Rashba and the Rambam hold A named category, not a guessed one ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק א
How far the leniency reaches « כגון כדי שילך כדי מיל או יותר אפילו בעיר שכולה עובדי כוכבים ואפילו הודיעו שהוא מפליג » A measured span, and two aggravations that do not block it שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א
The Shach's restriction upon it « מיהו נראה דהיינו דוקא אם הוא בענין שאין לחוש שנגעו לשתות או להנאה אחרת » It lapses where one may fear he touched in order to drink ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק א
Non-Jews of חוצה לארץ — the Rama's gloss « עובדי כוכבים שבחוצה לארץ אע״פ שאינן עובדי עבודת כוכבים מ״מ אין מייחדים יין אצלם » Wine is not left alone with them רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ח:א
The Shach on the reach of that gloss « דאם יחדו אצלו שרי בדיעבד כמו גר תושב וישמעאלים אלא דלכתחלה מיהת אסור » A rule at the outset, not a verdict after the fact ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ד
⚠ Read the Rama's gloss together with the Shach: taken alone it seems to cancel the Mehaber's exception for our whole time and place. The Shach reduces it to what it is — a demand lechatchila — and he draws his proof from seif 4 itself, where the Rama permits a far broader case of yiḥud. The Baer Hetev sums up the Shach in the same direction, and the Pitchei Teshuva reports in the name of the Tzemach Tzedek a further restriction, turning on the shape of the vessel.

« דדוקא בזמן מועט שרי בדיעבד ודוקא במקום שאין לחוש לנגיעה ולא לשתיה להנאתו » (פתחי תשובה יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק א).

4. Master grid A — יחוד and הפקדה: the two regimes of seif 1

The whole of seif 1 rests on a distinction the Mehaber does not define and the Taz spells out: יחוד and הפקדה are not two words for one thing.

The testיחודהפקדה
The place Our domain — ברשותנו His own house — בביתו
The span לזמן מועט — the time it takes to walk a mil, or somewhat more לזמן מרובה — and then it is forbidden even in our own houses
What was handed over Nothing: he was left there, he was not made a keeper The wine, placed in his hands
With one who does not serve idols Permitted Forbidden
If he transgressed and deposited anyway — אסור בשתיה — and not בהנאה

« אבל אין מפקידין אצלו יין בביתו ואפילו בבתינו אסור אם הוא לזמן מרובה » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א).

The Taz gives the two possible readings of that boundary, and for the word יחוד he adopts Rashi's sense: « דיחוד היינו שמייחדו עם העובד כוכבים ברשות ישראל ולא שם את העובד כוכבים שומר עליו כלל » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק א).

He adds why the stringency on deposit survives in our own day: « דלענין לכתחלה להפקיד אצלם מחזקינן להו כעובדי עבודת כוכבים » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק א).

⚖ Why a deposit is graver than being left alone

It is not merely a matter of duration. The Shach supplies the reason, and the fear itself changes kind: in a deposit one does not fear that he will touch — one fears that he will swap.

« דטעמא דהפקיד אצלו אסור משום שאנו חוששין שמא החליפו בשלו ושלו ודאי אסור » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ג).

His own wine is forbidden with certainty; ours is forbidden only out of fear. A long deposit opens the door to substitution, which is no doubt at all but a certain prohibition in disguise. And the Shach notes that the ban on depositing does not depend on the neighbourhood: summary: it holds even in a town whose majority is Jewish (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ב).

5. Master grid B — the ladder of the three verdicts

The siman has three outcomes rather than two, and half the misreadings start there. To forbid wine is not always to forbid it in the same measure.

The verdictWhat it meansWhere it appears in the simanSource
מותר בשתייה It may be drunk Unbolted door; seized as a thief for entering; a powerless non-Jew in the marketplace; barrels visible through a hole in the door שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ג ; שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ד ; שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ה
אסור בשתיה It may not be drunk, but benefit is allowed The one named case: having transgressed and deposited with a non-idolater שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א
אסור בהנאה Neither drinking nor benefit of any kind Yiḥud with an idolater; barrels that could not be seen; one who is not seized as a thief, indoors and in the marketplace alike שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א ; שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ג ; שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ד ; שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ה
⚠ Seif 1 is not uniform: its opening forbids בהנאה and its close only בשתיה. The Shach infers that in the case of a deposit benefit stays permitted in every event — and reports that the Bach disputes it, allowing benefit only where it is known that he did not touch (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ג). This is one of the live disagreements of the siman, and real money hangs on it: selling the wine, or pouring it away.

6. The non-Jew found beside the wine: the מלוה, the קצף, the ברזא (seif 2)

Seif 2 leaves deliberate yiḥud behind and turns to chance discovery: one walks in, and he is there. And the Mehaber overturns the intuition — it is not nearness that decides, but interest.

The situationThe Mehaber's wordingVerdictSource
He holds a loan on this very wine — אפותיקי « עובד כוכבים שנמצא בבית שבו יין אם יש לו מלוה על אותו יין כגון שעשאו אפותיקי » Forbidden שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
And even had the wine borne a seal « אסור ואפילו היה היין חתום בחותם אחד » A single seal does not redeem the loan שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
He holds a loan on the Jew but not on the wine, and it has fallen due « אבל אם אין לו מלוה על היין אף על פי שיש לו מלוה על ישראל בעל היין והגיע זמן המלוה מותר » Permitted שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
Even standing right against the barrel, within arm's reach, with no seal at all « ואפילו היה עומד בצד היין סמוך לו ממש בכדי פישוט ידים » Still permitted שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
The reason, from the Rosh « מפני שמאחר שהוא יודע שיפסיד ישראל יינו הוא ירא ליגע » He is afraid to touch שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
Foam in his hand « ואפילו נמצא בידו קצף שרגיל לעלות על פי היין מות׳ שאנו תולי׳ לומ׳ מדופני החבית לקחו ולא מפיו » We ascribe it to the side of the barrel שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
But whatever is found only at the mouth « אבל דברים שאינם נמצאים אלא על פי החבית אסור » Forbidden רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
The tap in his hand « וכן אם מצא שהעובד כוכבים תופס הברזא בידו היין אסור ולא אמרינן דהברזא מנפשיה נפל והעובד כוכבים הגביהו » Forbidden — we do not assume it fell רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
💡 The key to seif 2, in one sentence: a loan secured on the wine wipes out the fear of loss, and it is the fear of loss that guards the wine. A man who will forfeit his pledge by spoiling the wine does not touch it; a man who will be repaid out of that very wine has nothing left to lose. Bodily nearness — בכדי פישוט ידים — weighs nothing against that reckoning.

The same logic governs the foam found in his hand: we ascribe to the commonplace. The Rama states the principle in four words, דתלינן תמיד בדבר הרגיל, and the gemara had already set it down on this very case.

« חמרא שרי אימר מגבה דחביתא שקלתיה ואף על גב דליכא תו אימר אתרמויי אתרמי לה » (עבודה זרה ע׳ ע״ב).

⚖ The ברזא: where the Shach and the Taz part company

The Shach limits what is feared, and draws a consequence for our own time: « ומשמע דחיישינן שהעובד כוכבים הוציא ברזא ולא חיישינן שהכניס אצבעו לפנים ונגע בו » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ח).

The Taz refuses that opening, and forbids the whole barrel: « משמע כל היין שבחבית ולא מהני כאן היתר דהפסד מרובה דניצוק חיבור » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ג).

The Baer Hetev reports both and notes that the Nekudot HaKesef upholds the Shach. On this point the siman does not close: for halacha le-ma'aseh, ask your Rav.

7. The shared house: the bolt drawn from within, and the holes in the door (seif 3)

Seif 3 is the harshest case of the siman, and for a precise reason: the non-Jew has wine of his own inside that house. The Taz stresses it — this is a רבותא, a deliberate sharpening of the case.

« רבותא קאמר אע״ג דיש גם לעובד כוכבים שם יין שלו או שיש לו בשותפות יין עם הישראל » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ד).

■ HE ENTERS AND BOLTS THE DOOR FROM WITHIN — so that a Jew wishing to come in could not do so without his consent. → The wine is forbidden.
↓ unless a line of sight remains
■ THERE ARE HOLES IN THE DOOR — and the Jew can look and see all the barrels from there. → All are permitted to drink, open ones included.
↓ and if sight reaches only some of them
■ WHAT CAN BE SEEN IS PERMITTED, THE REST IS FORBIDDEN IN BENEFIT — sealed barrels included, if he stayed long enough to open, re-bung and let dry.

« בית שיש לישראל ועובד כוכבים יין בתוכו ונכנס העובד כוכבים וסגר הדלת אחריו במנעול מבפנים » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ג).

« ואם היו נקבים בדלת שישראל יכול להסתכל ולראות משם כל החביות מותרות בשתיה אפילו הן פתוחות » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ג).

« ואם לאו את שיכול לראות דרך שם מותר והשאר אסור בהנאה » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ג).

« אפילו חביות סתומות אם שהה כדי שיפתח ויגוף ותגוב » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ג).

The case comes from an incident in the gemara, and the division there is already the Mehaber's own: « כל דלהדי ביזעא שרי דהאי גיסא והאי גיסא אסיר » (עבודה זרה ע׳ ע״א).

The refinementWhat it addsSource
The bolting must be known, not supposed « דוקא שידוע שנעלו בפנים אבל אם אין ידוע אע״פ שיש מנעול בפנים אין אוסרים מספק » ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ה
But that holds by day; night reverses the presumption « והיינו ביום אבל בלילה אמרינן מסתמא שנעלו » ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ה
The Shach says the same and extends it to the holes in the door « מיהו הא דבעינן שידוע שסגר היינו ביום אבל בלילה מסתמא סגר כשיש לו שייכות בבית ואסור » ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י
Why even a sealed barrel does not save the case « דיכול לישמט ולומר ישן הייתי ולכך לא פתחתי לך » ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ו
And why a Jew living in the house is not enough summary: because the non-Jew has a stake in that house ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י
⚠ The decisive word is לישמט: the whole seif turns on whether the non-Jew has a way out. Bolting from within hands him one — “I was asleep, that is why I did not open to you” — and it is that escape, not the bolt itself, that forbids the wine. A hole in the door takes it away from him: he can no longer claim that he could not be seen.

8. The house holding only the Jew's wine: נתפס כגנב (seif 4)

Take away from seif 3 the non-Jew's wine and his stake in the house, and nothing is left but the Jew's wine and an intruder. The test then changes altogether — the question is no longer whether he could slip away, but whether he could be seized.

The situationThe Mehaber's wordingVerdictSource
The door is not bolted « בית שאין בו אלא יינו של ישראל ונמצא עובד כוכבים בתוכו אם אין הדלת נעול מותר בשתיה » Permitted to drink שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ד
Why — the Rosh's reason as the Shach gives it « אע״פ שאינו נתפס כגנב על הכניסה דמירתת שמא יבא ישראל ויראהו נוגע » He dreads being caught ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י״א
Bolted, and he is not seized as a thief « ואם הוא נעול במפתח מבפנים אם אינו נתפס כגנב אסור בהנאה » Forbidden, benefit included שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ד
Bolted, but he is seized as a thief for entering « ואם הוא נתפס כגנב על הכניסה מותר בשתייה » Permitted to drink שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ד
What the phrase means « בכ״מ שהוזכר נתפס כגנב פירושו שאם ימצאוהו יהא נתפס כגנב » A liability, not a sentence ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י״ב
The Taz sharpens it further « פי׳ שאם ימצאהו ישראל יהיה נתפס כדין גנב ע״פ המושל והוא אינו אלם » A power above him is required ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ח

The formula comes straight from the gemara, where it settles two successive incidents: « אם נתפס עליו כגנב שרי ואי לא אסיר » (עבודה זרה ע׳ ע״א).

And in the second: « אם נתפס עליו כגנב חמרא שרי ואי לא אסור » (עבודה זרה ס״א ע״ב).

⚖ The Rama's gloss, and its two boundaries

Here the Rama opens, for our own time, the widest door in the siman: « וי״א דבזמן הזה שאין העובדי כוכבים מנסכין אפילו אינו נתפס כגנב שרי » (רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ח:ד).

Yet he shuts it again at once on one side: « אא״כ יש לחוש שנגעו לשתות ממנו או להנאה אחרת » (רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ח:ד).

And on the other: « מיהו אם פי החבית רחב או שהוא בקנקן שיש לחוש שמא נגע בו דרך מתעסק חיישינן » (רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ח:ד).

The Shach adds that even in the excluded case benefit stays permitted where there is loss: « ואפ״ה שרי בהנאה במקום הפסד בזמן הזה » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י״ג).

9. The barrels in the marketplace (seif 5)

The last seif steps outdoors. In the marketplace there is no door, no bolt and no hole to look through: nothing is left but the non-Jew's own dread, and that dread is measured by his standing.

« היו בשוק חביות יין ונמצא עובד כוכבים ביניהם » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ה).

« אם הוא עובד כוכבים חלש שאימת שופטי העיר עליו ואם ימצאוהו נוגע נתפס כגנב מותר בשתייה » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ה).

What seif 5 asksWhat it draws from itVerdict
Is he חלש — without power or protection? Then the city judges count for him Condition met
Does dread of the judges weigh on him — שאימת שופטי העיר עליו? That is what makes a נתפס כגנב outside a house Condition met
Would he be seized for touching, and not for entering? In a marketplace entering is no offence: the act alone is Permitted to drink
One of the three is missing Nothing was holding him back Forbidden in benefit
💡 Why the siman ends there: the Mehaber closes with a cross-reference — summary: further laws of this matter are found below in siman 129 (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ה). Siman 128 has set out the static situation — the non-Jew is there, and he is found there. Siman 129 will take up the moving one: the Jew who goes out and comes back, and the wine entrusted meanwhile.

10. מירתת — what removes the fear, and on what conditions

One mechanism runs through seifim 2 to 5 in different dress: the non-Jew dreads, and therefore does not touch. But that dread is never assumed for free — each time it demands a foothold, and the siman names which.

What holds him backWhereIts conditionSource
He would forfeit what he counts on being paid from Seif 2 Holding no loan on that very wine שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב
A Jew may walk in and see him touching Seif 4 He must know that his touch ruins the wine ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י״א
Sight may reach him through the door Seif 3 By day; at night the presumption reverses ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י
He would be seized as a thief Seifim 4 and 5 A power above him, and he himself no strongman ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ח
The street runs past the house Seif 2, as the Bach holds That Jews actually pass along it ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ז

The condition laid down by the Bach and reported by the Shach is the easiest to forget: « ומיהו ודאי דוקא בבית הפתוח לר״ה וישראלים עברי ברה״ר הא לאו הכי לא מירתת » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ז).

And the Shach's condition on seif 4 belongs to the same family: « והיינו דוקא כשהעובד כוכבים יודע דמגע עובדי כוכבים אסור לישראל ויפסיד יינו בכך » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י״א).

⚠ The opposite of מירתת is לישמט: wherever the non-Jew has a ready excuse, the dread collapses and the prohibition returns. The gemara frames it as a test in its own right, and the severity of seif 3 comes from there: « אי אית ליה לאישתמוטי חמרא אסיר ואי לא חמרא שרי » (עבודה זרה ע׳ ע״א).

11. The 6 golden rules

  1. Yiḥud forbids on its own. No contact has to be established: the situation suffices, even in our domain and even for a moment (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א).
  2. Who the non-Jew is comes before everything. Seif 1 turns on knowledge — מי שנודע לנו שאינו עובד עבודת כוכבים — not on a lenient assumption (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א).
  3. A deposit is not a long yiḥud. It is a different fear: not that he will touch, but that he will swap (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ג).
  4. What guards the wine is what the non-Jew stands to lose. A loan on the wine erases that loss, and that is why it forbids — whereas nearness settles nothing (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב).
  5. Possible sight counts as watching. Barrels visible through a hole are permitted and the others are not — the line runs between them (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ג).
  6. נתפס כגנב is no punishment but a restraint. It is enough that he be exposed to seizure, and enough that it be for entering (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ד ; ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י״ב).

12. Mnemonic — the memory aid « יחוד »

י-ח-ו-ד — the four letters of the word יחוד
  • י — יחוד: unwatched presence on its own, even in our domain and even for a moment — the whole of seif 1 (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א).
  • ח — חותם and חלש: the single seal that does not redeem a loan on the wine (seif 2), and the powerless non-Jew on whom dread of the judges weighs (seif 5).
  • ו — ולא מפקידין: one does not deposit — neither in his house nor for long in ours; and if one transgressed, the wine is forbidden only to drink (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א).
  • ד — דלת: the door. Bolted from within it forbids, pierced it permits what can be seen, open it permits all (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ג ; שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ד).
The ladder of the siman — from the most permitted to the most forbidden
  • אין הדלת נעול → מותר בשתייה (seif 4)
  • נתפס כגנב על הכניסה → מותר בשתייה (seif 4)
  • עובד כוכבים חלש בשוק → מותר בשתייה (seif 5)
  • עומד בצד היין בלא מלוה עליו → permitted, even within arm's reach (seif 2)
  • עבר והפקיד → אסור בשתיה (seif 1)
  • יש לו מלוה על אותו יין → forbidden, even under a seal (seif 2)
  • סגר הדלת במנעול מבפנים → אסור בהנאה for whatever was out of sight (seif 3)
  • אינו נתפס כגנב → אסור בהנאה (seifim 4 and 5)
  • נתייחד עם עובד עבודת כוכבים → אסור בהנאה (seif 1)

13. The 5 classic pitfalls

❌ Pitfall 1 — Taking יחוד and הפקדה for two words with one meaning: they are two distinct regimes, and seif 1 permits the first to the very man to whom it forbids the second. The Taz gives the two ways of drawing the line: by duration, as the Tur has it, or by whether the non-Jew was made a keeper, as he reads Rashi. Merging the two words loses the exception of seif 1 entirely.
❌ Pitfall 2 — Believing that a seal settles the question: in seif 2 a man holding a loan on the wine forbids it even under a seal. And conversely one who holds none does not forbid it even with no seal at all. The seal is not the subject of siman 128 — it is the subject of siman 130, and the Shach says so outright, referring there for the substance of the matter (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ו).
❌ Pitfall 3 — Believing that nearness forbids: the Mehaber takes pains to say the opposite, and says it three times in one sentence — standing beside the wine, right next to it, within arm's reach, and with no seal at all: permitted. What forbids is never distance; it is the absence of a reason to hold back.
❌ Pitfall 4 — Reading נתפס כגנב as “he would be punished”: the Shach notes that the phrase everywhere denotes a liability — were he found, he would be seized as a thief — and the Taz adds two conditions that get forgotten: that a power stand above him, and that he himself not be a man of force. A non-Jew whom nobody can touch does not fall under this leniency.
❌ Pitfall 5 — Stretching the Rama's gloss on seif 4 across the whole siman: it is broad, but the Rama himself fences it twice — the fear that he touched in order to drink, and the wide-mouthed vessel — and the Pitchei Teshuva narrows it further in the name of the Tzemach Tzedek. Above all it was stated on seif 4, about a house holding only the Jew's wine. Carrying it over to seif 3, where the non-Jew has wine of his own and a stake in the house, erases the very thing that makes that seif grave.

For halacha le-ma'aseh, ask your Rav.

14. Practical cases

The siman supplies tests, not ready-made rulings. Each case below states which test of the siman is at stake, and what the siman leaves open.

The situationThe test in the simanWhat remains to be settled
Bottles of non-cooked kosher wine sit for an hour in a non-Jewish mover's van while he takes his break This is יחוד and not a deposit: nothing was handed over. What remains is who he is, and whether the van is our domain or his (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:א) How the man and the place are classified
A courier carries the cartons up to the flat alone while the owner waits downstairs The question is not the span but the escape: can he be walked in on at any moment? This is the מירתת of seif 4 (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י״א) Whether he knows that a touch ruins the wine
Wine left in pledge with a non-Jewish lender This is the named case of seif 2: a loan on that wine. A single seal changes nothing here (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ח:ב) Nothing in principle; everything in the facts
An opened bottle is left on the table while one steps out of the room The Rama on seif 4 aims at exactly this shape: פי החבית רחב, and the fear of an absent-minded touch (רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ח:ד) The line between a stoppered bottle and a broached one
A cellar whose door stays open onto a busy corridor This is seif 4 in its simplest form — an unbolted door — with the Bach's condition as the Shach reports it: that Jews actually pass by (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק ז) Traffic in fact, and not merely in principle
The same cellar, at night The Shach and the Taz reverse the presumption once darkness falls, and sight through the door no longer avails (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ח ס״ק י) What counts as “night” in a lit and frequented place

⚖ The question that comes before all the others

None of these cases is settled on siman 128 alone. Cooked wine, pasteurised wine, the standing of non-Jews today, the measure of הפסד מרובה — all of that belongs to the neighbouring simanim and to the poskim, and it is the business of Level 4. What siman 128 gives, and gives in full, is the grid: who, where, how long, and what was holding him back.

For halacha le-ma'aseh, ask your Rav.

15. Recap of all 5 seifim — one line each

SeifSubjectThe essentials
1יחוד and הפקדהA non-Jew left alone with the wine — even in our own domain, even for a brief while — renders it forbidden in benefit as well. But one who is known to us not to serve idols: wine may be left alone with him in our domain and for a short time — the time it takes to walk a mil or somewhat more — and this even in a wholly non-Jewish town, and even if he was told that the Jew is going far off. But one does not deposit wine with him in his house; and even in our houses it is forbidden if it is for a long time; and if one transgressed and deposited, the wine is forbidden to drink. Rama: non-Jews abroad, though they do not serve idols, are likewise not left alone with wine.
2Found in a house where there is wineIf he holds a loan on that very wine — say he made it an אפותיקי — it is forbidden, even had the wine borne a seal. But if he holds no loan on the wine, even though he holds a loan on the Jewish owner and it has fallen due, it is permitted — even if he stood against the wine, right beside it, within arm's reach, and even with no seal at all, since knowing that the Jew would lose his wine he is afraid to touch. And even if the foam that usually rises to the surface was found in his hand, it is permitted: we ascribe it to the side of the barrel and not to its mouth. Rama: but whatever is found only at the mouth is forbidden; likewise if he was found holding the tap, the wine is forbidden, and we do not say the tap fell of itself — for we always ascribe to what is usual.
3The house where Jew and non-Jew both have wineThe non-Jew goes in and bolts the door behind him from within, so that a Jew wishing to enter could not do so without his consent: the wine is forbidden. And if there were holes in the door through which the Jew can look and see all the barrels, all are permitted to drink, open ones included. If not, what he can see is permitted and the rest forbidden in benefit — sealed barrels included, if he stayed long enough to open, make a new bung and let it dry. And this even if a Jew was living in that house.
4The house holding only the Jew's wineA non-Jew is found inside. If the door is not bolted: permitted to drink. If it is locked with a key from within: if he is not seized as a thief, it is forbidden in benefit; and if he is seized as a thief for entering, it is permitted to drink. Rama: and some say that nowadays, when non-Jews pour no libations, it is permitted even where he is not seized as a thief, unless there is reason to fear that he touched it to drink from it or for some other benefit; yet if the mouth of the barrel is wide, or it is in a קנקן where one may fear he touched it inadvertently, we do take that into account.
5The barrels in the marketplaceThere were barrels of wine in the marketplace and a non-Jew was found among them. If he is a powerless non-Jew upon whom the dread of the city judges weighs, and were they to find him touching he would be seized as a thief: permitted to drink. If not: forbidden in benefit. And the Mehaber refers to siman 129 for further laws of this matter.

⚖ The reflex in 3 questions

  1. Who was it? An idolater, or one known to us not to be. The whole first half of seif 1 depends on that.
  2. Where, and for how long? Our domain and briefly is yiḥud; his house or a long span is a deposit — and a deposit stays forbidden.
  3. What was holding him back? A loss he dreads, a possible line of sight, seizure as a thief. If nothing held him back, the wine is forbidden in benefit too.
