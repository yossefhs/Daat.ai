1. The siman in one sentence

One may attach only to a status that a man's word could install. Pork, נבלה, the idol, one's mother, יין נסך are forbidden of themselves: nothing attaches to them, and the word does not bind. But the Shulchan Arukh nonetheless requires of an עם הארץ that he go and seek release — by a פתח, not by a mere חרטה — כדי שלא ינהגו קלות ראש בנדרים; and the Rama adds that nowadays all are so judged.

4. The three golden rules

1. The criterion is not “forbidden or permitted”, it is “producible or not by a word”. נותר and פיגול are forbidden by the Torah and yet one may attach to them, because the flesh of a קרבן is installed by a word. Pork cannot be. The Rambam formulates it in one line, and the Shach copies it at ס״ק א.
« זֶה הַכְּלָל כָּל הַמֵּשִׂים דְּבָרִים הַמֻּתָּרִים כַּדְּבָרִים הָאֲסוּרִים אִם אוֹתוֹ דָּבָר הָאָסוּר יָכוֹל לַעֲשׂוֹתוֹ בְּנֵדֶר הֲרֵי אֵלּוּ אֲסוּרִים. וְאִם אֵינוֹ יָכוֹל לַעֲשׂוֹתוֹ בְּנֵדֶר הֲרֵי אֵלּוּ מֻתָּרִין » (רמב״ם הלכות נדרים פרק א׳ הלכה ט)
2. “It did not take hold” is not a practical conclusion. The seif says in the same sentence that nothing binds and that an עם הארץ must seek release — and the Rama, in his gloss, adds that nowadays everyone is so judged. The consequence is simple: one never concludes for oneself from “it was nothing” to “I have nothing to do”.
3. A conditional formulation is not an attachment. “If I do this, my wine shall be יין נסך” changes the nature of the act: it is no longer an התפסה but a stringency one imposes on oneself. That is why the authorities divide on it, while they do not divide on the simple formulation. And that is why the Rama can draw from it the power of the community.

5. The classic pitfalls

  • Thinking that “forbidden by the Torah” is stronger, so binds better. It is the reverse: it does not bind at all.
  • Thinking that “nothing took hold” closes the matter. The seif nonetheless requires a שאלה of an עם הארץ, and the Rama extends the category to our own day.
  • Thinking a חרטה suffices. Here it does not: a פתח is required, and not just any (Shach ס״ק ג in the name of the Ran).
  • Confusing the simple utterance with the conditional one. The first does not bind, undisputedly; the second is disputed.
  • Forgetting the Shach's precision on “יין נסך”. He must have said explicitly that he meant the wine of the cult (ס״ק ו).
  • Forgetting the exception of אונס. One who was compelled is not subject to the requirement (Shach ס״ק ב, in the name of the Rashbatz).

7. The revision checklist

  1. I can state the Rambam's rule on what may serve as an anchor.
  2. I can explain why נותר binds while pork does not.
  3. I know that “it does not bind” and “there is nothing to do” are two different things.
  4. I know the difference between חרטה and פתח, and the limit the Ran sets on the latter.
  5. I know the ma'hloket between the Bach and the Shach on the extent of the requirement today.
  6. I know why the utterance said בדרך קנס is treated apart, and what the Rivash brings to it.
  7. I know what the Pitchei Teshuva leaves open, and that the Nekudot HaKesef have but one entry here.
  8. I know that this chapter is for study, and that any real vow is brought before a Rav.
