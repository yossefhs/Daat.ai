📜 The text of the Shulchan Aruch — a single seif

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

כָּל דָּבָר שֶׁאָסוּר מִן הַתּוֹרָה, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמֻּתָּר בַּהֲנָאָה, אִם הוּא דָּבָר הַמְּיֻחָד לְמַאֲכָל — אָסוּר לַעֲשׂוֹת בּוֹ סְחוֹרָה (וְאַף לְהַלְווֹת עָלָיו, וַאֲפִלּוּ לִקְנוֹתוֹ לְהַאֲכִילוֹ לְפוֹעֲלָיו עוֹבְדֵי כּוֹכָבִים — אָסוּר); חוּץ מִן הַחֵלֶב, שֶׁהֲרֵי נֶאֱמַר בּוֹ "יֵעָשֶׂה לְכָל מְלָאכָה". וְאִם נִזְדַּמְּנוּ לְצַיָּד חַיָּה וְעוֹף וְדָגִים טְמֵאִים (וְכֵן מִי שֶׁנִּזְדַּמְּנָה לוֹ נְבֵילָה וּטְרֵיפָה בְּבֵיתוֹ) — מֻתָּר לְמָכְרָם, וּבִלְבַד שֶׁלֹּא יִתְכַּוֵּן לְכָךְ. (הגה: וְצָרִיךְ לְמָכְרָהּ מִיָּד, וְלֹא יַמְתִּין עַד שֶׁתְּהֵא שְׁמֵנָה אֶצְלוֹ. וְכֵן מֻתָּר לִגְבּוֹת דְּבָרִים טְמֵאִים בְּחוֹבוֹ מִן הָעוֹבֵד כּוֹכָבִים, דְּהָוֵי כְּמַצִּיל מִיָּדָם; וְאָסוּר לִמְכּוֹר לְעוֹבֵד כּוֹכָבִים נְבֵילָה בְּחֶזְקַת כְּשֵׁירָה — וְעַיֵּן בְּחֹשֶׁן הַמִּשְׁפָּט סִימָן רכ״ח. וְכָל דָּבָר שֶׁאֵין אִיסּוּרוֹ אֶלָּא מִדִּבְרֵיהֶם — מֻתָּר לַעֲשׂוֹת בּוֹ סְחוֹרָה.)

1. Opening the sugya — the ban on trading in what is set aside for food

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

The foundation — trading stays forbidden even where benefit is permitted

The siman holds in one sentence: anything forbidden to eat by Torah law — even those things from which benefit remains permitted, such as nevela and most of the prohibitions — may not be made the object of trade, so long as it is a thing set aside for food. And the difficulty rises at once: if benefit is permitted, and there is here only a gain of money and no eating at all, where does the ban come from? On this the Rishonim and the A’haronim part ways, and this is the ḥakira of principle running through the whole siman.
« כל דבר שאסור מן התורה אף על פי שמותר בהנאה אם הוא דבר המיוחד למאכל אסור לעשות בו סחורה » (שולחן ערוך יו״ד קי״ז)
What the seif extends, and to whom it belongs. The seif does not stop at buying and selling: two widenings follow it. In the text Sefaria serves, as in the printed editions, they are given in parentheses, with their source, and set within the gloss body — that is to say they are the Rama’s, not the Mehaber’s. The first is lending against a pledge.
« או להלוות עליו » (רמ״א יו״ד קי״ז — תרומת הדשן סימן ר׳)
The second is buying the forbidden thing in order to feed one’s non-Jewish workers.
« …לקנותו להאכילו לפועליו עובדי כוכבים אסור » (רמ״א יו״ד קי״ז — הגהות מיימוני)
These two widenings are precisely the nafka mina of the ḥakira: on one root they follow of themselves, on the other they call for examination — and it is the Taz who works each of them, each at its own ס״ק.

2. The ḥakira on the root of the ban — rabbinic or from the Torah

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

This is the ḥakira of principle, and most of the siman’s apparatus turns on it.

Where does the ban on trading come from?
  • First side — a fence of the Sages. The Taz reports that the Beit Yosef gives, in the name of the Rashba, a reason of apprehension: handling continually a forbidden thing that is made to be eaten, one will end up tasting it. The ban would then be no more than a rabbinic distancing.
    « בב״י בשם רשב״א הטעם גזירה שמא יבא לאכול מהם » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
  • Second side — a din of the Torah. The Taz is struck at once: the gemara of Pesachim, in the chapter כל שעה, makes it a Torah prohibition, drawn from the word לכם.
    « ותמהתי שהרי בפרק כל שעה… איתא שהוא איסור דאורייתא » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
    « שָׁאנֵי הָתָם, דְּאָמַר קְרָא: ״לָכֶם״ — שֶׁלָּכֶם יְהֵא » (פסחים כ״ג ע״א)
    « אִי הָכִי, אֲפִילּוּ לְכַתְּחִלָּה נָמֵי! שָׁאנֵי הָכָא, דְּאָמַר קְרָא: ״יִהְיוּ״ — בַּהֲוָיָיתָן » (פסחים כ״ג ע״א)
The Taz’s answer to his own objection. The Taz does not let the two sides stand against each other: he reads both faces in the verse itself — a permission through לכם and a prohibition through יהיו — and concludes that the Torah handed the Sages the drawing of the line. The Rashba’s גזרה is therefore no rabbinic invention set beside the Torah: it is the exercise of a power the Torah entrusted to them.
« הרי הדבר מסור לחכמים שהם יבחרו מה יהיה אסור ומה יהיה מותר » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
« דנו חכמים שבכל מידי דהוא מאכל יש לחסרו שמא יבוא לאוכלו אבל דבר המיוחד למלאכה מותר » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
And a third reading, which the Taz reports without adopting. He then saw that the Terumat HaDeshen, in the name of a gloss of Tosafot, holds the דרשה to be an אסמכתא — a support, not a derivation: the ban would then be purely rabbinic.
« שוב ראיתי בת״ה סי׳ ר׳ שכתב בשם גליון תוספות דהך דרשא הוה אסמכתא » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
« דבאמת אינו אלא מדרבנן דאסמכתא בעלמא הוא » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
summary: it is out of this third reading that the Taz’s difficulty will be born, and out of the difficulty his rule (section 3).
What changes according to the side one holds:
  1. Lending against a pledge. The Terumat HaDeshen saw in it only something unseemly; but on the Rashba’s reason as reported by the Beit Yosef — the fear he will come to eat of it — there is here, says the Taz, an outright prohibition.
    « בת״ה סי׳ ר׳ צידד בדבר ומסיק שמכוער הדבר להלוות על כותלי חזיר » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ב)
    « ולפי מה שכתב ב״י בשם רשב״א שיש לחוש שיבוא לאוכלו איסור גמור יש גם בהלואה » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ב)
  2. Buying in order to feed one’s non-Jewish workers. The Pitchei Teshuva reports that for the Chakh and the Peri Chadash this purchase is not “a kind of trading”, and therefore does not fall under the ban.
    « אמנם לקנות כדי להאכיל לפועליו בלא״ה שרי לדעת הש״ך סק״ג דאין זה כעין סחורה וכ״ד הפר״ח סק״ב » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ד)
  3. The reach of the ban — does it aim only at one who trades as a trade, or even at a single purchase made in order to hold the thing?
And in practice — ask your Rav.

3. The Taz’s rule — “what the Torah permitted explicitly”

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

This is one of the most contested passages of the siman: the Pitchei Teshuva sets against it the Chavot Yair, the Panim Meirot and a difficulty of his own, and refers to further responsa besides.

Where the rule comes from. The Taz had allowed that the ban might be merely rabbinic (אסמכתא). He then runs into the Tosafot of Sukka, who prove their point from a verse expressly permitting the sale of a nevela — a proof worth nothing if the ban on trading were only rabbinic. His answer is the rule: the Sages have no power to forbid what the Torah permitted explicitly.
« ונראה לתרץ דאין כח ביד חכמים לאסור דבר שפירשה התורה בפירוש להיתר » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
« דאין כח ביד חכמים להחמיר אלא במקום שאין בו לא איסור ולא היתר מפורש מן התורה » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
summary: and this is how chelev, permitted in so many words for “any use”, escapes the ban not by a local exception but by a rule.
The machloket over the Taz’s rule:
PositionWhat it holdsSource
The Taz The Sages have no power to forbid what the Torah permitted explicitly; chelev is the case ט״ז ס״ק א
The Chavot Yair (responsum 142) Objects to the Taz — the rule is not absolute פת״ש ס״ק ג
The Panim Meirot Objects likewise to this rule of the Taz פת״ש ס״ק ג
The Pitchei Teshuva himself Adds a difficulty of his own, drawn from the Tosafot of Chullin 12 פת״ש ס״ק ג
The root of the disagreement. The Taz’s rule assumes that an explicit permission of the Torah amounts to an objection lodged in advance against any future fence. The Pitchei Teshuva reports that this has been refused, and the way he sets it out is worth reading: he first states the Taz’s thesis, then lines up those who contest it.
« עט״ז מ״ש דדבר שהתירה התורה בהדיא א״א לומר שחכמים יאסרוהו » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ג)
« וע׳ בתשובת חות יאיר סי׳ קמ״ב שהשיג עליו » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ג)
« שהשיג ג״כ על האי כללא דהט״ז » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ג)
« גם לע״ד קשה על כלל זה מתוס׳ דחולין דף י״ב » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ג)
summary: for the Taz, every explicit permission is beyond the reach of a rabbinic fence; for those who contest him, each case must be examined on its own — and the permission of chelev is then only a local ruling, drawn from its own verse. The Pitchei Teshuva does not decide, and neither do we.

4. “A thing set aside for food” — the definition

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)
The Chakh’s reading. The Chakh presses on the Mehaber’s words דבר המיוחד למאכל: the ban bears only on what is ordinarily destined to be eaten. Hence horses, donkeys and camels, whose ordinary destination is work, fall outside it — one may trade in them, impure animals though they are.
« לאפוקי סוסים וחמורים וגמלים דסתמן למלאכה » (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
And the Chakh refines his definition further, against an objection drawn from the Mehaber’s own wording: what counts is the permanent destination, not the use assigned to the thing case by case.
« וי״ל דה״ק אם הוא דבר המיוחד לעולם לאכילה לאפוקי אם יחדו שלא לצורך אכילה » (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
Rabbenu Tam, and the case of the pig:
The thingThe dinReason
Horse / donkey / camel Permitted — not a thing set aside for food Their ordinary destination is work (ש״ך ס״ק א)
Other impure animals raised for another use (Rabbenu Tam) Permitted — raising them to smear hides with their fat, or selling them to a Jew for lighting This is not trading by way of food (ש״ך ס״ק א)
The pig Forbidden in every case — even for another use “Because of what once happened” (ש״ך ס״ק א)
Rabbenu Tam’s widening, and the pig’s departure from it. The Chakh reports, through the Beit Yosef and in the name of Tosafot, that Rabbenu Tam permits raising impure animals so long as it is not for food.
« שאר דברים טמאים שעומדין לאכילה אם מגדלן למשוח עורות בשומנן להשתכר בהן או למוכרן לישראל שימשח בהן או להדליק מותר » (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
But the pig escapes this reasoning: over it the Sages forbade in every case, and the reason is no longer the one of food.
« אבל בחזיר הכל אסור משום מעשה שהיה » (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
The episode the Chakh points to is the one in the gemara of Bava Kamma.
« אָרוּר הָאִישׁ שֶׁיְּגַדֵּל חֲזִירִים » (בבא קמא פ״ב ע״ב)
summary: two distinct regimes therefore stand side by side in the siman — that of “what is set aside for food”, out of which fall the horses and the hides, and that of “what once happened”, which holds the pig even where its use is not alimentary.

5. Likened to water — blood, and the limb taken from a living animal

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)
Why should trading in blood be permitted? Blood is forbidden to eat by Torah law, and it would seem plainly to be דבר המיוחד למאכל. The reason for the permission is a likening written into the verse: blood is there compared to water.
« עַל הָאָרֶץ תִּשְׁפְּכֶנּוּ כַּמָּיִם » (דברים י״ב:כ״ד)
summary: what has been likened to water is no longer, for this din, “food”.
What the Pitchei Teshuva reports (ס״ק א). The Peri Toar permits trading in blood for exactly this reason; the Noda BiYehuda agrees with him; and the Chatam Sofer adds to it the limb taken from a living animal, which is itself likened to blood.
« ע׳ בס׳ פרי תואר שמתיר את הדם לסחור בו כיון דאיתקש למים » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
« ועיין בתשובת נו״ב תניינא חיו״ד סי׳ ס״ב שהסכים עמו » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
« וכ״כ בתשובת ח״ס סימן ק״ו והוסיף עוד דה״ה אבר מ״ה דאיתקש לדם » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק א)
What this says about the ḥakira. This permission is not an objection against the ban, it is a definition: what has been likened to water is not “food” for this din, forbidden to eat though it is. And one sees here how the two roots behave differently: if the ban rests on the fear that one will come to eat, blood does not enter it, since it is not eaten in that way; if it rests on the verse, a text is needed to take it out — and that text is precisely the likening to water. We report, and we do not decide.

6. Chelev — “it shall be put to any use”

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

« חוּץ מִן הַחֵלֶב, שֶׁהֲרֵי נֶאֱמַר בּוֹ ״יֵעָשֶׂה לְכָל מְלָאכָה״ »

The word is חֵלֶב, with a tsere — the forbidden fat of an animal, whose eating carries karet — and not חָלָב, milk. It is the clearest case of “a thing set aside for food”, and yet the Torah expressly permitted every use of it.

« יֵעָשֶׂה לְכׇל מְלָאכָה » (ויקרא ז׳:כ״ד)
A permission written into the text. The Mehaber gives no other reason: he writes that chelev is the exception because it is said of it that it shall be put to any use. It is this verse that carries the whole of section 3 — the Taz drew a general rule from it, and those who contest him see in it only a ruling proper to chelev.
Chelev: a general rule, or a local ruling?
PositionReach of the permission of chelev
The Taz (ס״ק א) A rule — every explicit permission of the Torah is beyond the reach of a rabbinic fence
The Chavot Yair and the Panim Meirot (פת״ש ס״ק ג) A local ruling — it is of chelev alone that “it shall be put to any use” is said; no general rule is drawn from it

7. What comes by chance, and without intent — the hunter and the collecting of a debt

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

The text of the Mehaber and the gloss of the Rama

« וְאִם נִזְדַּמְּנוּ לְצַיָּד חַיָּה וְעוֹף וְדָגִים טְמֵאִים (וכן מי שנזדמנה לו נבילה וטריפה בביתו) (טור) מֻתָּר לְמָכְרָם, וּבִלְבַד שֶׁלֹּא יִתְכַּוֵּן לְכָךְ. (הגה: וְצָרִיךְ לְמָכְרָהּ מִיָּד, וְלֹא יַמְתִּין עַד שֶׁתְּהֵא שְׁמֵנָה אֶצְלוֹ. וכן מותר לגבות דברים טמאים בחובו מן העובד כוכבים, דהוי כמציל מידם; ואסור למכור לעובד כוכבים נבילה בחזקת כשירה — ועיין בחושן המשפט סימן רכ״ח.) »

Between the chance encounter and the trading that is willed. The whole ban aims at one who acts as a merchant, and on purpose. But one to whom a forbidden thing falls without his having willed it — the hunter whose trade it is and whose net brings up impure animals, or one to whom a nevela or a terefa comes in his house — may sell them, provided he does not aim at it — and the Rama requires selling without delay besides. The line is drawn by intent.
The Taz’s precisions (ס״ק ג–ד):
The pointThe dinSource
To whom is the permission of the chance case addressed? To the hunter whose trade it is, and not to another man ט״ז ס״ק ג
Where does the gloss “at once” belong? On the clause of the chance case — and not where it was printed ט״ז ס״ק ד
A rabbinic prohibition Trading is permitted in it בכל גוונא — in whatever manner one wishes ט״ז ס״ק ד
The Rama’s “at once”, and what the Taz makes of it. The Rama adds that one must sell without delay, and not keep the animal at home until it grows fat — for by feeding and keeping it one would fall back into trading that is willed.
« וצריך למכרה מיד ולא ימתין עד שתהא שמינה אצלו » (רמ״א יו״ד קי״ז)
The Taz, for his part, holds that this gloss was misplaced by the printer: it attaches to the clause of the chance case, and not to the end of the seif. His argument is one of substance: for a rabbinic prohibition, trading is in any event permitted in whatever manner one wishes, so that an “at once” would carry no sense there.
« כאן שייכא הג״ה שנדפסה בסמוך וצריך למכרה מיד » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ד)
« ובטעות נדפסה שם כי באיסור דרבנן מותר לעשות סחורה בכל גוונא שירצה » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ד)
summary: this is one of the pieces of the ḥakira — the Taz holds that a condition which applies only to a Torah prohibition presupposes that the ban on trading itself has a Torah degree.
Collecting a debt from a non-Jew — “as one who saves from their hand”. The Rama permits taking payment of a debt in impure things: this is not a way of trading, it is the saving of one’s own property (source: רשב״א בתשובה).
« וכן מותר לגבות דברים טמאים בחובו מן העובד כוכבים » (רמ״א יו״ד קי״ז)
« ואסור למכור לעובד כוכבים נבילה בחזקת כשירה » (רמ״א יו״ד קי״ז)
The reason for this last prohibition, says the Taz in the name of the Tur, is deception — and it holds even towards a non-Jew.
« בטור הטעם משום גניבת דעת שאסור אפילו לעובד כוכבים » (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ד)
And the Pitchei Teshuva adds, without making a ruling of it, that the same would apply to cooking the meat of a terefa that came by chance in order to sell it, for fear of a mishap.
« ונראה קצת דה״ה דאסור לבשל בשר טרפה שנזדמן כדי למכור לעובד כוכבים מפני שקשה למכור בשר חי דחיישינן לתקלה » (פת״ש יו״ד קי״ז ס״ק ז)

8. The ḥakira taken up again, and what follows in practice

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)
The question that runs through the whole siman: what is the root of the ban on trading in a forbidden thing, and what has been taken out of it?
  • The root — a fence of the Sages, “lest he come to eat of it” (Rashba, through the Beit Yosef); a din of the Torah drawn from לכם (Pesachim 23a, the Taz’s objection at ס״ק א); or an אסמכתא and therefore rabbinic (Terumat HaDeshen in the name of a gloss of Tosafot, reported by the Taz at the same ס״ק).
  • The Taz’s rule — an explicit permission of the Torah (chelev) escapes the fence; the Chavot Yair, the Panim Meirot and the Pitchei Teshuva himself oppose it (פת״ש ס״ק ג).
  • “Set aside for food” — horses, donkeys and camels fall outside; Rabbenu Tam permits impure animals raised for another use; the pig stays forbidden in every case (ש״ך ס״ק א).
  • Likened to water — blood, and with it the limb taken from a living animal (Peri Toar, Noda BiYehuda, Chatam Sofer; פת״ש ס״ק א).
  • Chance and absence of intent — permitted to the hunter whose trade it is, selling without delay and without aiming at it; and for a rabbinic prohibition, in whatever manner one wishes (ט״ז ס״ק ג–ד).
What follows for practice, each line set beside the entry of the apparatus that carries it:
  1. Trading in a thing set aside for food and forbidden by the Torah → forbidden, even where benefit from it is permitted (Mehaber, אף על פי שמותר בהנאה).
  2. Lending against a forbidden pledge → the Terumat HaDeshen saw in it only something unseemly, the Taz sees an outright prohibition on the Rashba’s reason (ט״ז ס״ק ב). Buying to feed one’s non-Jewish workers → the Pitchei Teshuva reports that for the Chakh and the Peri Chadash this is not “a kind of trading” (פת״ש ס״ק ד) — ask your Rav.
  3. Chelev, of which the Torah permitted every use (Mehaber); and blood, likened to water (פת״ש ס״ק א).
  4. A thing whose prohibition is only of the Sages → the seif permits it expressly, and the Taz adds that it is in whatever manner one wishes (ט״ז ס״ק ד).
    « וכל דבר שאין איסורו אלא מדבריהם מותר לעשות בו סחורה » (שולחן ערוך יו״ד קי״ז)
  5. What comes by chance to the hunter whose trade it is → he may sell, without delay and without aiming at it (Mehaber and Rama; ט״ז ס״ק ג). But it is forbidden to sell a nevela to a non-Jew by passing it off as kosher (Rama; see חושן משפט סימן רכ״ח).
And in practice — ask your Rav.
