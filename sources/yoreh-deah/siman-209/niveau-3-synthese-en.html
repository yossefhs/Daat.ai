Siman 209 — Answering “amen” to another man’s vow (השומע שחבירו נודר ואמר אמן)

1. The axiom: it is not the formula that binds, it is the acceptance

The axiom. The seif does not say that the word “amen” has force of its own. It says that this word is an acceptance of the words — שהוא קבלת דברים — and it is the acceptance that produces the effect. Two consequences follow at once: any other formula of acceptance is worth as much; and where no acceptance is called for, the question does not arise.
« או דבר שעניינו כענית אמן שהוא קבלת דברים אסור » (שו״ע יו״ד ר״ט:א)
« אבל כשחבירו מדירו משלו אין צריך לקבל דבריו » (שו״ע יו״ד ר״ט:א)
Three consequences. (1) Answering amen to a vow laid on you binds you as though you had uttered it. (2) “Yes”, “agreed”, “I accept it” do the same, and in any language. (3) But forbidding what is yours to another man does not ask his agreement — and that is a wholly different mechanism.

2. The three reflex questions

1. Who spoke? He himself — an ordinary vow, the preceding simanim. Another man, about him — next question.
▼
2. Did he accept? Amen, or any word of the same force, in any language — he is bound. Silence — the vow did nothing to him, except in the following case.
▼
3. Whose is the thing forbidden? The speaker’s — no acceptance is required. And how far does that power reach? Until the thing ceases to be his.

5. The open dispute among the commentators

Only one, but a complete one. What does מדירו משלו mean? The Beit Yosef and the Shach: the property belongs to the speaker, and the prohibition holds even against the other man’s will. The Taz: that reading is תמוה, since the stronger case would then have been written; the property belongs to the man addressed. The Nekudot HaKesef: four objections, and back to the Beit Yosef’s reading. The Ba’er Hetev reports all three voices, and notes in small type that the Nekudot HaKesef answered.
« אין צריך לקבל דבריו. שהרי זה יכול לאסור נכסיו עליו בע״כ ואם כן מה צורך שיקבל עליו דבריו » (ש״ך יו״ד ר״ט ס״ק ב)
« ונראה דהכי פירושו דאם ראובן מדיר את שמעון שלא יהנה מנכסי עצמו אין צריך לקבל שמעון את דבריו » (ט״ז יו״ד ר״ט ס״ק א)
« ועוד היאך שייך דין זה לכאן ועוד מאי אבל אלא הדבר פשוט כמו שפירש הב״י » (נקודות הכסף יו״ד ר״ט)

7. Rules of thumb, a mnemonic, and standard pitfalls

One word, two mechanisms. The first word binds because it accepts. The second half of the seif needs no word at all, because it rests on ownership. Do not confuse the two: accepting is not owning.
Rules of thumb
  • What binds is not the formula but the consent it expresses — hence בכל לשון.
  • The מתפיס is exempt because two things are missing: he did not speak and nobody adjured him.
  • Exempt does not mean permitted: Rambam still forbids the מתפיס to eat what he attached.
  • The second half of the seif is not a limitation on the first: it is a second subject.
  • The power to forbid one’s property stops where ownership stops.
Standard pitfalls
  • Thinking one must have said precisely “amen”: the seif itself writes או דבר שעניינו כענית אמן.
  • Reading clause c as a softening of clause a: the first is about accepted conduct, the second about forbidden property.
  • Settling the dispute over מדירו משלו from the Taz alone: the Nekudot HaKesef answer him point by point, and the Ba’er Hetev says so.
  • Forgetting that the power to forbid property assumes it is still his — the Pitchei Teshuva’s question.
  • Counting more than six entries in the apparatus: Sefaria addresses exactly six on this siman.
