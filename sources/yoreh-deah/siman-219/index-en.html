Siman 219 — The Vow With No Term Set

Three seifim, and the same silence acting twice in opposite directions. A man vowed to marry, to buy, to pay a teacher — and did not say when: he is not pressed, one waits for what suits him, because no one bound himself to whatever comes first. A man forbade himself a thing — and did not say until when: then nothing bounds the prohibition, and it lasts forever; the Taz and the Shakh extend the rule to an oath, against the nezirut model, which lasts thirty days. (Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De’ah 219 — 3 seifim)

מי שנדר או נשבע לישא אשה או לקנות בית וכיוצא בו ולא קבע זמן אין מחייבין אותו לישא או לקנות עד שימצא הראוי לו

One who vowed or swore to marry a woman, or to buy a house, or the like, and did not set a term — he is not obliged to marry or to buy until he finds what is fitting for him. The siman opens on the vow to act, and on the measure of its execution.

Choulhan Aroukh, Yoreh De'ah 219:1

Frequently asked questions — Siman 219

I vowed to buy a flat and did not say when. Must I buy at once?

No — this is exactly seif 1: one is not obliged to buy before finding what is fitting.

« ולא קבע זמן אין מחייבין אותו לישא או לקנות עד שימצא הראוי לו » (שו״ע יו״ד רי״ט:א)
But two reservations: the Pit’hei Teshuva reports that in a matter of mitzva one is bound as soon as the fitting appears, and notes that the braita spoke of a house in the Land of Israel. Ask your Rav.

I forbade myself something in a moment of anger, without saying for how long. Is it over?

No. Seif 3 is categorical, and deliberately short:

« מי שאסר דבר אחד עליו בלא זמן אסור בו לעולם » (שו״ע יו״ד רי״ט:ג)
The Taz and the Shakh extend the rule to an oath. A perpetual vow is lifted by a התרה before a sage or three men: it is to a Rav one must turn, not to the passage of time.

Why does an unspecified nezirut last thirty days, and not our vow?

That is the question of the commentaries. The Taz gives two reasons, and the first is the Ran’s:

« שאני בנדר שכולל בו נדרי הקדש ונדרי איסור וכשם שהמקדיש בהמה למזבח קדושה עולמית ה״נ בנדרי איסור » (ט״ז יו״ד רי״ט ס״ק א)
The second, of the Hagahot Mordechai, sets object against person. It is that one which reopens the question of an oath, which the Darkei Moshe raises and the Rosh resolves.

Our community passed a haskama without setting a duration. Does it hold indefinitely?

The Ba’er Hetev says so expressly, in the Rashach’s name, at ס״ק ב:

« וכן הסכמת הקהל כל שלא קצבו זמן הוא לעולם » (באר היטב יו״ד רי״ט ס״ק ב)
But the Pit’hei Teshuva at ס״ק ב shows the limit: what was voted as an act to be done is exhausted by its doing. Everything turns on the wording. Have the text read by a Rav before the vote.
