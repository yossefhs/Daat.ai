1. The axiom: where tevila comes from and what it applies to

The starting point:

Siman 120 deals neither with absorbed taste nor with kashering — that is Siman 121. It deals with a change of ownership: a meal vessel that passes from a non-Jew's hand into a Jew's must be immersed in a mikveh before being used, even if it is brand new and has never served. The mishna in Avodah Zarah teaches « הַלּוֹקֵחַ כְּלֵי תַּשְׁמִישׁ מִן הַגּוֹיִם », and the gemara adds three restrictions that govern the whole siman: tevila applies only to meal vessels — « כְּלֵי סְעוּדָה אֲמוּרִין בַּפָּרָשָׁה » —, only to metal — « כְּלֵי מַתָּכוֹת אֲמוּרִין בַּפָּרָשָׁה » —, and only to what is acquired — « לֹא שָׁנוּ אֶלָּא בִּלְקוּחִין… אֲבָל שְׁאוּלִין » (עבודה זרה ע״ה ע״ב).

💡 The landmark: do not confuse הגעלה / ליבון (expelling a forbidden taste — Siman 121) with טבילה (moving the vessel out of the non-Jew's ownership into Israel's). The Taz gives the reason for tevila: summary — that the vessel leave the non-Jew's impurity for the holiness of Israel (ט״ז יו״ד ק״כ ס״ק א). That is why a new vessel is included and why a borrowed one is not: everything turns on whose vessel it is.

⚖ A foundational machloket: Torah law or rabbinic?

The Rambam writes that this immersion of meal vessels bought from a non-Jew is not a matter of ritual purity at all and is מדברי סופרים, the verse serving only as a רמז (רמב״ם הלכות מאכלות אסורות פרק י״ז הלכה ה). The Taz, by contrast, writes explicitly that טבילת כלים דאורייתא, and from that he derives that a minor is not believed about the immersion (ט״ז יו״ד ק״כ ס״ק ט״ז). Note the practical difference: the more one holds tevila to be Torah law, the less one relies on a doubt — hence the Shach's constant reflex in every uncertain case: immerse without a berakha, or immerse the doubtful vessel together with one that is certainly obligated.

2. The 3 reflex questions

■ 1. WHAT IS IT MADE OF? (מתכות / זכוכית / עץ) — metal and glass: tevila with a berakha; vessels coated with lead (המצופים באבר) on the inside: tevila, but the Rama rules without a berakha, « וכן נוהגין »; bare wood, stone and earthenware: nothing at all (seifim 1, 6, 7).
↓ if the material obligates, what is it used for?
■ 2. WHAT IS IT USED FOR? (כלי סעודה or not) — is the food itself placed on it or eaten from it as it is? The grill on which the food itself is laid: yes; the trivet that only holds the pot: no; the shechita knife and the matza irons, which serve at a stage when the food is not yet edible: no (seifim 4, 5).
↓ if it is indeed a meal vessel, how did it reach you?
■ 3. HOW DID YOU ACQUIRE IT? (לקוחין or not) — bought from a non-Jew: tevila. Borrowed or rented: nothing. Pledged with you, seized by an official, bought back after a sale, made by a non-Jewish craftsman: this is where the whole middle of the siman is decided (seifim 8-11).

📌 Key reading: this whole grid reduces to one pivot — לקוחין against שאולין. Every case asks only: has the vessel truly become the Jew's? A pledge is doubtful because its ownership is doubtful; partnership does not exempt because the non-Jew keeps a share; theft does not obligate because the thief could never call himself the owner; seizure does obligate because the authority genuinely made it his own.

6. The mikveh, the berakha and a valid immersion

The place and the measure

A mikveh or spring of forty se'ah (seif 1) — the gemara derives it from the words « בְּמֵי נִדָּה » and teaches « וְכוּלָּן צְרִיכִין טְבִילָה בְּאַרְבָּעִים סְאָה » (עבודה זרה ע״ה ע״ב). The Taz discusses the question of the spring and of the required measure (ט״ז יו״ד ק״כ ס״ק ב).

7. The 6 golden rules

  1. Metal and glass, even brand new, require tevila. Newness is no exemption: tevila is not a kashering (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:א).
  2. Meal vessels only. Whatever serves at a stage when the food is not yet edible — the shechita knife, the matza irons — is exempt (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:ד-ה).
  3. Borrowed or rented: nothing. Only what is acquired obligates — the gemara's « לֹא שָׁנוּ אֶלָּא בִּלְקוּחִין » (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:ח).
  4. In a composite, follow the עיקר. Usable without the metal → a wooden vessel; unusable without it and the metal is inside → a metal vessel (רמ״א יו״ד ק״כ:ז).
  5. In doubt: immerse without a berakha, or immerse the doubtful vessel together with one certainly obligated — the Shach's constant reflex (ש״ך יו״ד ק״כ ס״ק ה ; ס״ק ט״ז).
  6. Forgotten before Shabbat: gift it to a non-Jew, then borrow it. The Taz limits the expedient to that Shabbat, or to a time when one has no mikveh (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:ט״ז ; ט״ז יו״ד ק״כ ס״ק י״ח).

8. Mnemonic — the memory aid « טבילה »

ט-ב-י-ל-ה — the five letters of the word טבילה
  • ט — טעם: tevila moves the vessel from the non-Jew's ownership to Israel's, not from forbidden to permitted (ט״ז יו״ד ק״כ ס״ק א).
  • ב — ברכה: על טבילת כלי in the singular, על טבילת כלים in the plural; and in doubt, without a berakha (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:ג).
  • י — ידות: immerse up to the handles, remove the rust, hold the vessel loosely (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:ב, י״ב, י״ג).
  • ל — לקוחין: acquired only — borrowed and rented are exempt (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:ח).
  • ה — הערמה: forgotten before Shabbat, one gives it to a non-Jew and borrows it back (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:ט״ז).
The ladder of acquisition — from the most obligated to the most exempt
  • קנייה (purchase) → tevila with a berakha (seif 1)
  • משכון לשקעו (a pledge meant to be kept) → tevila: the intent counts as a sale (seif 9)
  • משכון בספק (a pledge of uncertain outcome) → tevila without a berakha (seif 9)
  • שותפות (partnership with a non-Jew) → exempt: his share remains (seif 11)
  • שאלה ושכירות (borrowing and renting) → exempt: it is not לקוח (seif 8)

9. The 5 classic pitfalls

❌ Pitfall 1 — Confusing tevila with kashering: a brand-new vessel, never used and holding nothing, still requires tevila — « אֲפִילּוּ כֵּלִים חֲדָשִׁים בַּמַּשְׁמָע » (עבודה זרה ע״ה ע״ב). Conversely, tevila never replaces the הגעלה of a vessel used with forbidden food: that is Siman 121, and the order is first kasher, then immerse.
❌ Pitfall 2 — Thinking every metal object is included: the gemara restricts twice — « כְּלֵי סְעוּדָה אֲמוּרִין בַּפָּרָשָׁה » and « כְּלֵי מַתָּכוֹת אֲמוּרִין בַּפָּרָשָׁה ». A trivet that only holds the pot, the shechita knife and the matza irons are not meal vessels and require nothing — even if they are solid steel (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:ד-ה).
❌ Pitfall 3 — Immersing the vessel while gripping it tightly: gripped, that is a חציצה and the immersion is worthless. It must be held רפוי, or one must have wet one's hand first — and the Rama specifies: in mikveh water, not in drawn water (שו״ע ורמ״א יו״ד ק״כ:ב). Another common omission: the handles and grips, which must be immersed too (שו״ע יו״ד ק״כ:י״ב).
❌ Pitfall 4 — Taking a pledge for a purchase, or a purchase for a pledge: the siman's two pledges run in opposite directions. A non-Jew who pledges with a Jew: if it is clear he means to forfeit it → tevila; otherwise → without a berakha (seif 9). A Jew who pledges with a non-Jew and redeems it: no tevila at all, the vessel never ceased to be his (seif 11). Nor should this be confused with a sale followed by a repurchase, which does obligate.
❌ Pitfall 5 — Making the gift to a non-Jew a permanent solution: the expedient of seif 16 — giving the vessel to a non-Jew and then borrowing it back — is, according to the Taz, only a stopgap: for that Shabbat, or as long as one has no mikveh. Afterwards, the vessel remaining permanently with the Jew, it becomes לקוח again and requires tevila (ט״ז יו״ד ק״כ ס״ק י״ח). And if one transgressed and used the vessel without tevila, what was prepared in it is not forbidden — one immerses it afterwards (רמ״א יו״ד ק״כ:ט״ז).

For halacha le-ma'aseh, consult your Rav.

4Trivets and grillsTrivets (טריפיד״ש) on which pots are set do not require immersion; grills (פדיליא״ש) do, because the food itself is placed on them.
5Shechita knife, matza irons, coversA shechita knife (and a skinning knife): יש מי שאומר that it needs no immersion. Rama: יש חולקין (תשב״ץ), and it is good to immerse it without a berakha; the irons used to prepare matzot need no immersion, nor does the cover laid over the bread to bake it — but a pot lid does require immersion.
6Wood with iron hoopsA wooden vessel with iron hoops on the outside that hold it together does not require immersion.
7Composite vesselsA silver cup joined to a wooden vessel requires immersion. Rama: so too a pepper mill with a fixed iron shaft inside the wood, and iron funnels and taps and those of other metals. But only where the essence of the vessel is metal; if its essence is wood with only a little iron fixed in it, and it can be used without the iron — nothing; and a vessel mounted on inner iron pegs, without which it could not be used — immersion.
8Borrowing, renting, lending between JewsOne who borrows or rents a vessel from a non-Jew need not immerse it; but if a Jew bought it from the non-Jew and lent it to his fellow, it requires immersion, since the obligation already arose in the first one's hand. יש מי שאומר: if the first did not take it for meal use but to cut parchments and the like, it need not be immersed. Rama: yet the first may not use it for meal purposes, even occasionally, without immersion; and if a second Jew buys it from the first for meal use, the obligation falls on the second.
9A non-Jew's pledgeA non-Jew who pledges a vessel with a Jew: if it is apparent that he means to leave it (לשקעו) in his hand, it requires immersion; if not, he immerses it without a berakha, or buys another vessel and immerses it together with it. Rama: and if in the end it is forfeited to him, he immerses it again without a berakha.
10The craftsmanA Jew who gave silver to a non-Jewish craftsman to make him a vessel from it need not immerse it. Rama: יש חולקין, and one should immerse it without a berakha; and if part of the silver from which the vessel was made was the non-Jew's, it requires immersion. Likewise a Jewish craftsman who made a vessel for a non-Jew and bought it from him immerses it too without a berakha if the non-Jew supplied all the metal; but if he made it for himself and only bought the metal from the non-Jew, or supplied part of the metal himself, it needs no immersion.
11Sale, reverse pledge, partnership, theftA Jew who sold a vessel to a non-Jew and bought it back from him must immerse it; but if he had pledged it with him and redeemed it, he need not. Rama: a Jew and a non-Jew who bought a vessel in partnership — no immersion; a Jew whose vessels were stolen or robbed from him and returned — no immersion; but an officer or a ruler who coerced a Jew and took his vessels, and they were returned to him — they require immersion, since they had already been נשתקעו in the non-Jew's hand.
12The handlesOne must immerse the handles of the vessel (ידי הכלי).
13Rust and חציצהOne must remove the rust before immersion; if one did not remove it: if one is particular about it, it interposes; if one scrubbed it and put it in coals and a little rust still remains which cannot be removed that way, it is a מיעוטו שאינו מקפיד and does not interpose. An explicit cross-reference to the laws of חציצה in Siman 202.
14A minor's testimonyA minor is not believed about the immersion of vessels. Rama: but if he immersed them in front of an adult, the immersion counts (ת״ה סימן רנ״ז).
15Immersion performed by a non-JewIf one had the vessels immersed by a non-Jew, the immersion counts for them. Rama: but he is not believed that he performed it.
16Forgotten before ShabbatIf one forgot and did not immerse a vessel before Shabbat or before Yom Tov, he gives it as a gift to a non-Jew and afterwards borrows it from him, and it is permitted to use it. Rama: one may do the same on a weekday too, in a place where one has no mikveh; and if one transgressed and used the vessel without immersion, what was prepared in it did not become forbidden — one immerses it afterwards.

⚖ The reflex in 3 questions

  1. Material? Metal and glass → tevila with a berakha; lead-coated or enamelled → without a berakha; wood, stone, earthenware → nothing. In a composite, follow the עיקר.
  2. Use? A meal vessel → tevila; whatever serves at a stage when the food is not yet edible → nothing.
  3. Acquisition? Bought → tevila; borrowed, rented, in partnership, stolen and returned → nothing; pledge and craftsman → according to the intent and to who owns the metal, usually without a berakha.
For halacha le-ma'aseh, consult your Rav.
