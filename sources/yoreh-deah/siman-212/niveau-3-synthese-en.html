1. The axiom: one attaches, and it is the term of attachment that decides

The axiom. A vow by comparison is worth only what the thing compared to is worth. The parchment of a Sefer Torah is holy, but no one vowed it: the attachment therefore falls into a void. Naming what is written in it, or holding the scroll, changes the object — and the vow takes hold.
« הנודר בתורה כגון שאמר פירות אלו עלי כזו לא אמר כלום » (שו״ע יו״ד רי״ב:א)
« לא אמר כלום. לפי שדעתו אגוילין ואינן כלום: » (ש״ך יו״ד רי״ב ס״ק א)
« פירות אלו עלי כזו. דדעתו אגוילים שבה: » (ט״ז יו״ד רי״ב ס״ק א)
Three consequences. (1) A vow “by the Torah” does not take effect. (2) A release is nevertheless required, because the layman does not know why it does not. (3) The oath is not concerned: it binds the man, not the object.

2. The three reflex questions

1. Is it a vow or an oath? An oath — a release in every case, and one goes no further. A vow — next question.
▼
2. Did he say “by what is written in it,” or hold the scroll? Yes to either — a true vow. No to both — he has said nothing.
▼
3. And so, is a release needed? Following the Shach at ס״ק ב: yes, since all today are judged as עמי הארץ.

7. Golden rules, mnemonic and classic pitfalls

The golden rules. (1) Parchment is not a vowed thing: a vow “by the Torah” falls into a void. (2) Three words or one gesture suffice to raise it. (3) A release is required even where nothing took effect. (4) The oath does not enter this calculation.
גויל → nothing · במה שכתוב בה → vow · נטלה בידו → vow · שבועה → always התרה
Pitfall 1 — “therefore I am free.” That is exactly the use the Beit Yosef saw made of this braita, and which he calls a profanation of the Name. The seif itself closes the door with its final parenthesis.
Pitfall 2 — believing the ת״ח / עם הארץ distinction still applies. The Shach, at ס״ק ב, writes that today all are judged as עמי הארץ; the Ba’er Hetev repeats it. The seif’s exemption has no practical use.
Pitfall 3 — conflating the two reasons for the prohibition. They do not yield the same cases. On the Mehaber’s reason, sheets on which the word is not written would say nothing; on that of the Raavad and the Ran, they suffice. The Shach says so expressly.
