1. How the siman is built

Seventy-four seifim are not revised in order. They are arranged here by mechanism — who annuls, when, at what stage of the vow, by what words, which vows — and the master table of the last section restores the order of the book, which remains the only reference point.

BlockSeifimMatter
I1–4The father, the husband, and marriages that are none
II5–20The betrothed נערה and the passage of authority
III21–27The day, the hearing, the capacity
IV28–35The hour of the vow and the annuller’s intention
V36–44The division of annulment, language, mouth and heart
VI45–50The formulas limited in time
VII51–54A vow attached to another vow
VIII55–74Which vows the husband and father annul
The governing word. At seif 51: husband and father אינם עוקרים הנדר מעיקרו כמו החכם. From this come the one-day window, the two categories of vow, the survival of the third party’s vow, and the fact that the sage’s words do not avail here.

Between betrothal and the חופה, annulment takes two hands, and one hand only weakens — that is the word קלוש of seif 5. On the day of hearing, two views, and the Shakh reports from the Bach that the stringency is taken (ס״ק י״ג).

3. The passage from one authority to the other

Seifim 8 to 20 follow one question: when one of the two holders disappears or changes, what becomes of the power? They reduce to the rules below.

The betrothed dies → his domain empties into the father’s, who annuls alone (seif 11) — under the conditions of time the seif sets. The father dies → nothing empties into the betrothed, who can do nothing (seif 15). The betrothed divorces her → it is doubtful whether divorce counts as upholding, so one does not annul (seif 20). She is betrothed again → the father and the last betrothed annul, so long as she has not passed under her own authority (seifim 13, 17, 18, 19).
The order of hearings changes everything: an annulment made in the betrothed’s lifetime “spends” the father’s share (seif 16, Shakh ס״ק ל״א). Seif 10 reports the usage that guards against it — two sentences said in time.

4. The day he heard

The rule. Seif 21: אלא ביום שמעם ודוקא כל היום ולא מעת לעת — the calendar day, not twenty-four hours. Heard at the start of the night: all night and the next day. Heard near evening: until evening.

5. At what stage of the vow, and what the intention bears on

1. Before she vows: never (seif 28). 2. After the vow, before it takes effect: yes (seif 28) — and the Rama restricts it to vows that take effect of themselves, which the Shakh discusses at ס״ק מ״ה from seif 69. 3. What decides is the woman’s status at the hour of the vow, not the one she will have (seifim 34 and 35).
The intention has two objects: the person and the vow (seif 31). An error about either brings the annulment down, unless repaired within the span of an utterance — but a vague designation (“a woman in your house”) reaches whoever falls under it (seif 33).

The third word is not settled: the Mechaber closes seif 37 with a contrary view, that of the Rosh and the Tur — « ויש מי שחולק ואומר דאומר אין נדר זה כלום לא הוי הפרה » (שו״ע יו״ד רל״ד:לז) — and the Shakh notes that this view nonetheless concedes the word בטל: « ויש מי שחולק כו׳. אבל באומר בטל מודה » (ש״ך יו״ד רל״ד ס״ק נ״ז). The Shulchan Arukh does not decide, and neither does this page: the question goes to a Rav.
The Rambam separates annulment (mouth) from voiding (heart, and compulsion) — seifim 39 and 40. The Rama refuses the separation and concludes וכן עיקר: in both, the words “take and eat” are required.

8. A vow attached to another vow

Four cases, four answers

  • A third party attaches himself to her vow — she is permitted, he stays bound: the husband does not uproot (seif 51).
  • The husband attaches himself to her vow — he has upheld, and can no longer annul (seif 52).
  • She attaches herself to his vow — he annuls hers, his own stands (seif 53).
  • He made her vow like him — as a statement: he cannot annul; as a question: he annuls, and his own vow falls with it (seif 54, left בצריך עיון by the Nekudot HaKesef).

For the father the Shulchan Arukh does not decide: three views at seif 58. The Shakh (ס״ק ע״ב) leans to assimilating her to the wife; the Ba’er Hetev reports it (ס״ק נ).

10. הפרה is not התרה: the thread running through the siman

The same difference is put to work in several places in the siman. To hold them together is to hold the siman.

SeifWhat is at stake
23Does a husband’s upholding bar a sage from releasing? The Mechaber says no; the Rama at length reports the contrary view and returns to the Mechaber.
24One annuls on Shabbat without a Shabbat need, whereas the sage releases only for a need — Shakh ס״ק מ.
36The principle “released in part, released in whole” does not pass to annulment — Taz ס״ק ל״ג.
37The words that release with a sage do not annul with the husband — Shakh ס״ק נ״ו.
49One asks release of an upholding, never of an annulment.
51The governing sentence: they אינם עוקרים הנדר מעיקרו כמו החכם, and the attached third party stays bound.
57The father may sit as a sage for his daughter; the husband may not for his wife.
This page is a revision, not an authorisation. Nothing above is practised without a Rav.
