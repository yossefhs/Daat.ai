How to read this level. Every statement here is anchored either in the text of the Shulchan Aruch and its nossei kelim (Taz, Shach, Pitchei Teshuva), or in a named and checkable posek (Rambam, Gemara Avodah Zarah, Tur, Beit Yosef). On Yoreh De'ah there is no Mishnah Berurah (which comments only on Orach Chaim) and no Shulchan Aruch HaRav / Daat HaRav (the Alter Rebbe did not write Yoreh De'ah). The Aruch HaShulchan is not cited here: his text on this siman is not accessible to us, and one does not quote what one cannot verify — which says nothing about his printed work, which does cover this siman. Siman 129 is the longest of the yayin nesekh block — twenty seifim — and the most everyday: it is the siman of wine one leaves, for an hour or for a month, in a place someone else can enter. Where the question outgrows the siman — and the camera, the alarm and the electronic seal do outgrow it — this level says so and stops: halakha lema'asse goes through your Rav.

📜 The text of the Shulchan Aruch — Seif Alef

דין המניח יין ביד עובד כוכבים וישראל יוצא ונכנס (וכה״ג). ובו כ׳ סעיפים:
המניח עובד כוכבים בחנותו ויצא או שהיה לו יין בספינה או בקרון והניחו עם העובד כוכבי׳ והלך לו לבית (הכנסת) או לבית המרחץ או שהיה עובד כוכבים מעביר לו חבית יין ממקום למקום והניחו לבדו עם היין ויצא מותר … אפילו שהה זמן רב מפני שהוא ירא בכל שעה עתה יבא ויראני והוא שלא נודע שסגר העובד כוכבים החנות או שהרחיק הקרון והספינה בענין שאין יכולים לראותו …

The founding case. A non-Jew is left alone with wine: in the shop, on the boat, on the wagon, or carrying the barrel himself from place to place. The owner steps out — to the bathhouse, to the synagogue — and comes back. The Mehaber permits, even after a long absence, and the reason he gives is neither a lock nor a witness: « מפני שהוא ירא בכל שעה עתה יבא ויראני » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א). The permission rests not on what was seen, but on what the other man feared.

And at once, the two conditions. It must not be known that he closed the shop, nor that he moved the wagon or the boat out of sight. The whole siman — twenty seifim — is nothing but the list of what extinguishes that fear: one word too many, a closed door, a second employee, an announced departure.

— Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 129:1 · Sefaria YD 129:1

1. שורש הסימן — mirtat, and why the watchman need not sit and watch

The siman does not require watching; it requires fear. Siman 128 dealt with yiḥud: a non-Jew found alone beside the wine. Siman 129 asks the reverse — and far more common — question: the wine one leaves, knowing one will not be there. The mishnah that opens the subject is remarkably bold in practice: it does not demand a seated guard.
« אֵין הַשּׁוֹמֵר צָרִיךְ לִהְיוֹת יוֹשֵׁב וּמְשַׁמֵּר אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהוּא יוֹצֵא וְנִכְנָס מוּתָּר » — משנה (עבודה זרה ס״א ע״א)
— Mishnah, Avodah Zarah 61a

And the mishnah of our own siman, a few dapim later, applies the same measure to the shop: « המניח נכרי בחנות אף על פי שיצא ונכנס מותר » (עבודה זרה ס״ט ע״א). The Rambam restates it in the Mishneh Torah, stressing the duration.

« וְכֵן הַמֵּנִיחַ עַכּוּ״ם בַּחֲנוּתוֹ אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהוּא יוֹצֵא וְנִכְנַס כָּל הַיּוֹם כֻּלּוֹ הַיַּיִן מֻתָּר » — רמב״ם הלכות מאכלות אסורות פרק י״ב הלכה י״ז
— Rambam, Ma'akhalot Assurot 12:17

What יוצא ונכנס means exactly

The Shach, in his very first ס״ק, cites the Perisha and gives the mechanism in one sentence: « כיון שהעובד כוכבים ידע שהישראל יודע שהוא שם אצל היין מירתת » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק א). Three elements, and all three are needed: the non-Jew knows that he is known to be there beside the wine; he cannot predict the moment of return; and he has something to lose if he is seen. Remove any one of the three, and the siman moves from one seif to another.

מירתת — here fear is the halakhic object itself, not merely evidence. Nowhere in this siman does the Shulchan Aruch measure the quantity of watching; it measures whether the man left alone could believe himself safe from an eye. That is why an absence of several hours can be permitted and an absence of three minutes forbidden.

Seif 20 returns to the first register, and ends with the sentence that governs the practical reading of the whole block.

3. יוצא ונכנס ודרך עקלתון — when coming and going is enough

The two conditions of seif 1

The Mehaber first sets out what must not have happened: that it be known he closed the shop, or moved the wagon and the boat away in such a way that he can no longer be seen. The Rama immediately adds the qualification that applies today to all our doors.

« ואפי׳ סגר החנות כל זמן שיש שם סדק או חור שיכולים לראות בתוכו חשוב כפתוח » — הגה (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א)
— Rama, YD 129:1 (Mordekhi, Smag)

The criterion, then, is not the door but the line of sight: a closed door with a gap in it remains, halakhically, an open door. The Shach adds a limit of plain sense that is often forgotten: « ודוקא ביום אבל בלילה לא מהני סדק » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י״ח) — at night a crack shows nothing, and therefore reassures no one.

The דרך עקלתון: the unpredictability of the return

Then comes the most debated condition in the siman: « בד״א שיש דרך עקלתון שיכול לבא עליו פתאום שלא יראנו » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א).

A roundabout way, by which one can return suddenly and without being seen coming. That is the halakhic definition of what we would call unpredictable passage. The Mehaber immediately draws the consequence for the bathhouse: one must have gone there by a roundabout way, so that the non-Jew does not know where one has gone; for if he saw him go into the bathhouse, he knows he will not be quick to return.

Is a דרך עקלתון required in every case? The Shach cites the Ran: the requirement holds only when the non-Jew is holding the barrel — « והר״ן כתב הא דבעינן דרך עקלתון דוקא כשהעובד כוכבים אוחז החבית בידו או על כתפו » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק ח) ; but the one left in the shop, with a Jew coming and going, does not need it.

« דכל שעה מתירא ממנו דילמא השתא אתי ישראל ויראהו מתקרב לחבית » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק ח).

The Bach objects: without a roundabout way, the non-Jew in the shop leaves the door open, watches from afar, and fears nothing any more. The Shach defends the Ran and shows that the disagreement is narrower than it looks — the Ran himself speaks only of the case where the non-Jew cannot see the Jew coming; and when he holds the barrel in his hand a roundabout way is still needed, since otherwise he will see him approach and withdraw his hand at once, which he cannot do if he must move away from the barrel. The Taz (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק ב) reports the same exchange.

Two men, and the law changes (seif 2)

« אבל אם הם שנים או יותר אסור שאפשר לאחד מהם לשמור הדרך עקלתון והאחר יגע » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:ב)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:2

The Shach, in the name of the Bach, closes off any attempt to distinguish by number: « אבל הכא עצה אחת להם ולא מירתתי מהדדי » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י״ב) — even ten, even more: they are of one counsel and do not fear one another. This seif alone decides half of the modern situations: a single employee and a team of two do not fall under the same seif.

👈 The Shach's remark that moves everything: the town and the field

At ס״ק י״ג the Shach cites the Massat Binyamin and draws a general rule from it: « דבעיר לא סמיך העובד כוכבים אשומר משום דאפשר שיבא פתאום ולא יראו אותו מרחוק אבל בשדה סמיך שפיר על השומר » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י״ג).

And the Shach adds that this is not a matter of town or field: wherever he can see one coming from afar and one will not arrive unexpectedly, the prohibition stands, even in the middle of a city. This is the most directly transposable principle in the siman: what counts is not the place but the field of vision available to the one who stays behind.

4. מה שובר את המירתת — the four phrases of seif 1

Seif 1 lists four ways of destroying the fear, and all four are verbal or visual: nothing was touched, nothing was closed; it was enough that the non-Jew knew. That is what makes this seif so dangerous in practice: one thinks oneself careful by saying something, and it is that very sentence that forbids.
What was done or saidWhy the fear falls away
He saw him go into the bathhouseHe knows he will not be back quickly — יודע שלא ימהר לבא (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א)
“Watch it for me” (שמור לי)« וכן אם אמר לעובד כוכבים שהניח בחנות שמור לי יודע שנסתלק משמירתו » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א)
“Go ahead, I'll follow” (לך ואני אבא אחריך)He controls the interval: he will see the other coming (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א)
He told him he was going away (הודיעו שהוא מפליג)The absence is bounded and known — the one place in the siman where time is counted (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א)

What follows: open or sealed

Once the fear has fallen away, the Mehaber distinguishes according to the state of the barrel. If it is open, there is nothing to calculate: « אם החביות פתוחות כיון שנעלמו מעיניו אסורות » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א). If it is sealed, the time is measured.

« ואם הם סתומות אם יש בהפלגתו שיעור כדי שיוכל להסיר מגופות החבית כולה » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:1

— and one adds the time to put it back and let it dry, whether the stopper is of clay or of lime. This is the siman's only measure of time, and it comes from the mishnah in Avodah Zarah (עבודה זרה ס״ט ע״א): כדי שישתום ויסתום ויגוב.

Must the announcement be explicit? The Taz puts the question squarely: the Raavad and the Rashba hold that he must have been told expressly that the owner is away for the duration of the prohibition, while the Rosh forbids even on a vague announcement. And the Taz rules: « ויש להקל בזה כדעת הראב״ד והרשב״א דהם רבים לגבי הרא״ש » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק ד).

The Shach, at ס״ק ך׳, reaches a similar practical result by another road: he follows the Tur against the Rashba, holding that the reason עתה יזכור יינו ויבא applies even to an errand that cannot be done on the spot. He then cites the Ra'ah in the Bedek HaBayit, and draws from it the real dividing line: « אבל כל שהוא פורש משם בלא טירדא אלא ביישוב דעתו אסור » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק ך׳). A hurried departure — the shofar, the call to prayer — leaves room to expect an abrupt return; a deliberate, premeditated one does not.

5. פתוחה, פקוקה וסתומה — the three states of the vessel

The siman reasons throughout in three states, and confusing them is the commonest error in reading it.

StateIn HebrewWhat it is worth
OpenפתוחהNothing protects it; the only question is the fear
Closed with a wooden stopperפקוקהCounts as open in seif 12 — a wooden stopper is put back out of habit, not out of fear (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק כ״ב); yet it suffices in seif 4 where there is traffic
Sealed with a מגופה of clay or limeסתומהThe seal speaks: one needs time to remove it, replace it and let it dry

The carrier of the barrel is not the keeper of the shop (seif 3)

Seif 3 is among the subtlest in the siman. For the shop or the boat, even a wholly open barrel may still be permitted: the non-Jew has no right to touch it, and to touch it is to give himself away. But the man carrying it has an excuse ready.

« דחיישינן דילמא נגע ולא יירא כלל שיכול להשמט ולומר להחזיק בה כוונתי שלא תפול » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:ג)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:3

summary: a man who can explain his gesture fears nothing, and it is the existence of the excuse — the אשתמוטי of the Gemara — that forbids, not the gesture. Exactly the same mechanism will return in seif 5, about the man who went into the shop.

Passers-by as a seal (seif 4)

« והוא בזמן שיש הרבה עוברי דרכים כגון בין הגתות מותר שהוא מתירא מעוברי דרכים אפילו הם עובדי כוכבים » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:ד)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:4

A crucial detail: the fear need not come from a Jew. A crowd suffices. The Taz, at ס״ק ט׳, cites Tosafot in the name of the Rashbam, who are lenient where there is traffic — but with a reservation that practice must retain: this holds for a narrow opening, not for a vat or a wide-mouthed cup, where touching is easy and happens absent-mindedly.

One seal or two? — the one place where the Rama is openly lenient

« וי״א דאם הם סתומות שרי בכל ענין דיין סגי ליה חותם א׳ » — הגה (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:א)
— Rama, YD 129:1 (the view of Rabbenu Tam)

And the Rama adds: ויש לסמוך על זה בהפסד מרובה, referring to סימן ק״ל · 130. The Shach explains why the restriction to a great loss, and where it does not apply.

« היינו משום דיש פוסקים דסתימת מגופה לא הוי כחותם אבל בחותם אחד ממש סגי בדיעבד אפילו בלא הפסד מרובה »
— ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י״א
Lema'asse. One must distinguish a lid from a seal. The question is not “was the bottle closed?” but “was there a mark that cannot be remade without showing?”. The Taz adds that where the Rama is lenient בהפסד מרובה, one is likewise lenient when the absence was long (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י״ג), and he recalls at ס״ק ו׳ that the clay seal must be דוקא טיחה יבישה בעינן — dry, failing which it proves nothing. The detail of seals — one seal, two seals, what counts as each — is the proper subject of siman ק״ל · 130, and that is where one must go.

6. מסירת המפתח — the key, and what it does not give

Seif 5 is the seif of the key, and it comes from an episode in the Talmud: an innkeeper had handed her key to a non-Jewish woman, and the beit midrash ruled לא מסרה לה אלא שמירת מפתח בלבד (עבודה זרה ע׳ ע״ב). The Rambam formulates it thus.

« שֶׁלֹּא מָסַר לוֹ שְׁמִירַת הַבַּיִת אֶלָּא שְׁמִירַת הַמַּפְתֵּחַ » — רמב״ם הלכות מאכלות אסורות פרק י״ג הלכה ב
— Rambam, Ma'akhalot Assurot 13:2

From this the Mehaber draws a remarkably broad permission.

« המוסר מפתח חנותו לעובד כוכבים היין שבו מותר אפילו לא נשאר שום יהודי בעיר … שלא מסר לו אלא שמירת המפתח והוא ירא ליכנס בו » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:ה)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:5

Must it have been said? The Taz notes that the Rambam's wording suggests one must expressly state that only the key is entrusted, whereas the other poskim, according to the Beit Yosef, hold that even handed over without a word, a key is only a key (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י׳).

The Shach concludes the same way: « דאפי׳ מסר לו מפתח סתם אמרינן לא מסר אלא שמירת המפתח » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק ט״ו).

And if he went in?

The Mehaber reports two views, and the Rama rules like the first — that of Rabbenu Chananel, the Rivash and the Terumat HaDeshen — and then lays out the grid still in use.

The Shach explains the logic of this grid: the prohibition in the case of “he went in” holds even when he is not held as a thief for the entry — « כלומר הא דבעבר ונכנס אסור אפי׳ אם אינו נתפס כגנב על הכניסה » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י״ז) — but only where he cannot be seen; for if he can be seen, « מכל מקום כיון דנתפס כגנב על הנגיעה מירתת וסבר השתא אתי ישראל וחזי ליה » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י״ז).

Two notions govern this entire seif and must be kept apart. נתפס כגנב: can he be caught out simply by being there? Then he is afraid. אית ליה לאשתמוטי: has he an acceptable explanation to give? Then he is no longer afraid. The same man, in the same room, moves from one category to the other according to whether or not he was given a reason to be there.

7. היוצא פתאום — the shofar, the synagogue, the rush and the forgotten bolt

The hurried departure (seifim 6 and 7)

Two talmudic scenes, two permissions. On the boat, the Jew hears the Friday-evening shofar and goes up into the town; the Mehaber permits, even beyond two thousand amot, and gives a reason that never ceases to surprise.

« שאין אנו בעיניהם בחזקת שומרי שבת כראוי » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:ו)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:6

summary: the non-Jew's fear rests not on what the Jew will do, but on what the non-Jew believes he will do — and he does not credit him with being so scrupulous that he would not turn back for his wine. The Gemara puts it in Rava's words: השתא מדכר ליה לחמריה והדר אתי (עבודה זרה ע׳ ע״א).

Seif 7 adds the scene at the table, and it is the one that applies to every meal: « ואת שחוץ לשלחן את שתוך פישוט ידיו אסור ושחוץ לפישוט ידיו מותר » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:ז). And if one said “pour and drink” — הוי מוזג ושותה — every open bottle in the house is concerned, sealed ones are not, unless the absence lasted the time of the seal.

The bolt drawn and the key forgotten (seif 8) — and the objection of the Taz and the Shach

A Jew rents a room in a non-Jew's courtyard, puts wine in it, closes the door, slides the bolt into the ring, forgets to turn the key, and leaves. Days later the non-Jew notices and comes to tell him. The Mehaber permits; the Rama adds an explanatory parenthesis: « הואיל ולא ידע העובד כוכבים שמפליג הישראל מירתת ולא נגע » (הגה יו״ד קכ״ט:ח), referring to the responsum of the Rosh.

Yet the Taz and the Shach both say that the parenthesis does not say what its source says. The Taz writes curtly that the Rama ולא עיין בתשובת הרא״ש עצמה (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק י״ד): the responsum expressly permits even where he knew the Jew was going away, because the non-Jew tells himself that the Jew, remembering the unlocked door, will come back and lock it. The Shach raises the same objection, quotes the Rosh's responsum in both its printings, and concludes: « הרי בהדיא דאפילו הודיעו שהוא מפליג מותר » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק כ״א) — then ends with a וצ״ע.

👈 Why this disagreement matters, and not only for scholarship

Because it bears on the commonest case of all: premises closed but badly closed, left for a long absence that everyone knows about. On the Rama's parenthesis, the permission rests on the absence not being known. On the Taz and the Shach reading the Rosh, it rests on something else: the non-Jew cannot know whether the Jew, recalling his oversight, will not come straight back — and that uncertainty alone suffices. The difference is decisive for everything that follows, and it is open among the poskim. Do not settle it yourself: a question for your Rav.

The rush (seif 9) and the lion (seif 10)

Two neighbours run out to see a bridegroom or a eulogy; the non-Jew comes back first and shuts the door behind him. The open wine remains permitted, « שלא סגר העובד כוכבים אלא על דעת שכבר נכנס הישראל לביתו » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:ט). The Rama restricts it: ודוקא שיצאו בבהלה וחזרו. And the Taz gives the reason for the restriction: « דאם לאו דרך בהלה יש לחוש שמא ראה את הישראל בשוק והניחו שם ובא לבית לנסך » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק ט״ו).

Finally seif 10, the most elegant in the siman: a non-Jew hears a lion roar, hides among the open barrels and shuts the door upon himself — and the wine remains permitted, « מפני שהוא אומר שמא ישראל אחר נחבא כאן ויראה אותי כשאגע » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:י). The fear does not even need a real Jew: it is enough that he can imagine one.

8. גנבים, חיל ושפחה — majorities, anomalies and circumstantial evidence

Thieves in the cellar (seif 11)

The Mehaber piles up criteria: the majority of the town's thieves; a Jewish quarter that non-Jewish traffic does not cut through; the custom of hiding money in barrels. Two lines deserve to be kept as they stand.

« ואם רובן ישמעאלים אסור בשתיה ומותר בהנאה » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:י״א)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:11
« ואם יש ליהודים שכונה לבדם שאין דרך העובדי כוכבים מפסיקתה הולכים אחר רוב בני השכונה » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:י״א)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:11

On the first, the Shach notes that the Ishmaelites are not idolaters — « וה״ה לשאר עובדי כוכבים בזמן הזה » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק כ״ה) — which extends the rule well beyond its letter.

The Rama's contribution, which decides most real cases. All of the above applies only where an anomaly has been seen:

« אבל אם היו סתומות ונמצאו סתומות או שהיו כולם פתוחות ולא נמצא בהם ריעותא הכל שרי » (הגה יו״ד קכ״ט:י״א), and the reason: דסתם גנב אינו לוקח יין.

The Rama adds that if the anomaly affects only part, the rest remains permitted: « ואם היה במקצתן ריעותא ונפתחו האחרים שאין אנו רואים בהן ריעותא הכל שרי » (הגה יו״ד קכ״ט:י״א). In other words: without a visible ריעותא, there is no question to ask. That is the first thing to check, before anything else.

The troop (seif 12), and the Taz's warning

The mishnah in Avodah Zarah distinguishes peacetime from wartime: « בולשת שנכנסה לעיר בשעת שלום חביות פתוחות אסורות סתומות מותרות » (עבודה זרה ע׳ ע״ב), and in wartime ובשעת מלחמה אלו ואלו מותרות, for lack of leisure.

The Mehaber at once shuts the door on excess: « ודוקא בפתוחות מתחלתן אבל סתומות שנפתחו חיישינן להו » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:י״ב). And the Taz refuses to derive from it any leniency for the soldiers of his own day: « ואין ללמוד מזה קולא לבעלי מלחמה דהאידנא שבאים לשלול ממון » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק כ״ב), since they come to drink as well. It is a reminder of method: a permission of the mishnah does not transport itself into another world.

The storeroom and the maidservant (seif 20)

The last seif is also the closest to our own habits. A storeroom locked with a key is found open, and a non-Jew admits that he opened it to draw water from the cistern in order to build his house.

« ועובד כוכבים אחד מודה שהוא פתחו לשאוב מים מבור שבאוצר כדי לבנות ביתו מותר » — (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:כ)
— Shulchan Aruch, YD 129:20

The Taz gives the reason, and it is precious: « כיון שהיה סגור תחילה במפתח לא היה לו לזייף לפתוח המנעול של הישראל בלי ידיעתו ונתפס כגנב ומרתת » (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק כ״ט). It is the forced lock that makes him a thief, and it is being a thief that makes him afraid.

The Rama then adds three cases of a maidservant, and they draw a clear line. She says she took the wine from the cellar? « דאין העובדת כוכבי׳ נאמנת ואימר במקום אחר לקחה » (הגה יו״ד קכ״ט:כ) — the cellar stays permitted, what is on the table does not.

But if thieves left the taps open, they are later found closed, and she says she closed them: « כיון שנתייחדה העובדת כוכבים עם היין ואינה נתפסת כגנב ואיכא רגלים לדבר שהיא סתמתן היין אסור » (הגה יו״ד קכ״ט:כ).

And the very last sentence of the siman, to be read for what it is — a sentence of the Rama, anchored in simanim 123 and 124: « מיהו בזמן הזה דאין העובדי כוכבים מנסכים שרי » (הגה יו״ד קכ״ט:כ). The siman closes on its own leniency.

9. הגת, המסיבה והחצר — seifim 13 to 16

The winepress (seif 13)

A non-Jew is found standing by the vat. The criterion is not his presence but the moisture: אם יש בה טופח על מנת להטפיח — enough to wet a hand that will in turn wet another — and then « צריך הדחה וניגוב ואי לא בהדחה סגי » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:י״ג).

The Shach explains why one is concerned here where elsewhere one is not: « דחיישינן שמא נגע בו דלא מירתת למיגע שהוא סבור שאינו מפסידו כיון שאין בו יין עוד » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק ל״ד). He does not think he is spoiling anything — so he is not afraid.

The Rambam, as the Taz notes, closes this law with וזו הרחקה יתרה (רמב״ם הלכות מאכלות אסורות פרק י״ב הלכה כ״ז).

The gathering (seif 14), and the Taz's stringency

The Mehaber transmits the Gemara: « זונה עובדת כוכבי׳ במסיבת ישראל היין מותר שאינם מניחים אותה לנסך » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:י״ד), and the reverse is forbidden. The Taz refuses to make a rule of it for his own time.

« אבל עכשיו אין אנו מרוחקים כ״כ שהרי מותר בהנאה »
— ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ט ס״ק כ״ד

summary: the Gemara's reasoning presupposed complete distance from סתם יינם; where the prohibition has become light in people's eyes, says the Taz, those suspected of one thing are suspected of the other. This is the only place in the siman where a nossé kelim tightens the Mehaber in the name of the present time — and it is worth noting, precisely because everything else runs the other way.

The courtyard and the roofs (seif 16)

A shared courtyard divided by stakes: « אף על פי שחביות פתוחות עומדות סמוך לחלקו של עובד כוכבים תוך פישוט ידיו מותרות » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ט:ט״ז). The Rambam states it as a general principle.

« אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁיַּד הָעַכּוּ״ם מַגַּעַת לְחֵלֶק יִשְׂרָאֵל אֵינוֹ חוֹשֵׁשׁ מִשּׁוּם יֵין נֶסֶךְ » — רמב״ם הלכות מאכלות אסורות פרק י״ג הלכה ז
— Rambam, Ma'akhalot Assurot 13:7

The Gemara gives the reason in passing, in a discussion of טהרות: a man who has an explanation to give is not afraid — אית ליה לאשתמוטי (עבודה זרה ע׳ ע״ב). Even a symbolic partition changes the standing of the man who reaches across: it turns his gesture into a trespass.

10. הנמצא ואין בעליו ידוע — seifim 17 to 19

These three seifim no longer speak of fear at all. A vessel is found — floating on the river, standing in a vineyard, thrown on the road — and one knows neither where it came from nor whose it is. The siman then shifts into the apparatus of majorities: רוב וקרוב, manifest proximity, the owners' despair of recovery. It is the same toolkit as siman 128 on what is found, and it is worth knowing that it is there — for it governs the modern cases in which one knows nothing.

11. מקרים מודרניים — siman 129 in our cellars

The siman's six questions, in the order in which they must be asked. (1) Is the wine even involved? — if it is mevushal, this whole siman does not touch it: that is the subject of siman קכ״ג · 123, and it is the first thing to establish. (2) Is there an anomaly? — without an observed ריעותא, the Rama of seif 11 often closes the question. (3) The vessel: open, stoppered, or sealed? (seifim 1, 3, 4, 12). (4) What did the one who stayed know? — was he told, was he charged with watching, did he see where one was going? (seif 1). (5) Could one have come back unexpectedly? — the roundabout way, the crack, the field of vision, day or night (seif 1; ש״ך ס״ק י״ג, ס״ק י״ח). (6) Was he alone? (seif 2). What is not in this siman: what makes wine forbidden by contact (simanim 124-125), the detail of seals (siman 130), and the status of cooked wine (siman 123).

Lema'asse. Before asking, gather what the siman requires, in this order: which wine (cooked or not, open, stoppered, sealed); who stayed (one, or several); what he knew (was anything said, did he see where one was going, did he know how long); what he could see coming (the place, the hour of the day, the crack, the field of vision); by what right he was there (a reason to enter, or a trespass); how long; and what was found on return (an anomaly, or nothing). With these seven elements the question becomes askable. Without them no table can answer — and with them, it is your Rav who answers.

Mehaber (כ׳ סעיפים)s.1: the rule of יוצא ונכנס, its two conditions, the דרך עקלתון, the four phrases that forbid, and the measure of the seal; s.2: two non-Jews; s.3: the carrier and his excuse; s.4: passers-by as a guarantee; s.5: the key, and the dissenting view; s.6-7: the shofar and the table; s.8-10: the forgotten lock, the rush, the lion; s.11-12: the thieves and the troop; s.13-16: the winepress, the gathering, the window, the courtyard; s.17-19: what is found; s.20: the storeroom.
Rama (הגה on s.1, 5, 8, 9, 11, 17, 20)s.1: lekhatchila one leaves nothing alone with him; a gap counts as an open door; Rabbenu Tam's view on a single seal, בהפסד מרובה. s.5: rule like the first view; נתפס כגנב; the inner bolt; those who do not fear. s.8: the reason — contested by the Taz and the Shach. s.9: only if they went out in haste. s.11: the rule of the anomaly, which governs practice. s.17: only if the vessel is stoppered. s.20: the maidservant, the taps, and the closing leniency about our own time.
Taz (טורי זהב, ל״ב ס״ק)ס״ק א: the Gemara's צריכותא on the three cases; ס״ק ב: the debate over the דרך עקלתון; ס״ק ד: to be lenient like the Raavad and the Rashba on the announcement; ס״ק ו: the clay seal must be dry; ס״ק ח: the wooden stopper and the carrier; ס״ק ט: Tosafot, the Rashbam, and the reservation about a wide mouth; ס״ק י׳: a key handed over without a word; ס״ק י״ג: to be lenient also where the absence was long; ס״ק י״ד: the Rama's parenthesis against the Rosh's responsum; ס״ק ט״ו: why haste is needed; ס״ק כ״ב: not to transport the mishnah's permission to the soldiers of today; ס״ק כ״ד: to be stringent about the gathering; ס״ק כ״ט: the forced lock makes the thief.
Shach (שפתי כהן, מ״א ס״ק)ס״ק א: the mechanism of מירתת according to the Perisha; ס״ק ד: those under one's authority are presumed honest, and yet lekhatchila one does not do it; ס״ק ה: שייכות and אשתמוטי; ס״ק ח: the Ran, the Bach, and the defence of the Ran; ס״ק י׳: nowadays contact is feared only where there is a benefit — drinking or substitution; ס״ק י״א: seal and מגופה; ס״ק י״ב: ten or more, one single counsel; ס״ק י״ג: what decides is being able to see someone coming; ס״ק י״ד: the great remark on what the Mehaber writes מצד הדין and on our own time; ס״ק ט״ו: a key handed over without a word; ס״ק י״ז: held as a thief for touching; ס״ק י״ח: the gap, by day only; ס״ק ך׳: the Ra'ah, and the hurried departure against the deliberate one; ס״ק כ״א: the Rosh's responsum, and צ״ע on the Rama; ס״ק כ״ה: the non-idolaters of our time; ס״ק כ״ו–כ״ח: the quarter, the majorities, the double doubt about thieves; ס״ק ל״ד: why one is concerned by the vat; ס״ק ל״ט: the vessel found belongs to חושן משפט.

👈 הלכה למעשה — the golden rule of this level

  1. In substance, remember that this siman never measures watching: it measures fear. The founding permission — אין השומר צריך להיות יושב ומשמר (עבודה זרה ס״א ע״א) — says that an absent guard can suffice, provided the one who stays cannot predict his return.
  2. It is not deeds that forbid, it is words. Four phrases are enough to overturn seif 1: “watch it for me”, “go ahead, I'll follow”, the announcement of an absence, and the mere fact of having been seen leaving for a distance. One loses the permission while believing oneself careful.
  3. One man, or several. Seif 2 is no detail: as soon as there are two, one keeps watch for the other, and the Shach writes that the same holds at ten.
  4. Look before asking. The Rama of seif 11 puts the anomaly first: where everything is found sealed as it was left, there is often no question at all.
  5. The key is not the house. A key handed over, even without a word, entrusts only the keeping of the key; and a forced lock makes the one who enters a thief — which, in this siman, is good news.
  6. A seal and a lid are two different things. What protects is not the closure but the trace its opening would leave. For the detail, go to siman ק״ל · 130.
  7. Our own time is present on both sides. The Rama closes the siman with « מיהו בזמן הזה דאין העובדי כוכבים מנסכים שרי » (הגה יו״ד קכ״ט:כ), and the Shach gives it broad scope (ס״ק י׳, ס״ק י״ד); but the Taz, at ס״ק כ״ד and ס״ק כ״ב, tightens where usage has changed the other way. Both movements are in the siman, and it is a Rav who says which applies to your case.
  8. And for any real case — an employee, a delivery, a storage unit, a forced cellar, a camera — halakha lema'asse goes through your Rav: consult your Rav.
