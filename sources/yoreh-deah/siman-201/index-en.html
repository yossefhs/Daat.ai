Siman 201 — The laws of the mikveh and its waters: what makes water water that purifies

The longest siman in all of Yoreh De’ah — seventy-five seifim. It treats of one thing only, and treats it from every side: on what conditions water purifies. Neither cleanliness nor abundance suffices (« ואפילו עלו עליה כל מימות שבעולם עדיין היא בטומאתה » — שו״ע יו״ד ר״א:א): there must be forty se’ah of gathered water, not drawn, that does not flow away and is not shut up in a vessel. From this come the nine movements of the siman — the origin of the water, the vessel, drawing, channelling, joining, the immersion and doubt (Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De’ah 201 — ע״ה סעיפים).

« אין האשה עולה מטומאתה ברחיצה במרחץ ואפילו עלו עליה כל מימות שבעולם עדיין היא בטומאתה וחייבים עליה כרת עד שתטבול כל גופה בבת אחת במי מקוה או מעיין שיש בהם מ׳ סאה » (שו״ע יו״ד ר״א:א)

« ארבעים סאה שאמרו צריך שלא יהיו שאובים שאם הם שאובים פסולים » (שו״ע יו״ד ר״א:ג)

A woman does not emerge from her impurity by washing in a bath-house — and even if all the waters of the world came upon her, she is still in her impurity — until she immerses her whole body at once in the water of a mikveh or a spring containing forty se’ah. And those forty se’ah, says seif 3, must not have been drawn.

Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De’ah 201:1 and 201:3

Frequently asked questions — Siman 201

What is a valid mikveh, according to siman 201?

A valid mikveh is a body of at least forty se’ah — one אמה by one אמה by three אמות high, that is, by the reckoning of seif 1, forty-four thousand one hundred and eighteen fingerbreadths of a thumb and a further half — of gathered water, in which a whole body is covered at once. Three defects disqualify it: it must not have been drawn (seif 3), it must not be inside a vessel (seif 6), and — if it comes from rain and not from a spring — it must not be flowing away (seif 2). The building and supervision of a mikveh are done under the authority of the Rav; this page sets out the source, it does not replace a ruling.

What is “drawn water” (מים שאובים), and when does it invalidate?

It is water that a man’s hand detached from the ground by gathering it into a vessel. So long as the mikveh does not hold its forty se’ah, three log of drawn water suffice to invalidate it (seif 15); once the forty se’ah are gathered, drawing no longer invalidates. The Rama, at seif 3, sets out the distinction that governs all the rest: a mikveh entirely drawn is invalid by Torah law and its doubt is stringent; a drawn minority is only a rabbinic prohibition and its doubt is lenient. The Rambam, for his part, holds that even entirely drawn the prohibition is only scribal (הלכות מקואות פרק ד הלכה ב).

May one immerse in a river or in the sea?

Seif 5 lays down that all the seas have the status of a spring and therefore purify even while flowing. For a river, seif 2 distinguishes: so long as the spring waters predominate, it purifies while flowing; but if the rainwater prevails, it purifies only in an אשבורן, that is, where the water comes to rest. The Mehaber teaches stringency; the Rama reports the lenient custom of places without a mikveh, “and one does not protest against those who are lenient, for they have whom to rely upon.” The Arukh HaShulchan adds the practical condition: a true river that does not run dry, and not a seasonal pond. Each case is settled with the Rav.

May the water of the mikveh be heated?

Seif 75 brings an opinion forbidding it — casting a cauldron of hot water into the mikveh to warm it — and the Taz gives the reason in five words: גזירת מרחצאות, lest one end by immersing in the bath-house. The Rama brings the lenient, writes that one should be stringent except where the custom is to be lenient, and notes that in the hot springs of Tiberias it is permitted according to all. The Arukh HaShulchan writes that in all his lands, for several generations, the mikveh has been heated — by hot water or by a machine — and that there is no room to cavil. This is the universal practice today, and it rests on that line.
