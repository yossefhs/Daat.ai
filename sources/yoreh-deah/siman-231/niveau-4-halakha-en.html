What can be undone, what cannot, and what is not settled by reasoning alone

Subject:
שולחן ערוך יורה דעה סימן רל״א (1 seif)
With the apparatus: ש״ך (ב) · ט״ז (ג) · באר היטב (ב)
Foundations: רמב״ם הלכות שבועות פרק ו׳ · טור ובית יוסף יורה דעה רל״א · רא״ש כלל י׳ · ריב״ש · שו״ע יו״ד רכ״ח:י״ז, ל״ז, ל״ח

Written by:
הרב יוסף חיים סממה · DAAT

What this page covers

  1. What the siman settles, and what it leaves open
  2. The self-annulment clause: what it produces
  3. A third party’s refusal: what it produces, and on what condition
  4. The vow for a term
  5. A third party’s confirmation, and what can no longer be undone
  6. Four situations, and the question to ask in each
This level sets out the halakha as the Shakh, the Taz and the Ba’er Hetev give it on this siman. It does not replace a Rav. A note on the compartment: the Admur HaZaken wrote no Shulḥan Arukh on Yoreh De’ah. This level is therefore a level of psak, not a “Daat HaRav”; not one of its lines rests on a text that does not exist.

1. What the siman settles, and what it leaves open

Settled: there are only two ways of release, and no clause creates a third. Also settled: a vow for a term needs nothing. Left to judgement: whether, in a given case, the third party reacted “immediately”, and whether he had the power to do so at all.
« אין הנדר והשבועה ניתרים אלא על פי מומחה או בג׳ הדיוטות » (שו״ע יו״ד רל״א:א)
« דמיד כשחל הנדר אין לו התרה אלא על ידי מומחה או ג׳ הדיוטות » (שו״ע יו״ד רל״א:א)

2. The self-annulment clause

« שאפילו פירש והתנה בשעת הנדר או השבועה אימתי שירצה שיבטלנו מעיקרו » (שו״ע יו״ד רל״א:א)
« או שתלה (באחר) שיבטלנו אימתי שירצה אינו כלום » (שו״ע יו״ד רל״א:א)
The foundation, which the Rambam gives in four words:
« וְאֵין אָדָם יָכוֹל לְהַתִּיר שְׁבוּעַת עַצְמו » (רמב״ם הלכות שבועות פרק ו׳ הלכה ג׳)
A practical consequence, and an everyday one. Someone who wishes to take on a practice for a time must not say “and I shall be able to release myself whenever I wish”: that clause produces nothing, and he will be left with the whole vow on his hands. He should say for such a time — the limit inside the vow works, the limit upon the vow does not. And better still: let him take it on בלי נדר.

3. A third party’s refusal

« אבל אם לא קיימו ומיד ששמעו אמר שלא יהיה נדר הנדר בטל » (רמ״א יו״ד רל״א:א)
« וכ״ש אם התנה בפירוש שחבירו יוכל לבטלו דיוכל חבירו לבטל כל זמן שלא חל הנדר » (רמ״א יו״ד רל״א:א)
The Shakh specifies the reach of the power and, what matters most, the form it may take:
« אבל אם נדר על תנאי שיסכים חבירו או על תנאי שלא יבטלנו יכול חבירו לבטלו בכל לשון כמו שכתבתי בסימן רכ״ח ס״ק צ״ח עיין שם » (ש״ך יו״ד רל״א ס״ק א)
The Taz sets the time condition, and it is strict:
« פירוש תיכף שישמע יוכל לבטלו אבל לא אחר כך » (ט״ז יו״ד רל״א ס״ק ג)
And the Taz also sets a substantive condition, which siman 228:37 confirms:
« וצריך לומר דגם הכא מיירי שאומר שהיתה דעתו תחלה לכך » (ט״ז יו״ד רל״א ס״ק ב)
« אלא אם כן אומר שדעתו היה כשנשבע שיוכל אותו פלוני להתירו » (שו״ע יו״ד רכ״ח:לז)
What to retain in practice. A vow made “on the consent of” someone is not automatically revocable by that person. Two things must come together: that the voweer meant to give him that power, and that the third party exercise it immediately. On the other hand, the form of words does not matter: the Shakh says expressly “בכל לשון”. Establishing whether both conditions are met in a real case is not an act of reading: it is a judgement, and it belongs to a Rav.

4. The vow for a term

« ומיהו אם נדר לזמן אין צריך התרה אלא לכשיגיע הזמן בטל ממילא » (שו״ע יו״ד רל״א:א)
Nothing to do at the term. No three men, no formula, no procedure. It is the only clause of the siman that opens no question — and that is why it is the right advice to give. One caution, however: the term must actually have been uttered at the moment of the vow. A term one recalls having had “in mind” is not an uttered term, and there the question returns to the Rav.

5. A third party’s confirmation, and what can no longer be undone

« ואפילו אם תלאו בדעת אחר וקיימו ושוב נשאל על הקיום אינו מועיל » (שו״ע יו״ד רל״א:א)
« פירוש אותו פלוני שתלה בדעתו הסכים שיהיה נדר ואחר כך נתחרט והלך לחכם והתיר לו הסכמתו בזה לא בטל הנדר » (ט״ז יו״ד רל״א ס״ק א)
The case is commoner than it looks. Two people agree that one’s undertaking will hold if the other approves it. The other approves. Then, later, he changes his mind and has his approval released. The seif says that avails nothing: what he released was his own act, and the vow took effect at the moment of approval. To undo it, a proper release of the vow itself is required.

6. Four situations

“I took a practice upon myself, reserving the right to end it whenever I wish.” The reservation produced nothing. If the undertaking was framed as a vow, a proper release is required. Ask a Rav whether what you said constitutes a vow: that is the first question, and not the easiest.
“I vowed on condition that my spouse agreed, and he refused on the spot.” Very probably the vow never took effect, and his words hardly matter. But “on the spot” is counted in moments, not in days. A question of fact, to be brought to a Rav.
“I vowed for thirty days.” At the term there is nothing to do. This is the simple case, and the one to learn to produce.
“My wife and I undertook to one another by a תקיעת כף.” The Taz treats exactly this case in the second paragraph of his ס״ק ג, and his conclusion is twofold: he proves that nothing took effect, and he nevertheless says to perform a release — “ ומעתה גם נדרה אינוחל כי לא נדרה אלא על דעת אם הוא יקיים נדרו ותקיעת כף יש לו התרה ולרווחא דמילתא יתירו לה ” (ט״ז יו״ד רל״א ס״ק ג). An undertaking between spouses is never dismantled by reasoning alone. Bring it to a Rav, with the exact words that were spoken.
What this level does not say. It does not say how the release is performed — that is at siman 228, especially at seifim 37 and 38, which are the counterpart of ours. It does not say whether one should ask for a release — that is at siman 230. And it does not say what becomes of a vow released in part — that is at siman 229.
