Siman ק״ע — the Jew who stands surety, and the interest that passes through him

1. The axiom: what is forbidden is an order of recovery

Nobody pays a penny more, and yet it is interest. The whole of siman ק״ע rests on this paradox: what makes the guarantee unlawful is not a sum but the order in which the lender gets paid. Claimed first, the surety becomes a creditor — and a creditor for a sum that contains interest.

The Mehaber writes the reason into the body of the rule itself, which he rarely does:

« שכיון שבדיניהם תובע הערב תחלה נמצא הערב תובע את ישראל ברבית שהערב חייב בה לעובד כוכבים » (שו״ע יו״ד ק״ע:א)
« כי בדיניהם הלוה דוחה את המלוה על הערב שממנו יתבע החוב » (ט״ז יו״ד ק״ע ס״ק ד)

2. The 3 reflex questions

Three questions in order, and any guarantee finds its place.

Who is the lender, who is the borrower?
Non-Jewish lender → seif 1. Jewish lender and non-Jewish borrower → seif 2, stricter.
→
Who is claimed first?
The borrower → there is no prohibition. The surety, or either at will → we enter the Rashi–Rashba dispute.
→
Has payment already been made?
No → the question is one of permission. Yes → the question is one of recovery, and the Rama does not recover.

4. The three figures of surety

Three terms often confused, on which the whole siman depends.

FigureWho is claimed firstStatus in the siman
ערב — ordinary suretyThe borrower, alwaysPermitted per the Rashba; forbidden lekhatchila per Rashi
ערב קבלן — joint guarantorEither, at the lender’s choiceThe heart of the dispute; the Shach equates him with the borrower
ערב שלוף דוץ — substituted suretyThe surety alone; the borrower is out of reachForbidden by all views
« דהיינו שאין דין המלוה עם הלוה כלל שהוא יכול לדחותו אצל הערב » (שו״ע יו״ד ק״ע:א)
« מיהו כ״ז בסתם ערב אבל ע״ק דינו כלוה עצמו והרי הוא כאילו פטר הלוה עצמו » (ש״ך יו״ד ק״ע ס״ק ט)

5. רבית קצוצה and אבק רבית — what the court recovers

The difference between the two regimes is not one of moral gravity: it is a difference of court. רבית קצוצה is recovered at law; אבק רבית is not. The whole controversy of the siman reduces to which of the two we are in.
WhoWhat he holdsConsequence
Ramaאבק רבית — צד אחד ברבית is only rabbinicWhat was paid is not recovered
Tazרבית קצוצה — proved from the gemara, and matching siman קע״ד · 174What was paid is recovered at law
Beit Yosefספיקא דדינא: stringent in teaching, possession decidesThe money stays where it is
Shach and Nekudot HaKesefThe gemara speaks only of the ערב שלוף דוץ; the undertaking alone is never forbiddenThe Rama stands
« ואי כבר פרע לו הרבית אין מוציאין מידו דלא גרע מאבק רבית » (שו״ע יו״ד ק״ע:א)
« על כן נראה לע״ד ברור דרבית דאורייתא יש כאן כל שיש איסור ברור בהאי ערבות » (ט״ז יו״ד ק״ע ס״ק א)
« אבל בדיעבד סמכינן אהי״א והיכא דקיימי זוזי שבקינן להו כדין הספיקות שהמע״ה » (ש״ך יו״ד ק״ע ס״ק ב)

6. Seif 1: surety given to a non-Jewish lender

What is forbidden. To sign for a Jew borrowing at interest from a non-Jew, once the lender may claim the surety before the borrower.
What is permitted. The very same signature, if the lender has undertaken to claim the borrower first — and, per the Rama, if the local custom is to claim that way.
« והאידנא מסתמא הוי כאילו קבל עליו הכי דסתם ערבות כך הוא » (שו״ע יו״ד ק״ע:א)
« ודוקא במקום שהמנהג כך שתובעין הלוה תחלה » (שו״ע יו״ד ק״ע:א)
The Rama’s condition is a condition of fact, not a formula. It requires knowing how the debt is actually recovered — what the contract says, and what the creditor does.

7. Seif 2: surety given for a non-Jewish borrower

Seif 2 is the longest of the siman, and almost all of it is the Rama’s. Five clauses, in the order of the text.

The clauseThe ruling
The stipulationNo recourse to the surety while the debtor can pay — then one may receive principal and interest from the Jew if the non-Jew does not pay
Principal alone, interest alonePermitted per the Rama; the Nekudot HaKesef specify that this “permitted” bears on performance, not on undertaking
Who takes the moneyThe non-Jew from the lender’s hand; if the Jew takes it, forbidden even without a guarantee
The pledgeA non-Jew’s pledge for the principal permits guaranteeing the interest; settled custom
The substitute and the זקיפהPrincipal and accrued interest; not future interest — except in זקיפה, where all is principal
« אלא אם התנה עם העובד כוכבים שכל זמן שיש לו לפרוע לא ידיחנו אצל הערב אז שרי לקבל הקרן והרבית מישראל » (שו״ע יו״ד ק״ע:ב)
« ודוקא שהעובד כוכבים לוקח המעות מיד המלוה אבל אם ישראל ערב ולקחן מידו אע״פ שעושה בשביל העובד כוכבים אסור » (שו״ע יו״ד ק״ע:ב)

The questionStringentLenient
The scope of the prohibitionRashi: the lender must exhaust the borrowerRashba: it suffices that the borrower be reachable
The rank of the interestTaz: biblical, proved from the gemaraR. Yitzchak Korkos: rabbinic, צד אחד ברבית
Reimbursing the surety’s interestTur, Rama: we tell nobody to hand over interestR. Yitzchak Korkos: he repays the whole loss
What has already been paidBeit Yosef, Taz: recoveredRama, Shach, Nekudot HaKesef: not recovered
Guaranteeing the principal aloneTaz: צ״ע, and he hesitates to act on itRama, Nekudot HaKesef: permitted — the undertaking is never the offence
Guaranteeing the interest aloneLevush, Taz: it carries the principal with itShach: not where he does not answer for the principal; Noda BiYehuda: at a fixed term and rate
The non-Jew’s pledgeBach, Taz: קרוב לשכר ורחוק להפסדRama, Nekudot HaKesef: the loan rests on the pledge — and such is the custom
The substituted surety’s kinyanTaz: with a kinyan he owes everythingShach citing the Beit Yosef: even with a kinyan, not the future interest

10. Golden rules, mnemonic, pitfalls and flashcard

The 5 golden rules

  1. What forbids is not a sum but an order of recovery.
  2. The Mehaber’s way out is procedural: that the surety not be claimed first.
  3. The Rama’s “nowadays” gloss carries a factual condition, to be verified and not assumed.
  4. Seif 2 is stricter than seif 1, the interest there being collected by a Jew.
  5. The undertaking is never the offence; the payment is — that is the Nekudot HaKesef’s key.

Mnemonic — ס־ק־ת

ס סדר — What is the order of recovery? → forbidden or permitted

ק קבלן — Am I a plain ערב or a קבלן? → gravity of the case

ת תשלום — Has payment occurred? → a question of recovery, not of permission

The 4 classic pitfalls

  1. Thinking seif 2 is the mild mirror of seif 1. It is graver, because a Jew collects the interest there.
  2. Reading the Rama’s gloss as a blanket permission for the age. He expressly tied it to local custom.
  3. Confusing guaranteeing the principal alone, which the Rama permits, with guaranteeing the interest alone, on which the Levush, the Taz and the Noda BiYehuda set limits.
  4. Taking the Rama’s “permitted” for leave to undertake. The Nekudot HaKesef read it as leave to perform.
