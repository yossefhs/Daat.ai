1. שורש הסימן — two distinct prohibitions under one name

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

Siman 123 does not ask who renders wine forbidden — that is siman 124 — nor how far a mixture reaches — that is 126. It asks the first question: which wine is at issue, and on what grounds it is forbidden. The whole wine block rests on this opening distinction.

Two prohibitions, two regimes:
(א) יֵין נֶסֶךְ — wine actually poured as a libation. Forbidden by Torah law, in benefit as in consumption, through a hekesh linking libation to sacrifice:
« אֲשֶׁר חֵלֶב זְבָחֵימוֹ יֹאכֵלוּ יִשְׁתּוּ יֵין נְסִיכָם » (עבודה זרה כ״ט ע״ב)
« מָה זֶבַח אָסוּר בַּהֲנָאָה, אַף יַיִן נָמֵי אָסוּר בַּהֲנָאָה » (עבודה זרה כ״ט ע״ב)
(ב) סְתָם יֵינָם — the wine of a non-Jew, where we do not know whether it was used for libation. Forbidden by the Sages, and yet in benefit as well. The Rambam says it in two lines:
« וְדָבָר זֶה מִגְּזֵרוֹת סוֹפְרִים הוּא. וְהַשּׁוֹתֶה מִסְּתַם יֵינָם רְבִיעִית מַכִּין אוֹתוֹ מַכַּת מַרְדּוּת » (רמב״ם הלכות מאכלות אסורות פרק י״א הלכה ג)
The question Tosafot raise at once: the mishnah in עבודה זרה speaks of סתם יינם, a rabbinic decree — why then does the Gemara ask “from where do we know it?”, as though a verse were at stake?
« תימה מאי קא בעי מנלן הא מתניתין מיירי בסתם יינם וגזרה דרבנן היא שגזרו על יינם משום בנותיהן » (תוספות עבודה זרה כ״ט ע״ב ד״ה יין מנלן)
Their answer — and it is the yesod of the whole siman: the Sages did not merely forbid; they shaped their prohibition on the Torah's, because it resembles it.
« אלא עבדו רבנן כיין נסך גמור שנתנסך לפני עבודת כוכבים שאסור מן התורה משום דדמי ליה » (תוספות עבודה זרה כ״ט ע״ב ד״ה יין מנלן)
Hence the consequence the whole siman will exploit: whatever bounds the Torah's wine bounds the Sages' wine too.
The map of the siman:
  • Seifim 1–2 — the principle: סתם יינם forbidden in benefit, and a non-Jew's contact with our wine; the Rama's leniency nowadays.
  • Seifim 3–8 — what no longer counts as “wine”: cooked, honeyed, in a dish, vinegar, unripe juice, diluted.
  • Seifim 9–16 — the by-products: temed, husks, lees, shards, leaven, crystallised residue.
  • Seifim 17–20 — the moment grapes become wine: הַמְשָׁכָה, and what it renders forbidden.
  • Seifim 21–22 — treading: possible contact, the non-Jew's force, and the foot.
  • Seifim 23–26 — the margins: a marker on the barrel, brandy, candied coriander, the honey of the Ishmaelites.

2. אחת גזרו או שתים ? — intermarriage, and the ban on benefit

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

The Talmud gives the wine decree a social, not a cultic, motive: their wine was forbidden on account of their daughters. But a decree against mingling ought to forbid drinking, like bread and oil — not benefit. Whence, then, the surplus?

The chain of decrees (עבודה זרה ל״ו ע״ב): the Talmud nests the prohibitions one inside another, each link guarding the next —
« עַל פִּיתָּן וְשַׁמְנָן מִשּׁוּם יֵינָן, וְעַל יֵינָן מִשּׁוּם בְּנוֹתֵיהֶן » (עבודה זרה ל״ו ע״ב)

The chakira: is the benefit-ban of סתם יינם constitutive — part of the decree itself, which meant from the outset to treat this wine “as though it had been poured” — or protective, a fence added so that real yayin nesekh not be taken lightly? The Ramban holds the first branch, the Rashba and the Ran the second. This is no academic question: it decides what happens on the day the motive disappears.
Who decreed, and when: the Beit Yosef lists three candidates — Pinchas (Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer), Daniel (« אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יִתְגָּאַל בְּפַת־בַּג הַמֶּלֶךְ », דניאל א׳), and the disciples of Shammai and Hillel among the eighteen decrees. His proof that Pinchas's decree was not accepted: the Gemara in חולין ד׳ ע״א, which faults Yehoshafat for Achav's table on the grounds that stam yeinam was not yet forbidden then.
« י״ל דפנחס גזר ולא קיבלו מיניה ואתו תלמידי שמאי והלל גזרו וקבלו מינייהו » (בית יוסף יו״ד קכ״ג)
The Shakh opens the siman by adding both motives (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק א):
« כלומר אסור מדרבנן משום יין שנתנסך לעבודת כוכבים שאסור מדאורייתא וכן משום גזירת בנותיהן »
Two causes, then, not one: fear of libation and fear of intimacy. It is this cumulation that will make the next debate so hard to settle.

3. בזמן הזה — a decree whose reason lapsed, or a חומרא forbidden by count?

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

The Rama, in his gloss on seif 1, relaxes the ban on benefit in our time. The question is not factual but structural: can a decree lapse because its reason lapsed? This is the weightiest machloket in the siman, and the Taz builds a third road through it.

The Tur first sets out the reason for the stringency (טור יו״ד קכ״ג):
« כיון שיין נסך גמור אסור בהנאה עשו סתם יינם כאילו ודאי נתנסך והחמירו לאסור בהנאה »
He then cites the Rashbam in the name of Rashi in the name of the Geonim: nowadays the nations no longer pour libations, and their touch is like a child's — forbidden in drinking, not in benefit.
The cut the Rosh introduces — and the Tur copies: the leniency can only reach a non-Jew's touch in our wine, which is forbidden solely out of fear of libation. But stam yeinam itself was forbidden in benefit because of their daughters — and that motive has not moved.
« וטעמים הללו מספיקין למגע נכרי ביין שלנו אבל סתם יין שאסרו בהנאה משום בנותיהן אין טעם להתירו » (טור יו״ד קכ״ג)
The Rosh answers himself — and half retracts: perhaps stam yeinam was at first forbidden only in drinking, like bread and cooked foods, and benefit was added only because libations were common; now that they are not, it should revert to the status of bread. Yet he will not conclude:
« והאידנא שאין מנסכין דין הוא שנעשה סתם יינן כפת ושלקות ומכל מקום ראיה ברורה אין לנו להתירו בהנאה » (טור יו״ד קכ״ג)
And he closes with the phrase that has governed practice in this whole chapter:
« הנח להם לישראל מוטב שיהיו שוגגין ואל יהיו מזידין » (טור יו״ד קכ״ג)
The Taz's chakira (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק א) — three branches, not two:
(א) If the benefit-ban is a gezeirah, then once its reason lapses, it lapses:
« דהיינו כיון שאין י״נ גמור שכיח בינינו בטל הטעם והותרה הגזירה »
(ב) If it is an alignment with libation wine — “they made it as though it had certainly been poured” — then the rule of decrees applies and nothing lapses:
« דדבר שנאסר במנין צריך מנין אחר להתירו דאין שייך לומר בזה בטל הטעם »
(ג) And the Taz adds his own reading: neither decree nor count, but an assumed stringency — and a stringency, precisely because it rests on no reason, does not loosen either:
« נמצא דמצד גזירה דרבנן אין כאן איסור אלא מצד החומרא ואם כן אף לדידן אסור מצד החומרא »
The Shakh works on the facts (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק ב-ג): he cites the Bach — even when a non-Jew does pour before his idol, it is not called ניסוך, since we hold that the nations outside the Land are not idolaters but follow ancestral custom. And in ס״ק ג he gives the exact measure of the leniency: the touch is a child's touch.
« דהוי כתינוק … דאינו אוסר יין במגעו אלא בשתיה » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק ג)
Nafka mina, as the Rama himself draws it: one may collect a debt from a non-Jew in stam yeinam, « מפני דהוי כמציל מידם » — and likewise in any loss. But lekhatchilah, buying and reselling for profit remains forbidden; some are lenient, « וטוב להחמיר ». The line falls exactly where the Taz predicted: where there is only a stringency, one does not turn it into a trade.

4. איסור הנאה למעשה — the sick man’s bath

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

Seif 2 is short and carries no visible machloket; in truth it is the test bench of seif 1. A ban on benefit is not measured in the cup: it is measured where no one drinks.

The logic: « אסור לעשות מרחץ מסתם יינם לחולה שאין בו סכנה » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:ב). Bathing is not drinking; were the wine forbidden only for consumption, the bath would be permitted. That it is forbidden proves the ban attaches to the body of the wine, not to its ingestion. And the limit teaches as much as the rule: שאין בו סכנה — mortal danger would set it aside, a reminder that we stand in a rabbinic decree and not in idolatry itself.
Immediate nafka mina: on the Rama's view in seif 1, which relaxes benefit nowadays, this seif loses its force for present-day wine — while keeping it in full for confirmed יין נסך, whose Torah prohibition no era loosens. The Mechaber writes it without reservation. Seif 2 thus measures exactly the distance between the two positions of seif 1.

5. יין מבושל — the gader of ראוי לניסוך, and what it costs

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

Seif 3 is the pivot of the siman: once a wine is unfit for libation it falls outside the decree. But that criterion, taken from the Rambam, collides with the decree's second motive — their daughters — and here the Rosh, the Tur and the Taz part ways.

The Rambam's yesod (רמב״ם הלכות מאכלות אסורות פרק י״א הלכה ט):
« אֵין מִתְנַסֵּךְ לְעַכּוּ״ם אֶלָּא יַיִן שֶׁרָאוּי לְהַקְרִיב עַל גַּבֵּי הַמִּזְבֵּחַ »
« לֹא גָּזְרוּ אֶלָּא עַל הַיַּיִן הָרָאוּי לְהִתְנַסֵּךְ »
The Tur puts it in the same words: « וכיון שמשום צד ניסוך אסרוהו לא אסרוהו אלא ביין הראוי לנסך » (טור יו״ד קכ״ג)
The talmudic source (עבודה זרה ל׳ ע״א):
« יַיִן מָזוּג אֵין בּוֹ מִשּׁוּם גִּילּוּי, יַיִן מְבוּשָּׁל אֵין בּוֹ מִשּׁוּם נִיסּוּךְ »
And Tosafot derive the halakhah from it, resting on an incident rather than an argument: « כן הלכה ומותר במגע עובד כוכבים מדשתו ליה שמואל ואבלט » (תוספות עבודה זרה ל׳ ע״א ד״ה יין מבושל)
The Rosh's kushya, cited by the Beit Yosef and the Taz: if the decree was made משום בנותיהן, how does boiling the wine remove it? Fellowship is no weaker around cooked wine. The Rosh himself writes that the matter is « תמוה » and offers his answer only « דרך אפשר »: perhaps they did not decree on what is uncommon.
The Taz's reconstruction (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק ג) — his finest passage in this siman: the Sages did not want a two-tier prohibition. Forbidding drinking alone would have suggested that real yayin nesekh is permitted in benefit; so they decreed only where they could forbid benefit as well — that is, on wine fit for libation.
« דאף משום בנותיהן לא גזרו ביין אלא במקום שיהיה אסור אף בהנאה דומיא די״נ »
There remains the objection of diluted wine, equally unfit and yet forbidden; hence the closing answer:
« ותירץ משום דמבושל לא שכיח על כן לא גזרו ביה כלל »
The threshold of “cooked”: the Mechaber writes « יין מבושל שלנו שנגע בו העובד כוכבים מותר ומאימתי נקרא מבושל משהרתיח על גבי האש » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:ג). The Shakh supplies the physical criterion behind the phrase, in the name of the Rashba and the Ran:
« דהיינו שיתמעט ממדתו על ידי רתיחה » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק ז)
So it is not heat that counts but loss of volume: proof that the substance itself changed, not merely its temperature. And the Shakh adds (ס״ק ו) that the leniency runs all the way: « ואפי׳ בשתיה ואפי׳ לכתחלה מותר לשתותו עם העובדי כוכבים »
Nafka mina: for the Taz, cooked wine is permitted because it is uncommon — so where pasteurisation becomes the industrial norm, the rarity argument runs out and only the Rambam's reason (unfit for libation) remains. For the Rambam and the Tur, by contrast, fitness for libation is decisive and frequency is irrelevant. The whole contemporary discussion of mevushal wine turns on this fork.

6. נשתנה טעמו — honey, pepper, a cooked dish, and cooked wine submerged

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

Seifim 4 and 5 extend the criterion of seif 3: wine whose taste has changed is no longer the wine the decree targets. But “changed” is an elastic word, and the Rama adds two glosses that shift the debate from taste to appearance.

The rule and its extension: « יין שמערבין בו דבש ופלפלין אם נשתנה טעמו מחמתם אינו נאסר במגע עובד כוכבים » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:ד), and the Rama adds that honey alone or pepper alone is the same. The Shakh generalises outright:
« כל שנשתנה טעם היין ונראה דה״ה שאר דברים כל שנשתנה טעמו מחמתן » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק ח)
The test is therefore no longer a list of ingredients but a fact: has the taste of wine given way?

7. חומץ ובוסר — when the prohibition rests only on our ignorance

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

Vinegar is unfit for libation; so is the juice of unripe grapes. By the principle of seif 3, both ought to fall outside the decree. Rabbeinu Meshullam said so; Rabbeinu Tam opposed him — not on the principle, but on our ability to recognise the boundary.

The chakira: when Rabbeinu Tam forbids vinegar, does he forbid vinegar — a new status created by our incompetence — or the wine that may hide within it, i.e. a mere factual safek? The two branches do not yield the same halakhah: on the first, an objective sign is useless, since the status is already fixed; on the second, a reliable sign dispels the doubt and permits. The Mechaber, by adopting the Rashba's sign, plainly takes the second branch.
The Shakh tightens the sign (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק י״ג):
« היינו כשמרתיח אבל כל שאינו מבעבע א״א בקיאין אם הוא חומץ או לאו »
In other words: the sign works in one direction only. It can establish “this is vinegar”, never “this is not vinegar”. The Shakh's whole doctrine of unverifiable rieutot rests on that asymmetry.
The retroaction of seif 7: a barrel found sour enough to bubble — and a non-Jew touched it within the preceding three days? « אין לחוש למגעו », for it was already full vinegar then. The Shakh gives the reason in the Ran's name: « שבודאי שלשה ימים קודם לכן היה חומץ גמור בריחו וטעמו » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק י״ד). This is a retrospective presumption — the present state proving an earlier one — rare in halakhah, and it presupposes a physical model of fermentation.
Nafka mina: the Mechaber rules « מי הבוסר נאסרים במגע עובד כוכבים » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:ח), and the Shakh gives the reason — « לפי שא״א בקיאים מתי נקרא בוסר » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק ט״ו). Vinegar, by contrast, is permitted when it bubbles. One and the same underlying reason, two opposite outcomes: because in one case a sign exists and in the other it does not. The prohibition thus tracks not the nature of the liquid but the availability of a criterion.

The Shakh thereby exposes the structure: the “three measures and one finds four” of seif 9 and the “six parts of water” of seif 8 are one rule, stated once as a volume drawn off and once as a proportion. Half a log of wine in three of water is one sixth — exactly the Rashba's threshold.
The word « לעולם » and the Maharshal / Perishah dispute (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק י״ז): the Tur writes of husk-temed that it is « לעולם » forbidden. The Maharshal reads: however thin the yield, yet only on the first and second rounds. The Perishah objects that this is not what “always” means. The Shakh rules with the Maharshal on a drafting argument: were it truly “always”, the Tur need not have written “the first and second time”; and he notes that the Mechaber himself dropped the word לעולם.

9. חרצנים, זגים ושמרים — the twelve months, and Rabbeinu Tam’s condition

The mishnah in עבודה זרה gives husks and lees a rule of time: twelve months. The Rambam supplies the reason — they have dried and no moisture remains. Rabbeinu Tam adds a condition that has cost a great deal of ink.

The rule (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:י״ד):
« החרצנים והזגים של עובדי כוכבים וכן שמרי יין שלהם תוך י״ב חדש אסורים בהנאה ולאחר י״ב חדש מותרים אפילו באכילה »

« וה״מ כשתמדן במים בתחילה אבל אם לא תמדן אסורים לעולם אפילו יבשם בתנור »

If Rabbeinu Tam's condition held, how did he know they had first been steeped?
« וְדִלְמָא מִיֵּין נֶסֶךְ … אֲמַר לֵיהּ: לְאַחַר שְׁנֵים עָשָׂר חֹדֶשׁ הֲווֹ » (חולין ק״י ע״א)

« וצריך לדחוק ולומר ידוע שבאותה העיר לא היו משליכים החרצנים לחוץ רק אחר שנתמדו תחילה »
The word « לדחוק » is the Taz's own: he does not claim to have resolved it, he shows the price of maintaining Rabbeinu Tam's shitah.
The Rama's gloss, and what it restores: this whole din applies only to husks that carried yayin nesekh; those drawn from the vat before הַמְשָׁכָה are not forbidden. The Shakh explains why this is a necessary consequence and not an indulgence:
« כמו שיתבאר בסעיף י״ז דלא נקרא יין ליאסר אלא כשיתחיל להמשך בשולי הגיגית » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק כ״ז)
Seif 14 thus depends entirely on seif 17: without הַמְשָׁכָה there was as yet no wine, hence nothing to absorb.

10. איסורי הנאה בגלגול שני — dough, ash, shard and bedpost

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

Seifim 13, 15 and 16 test how far the ban on benefit pursues the substance once it has changed form. This is the Shakh's own ground, where he deploys three distinct lines of reasoning.

The shards (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:י״ג): « חרסים שבלעו יין של עובד כוכבים הרבה עד שכששורין אותם במים פולטים יינם אסורים בהנאה ». The Shakh grafts on an unexpected talmudic rule:
« מַהוּ לִסְמוֹךְ בָּהֶן כַּרְעֵי הַמִּטָּה » (עבודה זרה ל״ב ע״א)
— and infers a fortiori that nothing may be laid on them, dried fruit or anything else (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק כ״ה). He expressly distinguishes siman 94, where cold food may be placed in vessels forbidden through meat and milk: there the ban is on eating, here on benefit.
Dough leavened with their lees (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:ט״ו): « המחמיץ בשמרי יין של עובדי כוכבים תוך זמן איסורם כל העיסה אסורה בהנאה ». The Shakh gives the reason that blocks any nullification:
« ואפילו באלף לא בטיל דלטעמיה עבידי » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק כ״ח)
A דבר המעמיד — what leavens, curdles, sets — is never nullified, because it is not there by accident but to act. The scope is clear: this is no longer a question of quantity but of function.
The ash — the Shakh leaves an open צ״ע (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק כ״ו): the Beit Yosef cites the Orchot Chayim, for whom husks burnt within twelve months yield permitted ash; the Darkhei Moshe copied it. The Shakh objects, on two supports:
« דבסתם יינם קיי״ל דאפרן אסור כמו עצי עובדי כוכבים » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק כ״ו)
He leans on the Rivash and on the Gemara in עבודה זרה ס״ב ע״ב, and leaves the Rama in צ״ע — one of the few places in the siman where the Shakh does not conclude.

What is then forbidden — and the Rishonim's divergence: the Mechaber writes that the touch forbids everything in the vat, « ונאסר כל מה שבגת », even if the non-Jew touched only the husks, provided they are wet enough to moisten. The Tur, however, records the opposing view — for the Ramban, « אינו אוסר אלא מקום מגעו »; and the Shakh refers to it: « הא לאו הכי לא נאסר אלא מה שנגע » (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק ל״ב). Nafka mina — seif 19: « גיגית מלאה ענבים דרוכים עומדת בבית העובד כוכבים יש לאסור שמא המשיך ממנה » (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:י״ט). Here the prohibition rests on no established fact but on the mere possibility of a past הַמְשָׁכָה. 12. הגת הסתומה והסל — Rav Huna’s sugya, and the Rashba’s astonishment

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)

Seif 20 is the densest passage in the siman. A sealed, full vat in which no הַמְשָׁכָה occurs; the juice descends with the husks into a straining basket; the non-Jew touches the basket. What is in the vat does not bear the name of wine — and yet, if the basket is poured back, everything is forbidden.

The text (שו״ע יו״ד קכ״ג:כ):
« אין על מה שבגת תורת יין אלא על מה שבסל בלבד »
« ואם החזירו לגת נאסר מה שבגת משום תערובת יין שבסל שנתערב בו »
The Shakh supplies the Rosh's reasoning: what remains in the vat does not acquire the name of wine merely because wine flowed out of the same place (ש״ך יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק ל״ט).
The Rashba's astonishment, as the Taz quotes it from the תורת הבית (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק י״ב): if the non-Jew's own touch does not forbid what is in the vat, how can a mere mixture with wine forbidden by that very touch forbid it?
« אדרבה אם במגע עצמו אינו נאסר היאך יאסר בתערובת יין שנאסר מחמת נגיעת עובד כוכבים »
The Rashba closes with the proverb — « יציבא בארעא וגיורא בשמי שמיא »: the native on the ground and the stranger in the highest heaven.
The Taz's answer (ט״ז יו״ד קכ״ג ס״ק י״ב): two distinct titles of prohibition must be separated. The Tur speaks here only of the first — « אין על הגת תורת יין », that is: in itself, the touch does not harm it. Mixture belongs to another chapter entirely, and the prohibition there operates not because the vat is wine but because forbidden wine was poured into it. What lacks the name “wine” can perfectly well take on a prohibition of mixture.

One liquid, two regimes, according to the question asked.

The Rama's closing gloss — a penance, not a halakhah: « מי ששתה יין נסך בשוגג י״א שיתענה ה׳ ימים נגד ה׳ פעמים גפן שבחומש » (רמ״א יו״ד קכ״ג:כ״ו), in the name of a responsum of the Rosh. That the siman should end thus is not incidental: after twenty-six seifim of technical definitions, the last word is not about the wine but about the man who drank it — and it counts the occurrences of גפן in the Chumash.

15. חקירת היסוד — three axes, and what follows from them

↩ Detailed explanation (level 1)
Axis א — does the prohibition attach to the wine or to the relationship? If to the wine (the hekesh to sacrifice, fitness for libation), then whatever is no longer “wine” leaves the decree — cooked, honeyed, soured, diluted. If to the relationship (בנותיהן), none of that should help. The siman advances along the first axis while the Rosh keeps invoking the second. The Taz reconciles them by showing that the second was activated only where the first allowed it.
Axis ב — definition, fact or act? Is wine born by a physical fact (the liquid separated, the vat slopes) or by an owner's act (he willed it wine, he drew it off)? Rashi holds the fact, Rabbeinu Tam the act; seif 18 reintroduces intent with « וכיון לשלותו », and seif 20 draws all its force from the sealed vat never having received the name of wine.
Axis ג — does a decree outlive its motive? This is the question of seif 1, and the siman offers three answers: it lapses (Rashbam in Rashi's name in the Geonim's name); it does not lapse, for דבר שנאסר במנין (the Taz's first road); it does not lapse, for it was never a decree but a stringency (his second road). And the Rosh adds a fourth, which is not a theory but a policy: « הנח להם לישראל ».
The unifying yesod: the whole siman can be read as an inquiry into the name. Nothing here is forbidden because it is harmful; everything is forbidden because it is called “wine”. Hence the chapter's peculiar form: it is not a list of prohibitions but a series of membership tests. Cooked wine, vinegar, unripe juice, temed, twelve-month lees, the vat before הַמְשָׁכָה — each seif asks whether the object before us still bears the name. Where the name is absent the prohibition finds no purchase; where the name remains, no dilution saves it.
Six nafka minot gathered:
  1. A non-Jew's wine nowadays: Mechaber — forbidden in benefit; Rama — permitted where there is loss, forbidden ab initio for trade (seif 1).
  2. Pasteurised wine become commonplace: on the Taz's view the rarity argument runs out; on the Rambam's and the Tur's, unfitness for libation suffices (seif 3).
  3. Vinegar that does not bubble: forbidden for want of a sign — though vinegar is by nature unfit for libation (seifim 6 and 8).
  4. Lees steeped a second time: permitted per Rabbeinu Yonah and the Mechaber, forbidden per the Raavad and the Taz (seifim 9 and 11).
  5. Husks over twelve months old never steeped: permitted on the Gemara in חולין as the Taz reads it, forbidden per Rabbeinu Tam and the Mechaber (seif 14).
  6. A basket poured back into the vat: the Shakh nullifies in sixty counting the husks, the Taz does not count the wine — unless one follows the Rama in siman 134 (seif 20).
