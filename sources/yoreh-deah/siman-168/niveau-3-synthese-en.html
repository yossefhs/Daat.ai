Siman 168 — Ten tables to hold twenty-seven seifim

What to have in mind before opening the text again

Twenty-seven seifim are badly remembered as a list. They are well remembered by their hinges: three questions asked in the same order for every case, and three words that answer them. This level does not repeat the text: it gives the structure, and shows where each seif hangs on it.

1. The axiom: whose loan is it?

The whole siman rests on one question, and it is not the one supposed. One spontaneously asks: where does the interest go? That is not the question. The Shulchan Arukh’s question is: whose is the loan? For the Torah forbids only interest passing from borrower to lender, and the interest is a Jew’s only if the loan is.

The Rambam gives the principle, and seif 4 is its one-line demonstration: interest paid to a Jew is permitted, once the lender is the non-Jew.

« שֶׁלֹּא אָסְרָה תּוֹרָה אֶלָּא רִבִּית הַבָּאָה מִן הַלּוֶֹה לַמַּלְוֶה » (רמב״ם הלכות מלווה ולווה פרק ה׳ הלכה י״ד)
« עובד כוכבים שמלוה לישראל ברבית על מנת שיתן הרבית לישראל מותר » (שו״ע יו״ד קס״ח:ד)
Three words answer the question, and never another. שליחות — is the non-Jew acting for a Jew? אחריות — who loses if everything unravels? מכר — was the right sold, or merely pledged? And a fourth prohibition is added to the three without replacing them: מראית העין.

2. The three reflex questions

For any case in the siman, four questions in this order and no other.

#The questionWhat it decides
1Did the non-Jew act for a Jew? (שליחות)If so, the transaction is the Jew’s — but the prohibition is only rabbinic, and Rabbeinu Tam denies it.
2On whom does the loss fall if the borrower repays nothing? (אחריות)This is the decisive criterion of seifim 10 to 24. Whoever loses is the lender.
3Was the right sold or only pledged? (מכר)A question proper to seifim 18 to 20. A sale severs the link; a pledge does not.
4What does the onlooker see? (מראית העין)It arises after the first three have permitted. Seifim 22, 23, 24.
The order matters. A transaction may be permitted by the first three questions and forbidden by the fourth — that is exactly seif 22. And the reverse never occurs: appearance has never permitted what substance forbids.

3. Master table — the twenty-seven seifim

The twenty-seven seifim, grouped by movement, with the word that governs each.

SeifimThe movementWhat is decided there
1-3A Jew’s debt to a non-Jew, taken over by another Jew.Forbidden (1); permitted if the non-Jew took and gave in hand (2), or if laid on the ground, or as a deposit (3).
4-8The circuits, and the question of agency.A non-Jew’s interest paid to a Jew is permitted (4); the three-way circuit is forbidden (5); the mirror of seif 1 is permitted (6); borrowing in a non-Jew’s name binds the borrower alone (7); an agent who deviates breaks the agency (8).
9-12The pledge: what the lender knows and what he does not.Ignorance protects (9); knowledge binds (9); a pledge taken by force is not presumed conveyed (10); a pledge lent to the non-Jew binds him (11); an agent who withholds is not the borrower (12).
13-17The agent: the formula, its burdens, its limits.The formula אתה תהיה שלוחי … באחריותי (13); the rate gap belongs to the agent, and he must not say he will pay (14); he has no say in the pledge’s sale (15); the risk decides (16, 17); and suretyship is referred to siman 170.
18-19The sale of the right in the pledge — and its counterfeit.The formula of sale permits (18); זקיפה and credit debts bound it (18); and where the non-Jew is only scenery, it is תחבולות רשעים (19).
20-24The reversal: a Jew’s money or pledge in a non-Jew’s hand.A Jew’s pledge with a non-Jew is permitted (20); deposited money follows the אחריות (21, 22); a wage does not shift the risk (23); nor does guardianship (24) — and appearance forbids what substance permits.
25-27Proof, and two closing cases.The presumption לא שביק היתירא dispenses with the oath (25); undertaking to pay is permitted when the loan comes from the non-Jew (26); the agent does not redeem but must produce the non-Jew (27).

4. שליחות — the map of the two views

Two views, and almost the whole siman is read in the gap between them.

Rashi and his schoolRabbeinu Tam
The principleNo Torah agency, but the Sages upheld it for stringency.No agency at all, not even for stringency.
Seif 6Forbidden — and rabbinic, per the Shach.Permitted.
Seif 9Lending on a Jew’s pledge handed over by a non-Jew is forbidden if one knows.Permitted even lekhatchila, even on his own books.
The rulingThe Mehaber, and the Rama: העיקר כסברא הראשונה.The Tur attributes it to the Rosh; the Beit Yosef disputes it. The Rama does not protest against the custom.
« אבל העיקר כסברא הראשונה » (רמ״א יו״ד קס״ח:ו)
« אבל במקום שנהגו להקל אין למחות בידם » (רמ״א יו״ד קס״ח:ו)
« הרי העובד כוכבים שלוחו של ישראל לחומרא ואסור מדרבנן כן הוא הסכמת רוב הפוסקים ודלא כהרמב״ם שכתב דהוי ר״ק » (ש״ך יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק ט״ו)
Two consequences not to be forgotten. (1) Wherever the seif forbids on account of agency, the prohibition is only rabbinic — except for the Rambam, who writes רבית קצוצה on seif 6. (2) The Rama opens the same door three times: במקום שנהגו להקל, and the medieval practice of pawnbroking passed through it.

5. אחריות — the three regimes of undertaking

This table is the most useful in the level. Three closely similar clauses, three regimes, and seif 16 distinguishes them in one sentence.

The regimeWhat it saysIts status
שומר“I answer for these funds as a bailee — even for force majeure.”Permitted. So long as the funds subsist in kind, he has borrowed nothing.
ערב“If you find nothing of his at the time of payment, I shall pay you principal and interest.”Permitted — כדין ערב. He pays only in default, not in place. See siman 170.
לוה“If the debts are lost I shall pay them to you.” Or: “so long as he has not paid, I pay.”Forbidden. It is a borrowing, and the loan becomes Jew to Jew.
« ואם אין האחריות עליו אלא כשאר שלוחים אם שומר חנם שומר חנם אם שומר שכר שומר שכר מותר » (שו״ע יו״ד קס״ח:ט״ז)
« אבל אם א״ל לישראל פלוני אלוה אותם ברבית ואם לא תמצא לו נכסים בשעת פרעון אני אפרע לך קרן ורבית מותר כדין ערב » (ש״ך יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק ס״ט)
« ויקבל עליו העובד כוכבים שאם יאבדו החובות יכרע׳ הוא לישראל כדין כל לוה » (ש״ך יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק ס״ח)
« דכיון שכל זמן שלא יתן לו העובד כוכבים קרן חייב לפרוע זה הרבית נמצא גם אחריות הקרן עליו » (ט״ז יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק כ״א)
The line is not one of degree but of kind: the bailee answers for the object, the debtor for a sum. And note the third row: the Taz shows that a guarantee on the interest alone entails the principal, since it has no term but the payment of the principal.

6. משכון — the six configurations of the pledge

The pledge occupies twelve seifim, and six configurations exhaust the matter. The table is read by crossing two data: whose the pledge is, and in whose hand it lies.

The pledge belongs to……and lies withIts status
Un non-Juif · של עובד כוכביםa Jewish lender, given willinglyPermitted (seif 9). And if a Jew comes to claim it, he is not believed.
Un non-Juif · של עובד כוכביםa second Jew, by sale of the rightPermitted (seif 18) — provided it is a sale and not a loan.
Un non-Juif · של עובד כוכביםa second Jew, the money serving the first JewForbidden (seif 19) — תחבולות רשעים.
Un Juif · של ישראלa non-Jew, lent by the Jew for that purposePermitted (seif 11); and the Rama settles the case of the violent non-Jew.
Un Juif · של ישראלa non-Jew, then re-pledged with a JewPermitted per the Rosh (seif 20); some require קנה מעכשיו.
Un Juif · של ישראלa Jewish lender who knows it is a Jew’sForbidden (seif 9); and if merely recognisable, מכוער הדבר.
One rule cuts across them all, which the Rama lays down at the end of seif 9 and which governs the rest: let the pledge be worth the sum lent. That is what guarantees the lender will never have to turn against a Jew — and so what shifts the אחריות once and for all.
« ולכן צריך גם כן בתחילה שיהא המשכון שוה נגד מעותיו כדי שלא יהא לו עסק עם הלוה כלל אם ירצה » (רמ״א יו״ד קס״ח:ט)
« שהמשכון טוב ושוה הקרן והריוח » (ש״ך יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק י״ג)

7. מכר — the היתר of seif 18 and its two limits

Seif 18 is the most relied-upon seif of the siman. Its permission has an exact form and two limits never to be forgotten.

« ובלבד שיאמר לו הריני מוכר לך כל כח וזכות ושעבוד שיש לי על משכון זה ואין לי עסק עמך ולא לך עמי » (שו״ע יו״ד קס״ח:י״ח)
What is requiredWho requires it
The formA sale of the right in the pledge, expressly stated.The Mehaber and the majority of decisors.
The leniencyThe sale is presumed: one who re-pledges without saying anything intends the licit.The Rama, in the name of the Mordechai and a responsum of the Rosh. And such is the custom.
The objectionA sale coupled with an obligation to reconvey is no sale.The Taz — פסק זה נ״ל תמוה; and the Shach states the same difficulty.
The rescueThe pledge not having been acquired by the first Jew, it remained the non-Jew’s: nothing passes from Jewish hand to Jewish hand.The Shach, through the Rosh and the Baal HaIttur; and the Nekudot HaKesef against the Taz.
The two limits, and they are firm. (1) זקיפה: if the first Jew has already merged principal and interest into one figure, the whole has become a Jew’s money, and the arrangement collapses entirely. (2) Credit debts: there is no route but express release — no mode of acquisition transfers a claim against a non-Jew.
« אבל אם כבר זקף הישראל על העובד כוכבים קרן ורבית ביחד » (רמ״א יו״ד קס״ח:י״ח)
« אבל אין שום תקנה אחרת להקנות חוב העובד כוכבים באשראי » (ש״ך יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק ס״ב)

8. מראית העין — the prohibition that remains when the first falls

Three consecutive seifim lay down a prohibition unrelated to what precedes, and not lifted in the same way.

« ואם הם באחריות העובד כוכבים מותר אבל אסור לעשות כן מפני מראית העין » (שו״ע יו״ד קס״ח:כ״ב)
« ועובד כוכבים שאמר לישראל הילך שכרך והלוה מעותי ברבית מותר אבל אסור לעשות כן מפני מראית העין » (שו״ע יו״ד קס״ח:כ״ג)
« מיהו אם הדבר מפורסם לרבים מותר » (רמ״א יו״ד קס״ח:כ״ג)
The caseIn substanceBy appearance
A non-Jew’s money deposited with a Jew, at the non-Jew’s risk (22).Permitted.Forbidden — and the Shach extends it even to a plain deposit.
A non-Jew hires a Jew to lend out his money (23).Permitted.Forbidden — unless the matter is publicly known.
A Jew is a non-Jew’s guardian over all his property (24).Permitted.Permitted too — the role is public, says the Taz: the appearance falls away of itself.
« פירוש על כל נכסיו אמרינן מסתמא שגם המעות שמלוה הוא של עובד כוכבים ואין כאן משום מראית עין » (ט״ז יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק ל״ד)
The only lifting is notoriety, and the Shach gives its exact measure: דהיינו שאומנתו בכך — that it be his recognised trade. And he expressly rejects the idea that informing the borrower and the witnesses would suffice.
« דהיינו שאומנתו בכך » (ש״ך יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק ע״א)
« הא לאו הכי אפילו מודיעו ללוה ולעדים שהם של עובד כוכבים אסור » (ש״ך יו״ד קס״ח ס״ק ע״א)

9. The vocabulary of the siman

Eleven words, and the siman is legible. Five come from the block on interest, six belong to it alone.

שליחות ביד עובד כוכבים — Agency through the hand of a non-Jew. There is none from the Torah; the Sages upheld it for stringency according to Rashi, whom the Mehaber follows. Rabbeinu Tam denies it entirely.
אחריות — The bearing of risk, not custody. It is the decisive test of seifim 10 to 24: the one who loses if everything unravels is the one whose money was lent.
ערב — The guarantor. It marks the exact limit of the permitted undertaking: he pays only in default, never in place of. The Rama refers to siman 170.
משכון — The pledge. It is both the object whose ownership qualifies the loan, and the instrument that shifts the אחריות when it is worth the sum lent.
אגר נטר — The wage of waiting. The Taz makes it an instrument of proof here: in ס״ק כ״ד, mere foregone profit suffices to constitute it — and the Nekudot HaKesef answer that the comparison does not hold.
רבית קצוצה — Stipulated interest, of Torah rank, extracted by the beit din. The siman finds it in seif 1 (gloss), seif 19 (Tur) and — for the Rambam alone — seif 6.
אבק רבית — The dust of interest, rabbinic, not extracted by the beit din. For the Shach it is the rank of the prohibition in seif 6 — against the Rambam.
מראית העין — Appearance. A second prohibition added once the substance has permitted. It is lifted only by notoriety: שאומנתו בכך.
זקיפה — The consolidation of principal and interest into a single figure. After it, the whole is a Jew’s money, and the arrangement of seif 18 becomes unworkable.
תחבולות רשעים — The name seif 19 gives to the purely decorative use of the non-Jew. It is the boundary of the whole siman, and the Tur sees stipulated interest in it.
לא שביק היתירא ואכיל איסורא — The presumption governing seif 25: a man does not forsake the permitted for the forbidden. The Taz limits it for his own age.

10. Golden rules, mnemonic and classic pitfalls

Golden rule 1. Never ask where the interest goes. Ask whose the loan is. Seif 4 demonstrates the rule in a single line.
Golden rule 2. From seif 10 onwards a single word decides: אחריות. And the אחריות that forbids is a debtor’s, not a bailee’s nor a guarantor’s.
Golden rule 3. What is permitted in substance may be forbidden by appearance, and never the reverse. Check both, in that order.
Golden rule 4. Every permission of this siman presupposes that the non-Jew is genuinely a party to the transaction. Where he is only scenery, it is seif 19: תחבולות רשעים היא זו.

Mnemonic. Four words in order, and neither the question nor its rank is forgotten: שליחות · אחריות · מכר · מראית. The first asks about the act, the second about the risk, the third about title, the fourth about the eye.

The classic pitfalls.

The pitfallWhat is supposedWhat the siman says
1Routing through a non-Jew suffices to make the loan licit.No — seif 1. So long as the money leaves the Jew’s hand as a loan, it is his debt, and it is stipulated interest.
2If the interest is paid to a non-Jew, there is no prohibition.No — seif 1 again. And the reverse is true: seif 4 permits interest paid to a Jew.
3Taking responsibility for the funds is forbidden in any case.No — seif 16. A bailee’s liability, even extended to force majeure, does not make a borrower.
4Telling the lender one will oneself pay principal and interest is always forbidden.No — compare seif 14 with seif 26. The direction of the loan decides: borrowed from a Jew, forbidden; from a non-Jew, permitted.
5Explaining the situation to the borrower lifts the prohibition of appearance.No — the Shach expressly rejects it. Only notoriety lifts it: שאומנתו בכך.
6The custom of seif 18 permits any assignment of a claim.No — two limits: זקיפה destroys the arrangement, and credit debts require an express release.
And to close, the sentence the Mehaber placed at the end of seif 24, which holds for all twenty-seven: וגם זה תלוי במי שאחריות עליו — “this too depends on the one upon whom the risk lies.” The whole siman is the unfolding of that line.
« וגם זה תלוי במי שאחריות עליו » (שו״ע יו״ד קס״ח:כ״ד)
