Siman 148 — The laws of the festivals of the idols

דיני חגי האלילים

Twelve seifim. The siman does not forbid trade: it fears a sentence — the one the non-Jew will say to his idol while holding what he holds from us

1. The axiom: the transfer, the echo, the limit

The starting point:

This siman does not forbid trade; it fears a sentence. The non-Jew, holding in his hand what he holds from us, will go on his festival day and thank his idol for it. The whole siman is the measure of the distance needed for that sentence not to be said.

The object — הֲנָאָה. Something passes from us to him: goods, a lent article, money, a gift, a greeting, presence.

The fear — הוֹדָאָה. It is not the transfer that is forbidden but its echo: the acknowledgement he will render for it to his idol. Hence the prohibition depends on the day, on the person, and on the age.

The limit — אֵיבָה. Where abstention would create enmity, the prohibition yields — but it only yields, it does not vanish.

Hence the axiom: before each case ask first what passes, then what he will say of it, then what would happen if one refused.

The two founding texts:

« משום דאזיל ומודה לפני אליל ביום חגו » (ש״ך יו״ד קמ״ח ס״ק א)
« כולהו משום דאזיל ומודה לעבודת כוכבים ביום אידו » (ט״ז יו״ד קמ״ח ס״ק א)

2. The 5 reflex questions

  1. Will the gain last until his festival? A durable object or money: yes. Vegetables, a cooked dish: no — and then one may sell, not buy.
  2. Is it a benefit or a rescue? Collecting a debt that was about to be lost enriches nobody: כמציל מידם.
  3. Is the day fixed and common? Annual and kept by the whole community: the siman applies. Otherwise — Shach sk 4, seif 7 — it does not.
  4. Does he really adhere to it? One who follows by custom or honour, without believing, falls outside the siman (seif 8); one known not to serve idols also falls outside it (seif 5).
  5. Would refusal create enmity? If so, the prohibition yields — but only to its measure, and the בעל נפש closes again what enmity had opened.

The second question is that of the baraita seif 1 copies:

« רַבִּי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בֶּן קׇרְחָה אוֹמֵר: מִלְוֶה בִּשְׁטָר אֵין נִפְרָעִין מֵהֶן, מִלְוֶה עַל פֶּה נִפְרָעִין מֵהֶן, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהוּא כְּמַצִּיל מִיָּדָם » (עבודה זרה ו׳ ע״ב)

5. The gift one cannot destroy — the Taz alone against three

Seif 5 assumes one can destroy the gift before his eyes. When one cannot, four authorities answer, and three say the same thing.

WhoWhat does one do when one cannot destroy it before him?
Shach sk 5He takes it and derives no benefit — in the name of the Rosh, the Ran, the Semag and Sefer haTerumot
Taz sk 5He takes it and may benefit; the Rambam’s “בפניו” means the face he shows
Nekudot HaKesefThe Taz’s words are far-fetched; the correct view is the Shach’s
Baer Hetev sk 5The Taz is astonishing, and the Nekudot HaKesef oppose him

Psak

Three voices out of four, including the Baer Hetev which is the practical digest, hold that one does not benefit from the gift. That is the position to follow, and lema’asse the question is put to a Rav.
« והט״ז פסק דאם א״א לאשתמוטי יקבל ממנו ומותר להנות ממנו ודבריו תמוהים וכן בנה״כ חולק עליו » (באר היטב יו״ד קמ״ח ס״ק ה)

7. The 5 golden rules

  1. The reason is single. Everything the siman forbids, it forbids because the non-Jew will go and thank his idol. One engine, twelve applications.
  2. What counts is what remains in hand. Not the value, not the nature of the bargain: the duration. That is the Raavad’s question, which the Rambam applies both ways.
  3. A rescue is not a benefit. And its measure is read in the state of the world, not in the form of the instrument — hence the Shach and the Pitchei Teshuva.
  4. The decree reaches only the fixed, common and acknowledged day. Remove regularity, community or adherence, and it no longer applies.
  5. Enmity suspends, it does not abolish. It always leaves a remainder to do: not to benefit, to keep away, to send on the eve.

8. Mnemonic — הֲנָאָה · הוֹדָאָה · אֵיבָה

הֲנָאָה — what passes. Goods, a lent article, money, a gift, a greeting, presence. If nothing passes, there is nothing to forbid.

הוֹדָאָה — what he will say of it. The siman fears not the transfer but its echo before the idol. Hence the prohibition depends on the day, the person and the age.

אֵיבָה — what would happen if one refused. It bends the prohibition without breaking it, and always leaves a remainder: not to benefit, to keep away, to send on the eve.

Three words, in that order, and the twelve seifim fall into place.

9. The 5 classic pitfalls

1. Thinking seif 12 erases the siman. It does not erase it: the Shach writes expressly that seif 9 remains forbidden today, and the Rama closes with the בעל נפש what enmity had opened.
2. Confusing the three days with the festival day. The three days are “themselves, without their festival”: four days in all in Eretz Israel. And the gain made during the three days is permitted after the fact, that of the day itself is not.
3. Thinking that selling and buying follow the same rule. The Mechaber follows the Rambam: the durable is forbidden both ways, but the perishable may be sold and not bought. Rashi and Rabbenu Tam each give another map — that is the machloket of §4.
4. Taking the “far away” of seif 11 for a condition. The Taz writes that it is an emphasis: if even from afar the outward journey is forbidden, all the more so from nearby.
5. Thinking seif 10 depends on the festival. It does not: the Shach writes in sk 8 that the prohibition of doubling holds even outside his festival day, and the Rambam filed it in another chapter.
« כלומר אפי׳ שלא ביום חגו » (ש״ך יו״ד קמ״ח ס״ק ח)
« נראה דלרבותא קאמר למרחוק דאפילו בהליכה אסור » (ט״ז יו״ד קמ״ח ס״ק ז)

Flash card. What passes? — if nothing passes, there is nothing to forbid. Will anything remain on his festival day? — if not, one may sell. Is it a benefit or a rescue? — a rescue enriches nobody. Is the day fixed, common, acknowledged? — if not, the decree does not reach it. Would refusal create enmity? — then the prohibition yields, but only to its measure. Five questions in order, and the twelve seifim fall into place.
