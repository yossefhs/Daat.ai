Siman 189 — The Menstrual Vesatot: Fixed and Non-Fixed Cycles, Onah Beinonit, Dilug and Haflagah, and Uprooting a Veset

The fixed and non-fixed veset, the onah beinonit (30 days), the veset ha-haflagot, dilug and sirug, the days of the month and the week, the veset ha-guf and the uprooting of a veset (Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 189 — 34 se'ifim)

כָּל אִשָּׁה שֶׁאֵין לָהּ וֶסֶת קָבוּעַ — חוֹשֶׁשֶׁת לְיוֹם שְׁלֹשִׁים לִרְאִיָּתָהּ, שֶׁהִיא עוֹנָה בֵּינוֹנִית לִסְתָם נָשִׁים; וְאִם יֵשׁ לָהּ וֶסֶת קָבוּעַ לִזְמַן יָדוּעַ — חוֹשֶׁשֶׁת לַזְּמַן הַיָּדוּעַ.

Every woman who has no fixed veset is wary of the 30th day from her sighting, which is the onah beinonit (the average interval) of most women; and if she has a fixed veset at a known time, she is wary of that known time.

Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 189:1

Frequently asked questions — Siman 189

What is the difference between a fixed veset (וסת קבוע) and a non-fixed one?

A veset kavua (fixed cycle) is a cycle that has recurred regularly and that — according to the Shulchan Aruch (YD 189:2) — is established by three identical sightings (וביניהם ג׳ זמנים שוין), four sightings for the veset ha-haflagot. As long as it has not been uprooted three times, the woman is bound to it and must perform a בדיקה even if the onah passed without her sensing anything. A veset she-eino kavua (non-fixed cycle) has not reached this regularity: one is wary of it only once, and if it passes without her seeing, she is permitted. The real complexity of the calculation requires consulting a Rav.

What is the onah beinonit (the 30th day)?

The onah beinonit is the marker of the 30th day: every woman who has no fixed veset is wary of the 30th day from her sighting (חוששת ליום ל׳ לראייתה שהיא עונה בינונית לסתם נשים — YD 189:1), since it is the average interval of most women. The Shulchan Aruch (189:4) clarifies that this onah beinonit of 30 days has the status of a fixed veset: even if it passed without a בדיקה, one does not relax it as for a mere non-fixed cycle. In practice it is the marker day of most women today; but applying it requires a Rav.

What are the types of veset (haflagah, dilug, days of the month or week, veset ha-guf)?

A veset is established in several ways. The veset ha-haflagot rests on the interval between sightings (e.g. every 28 days). The dilug (shift) is an interval that lengthens regularly (30, 31, 32…). One also establishes a veset by the days of the month (the 1st of each month) or of the week, equal or not, with the sirug (skipping a month, where the 1st sighting counts). Finally the veset ha-guf is established by a bodily sign: yawning (פיהוק), sneezing, abdominal pain, chills, or — according to the Rama (189:23) — eating garlic, onion or pepper. Each type is established in 3 times and does not combine with another. In practice, consult your Rav.

What is uprooting a veset (עקירת וסת)?

The akirat veset is the extinction of a cycle. A fixed veset is uprooted only after three times in which the onah passes without her seeing (YD 189:14-16); as long as it has not been uprooted three times, she continues to be wary of it. If she returns to her former veset, it regains its force (חזר הוסת למקומו). A non-fixed veset, on the other hand, is uprooted from a single time without a sighting. The exact calculation — establishing, uprooting, returning, as well as the laws of the ketanah, the zekenah, the pregnant and the nursing woman (mesuleket damim) — is one of the most technical chapters of halacha: never rule alone on a real case, consult your Rav.
