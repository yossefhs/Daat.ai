📜 The text of the Shulchan Arukh — seif 1

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

« צריכה שתטבול כל גופה בפעם אחת לפיכך צריך שלא יהיה עליה שום דבר החוצץ ואפילו כל שהוא ואם דרך בני אדם לפעמים להקפיד עליו חוצץ אפילו אם אינה מקפדת עליו עתה או אפילו אינה מקפדת עליו לעולם כיון שדרך רוב בני אדם להקפיד עליו חוצץ ואם הוא חופה רוב הגוף אפילו אין דרך בני אדם להקפיד בכך חוצץ: הגה ולכתחלה לא תטבול אפילו בדברים שאינן חוצצין גזרה אטו דברים החוצצין » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:א)

Translation. She must immerse her whole body at one time; therefore there must be upon her nothing that interposes, even the least thing. And if people are in the habit of minding it at times, it interposes — even if she herself does not mind it now, and even if she never minds it at all — since most people are in the habit of minding it. And if the thing covers the greater part of the body, it interposes even though people are not in the habit of minding such a thing. Gloss of the Rama: a priori she should not immerse even with things that do not interpose, a decree on account of things that do interpose.

1. Opening the sugya — כל גופה בפעם אחת and the gader of chatsitsa

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

The foundation — two laws held together: the wholeness of the immersion and the absence of an interposing thing

The siman is built on two pillars bound to one another. The first: כל גופה בפעם אחת — her whole body must be surrounded by the waters of the mikveh at one time, and not one part after another. The second: שלא יהיה עליה שום דבר החוצץ — any foreign thing interposed between her flesh and the water invalidates the immersion, ואפילו כל שהוא, even the least of things.

The second pillar follows from the first: since her whole body must be surrounded by water at one time, wherever there is an interposing thing the water does not reach the flesh beneath it, and it turns out that her body is not wholly immersed. And the gader of the interposing thing is not merely quantitative — the greater part or the lesser — it also turns on הקפדה, the mind one has to the thing.

The root of chatsitsa. The gemara in Eruvin derives from the verse that no thing may interpose between the flesh and the water; the parallel sugya is in Sukkah 6a, and the particulars of what interposes are gathered in the mishnah of Mikvaot, chapter 9 — the chapter of אלו חוצצין — and in Niddah 67a–67b.
« שֶׁלֹּא יְהֵא דָּבָר חוֹצֵץ בֵּין בְּשָׂרוֹ לַמַּיִם » (עירובין ד׳ ע״ב)

As for the first pillar — that the whole body enter the water at one time — the Taz gives its derashah in his very first entry on the siman, and it runs not through the verse of chatsitsa but through the verse of the setting sun:

« דדרשינן ורחץ ובא השמש וטהר מה ביאת שמשו כולו כאחת אף רחיצתו כולו כאחת » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק א׳)
The underlying chakirah — the matrix רובו / מיעוטו × מקפיד / אינו מקפיד. The Taz, on the words of seif 1 that speak of a thing covering the greater part of the body, lays out the whole matrix: what interposes from the Torah, what the Sages decreed, and what does not interpose at all.
  • ברובו ומקפיד עליו → interposes from the Torah.
  • ברובו שאין מקפיד → interposes rabbinically, a decree on account of the greater part that is minded.
  • המיעוט המקפיד → interposes rabbinically, likewise a decree on account of the greater part that is minded.
  • במיעוט ואינו מקפיד → does not interpose: two leniencies together, and it would be a decree upon a decree.
« דדבר תורה אינו חוצץ אלא ברובו ומקפיד עליו וגזרו רבנן ברובו שאין מקפיד משום רוב המקפיד וגזרו על המיעוט המקפיד גם כן משום רוב המקפיד אבל במיעוט ואינו מקפיד דתרתי לטיבותא לא גזרו כלל דהוה גזירה לגזירה » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק ד׳)

Torah-level chatsitsa therefore stands on two legs: quantity — רוב — and mind — הקפדה; and the two sides that are not from the Torah were forbidden by the Sages, each on account of the case where the two meet. The Taz adds that the Rama's decree at the end of seif 1, which reaches even the lesser part, speaks only a priori: after the fact we do not decree a decree upon a decree.

Where the gader of מקפיד is discussed — Rashba, Rosh, Tur and Beit Yosef. The table below states where the discussion is held, not what each one rules there: the text of these Rishonim is not reproduced here, and this page quotes word for word only what it has checked against its source — the Shulchan Arukh, the Taz and the Shakh.
The authorityWhat is sought thereThe place
The Rashba The gedarim of מקפיד and of what covers the greater part of the body תורת הבית הארוך, בית שביעי
The Rosh The relation between quantity and mind פסקי הרא״ש, הלכות נדה
The Tur and the Beit Yosef The gathering of the Rishonim's language, from which the wording of seif 1 comes טור ובית יוסף כאן
Covering the greater part of the body, even without minding — why? The Mehaber writes that if the interposing thing חופה רוב הגוף, it interposes even though it is in no way the way of people to mind it. And there is room to ask: is not "the greater part without minding" only rabbinic, as the Taz has just written? One may answer that when the thing actually covers the greater part of the body's surface, there is no כל גופה בפעם אחת at all in fact — the greater part of her body is not in the water — and it invalidates on that very count, not on the count of minding. There would thus be two ways of invalidating: that of quantity, which touches the wholeness of the immersion, and that of דעת, which makes the interposing thing important. This is a line of study and not a ruling: the Taz himself expressly ranks "the greater part without minding" among the decrees of the Sages.
The sources of the sugya. Eruvin 4b and Sukkah 6a for the derashah and the measures, Niddah 67a–67b for the concerns of chatsitsa at immersion, and the mishnah of Mikvaot chapter 9 for the particulars of what interposes. The Rambam (הלכות מקוואות, פרקים א׳–ב׳) fixed the parts of the matrix as halakhah; the Rashba (תורת הבית הארוך) and the Rosh developed the gedarim of מקפיד; the Tur and the Beit Yosef gathered and fixed the seifim here.

2. דרך בני אדם להקפיד — private minding and general minding

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

This is the theoretical heart of the siman: what is the "minding" that determines chatsitsa — that of this woman, or that of most people; and how is it assessed in practice.

The foundation — general minding binds her who does not mind.
The Mehaber's language carries two novelties in the gader of minding:

• ואם דרך בני אדם לפעמים להקפיד עליו חוצץ אפילו אם אינה מקפדת עליו עתה (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:א) — general minding binds even her who does not mind it at present.
• או אפילו אינה מקפדת עליו לעולם (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:א) — and it prevails even over a complete absence of private minding.

The Mehaber closes by giving the measure: כיון שדרך רוב בני אדם להקפיד עליו חוצץ (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:א).

But the Taz sets a limit to the first of these two formulations, and the limit counts: "people are in the habit of minding it at times" suffices only if the rest of the sentence holds — either that she herself usually minds it, or that most people mind it.

« נראה ביאור לשון זה דרך בני אדם להקפיד על זה אע״פ שאין רוב בני אדם מקפידין רק קצתן וזו האשה רגילה להקפיד ג״כ על זה בפעמים אחרים רק שעכשיו אינה מקפדת או אפילו אינה מקפדת בשום פעם ורוב בני אדם מקפידים על זה חוצץ » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק ב׳)
« אבל אם מקצת בני אדם מקפידין וזו אינה מקפדת לעולם לא הוה חציצה » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק ב׳)

It follows that if only a minority of people mind it and she herself never minds it, there is no chatsitsa. The wording of seif 1 is therefore not a rule of pure objectivity: it crosses the usage of the many with the usage of this woman.

The chakirah — the nature of minding: subjective or objective? What is the root of this law? Two sides:
  • First side — subjective: minding is a matter of will; anything a person has in mind to remove is not "nullified" to the body, and interposes on that account. On this side it would have been fitting to follow her own mind.
  • Second side — objective: minding is the mark of the interposing thing's importance; a thing that people mind is an important thing, which is not nullified, and it interposes regardless of her own view.
The Mehaber's language leans to the second side, since he binds her who never minds it. But the apparatus does not leave the question closed: the Shakh brings the reverse case — most people do not mind, and she does.
« והיכא דרוב בני אדם אין מקפידים והיא מקפדת כ׳ ב״י בשם הרמב״ם וטור דחוצץ » (ש״ך יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק ב׳)

The Taz brings the same thing in his third entry: the Beit Yosef had remained in doubt, and the Darkhei Moshe rules from the Mordekhai that it interposes. The Taz adds that so wrote מו״ח — that is, his father-in-law, the author of the Bayit Chadash, and not the Taz speaking of himself.

« בב״י נסתפק אם שאר בני אדם אינם מקפידים וזו מקפדת וכתב בד״מ דבמרדכי כתב בהדיא שחוצץ » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק ג׳)

If private minding also binds, then minding is not merely the mark of an objective importance. Both directions are held by the apparatus, and we do not decide between them.

מקצת בני אדם — minding toward stringency and toward leniency. Two directions cross in the siman. Toward stringency, at seif 1: if people are in the habit of minding it, it interposes even for her who does not mind. Toward leniency, at seif 17, in what belongs to a trade.
« צבע שצובעות הנשים על פניהן וידיהן ושער ראשן אינו חוצץ » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:י״ז)
« וכן מי שאומנותו להיות שוחט או קצב וידיו תמיד מלוכלכות בדם אינו חוצץ שרוב בני אומנות זו אינן מקפידים » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:י״ז)

The second sentence is the gloss of the Rama, not the text of the Mehaber. For a tradesman, then, we follow the usage of his fellows in the trade and not that of the world. There is room to examine how the two directions are to be reconciled: one may say that in the case of the trade the thing is not important to him — it is nullified for him as for his fellows — whereas a thing that people at large mind is important in itself, and her private lack of minding does not undo that importance. This reconciliation is a line of study; the text itself does not state it.

Nafka mina:
  1. A thing that most people mind → interposes even for her who does not mind it at all.
  2. A tradesman — shochet, butcher, dyer — for whom the thing is not important, nor for his fellows → it does not interpose (seif 17).
  3. A thing about which it is doubtful whether "people are in the habit of minding it" — today's interposing things: polish, dye, makeup → a question for the Rav.
And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher, the balanit.
The Acharonim on the gader of minding. The Taz treats the question at his ס״ק ב׳–ד׳: ס״ק ב׳ on "people are in the habit of minding it at times", ס״ק ג׳ on "since most people mind it", ס״ק ד׳ on the whole matrix. The Shakh answers at his ס״ק א׳–ב׳ — the first brings the Bayit Chadash on the word לפעמים, the second the reverse case. Over the siman as a whole the Shakh has sixty-one entries spread over thirty-nine of the forty-eight seifim, the Taz forty over twenty-six seifim, and the Pitchei Teshuvah twenty-nine over nineteen seifim. The Sidrei Taharah, the Chokhmat Adam (הלכות נדה) and the Arukh HaShulchan likewise treat general and private minding and the distinctions of trade; their text is not reproduced here. And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher.

3. The hair — threads, braiding, and the married woman

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

The text of the Mehaber and the Rama (seifim 2 to 6)

« חוטי צמר וחוטי פשתן ורצועות שכורכין בהם השער בראש לא תטבול בהם עד שתרפם ואם הם בתוך קליעת שערה אינו מועיל בהם רפיון » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ב)

« אם החוטין האלו חלולין עשוי מעשה רשת אינם חוצצין » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ג)

« חוטי שער אינם חוצצין » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ד)

« ואם היו מוזהבות חוצצין דמקפדת עליהם שלא תטנפם וכן אם היו מטונפים תחלה מקפדת עליהם שלא תתלכלך מהן במים וחוצצין » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ד — הגהת הרמ״א)

« שתי שערות או יותר שהיו קשורים כאחד קשר אחד אינם חוצצין » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ה)

« ושערה אחת שנקשרה חוצצת והוא שתהא מקפדת עליה » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ה)

« שיער שכנגד הלב ושבזקן הנדבק זה בזה מחמת זיעה חוצץ שבראש ושבבית השחי אינו חוצץ ושבאותו מקום באיש אינו חוצץ ובאשה בנשואה חוצץ בפנויה אינו חוצץ » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ו)

« ואותן שיש להן כמין קליעות שערות דבוקות זו בזו ונעשית בלילה על ידי שד וסכנה להסירם לא חייצי » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ו — הגהת הרמ״א)

Translation. Seif 2 — woollen threads, linen threads and straps with which the hair of the head is bound: she shall not immerse with them until she loosens them; and if they are inside the braid of her hair, loosening is of no avail. Seif 3 — if these threads are hollow, made in the fashion of a net, they do not interpose. Seif 4 — threads of hair do not interpose; gloss of the Rama: if they were gilded they interpose, for she minds not soiling them; likewise if they were soiled beforehand, she minds being dirtied by them in the water, and they interpose. Seif 5 — two hairs or more tied together in a single knot do not interpose; a single hair that was knotted interposes, provided she minds it. Seif 6 — the hair facing the heart and that of the beard, stuck together by sweat, interpose; that of the head and of the armpit do not interpose; and that of that place: in a man it does not interpose, and in a woman — in a married woman it interposes, in an unmarried woman it does not. Gloss of the Rama: those who have a kind of braid of hairs stuck to one another, formed at night, which it is dangerous to remove — these do not interpose.

The central chakirah — hair: part of the body, or a separate thing? The law of כל גופה בפעם אחת raises the question: is hair included in the body that must be immersed, or is it only an adjunct? The Shulchan Arukh itself gives a foothold at the end of seif 5, measuring the matter by the greater part of her hair:
« עד שיהא רוב שערה קשור נימא נימא בפני עצמו » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ה)

It emerges from the seifim that hair is adjunct to the body and yet counts for chatsitsa: on one side, what is wound into the hair — the threads — interposes, since it keeps the water from the hair; on the other, the hair itself, when stuck together, is held to be "interposing" in the places where it is minded. And the Taz, when he explains why the nail itself does not interpose, says it in a word by tying the nail to the hair:

« לפי שהוא מגוף האשה ודומה לשערותיה » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק כ׳)

An important consequence follows for the קליעה of seif 2: in a braid, loosening is of no avail, because the braided hair is gathered and the threads are buried within it, so that the water does not reach the place of the threads even if they are loosened — unlike loose hair.

Hair stuck together by sweat — the matrix of places (seif 6).
The placeThe lawThe reason
Facing the heart, and in the beard Interposes An exposed place — one is in the habit of keeping it clean
On the head and at the armpit Does not interpose A covered place — one is not in the habit of minding it
That place — in a man Does not interpose He is not in the habit of minding it
That place — in a married woman Interposes She minds it on account of her husband
That place — in an unmarried woman Does not interpose She has no one for whom to mind it
Married and unmarried — a minding that depends on the woman's state. Seif 6 carries a considerable novelty: the very same thing — the hair of that place, stuck together — interposes in a married woman and does not interpose in an unmarried one. It is the clearest application of the principle that minding determines: the married woman minds on account of her husband, and the thing is therefore important to her; the unmarried woman does not mind, and the thing is not important to her. The Taz says so in plain words.
« לפי שהיא מקפדת שלא תתגנה על בעלה » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק י׳)

It would seem that there is here a "private" side to minding, against the rule of seif 1 which follows most people. The reconciliation is given in the same entry, but it is not the Taz's own: he reports what מו״ח — his father-in-law, the author of the Bayit Chadash — corrected, namely that even a married woman whose husband does not mind the thing, it interposes, because we follow the usage of most married women.

« דאזלינן בתר רוב נשואות » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק י׳)

Here too, then, we follow the usage of the many — except that the many divide into two groups, the married and the unmarried. This is the case of "some people" defined by state and not by individual. The Taz further reports in the same entry that the Beit Yosef had written that the Tur need not have stated the law of the unmarried woman, there being no nafka mina in it for us.

Rishonim and Acharonim in the laws of hair. At seif 5, the Rama's gloss on the two knotted hairs is brought by the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba and the Ran — two authorities named side by side, not one citing the other. At seif 6, the Rama's gloss on braids formed at night comes from the Mordekhai (הלכות נדה) in the name of the Ravyah, the Agur and the Hagahot Shaarei Dura. The Taz follows the seifim of hair from his ס״ק ה׳ to his ס״ק י׳ — ס״ק ה׳ on the woollen threads of seif 2, ס״ק ו׳ and ז׳ on threads of hair and gilded threads at seif 4, ס״ק ח׳ and ט׳ on the knotted hairs of seif 5, ס״ק י׳ on the married woman of seif 6. The Shakh answers at his ס״ק ג׳ to י״א. The Pitchei Teshuvah has two entries on seif 6. And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher.

4. Chatsitsa in the laws of the body — rheum, blood, plaster, nail and mud

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

The text of the Mehaber (seifim 7 to 23, selections)

« לפלוף … שחוץ לעין חוצץ אפי׳ הוא לח ולפלוף שבעין אינו חוצץ ואם הוא יבש חוצץ והוא שהתחיל להוריק » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ז)

« דם יבש שעל המכה חוצץ וריר שבתוכה אינו חוצץ » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:ט)

« רטיה שעל המכה חוצצת » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:י)

« צואה שתחת הצפורן שלא כנגד הבשר חוצץ כנגד הבשר אינו חוצץ ובצק שתחת הצפורן אפילו כנגד הבשר חוצץ » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:י״ח)

« ולפי שאינן יכולות לכוין מה נקרא כנגד הבשר או שלא כנגדו נהגו הנשים ליטול צפרניהם בשעת טבילה » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:י״ח)

« אבל הצפורן עצמה אינה חוצצת » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:כ)

« השירים והנזמים והטבעות והקטלאות אם הם רפויים אינם חוצצים ואם הם מהודקים חוצצים » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:כ״ג)

Translation. Seif 7 — rheum outside the eye interposes, even when moist; that which is in the eye does not interpose; and if it is dry it interposes — that is, once it has begun to turn green. Seif 9 — dried blood upon a wound interposes, and the discharge within it does not. Seif 10 — a plaster upon the wound interposes. Seif 18 — filth under the nail, at the part not facing the flesh, interposes; facing the flesh it does not interpose; and dough under the nail interposes even facing the flesh. And since they cannot judge what is called "facing the flesh" and what is not, women have taken up the practice of cutting their nails at the time of immersion. Seif 20 — the nail itself does not interpose. Seif 23 — bracelets, nose-rings, finger-rings and neck-chains: if they are loose they do not interpose, and if they are tight they interpose.

The chakirah — "dry interposes and moist does not": the gader of what dissolves. A second thread runs through the seifim: a לח thing — moist — does not interpose, and a יבש thing — dry — does. The reason is that so long as it is moist it dissolves in the water and is nullified in the course of the immersion; but once dried it is a thing standing on its own, and it interposes. This is a further gader in chatsitsa: not only minding and quantity, but the consistency of the thing — what is bound to dissolve in the water does not "interpose", since the water reaches the flesh. The Mehaber states it at seif 14:
« ושאר כל הטיט כשהוא לח אינו חוצץ שהרי הוא נמחה במים וכשהוא יבש חוצץ » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:י״ד)

And yet the Rama, in the same seif, sets the limit: minding prevails even over moistness.

« מיהו אם היא מקפדת אפילו בדבר לח חוצץ » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:י״ד — הגהת הרמ״א)
"Not facing the flesh", the nail, and the practice of cutting the nails. The Mehaber distinguishes at seif 18 between filth under the nail facing the flesh — which does not interpose, not being a place that is minded, and being nullified to the flesh — and that which is not facing the flesh, which interposes because it is an exposed place. But since they cannot judge what is "facing the flesh", they took up the practice of cutting the nails at the time of immersion, to remove the doubt at its root. From this, at seif 20, the Rama's gloss draws a weighty consequence.
« ומאחר דכבר נהגו ליטול הצפרנים אפילו אם צפורן אחת נשאר בידה וטבלה צריכה טבילה אחרת » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:כ — הגהת הרמ״א)

And there is room to ask: the nail itself does not interpose, as the beginning of that same seif says. One might say that once the practice of cutting them is established, a nail that was not cut becomes a thing she minds — since her practice is to cut — and we are back at "people are in the habit of minding it". The practice would thus generate the minding. But that is not the only reading, and the Shakh does not follow it: he notes that this stringency is found in no posek, that the reason given by the Hagahot Shaarei Dura is that it is impossible that no mud remain beneath, and he further brings what he found in the ראב״ד — we keep the siglum as the Shakh prints it and do not expand it; he refers to siman שכ״ו and a folio, where the Rama's gloss at seif 43 refers for its part to the ראב״ן with the same siman and folio reference — for whom the nail itself interposes once it is due to be cut.

« באמת חומרא זו לא נמצאת בשום פוסק ואדרבה מהמשנה והרבה פוסקים וט״ו נראה מבואר דכל שהצפורן נקי בודאי אינו חוצץ » (ש״ך יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק כ״ה)
« אלא ההגהת ש״ד כתב דטוב להחמיר ותטבול שנית » (ש״ך יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק כ״ה)

The Taz goes the same way on the starting point, recalling that the taking of the nails is but a practice and not a law:

« דהא כל הפוסקים כתבו כאן דאינו אלא מנהג בעלמא ליטול הצפרנים ואינו מן הדין » (ט״ז יו״ד קצ״ח ס״ק כ״א)

Three explanations thus present themselves for the Rama's stringency: the practice that creates the minding, the impossibility that nothing remain under the nail, and the nail due to be cut which interposes of itself. The Shakh and the Taz discuss further the cases in which a second immersion is not imposed. We report, and we do not decide.

Jewellery — loose or tight (seif 23).
The caseThe lawThe reason
Bracelets, rings, nose-rings, neck-chains — loose Do not interpose The water passes beneath
The same — tight Interpose The water does not pass, and she minds them
In practice The practice is to remove everything a priori Because of the line between loose and tight, and the Rama's decree at seif 1 on things that do not interpose
Nafka mina:
  1. A moist thing, which dissolves in water → does not interpose, unless she minds it (Rama, seif 14).
  2. An uncut nail, the practice of cutting being established → the Rama's gloss at seif 20 requires another immersion; the Shakh and the Taz discuss its reach.
  3. A tight ornament → interposes; and in practice everything is removed a priori.
  4. Today's interposing things — nail polish, artificial nails, contact lenses, dental work → each one is a question for the Rav.
And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher, the balanit.
The Acharonim in the laws of the body. The Taz treats rheum of the eye at his ס״ק י״א, filth under the nail at his ס״ק י״ח and י״ט, the nail itself at his ס״ק כ׳, and the Rama's gloss of seif 20 at his long ס״ק כ״א. The Shakh answers at his ס״ק כ״ב to כ״ה. The Pitchei Teshuvah has his entry ט׳ on seif 18 and י׳–י״א on seif 20. The Sidrei Taharah, the Chokhmat Adam (הלכות נדה) and the Arukh HaShulchan arranged these rules for practice; their text is not reproduced here. And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher.

5. בית הסתרים, the order of the immersion, and the balanit

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

The text of the Mehaber and the Rama (seifim 24 to 43, selections)

« צריכה לחצוץ שיניה שלא יהא בהם דבר חוצץ » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:כ״ד)

« ויש נוהגות שלא לאכול בשר ביום לכתן לבית הטבילה » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:כ״ד)

« ואין לה לאכול בין הרחיצה לטבילה » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:כ״ד — הגהת הרמ״א)

« אם לא הדיחה בית הסתרים ובית הקמטים שלה ונמצא בהם דבר חוצץ לא עלתה לה טבילה » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:כ״ה)

« נתנה שערה בפיה או קרצה שפתותיה או קפצה ידה בענין שלא באו המים בהם לא עלתה לה טבילה » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:כ״ז)

« צריך להעמיד על גבה יהודית גדולה יותר מי״ב שנה ויום א׳ בשעה שהיא טובלת שתראה שלא ישאר משער ראשה צף על פני המים » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:מ)

« אספלנית מלוגמא ורטיה שעל בית הסתרים חוצצין » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:מ״ג)

Translation. Seif 24 — she must clean her teeth, that there be between them nothing that interposes; and some have the practice not to eat meat on the day they go to the immersion house. Gloss of the Rama: she should not eat between the washing and the immersion. Seif 25 — if she did not rinse the hidden folds and the creases of her body and an interposing thing was found in them, her immersion did not count. Seif 27 — if she put her hair in her mouth, or pressed her lips together, or closed her fist in such a way that the water did not come into them, her immersion did not count. Seif 40 — an adult Jewish woman, over twelve years and one day, must be set over her at the hour she immerses, that she may see that nothing of the hair of her head remains floating on the surface of the water. Seif 43 — a poultice, a compress and a plaster upon the hidden folds interpose.

The chakirah — the hidden folds: the coming of water, or fitness for the coming of water? A great distinction is at play here: for בית הסתרים — the creases, the armpit and the like — it is not required that the water come there in fact, but only that the place be fit for the coming of water, that is, that nothing interposing be found there. The Mehaber says so in plain words in the continuation of seif 43:
« אף על פי שאינם צריכים שיכנסו בהם המים צריכי׳ שיהיו ראוים ולא יהא בהם דבר חוצץ » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:מ״ג)

There is therefore a fundamental distinction between the exposed body, which requires actual contact with the water — hence the invalidation of seif 27, the hair in the mouth or the closed fist — and the hidden folds, where fitness suffices. And the reason for the distinction: in the hidden folds water has by nature no way of coming, and so fitness was deemed enough; but an exposed place requires the very contact of the water.

"One must be set over her" — the balanit and the floating hair (seif 40). The Mehaber requires that an adult woman — over twelve years and one day — stand over her at the hour of immersion, to see that nothing of the hair of her head remains floating on the surface of the water. This is the root of the office of the balanit: floating hair is a chatsitsa, since her whole body — and the hair is part of it — is not in the water. And if there is no one to stand over her, or if it is night, she shall bind her hair with a woollen thread or a strap, provided she loosens them, or with a hollow chain of threads, or she shall tie a loose cloth over her hair — all of which the seif itself enumerates. The law of seif 1, "her whole body at one time", thus generates the entire order of the balanit: the check that no hair floats and that nothing interposes is the practical fulfilment of the siman's opening.
The Acharonim in the order of the immersion. The Rama's gloss at seif 24 — that she not eat between the washing and the immersion — is given in the name of the Rosh, in his laws of niddah; the continuation of the same gloss, on dough and wax candles, comes from the Hagahot Shaarei Dura. The Rama's gloss at seif 43 comes from the Hagahot Shaarei Dura and the Raavan. The Taz treats seif 24 at his ס״ק כ״ד and כ״ה, and the Shakh at his ס״ק ל׳ and ל״א; seif 25 has the Shakh ס״ק ל״ב, seif 27 the Shakh ס״ק ל״ד, seif 40 the Shakh ס״ק נ״ב and the Pitchei Teshuvah ס״ק כ״ב, seif 43 the Shakh ס״ק נ״ה. The Sidrei Taharah, the Chokhmat Adam (הלכות נדה) and the Arukh HaShulchan arranged the whole order of chafifah and immersion for practice; their text is not reproduced here. And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher, the balanit.

6. Immersion performed without intent — is intent required?

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)

The text of the Mehaber and the Rama (seif 48)

« נדה שטבלה בלא כוונה כגון שנפלה לתוך המים או שירדה להקר הרי זו מותרת לבעלה » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:מ״ח)

« ויש מחמירין ומצריכין אותה טבילה אחרת … ויש להחמיר לכתחלה » (שו״ע יו״ד קצ״ח:מ״ח — הגהת הרמ״א)

Translation. A niddah who immersed without intent — for example that she fell into the water, or that she went down to cool off — she is permitted to her husband. Gloss of the Rama: and there are those who are stringent and require another immersion of her (the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba, Rabbenu Yerucham, the Rokeach and the Hagahot Asheri); and one should be stringent a priori.

The foundation — "does immersion require intent": a doubt among the masters.
A woman who immersed without intent of the mitzvah — having fallen into the water, or gone down to cool off — did her immersion count?

• The Mehaber: she is permitted to her husband — immersion does not require intent, provided her whole body entered the water as it should.
• Those who are stringent — the Rashba, Rabbenu Yerucham, the Rokeach and the Hagahot Asheri, brought by the Beit Yosef — hold that it does require intent, and that another immersion is therefore needed.
• The Rama: one should be stringent a priori only; after the fact the immersion counted.
The chakirah — the root of the machloket on intent in immersion. This is a branch of the question whether mitzvot require intent; but immersion has here a side of its own. Two sides:
  • The side of the Mehaber — immersion is not an "act of mitzvah" but the removal of a state of impurity: once she entered the water and the impurity was in fact removed, she is pure, whether she intended it or not — like the immersion of vessels, which requires no intent.
  • The side of those who are stringent — in the immersion of a person there is an act of the person: it is she who effects her own purification, and intent of the act is therefore required; on this view, she who falls into the water has not "immersed" but has been immersed under duress.
The Rama decides this doubt among the masters stringently a priori — another immersion — and leniently after the fact.
שנפלה לתוך המים against שירדה להקר — two ways of lacking intent. The Mehaber took care to give two pictures: she who falls — a complete absence of intent, a case of duress — and she who goes down to cool off — where there is an intent of act, but not an intent of mitzvah. And there is fineness in this: for the Mehaber, even she who fell has counted, since no intent at all is required; and for those who are stringent, even she who went down to cool off has not counted, since there is there no intent of immersion but an intent of cooling off. It remains to be asked whether the two pictures are to be separated according to the Acharonim — whether she who goes down to cool off, having an intent of act and having immersed her whole body, is lighter than she who falls. The Mehaber does not separate them, nor does the Rama; this page therefore does not decide the question, and leaves it open as the text leaves it.
Nafka mina:
  1. After the fact — having fallen, or gone down to cool off, her whole body having been immersed → for the Mehaber the immersion counted and she is permitted to her husband.
  2. A priori — the Rama: one should be stringent and immerse another time, with intent.
  3. She who immersed and is in doubt whether she intended, or who immersed to cool off → a question for the Rav, whether to immerse again.
And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher, the balanit.
Rishonim and Acharonim on intent in immersion. "Those who are stringent" are the Rashba (תורת הבית), Rabbenu Yerucham, the Rokeach and the Hagahot Asheri, whom the Beit Yosef brings — the Rama names them himself in parentheses within his gloss. The Taz treats the seif at his ס״ק מ׳, the Shakh at his ס״ק נ״ט to ס״א, and the Pitchei Teshuvah at his ס״ק כ״ו to כ״ט, that is four entries, which is the largest number of Pitchei Teshuvah entries on any single seif of this siman. And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher.

7. Chakirah and nafka mina for practice

↩ Detailed explanation (Level 1)
The chakirah that embraces the whole siman: what is the gader of chatsitsa, and how is it determined what interposes and what does not?
  • Her whole body at one time — the foundation; hence that an interposing thing, even the least, invalidates, and that floating hair interposes (seifim 1 and 40).
  • The matrix of quantity and minding — the greater part with minding, from the Torah; the greater part without minding and the lesser part with minding, rabbinically, each on account of the first; the lesser part without minding does not interpose (seif 1, per the Taz ס״ק ד׳).
  • The habit of people to mind — general minding binds her who does not mind; the tradesman follows his fellows; and the Taz limits the rule to most people (seifim 1 and 17, Taz ס״ק ב׳, Shakh ס״ק ב׳).
  • The hair — adjunct to the body yet counted for chatsitsa; the braid, the married and the unmarried woman, and "that place" (seifim 2 to 6).
  • Dry and moist, the hidden folds — the moist dissolves and does not interpose, unless she minds it; for the hidden folds, fitness for the coming of water suffices (seifim 14 and 43).
  • Immersion without intent — it does not require intent for the Mehaber; there are those who are stringent, and the Rama imposes stringency a priori (seif 48).
Nafka mina for practice — as the seifim set them down:
  1. She must immerse her whole body at one time and remove every interposing thing, even the least (Mehaber, seif 1).
  2. A thing that most people mind interposes even for her who does not mind it; and the tradesman follows the usage of his fellows (Mehaber and Rama, seifim 1 and 17).
  3. Threads in the hair: to be loosened; and in a braid, loosening is of no avail (Mehaber, seif 2). The hair of "that place", in a married woman, interposes (Mehaber, seif 6).
  4. The practice is taken up of cutting the nails; and if one is left, the Rama's gloss requires another immersion — the Shakh and the Taz discuss its reach (Mehaber and Rama, seifim 18 and 20).
  5. A tight ornament interposes; and in practice everything is removed a priori (Mehaber, seif 23).
  6. An adult woman must be set over her, to see that no hair floats (Mehaber, seif 40).
  7. Immersed without intent — having fallen, or gone down to cool off: she is permitted to her husband; and a priori she should immerse a second time (Mehaber and Rama, seif 48).
And in practice — ask your Rav, your kallah teacher, the balanit.
