1. The axiom: the חזקת כשרות and the one who loses it

The starting point:

Siman 119 does not ask what is forbidden, but whom one may rely upon. The baseline presumption is that every Jew stands בחזקת כשרות — the Shach puts it plainly, summary: apart from the ḥashud, every person is presumed kosher enough to eat with him (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק א). The siman deals with the person for whom that presumption has fallen — the ḥashud (חשוד) — and measures exactly how far the fall reaches: what the suspicion covers, what remains permitted, and what it costs. The Mehaber opens (seif 1): «החשוד לאכול דברים האסורים» — whether he is suspected on a Torah prohibition or on a rabbinic one — «אין לסמוך עליו בהם».
Axis of the simanThe question askedSeifim
חזקת כשרותIs he truly ḥashud, or merely unknown?1 (Mehaber and Rama)
היקף החשד (the perimeter)Does the suspicion spread to other prohibitions, to other people?4 to 8
חשש חילוף (the swap)Do we fear that he swaps, or only that he sells?2, 3, 19, 20
Special statusesA convert who reverted out of fear, a מסור, a מומר, the אנוסים9 to 12
ממון וקנסWhat does the seller of forbidden food return, and how does he come back?13 to 18
💡 The marker: this is not a siman about mixtures, nor about seals — it is the siman of the trustworthiness of people. Three words sum it up: חזקה (what we presume), חשד (what makes the presumption fall), חילוף (what we concretely fear). Everything else follows from them.

2. The 3 reflex questions

■ IS HE ḤASHUD, OR MERELY UNKNOWN? — an ordinary Jew keeps his חזקת כשרות; only one known to be ḥashud loses it (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק א). The Rama (seif 1) nonetheless brings a וי״א: even from one who is simply not known as מוחזק בכשרות, «אסור לקנות ממנו יין או שאר דברי׳ שיש לחוש לאיסור»; and the Taz (ס״ק ב) upholds that stringency, «ולא אכשר דרי».
↓ and if he is ḥashud —
■ ABOUT WHAT, AND FOR WHOM? — regarding his own food we do not rely on him in the matters he is suspected of (seif 1), and not even under oath (seif 8). But regarding another's food he remains נאמן, even about that very prohibition, to say that it is permitted (seif 7). And if he is suspected only of selling, not of eating: «מתארח אצלו ואוכל עמו» (seif 2).
↓ and in practice —
■ DO WE FEAR A SWAP (חילוף)? — not by default: one may hand him one's own food to cook, «כיון שאינו חשוד על הגזל» (seif 3). We fear it in three cases: one who has a stake in my welfare (the mother-in-law, the innkeeper — seif 3), one who is חשוד על הגזל (seif 19), and one who is suspected on things the public is not accustomed to be lenient about (seif 20).

⚖ The basic grid (seifim 1-2)

Suspected of eating forbidden things — Torah or rabbinic, the distinction changes nothing here — we do not rely on him for those things, and as a guest in his home one does not eat of his own food in the matters he is suspected of (seif 1). Suspected only of selling — one may lodge with him and eat with him, and what he sends to his own home is permitted: «דחזקה שממה שהוא אוכל משגר לו» (seif 2). The Shach gives the reasoning in the name of Rashi and the Ran: he is careless about לפני עור, but he himself does not eat forbidden food (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק ה). Rama: «עובר עבירה לתיאבון לא מקרי חשוד» — and the Shach specifies that he is ḥashud only if he transgresses שלא לתיאבון, that is, if the forbidden and the permitted were set before him and he would choose the forbidden (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק ו).

📌 The key reading: the suspicion is targeted, not contagious. It spreads downward (to the lighter prohibition, seif 5) and to the means (whatever serves that prohibition, seif 4), never sideways to unrelated prohibitions (seif 4). And it never touches his word about another person's food (seif 7) — only about his own, where he has something to gain.

4. Special statuses: convert, מסור, מומר, אנוסים (seifim 9-12)

Four short seifim settling questions of personal status: is this person still a Jew one may rely on for שחיטה and for wine?

CaseThe Mehaber's rulingScope
A convert who returned to his former religion out of fear; a Jew who sinned for fear of being killed (seif 9) «ישראל גמור הוא ושחיטתו מותרת ואינו אוסר יין במגעו» Coercion does not impair his standing as a Jew
A מסור (informer) — seif 10 «מסור שחיטתו כשרה ונאמן על האיסורים» The Mehaber himself refers to siman 2, where some invalidate a מסור's שחיטה; the Shach adds that although he is disqualified from writing a ספר תורה he is still trusted on prohibitions (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק כ״ג)
A מומר who professes עבודת כוכבים in one city before non-Jews and calls himself a Jew in another (seif 11) «אינו עושה יין נסך» The reasoning: before non-Jews he speaks for his יצר, among Jews he speaks wholeheartedly — but the Shach stresses that he is not thereby trusted on prohibitions (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק כ״ד; ט״ז יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק י״א)
The אנוסים who remained in their lands (seif 12) Two conditions: they conduct themselves in kashrut among themselves, and it is not in their power to escape → «סומכי׳ על שחיטתן ואין אוסרין יין במגען» The criterion is actual private conduct, not public appearance
💡 The common thread: these four seifim separate what a person is from what he displays. The coerced Jew remains ישראל גמור (seif 9); the מומר of circumstance is still a Jew as regards wine (seif 11) but not as regards נאמנות; and the אנוסים are judged by their private practice (seif 12).

5. The seller of forbidden food: money, removal, repentance (seifim 13-18)

Here the siman moves from the ground of נאמנות to that of ממון and of communal sanction. Three distinct questions: what is returned? who is removed? how does one come back?

6. The 6 golden rules

  1. The חזקה first, the חשד second. Every Jew is presumed kosher; only one known to be ḥashud loses that presumption (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק א). The Rama adds a stringency about buying from an unknown person, and the Taz upholds it (Rama seif 1; ט״ז יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק ב).
  2. Eating is not selling. Suspected of eating → we do not rely on him (seif 1); suspected only of selling → «מתארח אצלו ואוכל עמו» (seif 2). And one who transgresses לתיאבון is not called ḥashud (Rama seif 2).
  3. The suspicion is targeted. «החשוד לדבר אחד אינו חשוד לדברים אחרים»; it extends only to what serves that prohibition (seif 4) and to the prohibition lighter in punishment — unless the public takes the latter more seriously (seif 5).
  4. His own is not another's. On his own he is not believed, not even בשבועה (seif 8); on another's he is believed, even about that very prohibition (seif 7). The Shach notes that in matters of ממון the rule reverses: there, one suspected on one thing is disqualified as a witness even on another (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק י״ח, referring to חושן משפט סימן ל״ד).
  5. A swap is feared only where there is a motive. The ḥashud is not suspected of theft, so one entrusts him with one's food (seif 3) — unless he has a stake in my good (seif 3), or he is חשוד על הגזל (seif 19), or he is suspected on what the public is not lenient about (seif 20).
  6. The seller's repentance is paid in money. Going where he is unknown, returning a valuable lost object or declaring his own animal טריפה: the proof of repentance is that «אינו חס על ממונו» (seif 15).

7. Mnemonic — the memory aid חשוד

The four letters of the word חשוד
  • ח — חזקה: every Jew is בחזקת כשרות; only the established ḥashud loses it (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק א).
  • ש — שאר דברים: «החשוד לדבר אחד אינו חשוד לדברים אחרים» — except what serves that prohibition (seif 4) and the lighter one (seif 5).
  • ו — ובשל אחרים: on another's food he remains נאמן, even about that prohibition (seif 7); on his own, not — not even under oath (seif 8).
  • ד — דמים: the seller of forbidden food returns the money (seif 13), and his repentance is measured by what it costs him (seif 15).
The ladder of the חילוף — when is a swap really feared?
  • אינו חשוד על הגזל → one entrusts him with one's own food to cook (seif 3)
  • יש לו טובה בדבר — mother-in-law, innkeeper → forbidden: she swaps bad for good (seif 3)
  • מאיש פלוני מומחה קניתי → believed, since he fears they will ask that מומחה (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק ל״ה)
  • חשוד על הגזל → «כ״ש שהוא חשוד על החליפין» (seif 19)
  • שאין הרבים רגילים להקל → חשוד להחליף, and וי״א: a deposit only «בשני חותמות» (seif 20)

8. The 5 classic pitfalls

❌ Pitfall 1 — Treating every stranger as a ḥashud: the baseline remains the חזקת כשרות (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק א). What the Rama adds (seif 1) is not that the stranger is suspected, but that one does not buy from him wine or goods that raise concern — and he himself concludes: «מיהו אם נתארח אצלו אוכל עמו». The Taz (ס״ק ב) upholds the stringency in view of the state of the generations; the Shach maintains the חזקה.
❌ Pitfall 2 — Confusing “suspected of eating” with “suspected of selling”: these are two distinct statuses. One who sells without minding לפני עור yet eats no forbidden food himself → we eat at his table (seif 2; ט״ז יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק ג). Likewise one who transgresses לתיאבון: he is ḥashud only if he would choose the forbidden while the permitted lies before him (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק ו).
❌ Pitfall 3 — Extending the suspicion to everything: «החשוד לדבר אחד אינו חשוד לדברים אחרים» (seif 4). The extension runs in two directions only: toward the means (the butcher's nuts — «קנסיה רבא לזבוני אפילו אמגוזי», ט״ז יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק ז, from בכורות פרק עד כמה דף ל׳) and toward the lighter prohibition (seif 5) — which the Beit Yossef, as cited by the Taz (ס״ק ח), further limits to one and the same מין.
❌ Pitfall 4 — Forgetting “his own” versus “another's”: on another's food he is נאמן even about the prohibition he is suspected of, to say that it is permitted (seif 7); on his own he is not, even בשבועה (seif 8), «שכבר חשוד הוא לעבור על השבועה שמושבע ועומד הוא מהר סיני» (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק כ״א). And note: this leniency holds for איסור only — for ממון the Shach recalls the opposite rule (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק י״ח).
❌ Pitfall 5 — Thinking that removal entails payment: the butcher whose acts betray his intent is removed, and yet «ומכל מקום פטור מלשלם דמי הבהמה לבעלים» (seif 16): the animal is only a ספק טריפה. Conversely, the seller of forbidden food returns the money (seif 13). Sanction and restitution follow two different logics.

For halakha lema'asseh, consult your Rav.

9. Recap of the 20 seifim — one line each

SeifTopicThe essential
1The ḥashud: definition and effectOne suspected of eating forbidden things — whether a Torah prohibition or a rabbinic one — is not relied upon in those things; as his guest, one does not eat of his own food in the matters he is suspected of. Rama: וי״א that even from one not known as מוחזק בכשרות one does not buy wine or anything that raises concern; however if he hosts me, I eat with him.
2Suspected of selling, not of eatingHe is not suspected of eating forbidden things but of selling them → one lodges with him and eats with him; and what he sends to his home is permitted, for there is a חזקה that he sends of what he himself eats. Rama: «עובר עבירה לתיאבון לא מקרי חשוד».
3Entrusting him with one's own foodIt is permitted to give him one's own food to prepare or cook, without fearing a swap, «כיון שאינו חשוד על הגזל». But giving to one who cares about the giver's welfare and is himself ḥashud is forbidden, lest what he receives spoil and he swap it with his own: the mother-in-law (ashamed before her son-in-law, wanting her daughter's good) and the innkeeper (ashamed before her guest). Rama: a plain deposit, to be returned as it was, remains permitted.
4The suspicion is targeted — yet it catches the means«החשוד לדבר אחד אינו חשוד לדברים אחרים»; but whatever is needed for that thing he is suspected of too: one who was suspected of selling חלב in place of שומן and used to draw the boys to buy from him with nuts — he is penalized so as not to sell even nuts.
5From the grave to the lightOne suspected on a grave prohibition is suspected on the one lighter than it in punishment — unless that one is grave in people's eyes, who guard against it more than against the grave one.
6Relapse after repentanceOne suspected on two things who repented and was cleared of both suspicions, and was afterwards suspected on one of them — even the lighter of the two — we fear he has returned to his ways in both, and he is suspected on both.
7The notorious transgressor: what he loses and what he keepsOne notorious in one of the transgressions of the Torah — other than עבודת כוכבים and public Shabbat desecration — or who does not believe in the words of our Sages: he remains נאמן on the other prohibitions, and regarding another's food he is נאמן even about that very prohibition to say it is permitted. Rama: one suspected in a matter people do not perceive as a transgression is not called ḥashud — however on that very thing he is not trusted; and one who keeps some stringency, whether from his own ruling or a חומרא, may eat with others who permit it, for they will surely not feed him what he treats as forbidden.
8Even under oath«החשוד על הדבר אינו נאמן עליו אפילו בשבועה» — for he is already מושבע ועומד מהר סיני (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק כ״א; ט״ז יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק י׳).
9Sinning under coercionA convert from the non-Jews who reverted out of fear, and likewise a Jew who sinned for fear of being killed: «ישראל גמור הוא ושחיטתו מותרת ואינו אוסר יין במגעו».
10The מסור«מסור שחיטתו כשרה ונאמן על האיסורים» — and the Mehaber himself refers to siman 2, where some invalidate a מסור's שחיטה.
11The מומר of circumstanceOne who in one city professes עבודת כוכבים before non-Jews, and in another enters a Jew's house saying he is a Jew: «אינו עושה יין נסך». The Shach stresses that he is not thereby trusted on prohibitions (ש״ך יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק כ״ד).
12The אנוסיםThe אנוסים who remained in their lands: if they conduct themselves in kashrut among themselves and it is not in their power to escape to a place where they could serve God — «סומכי׳ על שחיטתן ואין אוסרין יין במגען».
13Selling forbidden food: the restitutionKnown before he ate → he returns the purchase and the seller returns the money; known after he ate → «מה שאכל אכל והוא יחזיר לו הדמים»; if the buyer resold it to a non-Jew or threw it to the dogs → «ישלם לו דמי טריפה». A prohibition only מדברי סופרים, or produce still intact → he returns the produce and takes back his money; if he ate it, the seller returns nothing. And all איסורי הנאה, even מדברי סופרים → the money is returned, «ואין בו דין מכירה כלל».
14Improperly checked meatOne who sold meat and it was afterwards known that the animal had not been properly checked: «דינו כמוכר דברים שאסורים מדברי סופרים». Rama: but an animal whose חזקת כשרות was undermined by a ריעותא that can no longer be checked, and which must be forbidden out of doubt — it is as though it were certainly טריפה, and the money must be returned (תשובת ריב״ש).
15The seller's sanction«המוכר דברים האסורים מעבירים אותו ומשמתים אותו»; no תקנה until he goes where he is unknown and returns a lost object of value, or slaughters for himself and declares an animal of his own טריפה, of value — «בלא הערמה כיון שאינו חס על ממונו».
16The ill-intentioned butcherA butcher whose acts prove he wished to make the public stumble by feeding them טריפות — for instance he cut the סירכות — is removed; «ומכל מקום פטור מלשלם דמי הבהמה לבעלים».
17No plea of שוגגA butcher from whose hand a טריפה emerged: «אין לו התנצלות לומר שוגג הייתי».
18Incompetence has a remedyA butcher from whose hands טריפות emerged for lack of expertise: «יש לו תקנה שילמד ויחכם». Rama: the duty to go where he is unknown applies to one who acted deliberately or is habituated to it; if we can say it was done in error, it suffices to accept «בקבלת דברי חבירות» and to repent as the judge sees fit (תשובת הרא״ש ותשובת הריב״ש), see the end of siman 64.
19An errand entrusted to a ḥashudOne who tells a man suspected of eating a non-Jew's cheese: buy me kosher cheese from the מומחה — and he returns saying “I bought kosher from the מומחה” → he is not believed; «מאיש פלוני מומחה קניתי נאמן». If he brought him a gift in the name of one of the מומחין → he is believed, «שאינו חשוד להחליף». All this when he is not חשוד על הגזל; and if he is, «כ״ש שהוא חשוד על החליפין». The Rama adds that none of this applies to one suspected on a Torah prohibition — which the Taz discusses (ט״ז יו״ד קי״ט ס״ק י״ח).
20The ḥashud on what the public takes seriously«החשוד לאכול דברים שאין הרבים רגילים להקל בהם אף הוא חשוד להחליף»; and וי״א that one deposits with him something forbidden by Torah law only «בשני חותמות» — the Mehaber himself referring to siman 118 seif 8.

⚖ The reflex in 3 questions

  1. חזקה or חשד? As long as he is not known to be ḥashud, his חזקת כשרות stands; the Rama's stringency bears on buying, not on the presumption.
  2. His own or another's? On his own, nothing — not even under oath; on another's, he remains נאמן even about that very prohibition.
  3. Is there a motive to swap? Personal stake, suspicion of theft, or suspicion on what the public is not lenient about → a swap is feared; otherwise not.
For halakha lema'asseh, consult your Rav.
