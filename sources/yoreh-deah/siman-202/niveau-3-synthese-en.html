What one must have in mind before immersing a vessel

1. The siman in one sentence

A vessel’s immersion is valid only if the mikveh water touches all of its surface — hence two obstructions: the substance stuck to it, and the air that stays in it.

3. The two obstructions

First obstruction — the substance (seifim 1 to 5). It is measured: by particularity and by the greater part. It is resolved by cleaning, and the cleaning is done before the immersion.
Second obstruction — the air (seifim 6 to 8). It is not measured: a base the water did not touch is not a lesser part, it is a failed immersion. It is resolved by a motion: tilt, fill, wait.

5. The handle — when it is one body with the vessel

The question to ask is not “is the handle wooden?” but “will it have to come off?”. A handle well inserted and meant to stay is as the vessel itself. A handle that will have to be pulled out and refitted is something other than the vessel, and it interposes.

6. A vessel within a vessel — three conditions

  1. The mouth — the outer vessel’s mouth must be as wide as a skin-flask’s spout. Unless that vessel also needs immersion: then a smaller opening suffices (Shach, ס״ק ד).
  2. The looseness — the inner vessel must be loose, never wedged. That is why one does not drive knives into a pail.
  3. The weight — the inner vessel must not press on the outer one’s base. Where it presses, neither is immersed, and the leniency of the mouth changes nothing (Arukh HaShulchan, seif 13).

8. The vocabulary

חציצה — interposition — whatever comes between the mikveh water and the surface of the object. Two thresholds: what one minds, even over a little; what one does not mind, if it covers the greater part.
מקפיד — one who minds — the measure is not dirt but attention: what one wants gone. The Arukh HaShulchan specifies that the way of the world decides, and so does the individual’s own particularity, in both directions.
רובו ומקפיד — the greater part and particularity — the Torah measure. The sages added two: the greater part without particularity, and the lesser part with it.
בית הסתרים — hidden place — a recess the water does not reach. In vessels the Rosh and Rabbenu Shimshon derive from the tosefta that we do not worry about interposition there at all; the Tosafot on חולין require that it at least be fit for water to enter.
שפופרת הנוד — the spout of a skin-flask — the measure of opening by which one water joins another. The gloss requires it for the outer vessel; the Shach at ס״ק ז specifies that for the vessel itself a smaller opening suffices.
שחרורית — soot — the black of smoke deposited on a cauldron. The gloss calls it “like the wall of the vessel” on the outside; the Taz at ס״ק ג infers that on the inside it interposes if one minds it.
קתא — socket — according to the Taz at ס״ק ה, the hollow metal part, of a piece with the vessel, into which the wooden handle is driven. The handle broken inside that socket is the Tur’s case.

9. The golden rules

  1. The measure of interposition is particularity, not dirt. What one wants gone interposes; what one leaves does not.
  2. The greater part interposes even without particularity. A pot entirely scaled therefore raises a question, even if one does not mind it.
  3. A handle is one body with the vessel so long as it is meant to stay in it. The question is not the material but the permanence.
  4. A temporary repair — string, tape, wire — interposes; a lasting repair — glue, welding — does not.
  5. Air obstructs as much as a substance. One immerses mouth upward, or turns the vessel under the water.
  6. A narrow neck is filled with water before entering the mikveh, or one waits until it fills.
  7. A vessel within another: a sufficient mouth, looseness, and nothing pressing.
  8. What is going to be cut need not be immersed — but nothing prevents immersing it, and the Arukh HaShulchan invites one to.

10. The classic pitfalls

  1. Thinking “clean” is enough. A price label, a residue of glue, a protective pad are clean and interpose.
  2. Removing the label after the immersion. Cleaning comes first; what was stuck on during the immersion invalidated it.
  3. Confusing seif 6 with seif 8. The first is about the vessel’s position, the second about filling it. A narrow-necked vessel held upright still needs filling.
  4. Trusting the width. The Shach writes it expressly: even a mouth wider than the measure exempts nothing if the water does not enter of itself.
  5. Stacking in the basket. Pieces set one on another are not immersed at the point of contact — and weight is not saved by the size of the mouth.
  6. Taking the gloss about soot for a general leniency. It was said of the outside; within, where one is particular, the Taz holds it an interposition.

11. Revision questions

  1. Which seifim treat the substance, and which the air? Why does the distinction change the way one reasons?
  2. What is the Torah measure, and what are the two rabbinic extensions?
  3. Three ways a handle breaks, three verdicts. Which, and why?
  4. Three conditions for immersing a vessel within another. Name them, with the source of each.
  5. A bowl immersed upside down: what do the Beit Yosef, the Bach, the Taz and the Nekudot HaKesef say? And what is done in practice?
  6. The Shach writes that a mouth wider than the measure does not always exempt. What is he speaking of, and why?
