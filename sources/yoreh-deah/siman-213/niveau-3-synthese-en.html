Siman 213 — What a vow does not take hold of (דבר שאין בו ממש)

1. The axiom: a vow forbids a thing, an oath binds a man

The axiom. A vow works by rendering a thing forbidden; it therefore needs a thing. An oath works by binding the man; it needs nothing else. From this comes the whole boundary of the siman, and its reason is given not by the seif but by the Rambam.
« נדרים אין חלין על דבר שאין בו ממש » (שו״ע יו״ד רי״ג:א)
« שֶׁהַנִּשְׁבָּע אוֹסֵר עַצְמוֹ עַל דָּבָר שֶׁנִּשְׁבַּע עָלָיו. וְהַנּוֹדֵר אוֹסֵר הַדָּבָר הַנָּדוּר עַל עַצְמוֹ » (רמב״ם הלכות נדרים פרק ג׳ הלכה ז)
« זֶה חֹמֶר בַּשְּׁבוּעוֹת מִבַּנְּדָרִים » (משנה נדרים ב׳:ב)
Three consequences. (1) Naming an act makes no vow. (2) Naming the limb makes one, from the Torah. (3) What does not take effect still calls for a rabbinic release — because the man believed he had vowed.

2. The three reflex questions

1. Vow or oath? An oath — this siman does not concern it. A vow — next question.
▼
2. What did he name? An act — no vow. A limb or an object — a Torah vow. The act with its object — machloket.
▼
3. And then? If it took effect: an ordinary release. If not: a rabbinic שאלה. If it is a נדר שוא: nothing at all — see siman 236.

5. The open machlokot

Why does “qonam my mouth” take effect? The Rosh: he is treated as though he had said “let my mouth be forbidden for my speech” — requalification. The Ran: the formula is ambiguous, and סתם נדרים להחמיר settles it toward the mouth — interpretation. Beit Yosef · Taz ס״ק ב.
What answers the objection from siman 206? The Tosafot: there is an object there, the food. The Taz: there the object exists at the moment of the vow. The two criteria do not sort the same cases. Shach ס״ק א · Taz ס״ק א.
Does mentioning the object suffice? The Rama reports a י״א holding “the speech of my mouth” to be a vow, and the Shach gives the reason: since he mentioned the חפץ. It is only a י״א, and the gloss does not decide. Rama · Shach ס״ק ו · Ba’er Hetev ס״ק ד.
What is “I shall study this chapter”? The Rosh, followed by the seif: like a vow of charity. The Tur: a full vow, because he vowed a thing of mitzvah. The Ran: an oath. Beit Yosef · Tur · Shach ס״ק ז.

7. Golden rules, mnemonic and classic pitfalls

The golden rules. (1) A vow wants a thing; it is the position of the word that decides, not the intention. (2) What does not take effect is not therefore without sequel. (3) A vow on sleep with no duration does not take effect at all. (4) In a conditional vow, one fears forgetting the condition, never forgetting the prohibition.
שאני מדבר → nothing · פי מדבר → vow · דבור פי → machloket · אין בו ממש → rabbinic שאלה
Pitfall 1 — believing intention suffices. It does not: the seif sets two formulas whose intention is identical and whose result is opposite. Even the Rosh, who invokes intention, makes of it a requalification of the speech, not a substitute for it.
Pitfall 2 — concluding from “it is not a vow” to “I am free.” The seif immediately goes on to ומיהו מדרבנן צריך שאלה, and the Rambam gives the reason: the man believed he had vowed.
Pitfall 3 — forgetting the requirement of a duration. The Shach, at ס״ק ה, sets it before everything else: without a duration the vow on sleep is a נדר שוא, and there is nothing to discuss.
