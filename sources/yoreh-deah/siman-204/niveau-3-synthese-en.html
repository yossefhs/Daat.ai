1. The siman in one sentence

A word creates a prohibition when it attaches (מתפיס) a permitted thing to a thing already forbidden by a man's word — a קרבן, or anything consecrated by the mouth. The attachment propagates: from loaf to loaf, from day to day, from man to man, up to a hundred; and it reaches even what is not yet in the world.

4. The three golden rules

1. It is not holiness that counts, but its origin. One attaches to what a mouth has made forbidden. The firstborn animal, holy from its mother's womb, cannot serve as an anchor — and it is the same reason that will exclude, at siman 205, pork and נבלה.
2. The first link itself becomes an anchor. As soon as a loaf is forbidden by vow, it is a דבר הנדור: another loaf may be attached to it — and one is stopped neither by number (“even a hundred”), nor by delay (Rambam: “even after several days”), nor by kind (Shach ס״ק ד: bread, meat, fruit).
3. Attachment exists only in the נדר. המתפיס בשבועה פטור ובנדרים חייב: one does not attach to an oath. The reason is the one that governs the whole block — the נדר forbids the thing, the שבועה binds the person, and a bond of the person is not copied from one object to another.
« הַמַּתְפִּיס בִּשְׁבוּעָה פָּטוּר וּבִנְדָרִים חַיָּב » (רמב״ם הלכות נדרים פרק ג׳ הלכה א)

5. The classic pitfalls

  • Confusing “holy” with “forbidden by a word”. Jerusalem binds; the firstborn animal does not. The criterion is not holiness but its provenance.
  • Thinking a קרבן must be named. “יאסר עלי ככר זה” names nothing at all, and binds nonetheless (seif א, Shach ס״ק ג).
  • Thinking the chain requires the same kind. It does not (Shach ס״ק ד, Baer Hetev ס״ק ג).
  • Taking the Rama's gloss on the table for a settled rule. The Shach (ס״ק ח) and the Taz (ס״ק ה) both contest it; one does not lean on it to permit.
  • Extending seif ד to another person. One may forbid to oneself a דבר שלא בא לעולם; one may not forbid it to another (Shach ס״ק ט, Pitchei Teshuva ס״ק א).
  • Letting the כדי דיבור pass. The “ואני” of seif ג is valid only within that interval.

7. The revision checklist

  1. I can say in one sentence what התפסה is and why it is the heart of the נדר.
  2. I can distinguish דבר הנדור from דבר האסור, and say what each can and cannot carry.
  3. I can name the five objects of seif ב and say what they have in common.
  4. I can explain why the בכור is excluded, and connect that exclusion to siman 205.
  5. I know the ma'hloket of the שלחן, and that the Shach doubts where the Taz decides.
  6. I know that the chain stops neither at number, nor at delay, nor at kind.
  7. I know what seif ד permits, and to whom it does not extend.
  8. I know that this chapter is for study, and that any real vow is brought before a Rav.
