Siman 188 — The Colors of Blood (Mar'ot Damim): Red and Black Forbidden, White and Green Pure, and Showing the Bedikah to a Rav

Everything depends on color: from the Tora, only five מראות render טמא ; the Sages stringently forbade (lest one confuse one blood with another) any shade tending toward redness, and permitted any shade with no suspicion of redness. Red and black → טמא ; white and green (ירוק) → טהור. Hence the golden rule today: one does not judge oneself, one shows the בדיקה to a מורה הוראה ; the נאמנות, the תליה במכה, and the בדיקת השרייה of a מפלת (Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 188 — 6 se'ifim). A grave matter: referral to the Rav.

דַּם הַנִּדָּה אֵינוֹ מְטַמֵּא אֶלָּא בְּחֲמִשָּׁה מַרְאוֹת מִן הַתּוֹרָה ; וְכָל מַרְאֵה אָדֹם — בֵּין כֵּהֶה בֵּין עָמֹק — טָמֵא, וְכֵן כָּל מַרְאֵה שָׁחֹר ; וְאֵינוֹ טָהוֹר אֶלָּא הַלָּבָן וְהַיָּרֹק, אֲפִלּוּ כְּמַרְאֵה הַשַּׁעֲוָה וְהַזָּהָב, וְכָל שֶׁכֵּן כְּמַרְאֵה הַכַּרְתִּי וְהָעֵשֶׂב — טְהוֹרָה.

Everything depends on color. From the Tora, the blood of a נדה renders טמא only in five sorts of מראות ; but the Sages were stringent. Any red shade — light or dark — is טמא, and likewise any black shade ; only white and green (ירוק) are טהור — even like the color of wax or gold, and all the more like the leek or grass.

Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 188:1 (abridged)

Frequently asked questions — Siman 188

Which colors of blood render טמא, and which are טהור?

From the Tora, only five sorts of מראות render טמא ; but the Sages, lest one confuse one blood with another, stringently forbade any shade tending toward redness and leniently permitted any shade with no suspicion of redness (Shach, sk1). In practice: any red shade (light or dark) and any black shade → טמא ; only white and green (ירוק) are טהור — even like the color of wax or gold, and all the more like the leek or grass (Shulchan Aruch 188:1). The borderline (brown, beige, yellow…) is subtle and is never decided oneself: one shows the בדיקה to your Rav.

Why, today, does one show the bedikah to a Rav rather than judge it oneself?

Because distinguishing the shades of red is a חכמת הראייה that we no longer master with confidence. The Acharonim stress how the color can mislead — depending on day or night, depending on whether the stain is still moist or already dry (Pitchei Teshuva, sk1 and sk3) — and the Shulchan Aruch itself teaches (188:2) that the woman is not believed to rely on herself where there is room to doubt. The golden rule today: a doubtful stain on the בדיקה cloth is never judged alone ; one shows it to a מורה הוראה who examines its color, and it is he who decides.

What are the נאמנות, the תליה במכה, and the בדיקת השרייה of a מפלת?

נאמנות (188:2): the woman is believed to say « כזה ראיתי ואבדתיו » (I saw a shade like this and lost it) if it is white or green → טהור ; but if she brings a blood held to be טמא and says a חכם declared a similar one pure, one does not rely on her: it must be shown. תליה במכה (188:3-5): blood that came out through a שפופרת, or within a חתיכה, or when the מקור has detached (נעקר), is טהור, since this is not the normal way of seeing, and one attributes it to a מכה (Rama). בדיקת השרייה (188:4-6): a מפלת shaped like a בריה (husks, hairs, red יבחושים) is טמאה if, soaked for 24h in tepid water (פושרים), it dissolves ; otherwise, dry and with no blood at all, it is טהורה. A grave matter: each of these cases is decided with your Rav (cf. sim. 194).
