Siman 178 — Not following the customs of the nations

שלא ללבוש כמלבושי עובדי כוכבים
Three seifim, reduced to one question: what does what I do say?

1. The axiom: the prohibition bears on meaning, not on form

The axiom. A usage coming from the nations is not forbidden because it comes from them but because it says something. Two signs reveal it: licentiousness, and the absence of a reason. What has a useful reason, or is done out of honour, is not a ḥok — and the Rama permits it expressly.
« וכל זה אינו אסור אלא בדבר שנהגו בו העובדי כוכבים לשם פריצות כגון שנהגו ללבוש מלבושים אדומים והוא מלבוש שרים וכדומה לזה ממלבושי הפריצות » (רמ״א יו״ד קע״ח:א)
« אבל דבר שנהגו לתועלת כגון שדרכן שכל מי שהוא רופא מומחה יש לו מלבוש מיוחד שניכר בו שהוא רופא אומן מותר ללובשו » (רמ״א יו״ד קע״ח:א)
Three consequences. (1) Seif 1 enumerates forms, but the gloss gives their measure: the enumeration is not closed, the criterion is. (2) Seif 2 lifts everything for one close to the government, which would make no sense if the prohibition bore on form alone. (3) Seif 3 leaves imitation for superstition, and yet belongs to the same siman: there too, the meaning of the gesture decides.

2. The three reflex questions

1. Does this usage carry a mark of licentiousness? Yes — forbidden, and that is the Rama’s first sign.
▼
2. Does it have a reason? None — forbidden, as the ways of the Emorites. A useful reason, or honour — permitted.
▼
3. Who does it, and why? One close to the government who must appear before it — all is permitted to him.

The Rama’s question is not “where does this garment come from?” but “what does it say, and what does it serve?”. That is why his example of a permission is a uniform — the most recognizable of garments, and the most innocent.

5. The hair — three readings of ס״ק א

WhoReading of ולא יגדל ציצת ראשו
Beit Yosef, Ateret ZekenimThe following clause explains it: it is the bellorit, said twice.
Bach, PrishaA rule of its own: growing the hair for beauty, as maidens do.
TazThe never-cut hair of fighting men — that too is among the מגדלי בלורית.
Baer HetevReports the last two without deciding.
A classic pitfall: thinking the seif forbids long hair. It nowhere says so. It names two haircuts — sides shaved with the hair in the middle, and the front shaved from ear to ear — and the Taz notes at ס״ק ב that the printed text has lost a word, מלאחריו, without which the second clause is no longer describable.

6. The building — temple or court

« ולא יבנה מקומות כבנין היכלות של עבודת כוכבים כדי שיכנסו בהם רבים כמו שהם עושים » (שו״ע יו״ד קע״ח:א)
« אבל כגון אותם העשוים לועד שלהם להתיעץ על עסקיהם ולמשפט אין איסור לבנות כתבנית אותם היכלות. » (ש״ך יו״ד קע״ח ס״ק ב)
What decides is not the plan but the purpose. A building made like their temples to gather many there is forbidden; a council, business or court building raised on the same model is not — so the Bach, reported by the Shach and the Baer Hetev. The Taz, for his part, holds both of the Raavad’s readings and concludes that one must forbid on either: neither the figures nor the sign of gathering.

7. One close to the government — and the Taz’s difficulty

« מי שהוא קרוב למלכות וצריך ללבוש במלבושיהם ולדמות להם מותר בכל: » (שו״ע יו״ד קע״ח:ב)
« ואע״ג דכל הנזכר בסי׳ זה הוא בכלל ובחוקותיהם לא תלכו ואין כח לחכמים לעקור דבר מן התורה מ״מ הכא שאני כיון שלא פירשה התורה שום דבר ומסרה לחכמים והם ראו להתיר לקרובים למלכות. ב״י: » (ט״ז יו״ד קע״ח ס״ק ה)
The Taz’s difficulty and his answer hold for the whole siman. Had the prohibition come from a verse with defined content, the sages could change nothing in it. But the Torah named neither garment nor haircut: it handed the measure to the sages. That is what allows both the exception of seif 2 and the criterion of the gloss.

The Levush wished to forbid the Shabbat-night loaf by analogy. The Taz rejects the analogy on the sense of the word: “Gad” means fortune, and the Shabbat loaf is set for the honour of the day. The answer teaches beyond the case: a likeness of form between two usages never suffices to give them the same status.

10. Rules of thumb, mnemonic and classic pitfalls

Rules of thumb
  • Two signs forbid: licentiousness, and the absence of a reason.
  • A useful reason permits; so does honour.
  • The positive duty precedes the prohibition: to be distinct from them in dress and deeds.
  • The building: what decides is the purpose, not the plan.
  • The table: bringing is forbidden, not finding.
Mnemonic. Three words for three seifim — form, function, fortune. Form: what seif 1 enumerates. Function: what the gloss asks, and which permits. Fortune: what seif 3 forbids, under the name of Gad.
Classic pitfalls
  • Thinking the siman forbids everything that comes from them: the gloss says exactly the contrary once there is a reason.
  • Thinking seif 1 forbids long hair: it names two haircuts, not a length.
  • Confusing the loaf brought with the loaf found, which the Shach expressly separates.
  • Holding the meal of the night before the circumcision to be forbidden: the Shach excludes it, and the Pitchei Teshuva only notes that it has no standing of a mitzva.
  • Ascribing an entry to the Nekudot HaKesef on this siman: they do not comment here.
