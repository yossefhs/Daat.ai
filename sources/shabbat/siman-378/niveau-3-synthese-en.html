1. The central axiom

Siman שע״ח in one sentence.
When courtyards open onto one another, does one forbid carrying to the other? Seif ה gives the rule: « זה הכלל רגל האסור׳ במקומה אוסר׳ שלא במקומה ורגל המותרת אינה אוסר׳ שלא במקומה » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:ה). In seifim א and ג, the Mishnah Berurah explains the rulings by the place of the eruv — where it is deposited, those who took part in it are as if living there (ס״ק ג and י״ד): « ועיקר העירוב משום דירה דהוי כאלו דר במקום העירוב » (משנה ברורה שע״ח ס״ק י״ד); the Shulchan Aruch HaRav says so in its seif 1: « שֶׁאָז דִּיּוּרֵי הָאֶמְצָעִית בַּחִיצוֹנוֹת, שֶׁהָעֵרוּב הוּא מִשּׁוּם דִּירָה, וְאֵין דִּיּוּרֵי הַחִיצוֹנִים בָּאֶמְצָעִית, שֶׁהֲרֵי אֵין עֵרוּבָן בָּהּ » (שו״ע הרב שע״ח:א). The siman then sets out the cases, each in its seif: three courtyards open to one another, each of the outer ones having made the eruv with the middle one (seif א), two courtyards one inside the other (seif ב), the eruv they make together (seif ג), the single resident of the inner courtyard (seif ד), and three courtyards one inside the other (seif ה).

3. Structure of the siman — from neighbouring courtyards to courtyards one inside the other

Seif א — three courtyards open to one another and to the public domain: each of the outer ones made the eruv with the middle one, and not with each other; the outer ones are forbidden with each other, the middle one is permitted with each — when the middle one gave its eruv in each, or the outer ones gave theirs in the middle one, in two houses. If the outer ones gave it in a single house of the middle one, all three are permitted with one another — in one vessel, or a vessel that was filled with some left over (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ה; the rule of Siman 366).
↓
Seif ב — two courtyards one inside the other: the inner one, open onto the outer one, has a right of passage over it (with no other way out, according to the Taz, ס״ק ב). If the inner one made the eruv and not the outer one, or each made it for itself but a resident of the outer one forgot: the inner one is permitted, the outer one forbidden. If the outer one made the eruv and not the inner one, or each made it for itself but a resident of the inner one forgot: both are forbidden. If each made the eruv for itself: each is permitted in its own courtyard. When it is a resident who forgot, the Mishnah Berurah writes that the prohibition ends if he renounces: in the first case, the outer one is forbidden unless he renounces his share to the others (ס״ק י); in the second, both are forbidden unless [the one of the inner one who forgot] renounces his domain to the residents of the inner one, and also his right of passage in the outer one to the residents of the outer one (ס״ק י״א). In the first case, the Shulchan Aruch HaRav (seif 2) writes of the resident of the outer one who forgot that he prohibits their courtyard to them until he renounces his domain. When it is the whole inner one that did not make the eruv, the Mechaber and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav write in siman 381 that it can renounce its right in favor of the outer one, so that it neither uses it nor passes through it except when it needs to go out (and come in, adds the Rav), and the outer one becomes permitted (שו״ע או״ח שפ״א:ב; שו״ע הרב שפ״א:ב).
↓
Seif ג — the eruv made together by the two courtyards: deposited in the outer one, the forgetting of one resident, of either courtyard, forbids both, until he renounces his domain (to both courtyards, Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ו); deposited in the inner one, the forgetting of a resident of the inner one forbids both (unless he renounces to both courtyards, Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ז), while that of a resident of the outer one leaves the inner one permitted.
↓
Seif ד — the single resident of the inner courtyard: if there is in the outer one a single resident, or two who made the eruv — between themselves alone, according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק י״ח) —, the one in the inner one does not forbid the outer one. The Rema's gloss: likewise several who are like a single one and do not need to make the eruv (siman 370).
↓
Seif ה — three courtyards one inside the other: a single resident in each; even though several pass through the outer one, they do not forbid, for each is permitted in its place. If there are two in the inner one, who did not make the eruv, they forbid the single ones in the middle one and in the outer one. The seif closes with the rule: a foot forbidden in its place forbids outside its place; a permitted foot does not forbid outside its place.

4. Decision tree — does one courtyard forbid the other?

Q1: Three courtyards open to one another and to the public domain, each of the outer ones having made the eruv with the middle one, and not with the other? (seif א)
↓ The middle one gave its eruv in each, or the outer ones gave theirs in the middle one, in two houses → the outer ones are forbidden with each other, the middle one is permitted with each. The outer ones gave it in a single house of the middle one → all three are permitted with one another — in one vessel, or a vessel that was filled with some left over (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ה; the rule of Siman 366); the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, which does not repeat it in seif 1, sets it down in Siman 366 (seif 9).
Q2: Two courtyards, the inner one having a right of passage over the outer one — did each make its own eruv? (seif ב)
↓ The inner one made the eruv, and the outer one did not, or one of its residents forgot → the inner one is permitted, the outer one forbidden. The outer one made the eruv, and the inner one did not, or one of its residents forgot → both are forbidden. Each made its own → each is permitted in its own courtyard. When it is a resident who forgot, the Mishnah Berurah writes that the prohibition ends if he renounces: in the first case, his share in favor of the others (ס״ק י); in the second, his domain in favor of the residents of the inner one and his right of passage in the outer one in favor of those of the outer one (ס״ק י״א). The whole inner one without an eruv: according to the Mechaber and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, it can renounce its right in favor of the outer one, so that it neither uses it nor passes through it except when it needs to go out; the outer one then becomes permitted (siman 381, seif ב).
Q3: Did the two courtyards make the eruv together? (seif ג)
↓ Where was the eruv deposited? In the outer one → the forgetting of one resident, of either courtyard, forbids both, until he renounces his domain (to both courtyards, Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ו). In the inner one → the forgetting of a resident of the inner one forbids both (unless he renounces to both courtyards, Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ז); that of a resident of the outer one leaves the inner one permitted.
Q4: How many residents are in each courtyard? (seifim ד and ה)
↓ Two courtyards, and in the outer one a single resident, or two who made the eruv — between themselves alone, according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק י״ח) → the one in the inner one does not forbid the outer one; likewise, according to the Rema's gloss, several who are like a single one (seif ד; siman 370). Three courtyards one inside the other, a single resident in each → they do not forbid; two in the inner one, who did not make the eruv → they forbid the single ones in the middle one and in the outer one (seif ה).
→ At the slightest doubt: consult your Rav.

5. The forbidden foot and the permitted foot — seifim ב to ה

Seif ה closes the siman with a rule: a foot forbidden in its place forbids outside its place, a permitted foot does not forbid outside its place. The Mishnah Berurah applies it, among other places, to seifim ב and ד, and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav gives it already in its seif 2. The table condenses these seifim, in their order; their full text and its translation are in Level 1.

Where the Shulchan Aruch HaRav's text differs (its own numbering, Level 4; the two texts have the same division into 5 seifim):
  • Seif 1 (the Mechaber's seif א): it says what the prohibition covers, « הֲרֵי הַחִיצוֹנוֹת אֲסוּרוֹת זוֹ עִם זוֹ, לְהוֹצִיא כֵּלִים שֶׁשָּׁבְתוּ בַּבָּתִּים מִזּוֹ לְזוֹ דֶּרֶךְ הָאֶמְצָעִית » (שו״ע הרב שע״ח:א), and bases each case on the residence that the eruv establishes where it is placed. Rashi, reported by the Beis Yosef, already gives the reason for both cases; the Taz (ס״ק א), that of the first and of the single house; the Mishnah Berurah, later than the Rav, that of both (ס״ק ג and ד).
  • Seif 2 (seif ב): the rule of the forbidden foot and the permitted foot, which the Mechaber sets down only at the end of his seif ה, it gives already in this seif, as the reason for its second and third cases, with, for the first of the two, the condition of the right of passage (the right of passage itself the Mechaber sets down at the start of his seif ב); the Taz (ס״ק ג), before it, and the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק י״א and י״ב), later than the Rav, also give the rule as the reason for seif ב. Of the resident of the outer one who forgot it writes « שֶׁהוּא אוֹסֵר עֲלֵיהֶם הֶחָצֵר עַד שֶׁיְּבַטֵּל רְשׁוּתוֹ » (שו״ע הרב שע״ח:ב), and refers to siman 380.
  • Seif 3 (seif ג): the same cases, in the reverse order — the Rav begins with the eruv deposited in the inner one, the Mechaber with the eruv deposited in the outer one; when the eruv is deposited in the inner one and a resident of the outer one forgot, the Rav further writes that the outer one is forbidden (חִיצוֹנָה אֲסוּרָה), which the Mechaber's seif ג does not say in words, but which the baraita (עירובין ע״ה ע״ב) and the Rambam (הלכות עירובין פ״ד הכ״א) already write (level 2, section 5); its seif 3 does not mention the renunciation that the Mechaber's seif ג writes for the eruv deposited in the outer one (to both courtyards, according to the Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ו); in siman 381, it writes the renunciation for an eruv made together without naming there the courtyard where the eruv was deposited — when a resident of the inner one forgot, to each of the residents of the inner one and of the outer one; when a resident of the outer one forgot, he need not renounce in favor of the inner one —, and its reasons, the eruv that brings the outer one into the inner one and the inner one's removing the outer one, are those that its seif 3 gives for the eruv deposited in the inner one (שו״ע הרב שפ״א:ב; שו״ע הרב שע״ח:ג; level 4, point ג); for the eruv deposited in the outer one, it does not write, in siman 378 or in siman 381, to whom the one who forgot renounces; in seif 2, it speaks of another case, the resident who forgot and forbids his own courtyard when each courtyard made its own eruv (reference to siman 380); the reason for the inner one's withdrawal is already in the Tur and the Taz (ס״ק ד).
  • Seif 4 (seif ד and the Rema's gloss): the Rav writes in his seif what the gloss says, with an example — the same one the Mishnah Berurah gives (ס״ק כ׳) —: « כְּגוֹן הָאָב עִם בָּנָיו שֶׁמְּקַבְּלִים מִמֶּנּוּ פְּרָס » (שו״ע הרב שע״ח:ד); and he specifies that the two in the outer one made the eruv between themselves, as the Mishnah Berurah also reads it (ס״ק י״ח): « אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ שְׁנַיִם דָּרִים בַּחִיצוֹנָה וְעֵרְבוּ בֵּינֵיהֶם » (שו״ע הרב שע״ח:ד).
  • Seif 5 (seif ה): where the Mechaber writes that several pass through the outer one, the Rav writes that these are the two of the inner courtyards, as the Tur does; and he names the courtyard of the two who did not make the eruv: the third, innermost one.

6. Mnemonic — one word per seif

Five words from the Mechaber's text, one per seif, in the order of the seifim:

ב — בבית א׳: given in a single house of the middle one, the eruv of the outer ones permits all three courtyards (seif א) — in one vessel, or a vessel that was filled with some left over (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ה; the rule of Siman 366).

ד — דריכת רגל: the inner courtyard passes through the outer one; if it did not make the eruv, or one of its residents forgot, it forbids the outer one too, even if the latter made its own (seif ב).

ע — עירבו ביחד: the joint eruv; the ruling depends on the courtyard where it was deposited and on the courtyard of the resident who forgot (seif ג).

י — יחיד: the single resident of the inner one does not forbid, when the outer one has a single resident, or two who made the eruv — between themselves alone, according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק י״ח) (seif ד).

ז — זה הכלל: the foot forbidden in its place, the permitted foot (seif ה).

7. Pitfalls to avoid

Pitfall 1: Thinking that the eruv of each outer courtyard with the middle one joins the outer ones to each other. In seif א: « החיצונות אסורות זו עם זו והאמצעי׳ מותרת עם כל אחת מהן והן מותרות עמה » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:א); « במה דברים אמורים כשנתנה אמצעי׳ עירובה בזו ועירובה בזו או שנתנו החיצונות עירובן באמצעי׳ בשני בתים » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:א). When each made its eruv only with the middle one, the seif joins them when their eruv was given in a single house: « אבל אם נתנו שתי החיצונו׳ עירובן באמצעי׳ בבית א׳ שלשתן מותרות זו עם זו » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:א) — in one vessel, or a vessel that was filled with some left over (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ה; the rule of Siman 366).
Pitfall 2: Thinking that, in seif ב, the two courtyards are treated alike. When the inner one made the eruv: « אם עירבה פנימי׳ לעצמה ולא החיצונה או שעירבה כל אחת לעצמה ושכח א׳ מן החיצונה ולא עירב פנימי׳ מותרת והחיצונה אסורה » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:ב). When the outer one did: « עירבה חיצונה ולא פנימית או שעירבה כל אחת לעצמה ושכח א׳ מהפנימי׳ ולא עירב שתיהן אסורות » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:ב). According to the Mishnah Berurah and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, the difference lies in the passage: the inner one can close its door, the outer one having no right of passage over it, whereas the inner one has a right of passage over the outer one (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט and י״א; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, seif 2). When it is a resident who forgot, the Mishnah Berurah writes that the prohibition ends if he renounces: in the first case, his share in favor of the others (ס״ק י); in the second, his domain in favor of the residents of the inner one and his right of passage in the outer one in favor of those of the outer one (ס״ק י״א). The whole inner one without an eruv: according to the Mechaber and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, it can renounce its right in favor of the outer one, so that it neither uses it nor passes through it except when it needs to go out; the outer one then becomes permitted (siman 381, seif ב).
Pitfall 3: Thinking that the joint eruv always leaves the inner one permitted when the one who forgot is from the outer one. That holds only if the eruv was deposited in the inner one: « אבל אם שכח א׳ מן החיצונה ולא עירב פנימית מותרת » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:ג). Deposited in the outer one: « עירבו ביחד ונתנו עירובן בחיצונה ושכח א׳ בין מן הפנימי׳ בין מן החיצונה ולא עירב שתיהן אסורות עד שיבטל רשותו » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:ג) — to both courtyards, the Mishnah Berurah specifies: « ר״ל לשתי החצירות וכמבואר בסי׳ שפ״א » (משנה ברורה שע״ח ס״ק ט״ו).
Pitfall 4: Reading, in seif ד, that the two residents of the outer one made the eruv with the inner one. The seif: « אם יחיד דר בפנימי׳ ויחיד בחיצונה או שנים בחיצונה ועירבו אין היחיד שבפנימי׳ אוסר על החיצונה » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:ד); the Mishnah Berurah: « היינו אותם השנים לבד אבל לא עם הפנימית » (משנה ברורה שע״ח ס״ק י״ח); the Shulchan Aruch HaRav: « אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ שְׁנַיִם דָּרִים בַּחִיצוֹנָה וְעֵרְבוּ בֵּינֵיהֶם » (שו״ע הרב שע״ח:ד).
Pitfall 5: Thinking that, in three courtyards one inside the other, several people passing through the outer one is enough to forbid it. Seif ה: « שלשה חצרו׳ זו לפנים מזו ויחיד בכל אחת אע״פ שרבים דורסים בחיצונה אינם אוסרים שכל אחת מותר׳ במקומה » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:ה); what forbids is two residents of the inner one who did not make the eruv: « ואם היו שנים בפנימי׳ ולא עירבו הרי הם אוסרים על היחידים שבאמצעי׳ ושבחיצונה » (שו״ע או״ח שע״ח:ה).
Pitfall 6: Mixing the two numberings. Levels 1 to 3 follow the Shulchan Aruch of the Mechaber; Level 4 follows the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, which also has 5 seifim, divided the same way: its seifim 1 to 5 correspond to seifim א to ה. But within its seif 3 the Rav presents the cases in the reverse order of seif ג, and its seif 4 writes in its body what the Rema's gloss says.

10. The practical commandments of Siman שע״ח

For daily conduct

  1. Three courtyards open to one another and to the public domain, each of the outer ones having made the eruv with the middle one: the outer ones are forbidden with each other, the middle one is permitted with each — when the middle one gave its eruv in each, or the outer ones gave theirs in the middle one, in two houses; given in a single house, the eruv permits all three courtyards (seif א) — in one vessel, or a vessel that was filled with some left over (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ה; the rule of Siman 366).
  2. Two courtyards, the inner one having a right of passage over the outer one: the eruv of the inner one alone permits it, even if the outer one did not make the eruv or one of its residents forgot, and the outer one is forbidden; the eruv of the outer one alone, or the forgetting of a resident of the inner one, forbids both; if each made its own, each is permitted in its own courtyard (seif ב). When it is a resident who forgot, the Mishnah Berurah writes that the prohibition ends if he renounces: in the first case, his share in favor of the others (ס״ק י); in the second, his domain in favor of the residents of the inner one and his right of passage in the outer one in favor of those of the outer one (ס״ק י״א). The whole inner one without an eruv: according to the Mechaber and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, it can renounce its right in favor of the outer one, so that it neither uses it nor passes through it except when it needs to go out; the outer one then becomes permitted (siman 381, seif ב).
  3. A joint eruv for the two courtyards: deposited in the outer one, the forgetting of one resident, of either courtyard, forbids both until he renounces his domain (seif ג; to both courtyards, Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ו); deposited in the inner one, the forgetting of a resident of the inner one forbids both (unless he renounces to both courtyards, Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ז), while that of a resident of the outer one leaves the inner one permitted (seif ג).
  4. A single resident in the inner one does not forbid the outer one, when there is in the outer one a single resident, or two who made the eruv — between themselves alone, according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק י״ח) — (seif ד); and, according to the Rema's gloss, likewise several who are like a single one (seif ד; siman 370).
  5. Three courtyards one inside the other, a single resident in each: they do not forbid; two in the inner one, who did not make the eruv, forbid the single ones in the middle one and in the outer one (seif ה).
  6. In case of doubt — consult your Rav. In-depth pilpul — Level 2; Chabad shitah — Level 4.
