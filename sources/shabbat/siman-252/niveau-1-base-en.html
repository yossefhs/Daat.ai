1. The text of the Shulchan Aruch

Seif Alef — The general rule and the exceptions of cooking

מֻתָּר לְהַתְחִיל בִּמְלָאכָה בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵינוֹ יָכוֹל לְגָמְרָהּ מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם וְהִיא נִגְמֶרֶת מֵאֵלֶיהָ בְּשַׁבָּת. כְּגוֹן לִשְׁרוֹת דְּיוֹ וְסַמָּנִין בַּמַּיִם וְהֵם נִשְׁרִים כָּל הַשַּׁבָּת. וְלָתֵת אוּנִין שֶׁל פִּשְׁתָּן לַתַּנּוּר כְּדֵי שֶׁיִּתְלַבְּנוּ. וְלָתֵת צֶמֶר לְתוֹךְ הַיּוֹרָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ עַל הָאֵשׁ — וְהִיא טוּחָה בְּטִיט. שֶׁאִם הִיא עַל הָאֵשׁ — אָסוּר שֶׁמָּא יְחַתֶּה. וַאֲפִלּוּ אֵינָהּ עַל הָאֵשׁ — אִם אֵינָהּ טוּחָה בְּטִיט אָסוּר שֶׁמָּא יָגִיס בָּהּ בְּכַף, וְהַמֵּגִיס בַּקְּדֵרָה אֲפִלּוּ אֵינָהּ עַל הָאֵשׁ — חַיָּב מִשּׁוּם מְבַשֵּׁל.
One may start a melakha on erev Shabbos near nightfall, even though one cannot finish it before night, and it concludes by itself on Shabbos. For example: soaking ink and dyes in water (tzeviya / soaking) so they steep all of Shabbos; placing bundles of flax in the oven so they whiten; placing wool in a vat (off the fire), sealed with clay (טוּחָה בְּטִיט). If it is on the fire — assur, lest one stoke the coals (shema yechateh). Even off the fire, if not sealed — assur, lest one stir (shema yagis). And one who stirs in a pot, even off the fire, is chayav for "cooking" (mevashel).
Seif Alef lays down 3 principles:
  1. General permission: one may start a melakha on Friday that concludes on Shabbos — provided it is autonomous.
  2. Exception A — shema yechateh: if the pot is on the fire, the fear is that one will stoke the coals on Shabbos. Therefore assur.
  3. Exception B — shema yagis: even off the fire, if the pot is not sealed, the fear is that one will stir on Shabbos. Therefore assur.
+ permission for traps, sale to a non-Jew departing before Shabbos.

Seif Beis — Giving garments to a non-Jewish launderer

מֻתָּר לִתֵּן בְּגָדָיו לְכוֹבֵס נָכְרִי וְעוֹרוֹת לְעַבְדָן סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה אִם קָצַץ לוֹ דָּמִים אוֹ שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה אוֹתָם בְּטוֹבַת הֲנָאָה. וְהוּא שֶׁלֹּא יֹאמַר לוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת וְגַם שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה הָעַכּוּ״ם הַמְּלָאכָה בְּבֵיתוֹ.
One may give his garments to a non-Jewish launderer and hides to a tanner near nightfall, on 2 conditions: (1) ketzitzah (agreed price) or b'tovas hana'ah (as a favor), and (2) not to tell him to work on Shabbos. And also that the non-Jew perform the melakha at his own place (not at the Jew's place).
Link with siman רמ״ז: we find the ketzitzah as a structural switch. When a price is agreed upon, the non-Jew acts for his own interest (adata d'nafshei) and not as the Jew's agent. Cumulative conditions: price + at his place + no explicit request for Shabbos.

The 20 seifim of the Admor HaZaken (Shulchan Aruch HaRav) — full coverage

The Shulchan Aruch of the Admor HaZaken structures the material in 20 seifim instead of the Mechaber's 7. Here is a concise reading of each; seifim א-ב cover the classical cooking exceptions (above). The rest detail the goy's labor, specific shevisas keilim, and the rules of leaving home near nightfall.

Seif א (Alef) — General rule + case of dyer on fire

מֻתָּר לְהַתְחִיל בִּמְלָאכָה בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה... וְאֵין אָדָם מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתַת כֵּלָיו.
It is permitted to begin a melacha on Friday near nightfall, even if it cannot be finished before night, and it finishes by itself on Shabbos. Examples: soaking ink, soaking barley, mugmar under garments, setting traps. "A person is not commanded regarding the resting of his vessels" (Beis Hillel).
Lomdishe: founding seif — the Beis Hillel principle: no shevisas keilim. Five examples. Note on automatic traps: "even if the trap itself does the tzeida on Shabbos, it is permitted".

Seif ב (Beis) — Case of wool in the dyeing pot

וְכֵן מֻתָּר לָתֵת צֶמֶר לְתוֹךְ הַיּוֹרָה מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם לְצָבְעוֹ... וְהוּא שֶׁתְּהֵא הַיּוֹרָה עֲקוּרָה מֵעַל הָאֵשׁ... וְגַם שֶׁתְּהֵא הַיּוֹרָה טוּחָה בְּטִיט.
Permitted to put wool in the dyeing pot before Shabbos to dye itself on Shabbos — on 2 conditions: (1) removed from the fire (else shema yechateh); (2) sealed with clay around the cover (else shema yagis — and stirring a boiling pot, even off the fire, is chayav mishum mevashel).
Lomdishe: exemplary structure of the double rabbinic gzeira — chasiya (poking fire) and hagasa (stirring). Even without fire, stirring a hot dye = mevashel obligation.

Seif ג (Gimmel) — Selling to a non-Jew leaving before Shabbos

מֻתָּר לִמְכּוֹר לְנָכְרִי וּלְהַטְעִינוֹ הַמִּקָּח סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה, וּבִלְבָד שֶׁיֵּצֵא מִפֶּתַח בֵּיתוֹ עִם הַמִּקָּח מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם.
Permitted to sell to a non-Jew and load his merchandise near nightfall, provided he leaves the Jew's house with the merchandise before night. If the non-Jew has reserved a room in the Jew's courtyard to leave merchandise before Shabbos, one may not let him take them on Shabbos (except if he is a powerful goy).
Lomdishe: the moment of exit of the merchandise. Exit from the Jew's premises before Shabbos "detaches" the merchandise from the Jew. Direct link to siman רמ״ו.

Seif ד (Daled) — Giving clothes to non-Jewish launderer in kablanus

וּמֻתָּר לִתֵּן בְּגָדָיו לְכוֹבֵס נָכְרִי וְעוֹרוֹת לְעַבְּדָן נָכְרִי סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה בְּקַבְּלָנוּת.
Permitted to give one's clothes to a non-Jewish launderer and skins to the tanner near nightfall b'kablanus (fixed price) — the non-Jew does on his own even on Shabbos. Conditions: (1) not telling him to do it on Shabbos; (2) not saying "have it done Sunday/Monday" when it would be impossible without working on Shabbos.
Lomdishe: this seif applies the principle of adata dnafshei — the non-Jew works for his own interest (completing the job and getting paid), not as agent of the Jew. Don't request any timing that necessarily implies Shabbos.

Seif ה (Heh) — Melacha at the non-Jew's vs. at the Jew's

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת בְּבֵיתוֹ... אֲבָל אָסוּר לְהַנִּיחוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת בִּרְשׁוּת יִשְׂרָאֵל.
All this applies when the non-Jew works at his own place. But it is forbidden to let him work in the Jew's domain — maris ayin: passersby will see and think it's Jewish labor on Shabbos. And even at the non-Jew's place, some hold it is forbidden if the Jew derives specific profit from the work being done on Shabbos (rather than later).
Lomdishe: two cumulative criteria — (1) place (at the non-Jew's, not the Jew's); (2) no specific Jewish profit from Shabbos completion. If the Jew leaves on a trip Sunday morning, he has interest in Shabbos completion — new problem.

Seif ו (Vav) — Ketzitza or unfixed price

וְכָל זֶה כְּשֶׁקּוֹצֵץ לוֹ דָּמִים בִּשְׂכַר מְלַאכְתּוֹ... אֲבָל אִם אֵין הַנָּכְרִי חָפֵץ לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּחִנָּם, אֶלָּא מְצַפֶּה הוּא לְתַשְׁלוּם שָׂכָר, אֲבָל אֵין דַּעְתּוֹ סְמוּכָה עָלָיו בְּוַדַּאי.
All previous permissions hold with ketzitza (fixed price). Without ketzitza, the non-Jew expects payment without certainty — he is then deemed the Jew's agent (shlucho). The work is not given pre-Shabbos unless he can finish before. Item with a known market rate (laundry today) = no need for explicit ketzitza.
Lomdishe: ketzitza is the pivot. Items with known market price = no explicit fixation needed. For other works: give early enough (Thursday) to avoid Shabbos suspicion.

Seif ז (Zayin) — Seeing the non-Jew working on Shabbos

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אִם רוֹאֶה אוֹתוֹ עוֹשֶׂה מְלַאכְתּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת — צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר לוֹ שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשֶׂה.
If one sees the non-Jew doing the work on Shabbos, one must tell him to stop — the Jew's silence signifies approval, and the non-Jew understands he is working for the Jew's benefit.
Lomdishe: psychological-halachic principle. Silence under one's gaze = approval. Thus obligation to protest (mecha'a). Modern application: if you notice the dry cleaner working Shabbos on your clothes, ask him to stop.

Seif ח (Ches) — Maris ayin: publicly recognized clothes

לְפִיכָךְ, הַחֲלוּקִים שֶׁלָּנוּ בִּמְדִינוֹת אֵלּוּ שֶׁהֵם נִכָּרִים שֶׁהֵם שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, כְּשֶׁמְּכַבְּסָן הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת עַל גַּבֵּי הַנָּהָר — צָרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת.
If clothes have a recognizable style as Jewish, and the non-Jew launders them on Shabbos in public — one must protest (maris ayin). But one may give them to the launderer Friday without fear that he will launder publicly on Shabbos.
Lomdishe: maris ayin transcends other permissions. Even with ketzitza + at the non-Jew's, if Jewish work is publicly visible on Shabbos, one must protest.

Seif ט (Tes) — When the material is the non-Jew's

וְכָל זֶה כְּשֶׁהַמְּלָאכָה נַעֲשֵׂית בַּחֲפָצִים שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל... אֲבָל אִם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לֹא נָתַן לוֹ כְּלוּם — אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר לוֹ כְּלוּם.
If the material and the work are both the non-Jew's (e.g., he makes shoes from his own leather and sells them), the Jew has no obligation to protest — the work doesn't yet have the Jew's "name on it." Permitted to commission even late, provided one doesn't say to work on Shabbos.
Lomdishe: key distinction between commissioned work (Jew's material) and off-the-shelf purchase (non-Jew's material). The second category is much more permissive.

Seif י (Yud) — Non-Jew lighting fire for the Jew

וְכֵן אִם הַנָּכְרִי מֵאֵלָיו בְּלֹא אֲמִירַת יִשְׂרָאֵל עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת מִשֶּׁלּוֹ... אֵין צָרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדוֹ.
If a non-Jew lights a fire on Shabbos from his own wood for his own profit (knowing he'll be well rewarded) — no need to protest, and the Jew may warm himself at the fire. But if he uses the Jew's wood — must protest. And even with his own wood, one may not bodily enjoy the warmth if it is solely for the Jew (else gzeira lest one ask him on Shabbos).
Lomdishe: two questions: (1) Does the non-Jew work with his own material? (2) Does the Jew directly enjoy the melacha on Shabbos? If yes to both, nuances apply — detail often overlooked.

Seif יא (Yud-Alef) — Vessel finished by the non-Jew on Shabbos

אֲבָל נָכְרִי שֶׁעָשָׂה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל כְּלִי בְּשַׁבָּת בְּקַבְּלָנוּת... מֻתָּר לוֹ לֵהָנוֹת מִמֶּנּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת עַצְמָהּ.
If the non-Jew finished a vessel for the Jew on Shabbos in kablanus with fixed price, the Jew may enjoy it on Shabbos (per Mechaber). But other opinions forbid and require waiting "k'dei sheya'asu" (the time it would have taken) at Motzaei Shabbos. Practice lechatchila: wait. If truly needed Shabbos: rely on Mechaber.
Lomdishe: classical Mechaber/Rema machlokes. Borderline case: if the non-Jew's work finished Friday and he only touched up on Shabbos (laces, small finishing), one may wear — not benefiting from Shabbos melacha but from weekday melacha.

Seif יב (Yud-Beis) — Merchandise for sale, majority non-Jew city

וְכָל זֶה בְּכֵלִים שֶׁעָשָׂה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, אֲבָל נָכְרִי הָעוֹשֶׂה עַל הַמִּקָּח לִמְכּוֹר לְכָל מִי שֶׁיִּזְדַּמֵּן לוֹ, אִם הִיא עִיר שֶׁרֻבָּהּ נָכְרִים — מֻתָּר לִקַּח מִמֶּנּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת.
If the non-Jew makes goods for general sale (not for a particular Jew), in a city with majority non-Jews, one may buy on Shabbos (without payment = hakafa) and even wear on Shabbos. Logic: "he produces with the majority in mind" — i.e., non-Jews.
Lomdishe: classic urban market case. Today chain stores and distributors fit this category — open production. But on Shabbos itself one may not buy (other seif); this seif only rules on the right to enjoy goods made on Shabbos.

Seif יג (Yud-Gimmel) — Taking from the artisan on Shabbos (forbidden)

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁשִּׁגְּרָן הַנָּכְרִי אוֹ הֱבִיאָן בְּעַצְמוֹ לְבֵית הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל. אֲבָל אָסוּר לִשְׁלוֹחַ אֶצְלוֹ לִטּוֹל כֵּלִים מִבֵּיתוֹ.
Previous permission holds if the non-Jew brings them himself. But forbidden to send to the artisan to retrieve vessels on Shabbos (even finished before Shabbos) — maris ayin. Exception: a vendor (chenvani) who buys from artisans and resells — permitted since not the artisan.
Lomdishe: practical rule: don't go to the artisan on Shabbos. If needed, wait till after Shabbos. Modern: don't pick up at tailor or repair shop on Shabbos — even if work was ready since Friday.

Seif יד (Yud-Daled) — Opening water, medicine, press

מֻתָּר לִפְתּוֹחַ מַיִם לְגִנָּה... וְכֵן מֻתָּר לְהַנִּיחַ קִילוֹר עָבֶה עַל הָעַיִן... וְכֵן מֻתָּר לִטְעוֹן קוֹרוֹת בֵּית הַבָּד עַל גַּבֵּי הַזֵּיתִים.
Permitted before Shabbos: (1) opening garden water (continuous irrigation Shabbos); (2) placing thick medicine on eye or compress on wound; (3) loading olive/grape press. But the oil/wine flowing on Shabbos is forbidden for use (gzeira against hand-pressing). Exception: if pressured with a heavy weight already during full day (juice already flowing), all permitted Shabbos.
Lomdishe: four cases where a mechanical melacha started Friday continues Shabbos without intervention. Important nuance: the result (oil, wine) may be forbidden to use on Shabbos even if the mechanics itself is permitted. Modern drip-irrigation systems = textbook case.

Seif טו (Yud-Heh) — Water mill with noise (hashma'as kol)

מֻתָּר לִתֵּן חִטִּים לְתוֹךְ רֵחַיִם שֶׁל מַיִם סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה... וְיֵשׁ חוֹלְקִים עַל זֶה וְאוֹסְרִין... מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהוּא זִלְזוּל שַׁבָּת.
Permission to put grain in mill before Shabbos (grinds all Shabbos) — machlokes: (1) Mechaber: permitted (all know the mill is mechanical); (2) other opinions: forbidden in case of hashma'as kol (audible noise) = zilzul Shabbos — disregard of Shabbos. Halacha: generally machmir, except in case of hefsed (significant loss) or urgent need (like near Pesach).
Lomdishe: central concept of hashma'as kol (causing noise on Shabbos). Modern application: noisy washing machine or programmed vacuum that makes noise on Shabbos = problematic per main opinion. Solution: quiet appliances or isolated rooms.

Seif טז (Yud-Vav) — Mechanical chiming clock (zug mekashkesh)

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, מֻתָּר לְהַעֲמִיד מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת זוּג הַמְקַשְׁקֵשׁ לְשָׁעוֹת... אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמַּשְׁמִיעַ קוֹל בְּשַׁבָּת.
Permitted to wind chiming clock before Shabbos — even though it sounds on Shabbos. Reason: all know it is wound the day before (no zilzul). Thus no maris ayin.
Lomdishe: very important exception to the previous principle — if all know the mechanism is obviously set up before Shabbos (since that's its nature), no maris ayin. Direct modern application: Shabbos timers — all know they are programmed beforehand. Also: fan, AC, light on timer.

Seif יז (Yud-Zayin) — Going out with a chefetz near nightfall

לֹא יֵצֵא אָדָם לִרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה וְחֵפֶץ בְּיָדוֹ.
Don't go out with an object (chefetz) in hand near nightfall: gzeira — fear of forgetting to set it down after dark and carrying it on Shabbos. But halachically, forgetting is not a Torah transgression (which requires melechet machshevet).
Lomdishe: classic example of a preventive rabbinic gzeira. Modern application: avoid holding objects (phone, keys) in hand when going out near end of erev Shabbos; pocket them or leave at home.

Seif יח (Yud-Ches) — Going out with a needle stuck in clothing

אֲבָל מֻתָּר לָצֵאת לִרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה בְּמַחַט הַתְּחוּבָה לוֹ בְּבִגְדּוֹ.
Permitted to go out with a needle stuck in garment (which would be patur — exempt — on Shabbos). One doesn't make gzeira l'gzeira (forbidding an already-rabbinic prohibition). Moreover today we have no pure reshus harabim (only karmelis) — thus no prohibition near nightfall.
Lomdishe: double halachic leniency. Modern application: all our urban spaces are karmelis per most poskim — strict rule of seif יז greatly softened today. But general precaution remains good practice.

Seif יט (Yud-Tes) — Going out with tefillin near nightfall

וּמֻתָּר לָצֵאת אֲפִלּוּ בִּרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים גְּמוּרָה בִּתְפִלִּין שֶׁבְּרֹאשׁוֹ סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה.
Permitted to go out, even in true reshus harabim, with tefillin on head (and arm) near nightfall. Reason: the prohibition of hesech hada'as from tefillin (not removing one's awareness) means one constantly remembers them — no risk of forgetting.
Lomdishe: beautiful illustration that permanent awareness of a mitzva removes the gzeira of forgetting. Modern: sacred objects (kippa, tzitzis, mezuza pendant) — no "forgetting" issue since constantly felt.

Seif כ (Khaf) — Mitzva to check garments before Shabbos

מִצְוָה עַל כָּל אָדָם שֶׁיְּמַשְׁמֵשׁ בִּבְגָדָיו עֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה, שֶׁלֹּא יִהְיֶה בָּהֶם דָּבָר שֶׁאָסוּר לָצֵאת בּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת... וְיַתִּיר חֲגוֹרוֹ.
Mitzva for everyone to feel his garments near nightfall on Friday, so there be no object forbidden to carry on Shabbos — even per current custom that we have no pure reshus harabim. Check the belt too.
Lomdishe: final seif of the siman — preventive mitzva. Modern application: before Shabbos, empty pant pockets (keys, phone, change, paper), remove watch, check belts. Ritual transition into Shabbos.

2. The general context

What does this siman discuss?

Siman רנ״ב deals with melakhos a Jew may start on Friday so they finish automatically on Shabbos. The fundamental question: are a Jew's vessels obligated to "rest" on Shabbos, or only the Jew himself?

The foundational Talmudic sugya — Beitza 16b: machlokes Beis Shammai vs. Beis Hillel. Beis Shammai require that even the vessels of the Jew rest on Shabbos (שביתת כלים). Beis Hillel: only the Jew and those who depend on him (slaves, animals) rest — not inanimate vessels. The halacha follows Beis Hillel.

3. Key concept 1 — shevisas keilim

שְׁבִיתַת כֵּלִים = "rest of the vessels". Question: can an inanimate object of a Jew (e.g. dyer's vat, oven, press) "work" on Shabbos?
  • Beis Shammai: no — the vessels must also rest
  • Beis Hillel (halacha): yes — only the Jew and humans/animals dependent on him rest. Inanimate vessels may operate.
Practical consequence: one may begin a mechanical process on Friday that will continue on Shabbos — provided it is truly autonomous and that there is no risk that the Jew will intervene on Shabbos (the exceptions shema yechateh / shema yagis).

4. Key concept 2 — shema yechateh / shema yagis

שֶׁמָּא יְחַתֶּה = "lest one stoke". If the pot is on the fire, we are concerned that the Jew may put coals back on Shabbos to cook faster. This is a chashash derabanan — a rabbinic precaution.

שֶׁמָּא יָגִיס = "lest one stir". Even without fire, if the pot is not sealed, we are concerned that the Jew may stir on Shabbos. The act of stirring = chiyuv mevashel (liability for "cooking") — a deOraisa issur.

The same ketzitzah as in siman רמ״ז — a price agreed upon in advance with the non-Jew transforms the agency: he acts for adata d'nafshei (his own interest). The Mechaber extends this logic to dyers and tanners.

6. Detail of the 7 seifim

Seif Alef — Basic rule + 6 examples + 2 exceptions

6 examples of permitted melakha (which finish automatically):
  1. Soaking ink/dyes in water
  2. Placing flax in the oven to whiten
  3. Placing wool in a sealed pot without fire
  4. Setting traps (animals, birds, fish)
  5. Selling to a non-Jew and loading the merchandise (if he leaves before Shabbos)
  6. Rema's case: non-Jew leaving on Shabbos if the destination is designated before Shabbos (disputed)

Seif Beis — Non-Jewish launderer/tanner (ketzitzah)

3 cumulative conditions:
  1. Ketzitzah (agreed price) or b'tovas hana'ah (as a favor)
  2. Not to explicitly tell him to work on Shabbos
  3. Work at the non-Jew's place (not at the Jew's)

Seif Gimel — Maaras ayin for visible melakha

If the melakha is publicly known as belonging to a Jew and is performed in a visible public place — "tov l'hachmir v'l'esor" (better to be machmir and forbid). It is a matter of maaras ayin — public appearance. Even if technically permitted, the visual effect on the community is not good.

Seif Daled — Wearing the garment on Shabbos

PositionHalacha
MechaberKetzitzah → one may wear the garment on Shabbos even if the non-Jew finished on Shabbos (adata d'nafshei)
Rema (יש אוסרין)If one knows the non-Jew finished on Shabbos — assur to wear on Shabbos. Wait until motzaei Shabbos + the time it would have taken to do (k'dei she'ya'aseh)
Lechatchila practiceFollow Rema (wait)
B'dieved / needFollow Mechaber (may wear)

Seif Heh — Permitted autonomous melakhos

4 permitted cases:
  • Opening garden water (continuous irrigation)
  • Applying thick medicine on the eye (kilor)
  • Perfuming garments (mugmar under the clothes)
  • Soaking barley
  • Loading the presses (olives, grapes) — the oil/wine flows on Shabbos
  • Placing wheat in the water mill (with Rema's machlokes on hashma'as kol)
Reasoning: no human intervention on Shabbos, so shevisas keilim does not apply (Beis Hillel).

Seif Vav — Needle / quill / tefillin

Near nightfall:
  • Going out with a needle/quill = assur — fear of forgetting and carrying on Shabbos (an ordinary object one forgets)
  • Going out with tefillin = permitted — one does not forget tefillin (sacred object, constant attention)

Seif Zayin — Mitzva l'mashmesh (checking one's garments)

Mitzva to check one's garments on erev Shabbos near nightfall, so that there is nothing in them that would be forbidden to carry on Shabbos (objects forgotten in pockets, etc.). Preventive practice — avoiding chilul Shabbos.

8. Modern practical cases

Case 1 — Programmable washing machine

Case: starting the washing machine Friday at 11pm so it finishes Shabbos at 4am.
Conduct:
  • Beis Hillel — shevisas keilim not required ✓
  • No human intervention on Shabbos ✓
  • No disturbing noise if in an isolated room — otherwise hashma'as kol (Rema: strict)
  • If visible/audible to neighbors or the street — maaras ayin problematic (Seif Gimel)
General conduct: permitted if the washing machine makes no perceptible noise. Otherwise, better to be machmir.

Case 2 — Dishwasher programmed for Shabbos

Case: starting the dishwasher to finish during the Shabbos seudah.
Conduct: permitted according to the same criteria. Many contemporary poskim permit (R. Moshe Feinstein, R. Ovadia Yosef). Check that the hot drying cycle is not running (could be considered mevashel).

Case 3 — Thermomix / cooking robot

Case: programming the robot to cook on Shabbos morning.
Conduct: problematic. Automatic cooking = shema yagis (modern equivalent). And active cooking on Shabbos even by timer raises questions of mevashel. Ask a Rav.

Case 4 — Giving shirts to non-Jewish dry cleaner late Friday

Case: I give my shirts to the dry cleaner Friday at 5pm to pick up Sunday.
Conduct: permitted if:
  • Ketzitzah (set price) ✓
  • Work at the dry cleaner (not at my place) ✓
  • No explicit request "that he work on Shabbos"
  • Pick up after Shabbos — wait k'dei she'ya'aseh (per Rema)

Case 5 — Smart home (programmed lights, heating)

Case: programming the connected home for Shabbos (lights, heating).
Conduct: permitted according to Beis Hillel — shevisas keilim not required. But check that no system requires manual intervention on Shabbos. Shabbos timers (zmanei Shabbos) are the standard contemporary solution.

9. Practical synthesis of the Siman

The 4 teachings of Siman רנ״ב:
  1. General permission to start a melakha on Friday that finishes automatically on Shabbos — Beis Hillel: no obligatory shevisas keilim
  2. Exceptions: shema yechateh (on the fire), shema yagis (not sealed), hashma'as kol (noise), maaras ayin (visible)
  3. Giving to a non-Jew with ketzitzah + at his place + no Shabbos request = permitted (recall from siman רמ״ז)
  4. Checking one's garments before Shabbos (preventive mitzva)

The 5 practical commands of Siman רנ״ב

  1. Verify autonomy — the melakha must take place without human intervention on Shabbos
  2. No active cooking on the fire (shema yechateh) nor in an unsealed pot (shema yagis)
  3. With a non-Jew — ketzitzah + at his place + no explicit Shabbos request
  4. Discretion — avoid that which is publicly visible and audible (maaras ayin, hashma'as kol)
  5. Check garments before Shabbos — preventive mitzva
