Siman רמ״ה · 19 Seifim

Jew and non-Jew in a commercial partnership (שותפות)
סימן רמ״ה
ישראל ונכרי איך יתנהגו בשותפות
🌱 Introduction Level · מתחילים
✦ ✦ ✦

The siman of the Jew/non-Jew partnership (shutfus). 19 seifim that codify how to share a commercial enterprise between a Jew and a non-Jew without being mechalel Shabbos. Three core issues: (1) equitable division of profits, (2) who works which day (= rotation), (3) maaras ayin and public appearance. Application: modern businesses, stores open 7 days a week, digital platforms.

Topic: Jew/non-Jew commercial partnership and Shabbos

1. The text of the Shulchan Aruch — excerpts

Seif Alef — Prior stipulation of the partnership

יִשְׂרָאֵל וְנָכְרִי שֶׁנִּשְׁתַּתְּפוּ בִּמְלָאכָה אוֹ בַחֲנוּת — אִם הִתְנוּ בִּתְחִלָּה שֶׁהַשָּׂכָר שֶׁל יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת יִהְיֶה לַנָּכְרִי לְבַדּוֹ, וְשָׂכָר שֶׁל יוֹם אַחֵר כְּנֶגֶד יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת לַיִשְׂרָאֵל לְבַדּוֹ — מֻתָּר.
When a Yid and a non-Jew enter into a partnership (shutfus) in a workshop or store — a prior stipulation is required: to agree at the time the partnership is formed that the Shabbos earnings will belong to the non-Jew alone, and that another weekday in exchange will belong to the Yid alone. With this prior stipulation (תְּנַאי מֵעִקָּרָא), it is permitted.
Logic: without a prior stipulation, the Yid is a co-owner of the Shabbos profits — so he has an interest in the business running. With the stipulation, the Shabbos profits structurally belong to the non-Jew → no Jewish profit on Shabbos.

Seif Beis — Without a prior stipulation

וְאִם לֹא הִתְנוּ בִּתְחִלָּה — אָסוּר. וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאַחַר כָּךְ הִתְנוּ — אֵין הַתְּנַאי מוֹעִיל אֶלָּא לְהַבָּא.
Without a prior stipulation — forbidden. A stipulation made after the fact is only effective going forward, not retroactively. Therefore all past Shabbos earnings (without a stipulation) remain in the partnership — forbidden to the Yid.

2. The general context

What is this siman about?

Siman רמ״ה deals with the halachic structure of a commercial partnership (shutfus) between a Yid and a non-Jew. The central problem: if the business runs on Shabbos (which is the norm in most sectors), the Yid must not benefit from it directly.

QuestionAnswer
How to prevent the Yid from receiving Shabbos profits?תְּנַאי מֵעִקָּרָא (prior stipulation)
How to prevent maaras ayin?Shared store closed on Shabbos or visible rotation
What to do if there is no prior stipulation?Past Shabbos profits are forbidden; going forward: rectify

3. Key concept 1 — תְּנַאי מֵעִקָּרָא

תְּנַאי מֵעִקָּרָא = "prior stipulation from the inception". To agree at the very moment the partnership is formed that the Shabbos profits will belong to the non-Jew alone, in exchange for another day for the Yid. Without this prior stipulation, the partnership is intrinsically problematic.

4. Key concept 2 — Equitable division

The ideal structure: day-for-day equity. Shabbos (average earnings of a standard weekday) to the non-Jew → a comparable day of the week to the Yid. No cheating, no disguised transfer.

5. Key concept 3 — Maaras ayin

Even with a prior stipulation, if the shared store is open on Shabbos with the Yid's name on the sign — maaras ayin (public appearance) is forbidden. Solution: the non-Jew's signage, or the store closed on Shabbos within the partnership.

8. Modern practical cases

Case 1 — Restaurant in a Yid/non-Jew partnership

  • Prior stipulation: Shabbos profits to the non-Jew
  • No Yid in the kitchen or at the cash register on Shabbos
  • If possible, close on Shabbos — the clearest solution
  • Otherwise: separate signage on Shabbos (non-Jew's name)

Case 2 — E-commerce with a non-Jewish partner

  • Website running 7/7 — automatic profits
  • Prior stipulation: 100% of Shabbos revenue to the non-Jew
  • No Jewish action on Shabbos (orders, deliveries)
  • Modern solution: separate legal entity for Shabbos (rare)

Case 3 — Automated industrial company

  • Plant running 24/7 without human intervention on Shabbos
  • See siman רנ״ב (autonomous melachos)
  • If a partnership: prior stipulation is still required

Case 4 — Passive stock-market investment (shares, funds)

  • Shares = participation in capital, not an operational partnership
  • No personal management on Shabbos
  • Permitted without a prior stipulation (case distinct from siman 245)

Case 5 — Hotel in partnership

  • Prior stipulation: Shabbos revenue to the non-Jew
  • No Yid at the front desk on Shabbos
  • Maaras ayin if the hotel carries the Yid's name → prefer neutral signage

9. Practical synthesis

The 4 lessons of Siman רמ״ה:
  1. Prior stipulation required — Shabbos profits to the non-Jew, counterpart on another day
  2. A late tnai is for the future only — not retroactive
  3. Maaras ayin — a visibly shared store on Shabbos = forbidden
  4. Automated industry — falls under siman רנ״ב

The 5 practical commandments of Siman רמ״ה

  1. Written prior stipulation at the very formation of the partnership
  2. Shabbos profits to the non-Jew alone — counterpart on another day
  3. No Yid active on Shabbos within the partnership
  4. Check maaras ayin — signage, visible presence
  5. Passive investment ≠ operational partnership (distinct case)
