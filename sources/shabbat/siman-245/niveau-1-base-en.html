A Jew and non-Jew in partnership

🌱 Introduction Level · מתחילים

The siman of the Jew/non-Jew partnership (shutfus). 19 seifim that codify how a Jew and a non-Jew may share a field, a business, an oven, or a workshop without the Yid drawing schar Shabbos (the profit of Shabbos). Three levers: (1) the prior stipulation (hisna mitechila), (2) profit taken b'havla'ah (absorbed), (3) whether or not the melacha rests on the Yid. Application: modern companies, stores open on Shabbos, rental income, startups.

General framework & key concepts

1. The general context

What is this siman about?

Siman רמ״ה deals with the partnership (shutfus) between a Yid and a non-Jew: a field worked together, a business, an oven, a bathhouse, a mill. The central problem is no longer that of siman רמ״ד (having a non-Jew do work) but that of the division of profits (chalukas revachim): if the enterprise runs on Shabbos — which is the norm — the Yid must not take any schar Shabbos (the profit of Shabbos).

QuestionAnswer
May the Yid take the profit-share of the day of Shabbos?No — except b'havla'ah or through a prior stipulation
How does one structure the partnership so the Yid takes no schar Shabbos?Hisna mitechila: Shabbos to the non-Jew, a weekday to the Yid
When is an equal split of the profits permitted?When the schar Shabbos is absorbed (b'havla'ah) and the melacha does not rest on the Yid
The central concern — שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת: the Yid may not draw profit from work done on Shabbos. If the non-Jew, by working on Shabbos, acts also on the Yid's behalf (k'shlucho), then the Yid draws schar Shabbos — forbidden. The whole siman is about determining when the non-Jew works for himself alone, and how the Yid may receive his share without the Shabbos being named or isolated.

2. Key concept 1 — שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת

שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת = "the profit of Shabbos". The profit that comes specifically from work done on the day of Shabbos. The halacha forbids the Yid to take it שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה (in an isolated, identified manner). The severity: if the Yid takes schar Shabbos explicitly, it reveals that the non-Jew was working also for him on Shabbos — as his agent (k'shlucho).
The tell-tale sign: the moment one names the day of Shabbos in the reckoning ("the Shabbos that you worked, I worked a weekday opposite it"), one reveals (megaleh da'ato) that the Shabbos share is reckoned in the Yid's name → schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah → forbidden.

3. Key concept 2 — בְּהַבְלָעָה

בְּהַבְלָעָה = "by absorption / inclusion". To take the schar Shabbos folded into the profit of the other days, without distinguishing or naming it. The opposite: שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה = taking it in isolation, by mentioning it. The halacha tolerates the schar Shabbos when absorbed, never when isolated.

ExampleA global split of the profit of all the days"For the Shabbos that you did, take 90 zuz"

4. Key concept 3 — הִתְנָה מִתְּחִלָּה

הִתְנָה מִתְּחִלָּה = "he stipulated from the start". To agree, at the very moment the partnership is formed, that the Shabbos profit will go to the non-Jew alone (הן רב הן מעט — whether much or little), and that an equivalent weekday will go to the Yid alone. This stipulation removes from the Yid any share in the Shabbos work: the non-Jew then no longer works for the Yid at all on Shabbos.
סְתָמָא (without a stipulation): when no stipulation was made, the Yid is a co-owner of each day's work, Shabbos included. To then come and reckon "weekday against Shabbos" reveals schar Shabbos. The remedy (Seif Tes-Vav): dissolve the partnership and reform it with a stipulation.

5. The 3 questions to ask about any partnership

To know whether the Yid may split the profits equally:
  1. Was there a prior stipulation? (hisna mitechila) — Shabbos to the non-Jew, a weekday to the Yid → permitted from the outset.
  2. Does the melacha rest on the Yid on Shabbos? — If yes and he compensates during the week, the non-Jew is his agent → forbidden. If no (the non-Jew acts for himself alone), the split b'havla'ah is tolerated.
  3. Is the schar Shabbos taken b'havla'ah? — Never name or isolate the Shabbos in the reckoning.

6. Seif Alef — The shared field: מעמיד פועל במקומו

Full text of the SHHaRav

יִשְׂרָאֵל וְנָכְרִי אֵיךְ יִתְנַהֲגוּ בְּשֻׁתָּפוּת וּבוֹ י"ט סְעִיפִים:

יִשְׂרָאֵל וְנָכְרִי שֶׁקִּבְּלוּ שָׂדֶה בְּשֻׁתָּפוּת שֶׁיִּהְיֶה מֻטָּל עַל שְׁנֵיהֶם לַעֲמוֹל לַחֲרוֹשׁ וְלִזְרוֹעַ וּשְׁאָר כָּל עֲבוֹדוֹת הַשָּׂדֶה וּכְשֶׁיַּגִּיעַ הַקָּצִיר יִטְּלוּ חֵלֶק בַּפֵּרוֹת בְּעַד עֲבוֹדָתָם — לֹא יֹאמַר הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְהַנָּכְרִי "עֲבוֹד אַתָּה בְּשַׁבָּת וּכְשֶׁיַּגִּיעַ הַקָּצִיר תִּטּוֹל חֶלְקְךָ בַּפֵּרוֹת כְּנֶגֶד עֲבוֹדָתְךָ גַּם בְּשַׁבָּתוֹת, וַאֲנִי אֶעֱבוֹד כְּנֶגֶד זֶה יוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל וְאֶטּוֹל חֶלְקִי בַּפֵּרוֹת כְּנֶגֶד עֲבוֹדָתִי בְּחֹל". אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי מִתְכַּוֵּן בַּעֲבוֹדָתוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת לְטוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ, וּמֻתָּר לוֹמַר לְנָכְרִי "עֲשֵׂה מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת לְצָרְכְּךָ" כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן ש"ז, מִכָּל מָקוֹם כֵּיוָן שֶׁעֲבוֹדַת הַשָּׂדֶה מֻטֶּלֶת בְּכָל יוֹם וְיוֹם עַל שְׁנֵיהֶם בְּשָׁוֶה חֲצִי יוֹם עַל זֶה וַחֲצִי יוֹם עַל זֶה, אִם כֵּן כְּשֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל אוֹמֵר לְהַנָּכְרִי שֶׁיַּעֲבוֹד בְּשַׁבָּת כָּל הַיּוֹם וְהוּא יַעֲבוֹד כְּנֶגֶד זֶה יוֹם אֶחָד שָׁלֵם בְּחֹל — הֲרֵי זֶה כְּמַעֲמִיד פּוֹעֵל בִּמְקוֹמוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה מְלַאכְתּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת:

English translation

A Jew and a non-Jew, how they should conduct themselves in partnership — contains 19 seifim:

A Yid and a non-Jew who took a field in partnership, where it rests on both of them to toil — to plow, to sow, and all the other field-work — and who at harvest will take a share of the produce in return for their labor: the Yid may not say to the non-Jew "You work on Shabbos, and at harvest you will take your share of the produce in return for your labor on Shabbos too; and I will work opposite a weekday and take my share of the produce in return for my weekday labor."

Even though the non-Jew intends his own benefit in working on Shabbos, and even though it is permitted to tell a non-Jew "do a melacha on Shabbos for your own need" (siman ש״ז) — nonetheless, since the field-work rests each day on both of them equally, half a day on this one and half a day on that one, when the Yid tells the non-Jew to work the whole Shabbos and he will work opposite a full weekday — it is as if he set up a worker in his place (מעמיד פועל במקומו) to do his melacha on Shabbos.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav opens the siman with the foundational case: a field worked in partnership, where each day's work rests on both equally. The question: may the Yid "trade" his Shabbos for one of the non-Jew's weekdays?

מַעֲמִיד פּוֹעֵל בִּמְקוֹמוֹ = "setting up a worker in his place". Since half of each day's work rests on the Yid, asking the non-Jew to cover his half of Shabbos amounts to commissioning him to work in his place on Shabbos. The non-Jew then becomes his agent (k'shlucho).

The halachic question

Why is this forbidden, when it is permitted to tell a non-Jew "work for your own need" (ש״ז)? Because here the work rests structurally on the Yid each day. By asking him to cover the Shabbos, the Yid is not telling him "act for yourself" — he is transferring to him his own obligation. That is shelichus.

The pesak of the Rav

The criterion: "does the work rest on the Yid on Shabbos?"
  1. A shared field = each day's work rests on both equally.
  2. Asking the non-Jew to cover the Shabbos = setting up a worker in his place → shelichus → forbidden.
  3. The fact that the non-Jew intends his own benefit does not save it: the Yid's share in the work remains.
  • A farming operation jointly held with a non-Jewish partner, work split equally each day: 🔴 saying "you handle Shabbos, I'll take a weekday" = מעמיד פועל במקומו.
  • An artisanal bakery in a 50/50 company, both present daily: 🔴 entrusting Shabbos to the partner against a weekday = forbidden as is.
  • A co-run professional practice, shifts in turn: 🔴 swapping his Shabbos shift for the non-Jewish partner's weekday shift = shelichus.
  • A market-garden farm, shared harvest: 🔴 "you work the Shabbosos, I'll make it up during the week" is forbidden; a prior stipulation is needed (Seif Gimmel).
  • Joint livestock raising, shared daily care: 🔴 same logic — the work rests on the Yid each day.

Points to remember

  • Criterion = does the work rest on the Yid each day? If so, trading Shabbos/weekday is forbidden
  • מעמיד פועל במקומו — the non-Jew becomes the Yid's agent for the Shabbos
  • "The non-Jew intends his own benefit" is not enough as long as the Yid's share in the work remains
Comprehension question: Why, when it is permitted to say "do a melacha for your own need" (ש״ז), is it forbidden to tell one's partner "work on Shabbos in my place"?

7. Seif Beis — Without a stipulation: the forbidden reckoning; מתירין / אוסרין

Full text of the SHHaRav

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם לֹא אָמַר לְהַנָּכְרִי כְּלוּם, אֶלָּא הַנָּכְרִי מֵעַצְמוֹ הָיָה עוֹבֵד כָּל יְמֵי הַשַּׁבָּתוֹת וְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל הָיָה עוֹבֵד כְּנֶגְדָּן בְּחֹל (בִּסְתָם, שֶׁלֹּא פֵרֵשׁ שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה כֵּן לִפְרוֹעַ לְהַנָּכְרִי שֶׁהִתְעַסֵּק בִּשְׁבִילוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת) — אָסוּר לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁיָּבֹא לְחֶשְׁבּוֹן עִם הַנָּכְרִי בִּשְׁעַת חֲלֻקַּת הַפֵּרוֹת וְלוֹמַר נַחֲלוֹק בְּשָׁוֶה, שֶׁכָּל יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת שֶׁעָבַדְתָּ עָבַדְתִּי כְּנֶגְדּוֹ בְּחֹל, לְפִי שֶׁבְּזֶה הוּא מְגַלֶּה דַעְתּוֹ שֶׁזֶּה שֶׁעָבַד יוֹם שָׁלֵם בְּחֹל עָבַד חֶצְיוֹ בִּשְׁבִיל הַנָּכְרִי כְּדֵי לִפְרוֹעַ לְהַנָּכְרִי שֶׁעָבַד בִּשְׁבִילוֹ חֲצִי יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, וְנִמְצָא עֲבוֹדַת חֲצִי יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת נֶחֱשֶׁבֶת עַל שְׁמוֹ, וַהֲרֵי זֶה נוֹטֵל שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה, שֶׁהֲרֵי מַזְכִּיר אֶת יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בְּפֵרוּשׁ, אֶלָּא יַנִּיחַ לְהַנָּכְרִי שֶׁמָּא יִרְצֶה יִטּוֹל לְעַצְמוֹ תְּחִלָּה כְּנֶגֶד כָּל הַשַּׁבָּתוֹת שֶׁעָבַד הוּא לְבַדּוֹ וְהַשְּׁאָר יַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ בְּשָׁוֶה.

וְאִם נִתְרַצָּה הַנָּכְרִי מֵאֵלָיו לַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ בְּשָׁוֶה בְּלֹא שֶׁיַּחְשׁוֹב עִמּוֹ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל יוֹם הַחֹל כְּנֶגֶד יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ נָטַל הַנָּכְרִי חֶלְקוֹ גַּם בְּעַד הַשַּׁבָּתוֹת שֶׁעָבַד וְהִנִּיחַ לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל חֶלְקוֹ גַּם בְּעַד יְמוֹת הַחֹל שֶׁעָבַד לְבַדּוֹ, אֶלָּא שֶׁעָשָׂה כֵּן מֵאֵלָיו בְּלֹא שֶׁיַּחְשׁוֹב עִמּוֹ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל — יֵשׁ מַתִּירִין, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁלֹּא גִלָּה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל עַצְמוֹ דַּעְתּוֹ בְּפֵרוּשׁ שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ חֵלֶק בַּעֲבוֹדַת הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּתוֹת — אֵין כַּאן שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת כְּלָל.

וְיֵשׁ אוֹסְרִין בִּשְׁנֵיהֶם, מִטַּעַם שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר. וְכֵן עִקָּר. וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, בִּמְקוֹם הֶפְסֵד גָּדוֹל יֵשׁ לִסְמוֹךְ עַל דִּבְרֵי הַמַּתִּירִין:

English translation

Even if he said nothing to the non-Jew, but the non-Jew of his own accord worked all the Shabbosos and the Yid worked opposite during the week (סְתָם — without specifying that he is doing so to repay the non-Jew for working on his behalf on Shabbos): it is forbidden for the Yid to come to a reckoning at the time of dividing the produce and say "let us split equally, for every Shabbos that you worked I worked opposite it during the week." For by this he reveals his da'as (megaleh da'ato): that whole weekday he worked, half of it was for the non-Jew, to repay him for working half a day of Shabbos for him — so the half-day of Shabbos is reckoned in his name, and he takes schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah, since he names the Shabbos explicitly. Rather, let him leave it to the non-Jew to take first, if he wishes, the equivalent of all the Shabbosos he worked alone, and the rest they split equally.

And if the non-Jew agreed on his own to split equally, without the Yid reckoning weekday against Shabbos, or even if the non-Jew took his share also for the Shabbosos and left the Yid his share also for the weekdays he worked alone, but he did so on his own initiative without the Yid's reckoning — some permit (יש מתירין), since the Yid did not explicitly reveal that he has a share in the non-Jew's Shabbos work → there is no schar Shabbos at all.

And some forbid in both cases (יש אוסרין), for a reason to be explained. And this is the main view (וכן עיקר). Nonetheless, in a case of great loss (hefsed gadol), one may rely on the view of those who permit.

What is this Seif about?

We move from the case where the Yid speaks (Seif Alef) to one where he says nothing: each one worked spontaneously (סְתָם). The question: at the time of the split, may one make the reckoning "weekday against Shabbos"?

מְגַלֶּה דַעְתּוֹ = "revealing one's da'as". Even without a prior agreement, the act of naming the Shabbos at the time of the split ("for your Shabbos, my weekday") retroactively reveals that the Yid considered himself a co-owner of the Shabbos work. That is what creates the schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah.

The halachic question

If the non-Jew splits on his own, without any reckoning by the Yid, is there still schar Shabbos? יש מתירין: no, because the Yid revealed nothing. יש אוסרין: yes, because in reality each was working for the other; the Rav rules like the osrin (וכן עיקר), except in a case of great loss.

The opinions

SourcePosition

יש אוסרין (Rishonim)Forbidden in both cases: in reality each worked for the other, the non-Jew was the Yid's agent on Shabbos.

The pesak of the Rav

Three levels:
  1. The Yid himself makes the weekday/Shabbos reckoning → forbidden according to all (schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah).
  2. The non-Jew splits on his own, without a reckoning → the matirin permit, but the Rav rules osrin (וכן עיקר).
  3. Hefsed gadol (great loss) → one may rely on the matirin.
  • A 50/50 farming company, at the annual close the Yid says "let's split equally, each of the partner's Shabbosos against a weekday of mine": 🔴 forbidden according to all.
  • The non-Jewish partner spontaneously offers an equal split, without any Shabbos calculation: 🟡 the matirin permit, but the Rav leans to forbid (וכן עיקר).
  • Imminent bankruptcy if the equal split is not accepted (hefsed gadol): 🟢 one may rely on the matirin.
  • An accountant who draws up the books flagging "the Yid's days / the non-Jew's days" with the Shabbos named: 🔴 reveals the da'as.
  • A flat-rate global split that never mentions the Shabbos: 🟡 safer, but belongs to the following cases (stipulation / b'havla'ah).

Points to remember

8. Seif Gimmel — התנה מתחילה: heter through stipulation

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְאִם מִתְּחִלָּה בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁקִּבְּלוּ הַשָּׂדֶה הִתְנָה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל עִם הַנָּכְרִי שֶׁבְּיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת לֹא יִהְיֶה מֻטָּל כְּלָל עַל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לַעֲבוֹד בַּשָּׂדֶה רַק עַל הַנָּכְרִי לְבַדּוֹ, וּבְיוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל יִהְיֶה מֻטָּל עַל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְבַדּוֹ — הֲרֵי זֶה מֻתָּר, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁהִתְנָה עִמּוֹ בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁקִּבֵּל עָלָיו עֲבוֹדַת הַשָּׂדֶה שֶׁלֹּא יִהְיֶה מֻטָּל עָלָיו כְּלָל בְּשַׁבָּת — נִמְצָא שֶׁבְּשַׁבָּת אֵין לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל חֵלֶק בַּשָּׂדֶה כְּלָל, אֶלָּא כֻּלָּהּ מֻטֶּלֶת עַל הַנָּכְרִי לְבַדּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, וְאֵין הַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה כְּלָל בְּעַד הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְכֻלָּהּ מֻטֶּלֶת עַל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְבַדּוֹ בְּיוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל.

וּלְפִיכָךְ, מֻתָּר לוֹ אַחַר כָּךְ לָבֹא עִמּוֹ בְּחֶשְׁבּוֹן לַחְשׁוֹב יְמֵי הַחֹל שֶׁעָבַד הוּא לְבַדּוֹ כְּנֶגֶד שַׁבָּתוֹת שֶׁעָבַד הַנָּכְרִי לְבַדּוֹ וְלַחֲלוֹק הַפֵּרוֹת בְּשָׁוֶה, וְאֵין זֶה נוֹטֵל שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת אֶלָּא שְׂכַר יְמֵי הַחֹל:

English translation

And if from the start, at the time they took the field, the Yid stipulated with the non-Jew that on the day of Shabbos it would not rest on the Yid at all to work the field, but only on the non-Jew, and that on one weekday it would rest on the Yid alone — this is permitted. For since he stipulated, at the time he took upon himself the field-work, that nothing would rest on him on Shabbos, it turns out that on Shabbos the Yid has no share at all in the field: it all rests on the non-Jew alone, and the non-Jew does nothing for the Yid; and it all rests on the Yid alone on one weekday.

Therefore, it is permitted afterwards for him to come to a reckoning and reckon the weekdays he worked alone against the Shabbosos the non-Jew worked alone, and split the produce equally: he is not taking schar Shabbos but the profit of a weekday.

What is this Seif about?

Here is the heart of the siman: the prior stipulation (hisna mitechila). By agreeing from the start that Shabbos rests only on the non-Jew and one weekday only on the Yid, one splits the days: the Yid structurally has no share in the Shabbos work.

The effect of the prior stipulation: it does not "redistribute" after the fact — it defines from the outset that the Shabbos work belongs to the non-Jew alone. As a result, the later reckoning is no longer "Shabbos against weekday" but "my weekday against his weekday": no schar Shabbos.

The halachic question

Why does the same reckoning, forbidden in Seif Beis, become permitted here? Because in Seif Beis the Yid was a co-owner of the Shabbos (he was merely repaying himself). Here, through the prior stipulation, the Shabbos was never his: the non-Jew does not work for the Yid, he works for his day. The reckoning therefore concerns the profit of a weekday, not of Shabbos.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Gemara / RishonimThe prior stipulation removes from the Yid the Shabbos share → permitted.
Mechaber (245:1)With a prior stipulation, permitted to split equally.
RemaLikewise; insists on the prior character of the stipulation.
Mishnah Berurah (245:7)The stipulation must be made בשעת השותפות, not afterwards.
SHHaRav (245:3)Logic: the prior stipulation makes it that "אין לישראל חלק בשדה כלל" on Shabbos; the reckoning becomes a weekday profit, not a Shabbos profit → permitted.

The pesak of the Rav

The prior stipulation — how it works:
  1. Stipulate at the moment the partnership is formed: Shabbos to the non-Jew alone, one weekday to the Yid alone.
  2. Result: the Yid has no share in the Shabbos work → the non-Jew does not act for him.
  3. The later equal split is permitted: it is a weekday profit, not a Shabbos profit.
  • Setting up a farming LLC with a non-Jewish partner: 🟢 articles stipulating that running the operation on Shabbos rests on the non-Jewish partner alone, and one weekday on the Yid alone.
  • A 50/50 services company, right at the drafting of the partnership agreement: 🟢 a clause "Saturday belongs to the non-Jewish partner alone; day X belongs to the Yid alone."
  • A bakery partnership, with the stipulation made at signing: 🟢 an equal split of the profits is then permitted.
  • A stipulation forgotten at formation, added a year later: 🔴 ineffective retroactively (see Seif Tes-Vav for the remedy).
  • A winery operation, with a clear prior stipulation: 🟢 the Yid takes his share without drawing any schar Shabbos.

Points to remember

  • הִתְנָה מִתְּחִלָּה — the stipulation must be made at the very moment the partnership is formed
  • Effect: the Shabbos rests only on the non-Jew → the Yid has no share in that work
  • The reckoning becomes a weekday profit, not a Shabbos profit → equal split permitted
Comprehension question: The same "weekday against Shabbos" reckoning is forbidden in Seif Beis and permitted in Seif Gimmel. What is the decisive difference?

9. Seif Daled — Business and workshop; the explicit reckoning forbidden

Full text of the SHHaRav

יִשְׂרָאֵל וְנָכְרִי שֶׁהָיוּ שֻׁתָּפִין בִּמְלָאכָה אוֹ בִּסְחוֹרָה בַּחֲנוּת, אִם הִתְנוּ מִתְּחִלָּה בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁנִּשְׁתַּתְּפוּ שֶׁיְּהֵא שְׂכַר יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת לְנָכְרִי בִּלְבַדּוֹ הֵן רַב הֵן מְעַט, וּשְׂכַר יוֹם אֶחָד אַחַר הַשַּׁבָּת כְּנֶגְדּוֹ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בִּלְבַדּוֹ — הֲרֵי זֶה מֻתָּר, מִטַּעַם שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר. וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם אַחַר כָּךְ נִתְרַצָּה הַנָּכְרִי לַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ כָּל הַשָּׂכָר בְּשָׁוֶה — אֵין בְּכָךְ כְּלוּם, שֶׁמַּתָּנָה הוּא שֶׁנּוֹתֵן לוֹ.

English translation

A Yid and a non-Jew who were partners in a workshop or in store merchandise: if they stipulated from the start, at the time they became partners, that the profit of the day of Shabbos would belong to the non-Jew alone — whether much or little (הן רב הן מעט) — and the profit of one day after Shabbos, opposite it, to the Yid alone — this is permitted, for the reason already explained. And even if afterwards the non-Jew agrees to split all the profit equally — that is no problem, for it is a gift (matanah) that he gives him.

But if they came to a reckoning afterwards, the non-Jew saying to the Yid "you took alone during the week a maneh, and I took on Shabbos 90 zuz, make it up to me until our shares are equal" — and all the more so if the Yid says this to the non-Jew — it turns out that the prior stipulation ("the Shabbos profit to the non-Jew alone, much or little") was nothing but a mere subterfuge (haarama b'alma): in reality the non-Jew was working on Shabbos also for the Yid, and in return the Yid was working during the week also for the non-Jew, hence the equal split afterwards. The Yid then also takes schar Shabbos, and shelo b'havla'ah, since the Shabbos is named explicitly. Therefore he must leave it to the non-Jew to take for himself, if he wishes, the profit of all the Shabbosos, and then they split equally.

What is this Seif about?

Application of the prior stipulation to business and the workshop (and no longer only the field). The Rav adds a warning: the prior stipulation must not be a sham. If it is circumvented by a "make it up to me" reckoning, it becomes a haarama.

הַעֲרָמָה בְּעָלְמָא = "mere subterfuge". A prior stipulation made for form's sake, but immediately emptied of meaning by a "make it up to me to equalize our shares" reckoning: one reveals that each was working also for the other, so the Yid does take schar Shabbos.

The halachic question

May the non-Jew give the Yid his Shabbos share? Yes, as a gift (matanah) — that is permitted. But if there is a reckoning ("make it up to me"), it is no longer a gift: it is an exchange of Shabbos work ↔ weekday, hence schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah. The boundary: a spontaneous gift (permitted) vs a reckoning that equalizes (forbidden).

The opinions

Mishnah Berurah (245:8)The gift (matanah) is permitted; the equalizing reckoning is forbidden.

The pesak of the Rav

The prior stipulation must remain real:
  1. Stipulation made + each keeps his day = permitted.
  2. The non-Jew gives spontaneously to the Yid a share = permitted (gift).
  3. A "make it up to me to equalize, since I took 90 on Shabbos" reckoning = haarama → forbidden; leave the non-Jew the profit of the Shabbosos, then split.

  • A balance sheet "you took X during the week, I took Y on Saturday, let's rebalance": 🔴 haarama — schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah.

10. Seif Heh — When the Shabbos profit is unknown (אינו ידוע)

Full text of the SHHaRav

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁהָיָה שְׂכַר הַשַּׁבָּת יָדוּעַ, אֲבָל אִם אֵינוֹ יָדוּעַ — יָכוֹל לַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ כָּל הַשָּׂכָר בְּשָׁוֶה, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהִתְנָה עִמּוֹ שֶׁיְּהֵא שְׂכַר יוֹם אֶחָד לוֹ לְבַדּוֹ כְּנֶגֶד שְׂכַר יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת שֶׁיִּטּוֹל הַנָּכְרִי לְבַדּוֹ, וּמִן הַסְּתָם שְׂכַר יוֹם אֶחָד שָׁוֶה לַחֲבֵרוֹ, לְפִיכָךְ חוֹלְקִין כָּל הַשָּׂכָר בְּשָׁוֶה:

English translation

In which case was this said? (במה דברים אמורים) — when the Shabbos profit was known. But if it is unknown (אינו ידוע), the Yid may split all the profit with him equally, since he stipulated that the profit of one day would be his alone against the profit of the day of Shabbos that the non-Jew takes alone; and by default (מן הסתם) the profit of one day equals that of another. Therefore they split all the profit equally.

What is this Seif about?

A clarification on Seif Daled: the warning against the reckoning only holds if the Shabbos profit is a known figure. If it is unknown (אינו ידוע), the equal split becomes permitted again — because we presume every day earns the same.

מִן הַסְּתָם שָׁוֶה = "by default, equal". When the precise Shabbos profit is unknown, the halacha presumes that the day of Shabbos (to the non-Jew) and the weekday (to the Yid) are equal. The equal split therefore isolates no schar Shabbos: each took "his" day, of presumed equal value.

The halachic question

Why does not knowing the amount change the din? Because the problem in Seif Daled was the numbered reckoning that isolated the schar Shabbos ("90 zuz"). If the amount is unknown, there is nothing to isolate: the equal split rests on the presumption that the days are equivalent, and remains b'havla'ah.

The opinions

RishonimUnknown profit → presumption of equivalence → equal split permitted.
Mechaber (245:1)When the Shabbos profit is not known, one splits equally.
RemaConcurs with the presumption that the days are equal.
Mishnah BerurahNot knowing the amount prevents isolating the schar Shabbos.
SHHaRav (245:5)במה דברים אמורים: the prohibition on the reckoning aims only at the known profit; unknown profit → מן הסתם שווה → equal split permitted.

The pesak of the Rav

Known vs unknown:
  1. Shabbos profit known (a figure) → equalizing reckoning forbidden (Seif Daled).
  2. Shabbos profit unknown → presumption that the days are equivalent → equal split permitted.
  3. The prior stipulation remains required in both cases.
  • A business where each day's takings fluctuate and are not isolated per day: 🟢 equal split presumed permitted (days equivalent).
  • A cash register that itemizes Saturday's takings down to the cent: 🔴 known profit → do not make an equalizing reckoning.
  • A consulting firm with smoothed turnover, no daily breakdown: 🟢 presumption of equivalence.
  • A platform that displays the precise Saturday revenue: 🔴 known profit → caution (leave the share to the non-Jew).
  • A farming operation where the day's yield is not measurable: 🟢 מן הסתם שווה → equal split.

Points to remember

  • The prohibition on the reckoning (Seif Daled) aims only at the known schar Shabbos
  • Unknown profit → presumption מן הסתם שווה → equal split permitted
  • The prior stipulation remains necessary in all cases

11. Seif Vav — Melacha without a stipulation: מתירין / אוסרין and the שביעית

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְאִם לֹא הִתְנוּ בַּתְּחִלָּה בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁנִּשְׁתַּתְּפוּ, וְגַם אַחַר שֶׁנִּשְׁתַּתְּפוּ לֹא אָמַר הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְהַנָּכְרִי שֶׁיִּתְעַסֵּק בְּשַׁבָּת וְהוּא יִתְעַסֵּק כְּנֶגֶד זֶה יוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל, אֶלָּא הַנָּכְרִי מֵעַצְמוֹ הִתְעַסֵּק בְּשַׁבָּת, וְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בִּלְבַדּוֹ הִתְעַסֵּק יוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל (סְתָם, שֶׁלֹּא פֵרֵשׁ שֶׁמִּתְעַסֵּק בִּלְבַדּוֹ בַּעֲבוּר שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי הִתְעַסֵּק בִּלְבַדּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת), אִם אַחַר כָּךְ נִתְרַצָּה הַנָּכְרִי לַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ כָּל הַשָּׂכָר בְּשָׁוֶה מֵאֵלָיו בְּלֹא שֶׁיַּחֲשׁוֹב עִמּוֹ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְגַם הַנָּכְרִי לֹא הִזְכִּיר לוֹ הַשַּׁבָּתוֹת כְּלָל אֶלָּא חָלַק עִמּוֹ סְתָם — יֵשׁ מַתִּירִין, שֶׁהֲרֵי אֵין כַּאן אִסּוּר מִשּׁוּם שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת, כֵּיוָן שֶׁנּוֹטְלוֹ בְּהַבְלָעָה עִם שְׁאָר הַיָּמִים.

וְיֵשׁ אוֹסְרִים, אֶלָּא יַנִּיחַ לְהַנָּכְרִי שְׂכַר כָּל הַשַּׁבָּתוֹת, וְאִם אֵין שְׂכַר הַשַּׁבָּתוֹת יָדוּעַ — יַנִּיחַ לוֹ שְׁבִיעִית הַשָּׂכָר וְהַשְּׁאָר יַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ בְּשָׁוֶה, שֶׁאִם יַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ כָּל הַשָּׂכָר בְּשָׁוֶה וְיֵהָנֶה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מִשְּׂכַר שַׁבָּת — נִמְצָא שֶׁלְּמַפְרֵעַ הָיָה לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל חֵלֶק בַּעֲבוֹדַת הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּתוֹת, וְהָיָה הַנָּכְרִי עוֹבֵד בִּשְׁבִילוֹ כְּאִלּוּ הָיָה שְׁלוּחוֹ. וְכֵן עִקָּר. אִם לֹא בְּהֶפְסֵד גָּדוֹל, שֶׁאָז יֵשׁ לִסְמוֹךְ עַל סְבָרָא הָרִאשׁוֹנָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר:

English translation

And if they did not stipulate at the start, and after the partnership too the Yid did not tell the non-Jew to work on Shabbos in exchange for a weekday, but the non-Jew worked on Shabbos of his own accord and the Yid alone worked one weekday (סְתָם, without specifying that it is in exchange): if afterwards the non-Jew agrees on his own to split all the profit equally, without the Yid's reckoning, and without even mentioning the Shabbosos — some permit (יש מתירין), for there is no schar Shabbos prohibition here, since he takes it b'havla'ah with the other days.

And some forbid (יש אוסרין): rather, let him leave the non-Jew the profit of all the Shabbosos; and if the profit of the Shabbosos is unknown — let him leave him a seventh of the profit (שביעית) and split the rest equally. For if he split all of it equally and benefited from the schar Shabbos, it would turn out retroactively that the Yid had a share in the non-Jew's work on the Shabbosos, and the non-Jew was working for him as if he were his agent (k'ilu hayah shelucho). And this is the main view (וכן עיקר), except in a case of great loss, where one may rely on the first view.

What is this Seif about?

The case where the partnership involves melacha (real work), without a prior stipulation, each having acted spontaneously. The new point: the numerical solution for an unknown schar Shabbos — the seventh (שביעית), one day out of seven.

שְׁבִיעִית הַשָּׂכָר = "a seventh of the profit". When the schar Shabbos is unknown, it is estimated to represent one day out of seven (the Shabbos out of the week). The Yid therefore leaves the non-Jew a seventh of the total profit, then splits the rest equally — thus taking no schar Shabbos.

The halachic question

When there is melacha, does the spontaneous split suffice (b'havla'ah)? יש מתירין: yes, the schar Shabbos is absorbed. יש אוסרין: no, because retroactively the non-Jew was the Yid's agent on Shabbos — one must leave him the profit of the Shabbosos, or its estimate at a seventh. The Rav rules osrin (וכן עיקר), except in great loss.

The opinions

SourcePosition
יש מתיריןSpontaneous split = schar Shabbos b'havla'ah → permitted.
יש אוסריןRetroactively the non-Jew was the Yid's agent; leave him the profit of the Shabbosos (or the שביעית).
Mechaber (245:1)Leave the non-Jew the schar Shabbos; failing an amount, a seventh.
Mishnah BerurahThe seventh corresponds to the Shabbos out of the week; hefsed gadol relaxes it.
SHHaRav (245:6)Rules osrin (וכן עיקר); unknown profit → leave the שביעית; hefsed gadol → rely on the matirin.

The pesak of the Rav

Melacha without a stipulation:
  1. The spontaneous equal split is disputed: מתירין (b'havla'ah) vs אוסרין (retroactive shelichus).
  2. The Rav rules osrin: leave the non-Jew the profit of the Shabbosos.
  3. Unknown profit → leave the seventh (שביעית); in hefsed gadol, rely on the matirin.
  • A workshop partnership with no initial clause, the non-Jew worked the Saturdays: 🔴 the Yid leaves the non-Jew the Saturdays' profit before splitting the rest.
  • Saturday's profit not quantifiable: 🟢 leave the seventh of the total profit to the non-Jew, split the rest.
  • Heavy loss if one does not split equally (hefsed gadol): 🟢 rely on the matirin (split b'havla'ah).
  • A production company where the non-Jewish partner covered the Saturdays alone: 🔴 without a prior stipulation, deduct the seventh.
  • A partnership where each worked spontaneously, split proposed by the non-Jew: 🟡 the Rav leans to forbid (וכן עיקר) except in great loss.

Points to remember

  • Melacha without a stipulation: מתירין (b'havla'ah) / אוסרין (shelichus); the Rav rules osrin
  • Unknown schar Shabbos → leave the non-Jew the שביעית (a seventh)
  • Hefsed gadol → rely on the matirin
Comprehension question: Why, absent a known amount, is precisely a seventh of the profit the figure assigned to the non-Jew?

12. Seif Zayin — Partnership without melacha: taking the profit (לדברי הכל)

Full text of the SHHaRav

אֲבָל אִם הָיוּ שֻׁתָּפִין בְּדָבָר שֶׁאֵין צָרִיךְ לַעֲשׂוֹת בּוֹ שׁוּם מְלָאכָה, כְּגוֹן שֶׁהָיוּ שֻׁתָּפִין בְּתַנּוּר שֶׁכָּל מִי שֶׁמַּסִּיק הַתַּנּוּר וְאוֹפֶה בּוֹ נוֹתֵן לָהֶם שָׂכָר, וְאֵין מֻטָּל עֲלֵיהֶם כְּלָל עֵסֶק הֶסֵּק הַתַּנּוּר רַק עֵסֶק קַבָּלַת הַשָּׂכָר מֻטָּל עַל שְׁנֵיהֶם בְּשָׁוֶה, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁלֹּא הִתְנוּ בַּתְּחִלָּה בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁנִּשְׁתַּתְּפוּ שֶׁיְּהֵא עֵסֶק קַבָּלַת הַשָּׂכָר מֻטָּל עַל הַנָּכְרִי לְבַדּוֹ שֶׁיְּקַבֵּל לְעַצְמוֹ שְׂכַר הַשַּׁבָּת הֵן רַב הֵן מְעַט וּשְׂכַר יוֹם אֶחָד לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְבַדּוֹ, אִם אַחַר כָּךְ לֹא אָמַר לְהַנָּכְרִי שֶׁיִּתְעַסֵּק לְבַדּוֹ לְקַבֵּל שְׂכַר הַשַּׁבָּת וְשֶׁיִּתְעַסֵּק הוּא לְבַדּוֹ לְקַבֵּל שְׂכַר יוֹם אַחֵר, אֶלָּא הַנָּכְרִי מֵעַצְמוֹ הִתְעַסֵּק לְבַדּוֹ לְקַבֵּל שְׂכַר הַשַּׁבָּת וְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְבַדּוֹ הִתְעַסֵּק בְּיוֹם אַחֵר, וְאַחַר כָּךְ בִּשְׁעַת חֲלֻקָּה נִתְרַצָּה הַנָּכְרִי מֵאֵלָיו לַחֲלוֹק כָּל הַשָּׂכָר בְּשָׁוֶה — הֲרֵי זֶה מֻתָּר לְדִבְרֵי הַכֹּל, שֶׁכַּאן אֵין לוֹמַר מִשּׁוּם שֶׁנִּמְצָא שֶׁלְּמַפְרֵעַ הָיָה לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל חֵלֶק בַּעֲבוֹדַת הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּתוֹת וְהָיָה הַנָּכְרִי עוֹבֵד בִּשְׁבִילוֹ, שֶׁהֲרֵי כַּאן לֹא עָבַד הַנָּכְרִי כְּלוּם, רַק שֶׁקִּבֵּל שְׂכַר הַשַּׁבָּת, וְשָׂכָר זֶה נוֹטְלוֹ מִמֶּנּוּ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בְּהַבְלָעָה שֶׁבִּשְׁאָר הַיָּמִים:

English translation

But if they were partners in something that requires no melacha — for example partners in an oven, where anyone who heats it and bakes in it pays them a fee, with no work of heating resting on them, only the collection of the fee resting on the two equally — then even though they did not stipulate at the start that the collection of the Shabbos fee would belong to the non-Jew alone and that of another day to the Yid alone: if afterwards the Yid did not tell the non-Jew to collect the Shabbos fee alone, but the non-Jew on his own collected the Shabbos fee and the Yid that of another day, and then at the split the non-Jew agreed on his own to split everything equally — this is permitted according to all (לדברי הכל). For here one cannot say that retroactively the Yid had a share in the non-Jew's work and the non-Jew was working for him: the non-Jew did no work at all, he merely collected the Shabbos fee — and that fee the Yid takes from him b'havla'ah with the other days.

What is this Seif about?

A major turning point of the siman: we leave the cases of melacha for those where the partnership concerns only the collection of passive income (renting out an oven). When there is no work, there is no possible shelichus — so the spontaneous split is permitted even according to the osrin.

שֻׁתָּפוּת בְּלֹא מְלָאכָה = "partnership without work". The asset (the oven) generates a fee; the partners merely collect it. Since neither of them "works" on Shabbos, the non-Jew cannot be the Yid's agent. The only residual concern is schar Shabbos — resolved by havla'ah.

The halachic question

Why is this case permitted לדברי הכל, whereas the previous one (Seif Vav) was disputed? Because the osrin's argument ("retroactively, the non-Jew was your agent") presupposes work. Here there is no work, so that argument falls away: only the schar Shabbos remains, neutralized by havla'ah.

The opinions

SourcePosition
יש אוסרין (of Seif Vav)Their objection (retroactive shelichus) presupposes melacha; without work, it does not apply.
Mechaber (245:3)Partnership without work (rented oven): spontaneous split permitted; the Shabbos fee is b'havla'ah.
Mishnah BerurahThe key distinction: melacha (machlokes) vs mere קבלת שכר (permitted לדברי הכל).
SHHaRav (245:7)Permitted לדברי הכל: without work, no shelichus; the Yid takes the Shabbos fee b'havla'ah with the other days.

The pesak of the Rav

Without melacha, the prohibition collapses:
  1. The non-Jew merely collected a fee, he did not work → he cannot be the Yid's agent.
  2. The spontaneous split is therefore permitted even according to the osrin (לדברי הכל).
  3. It only remains to take the Shabbos fee b'havla'ah with the other days.
  • Commercial premises held jointly with a non-Jew, rented to third parties: 🟢 collect the rents (Shabbos included) b'havla'ah, spontaneous split permitted.
  • A vending machine co-owned by a Yid/non-Jew, takings collected by the partner: 🟢 no melacha, splitting the overall figure permitted לדברי הכל.
  • A shared parking lot, the non-Jew empties the meter on Saturday: 🟢 mere collection of income, b'havla'ah.
  • A shared oven/kitchen rented by the hour, the non-Jewish partner collects on Saturday: 🟢 the Rav's exact case — permitted לדברי הכל.
  • But if the partners must themselves operate the machine (melacha): 🟡 we fall back on the machlokes of the previous seifim.

Points to remember

  • Without melacha (mere קבלת שכר) → no shelichus → split permitted לדברי הכל
  • The osrin's objection (retroactive shelichus) presupposes work; without work, it falls away
  • The Shabbos fee is always taken b'havla'ah with the other days
Comprehension question: Why does the absence of work (melacha) suffice to make the split permitted even according to the osrin, who forbade in Seif Vav?

13. Seif Ches — Melacha without a "counterpart": שאינו פורע כלום

Full text of the SHHaRav

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם הֵם שֻׁתָּפִין בְּדָבָר שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לַעֲשׂוֹת בּוֹ מְלָאכָה, אִם אֵין הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל עוֹשֶׂה בְּחֹל כְּנֶגֶד מַה שֶּׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת, אֶלָּא כָּל יְמוֹת הַחֹל הֵם מִתְעַסְּקִים בְּשָׁוֶה שְׁנֵיהֶם בְּיַחַד, אוֹ זֶה חֲצִי יוֹם וְזֶה חֲצִי יוֹם, אוֹ זֶה יוֹם וְזֶה יוֹם, לְבַד מִיּוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי מִתְעַסֵּק בּוֹ לְבַדּוֹ — אֲזַי מֻתָּר לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ כָּל הַשָּׂכָר בְּשָׁוֶה לְדִבְרֵי הַכֹּל, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁאֵין הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל פּוֹרֵעַ לְהַנָּכְרִי כְּלוּם בְּעַד עֲבוֹדָתוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁהֲרֵי אֵינוֹ עוֹבֵד כְּנֶגֶד זֶה בְּחֹל כְּלוּם, אִם כֵּן מַה שֶׁעוֹבֵד הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת לְבַדּוֹ הוּא מִתְכַּוֵּן לְטוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ בִּלְבָד כְּדֵי לְהִשְׂתַּכֵּר, וְאֵינוֹ כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, שֶׁאֵינוֹ עוֹשֶׂה בִּשְׁבִילוֹ כְּלָל. וְאַף שֶׁאַחַר כָּךְ מַגִּיעַ לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל הֲנָאָה מִזֶּה שֶׁחוֹלֵק עִמּוֹ בְּשָׁוֶה — אֵין בְּכָךְ כְּלוּם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי לֹא נִתְכַּוֵּן לַהֲנָאַת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל אֶלָּא לְטוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, יִזָּהֵר שֶׁלֹּא לַחֲלוֹק שְׂכַר הַשַּׁבָּת אֶלָּא בְּהַבְלָעָה עִם שְׁאָר הַיָּמִים, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמֵּעִקַּר הַדִּין אֵין כַּאן אִסּוּר מִשּׁוּם שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת, שֶׁהֲרֵי אֵין לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל חֵלֶק כְּלָל בִּמְלֶאכֶת הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת וּמַה שֶּׁחוֹלֵק עִמּוֹ בְּשָׁוֶה מַתָּנָה הוּא שֶׁנּוֹתֵן לוֹ, מִכָּל מָקוֹם יֵשׁ לֶאֱסוֹר, לְפִי שֶׁנִּרְאֶה הַדָּבָר כְּאִלּוּ הוּא נוֹטֵל שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת, כֵּיוָן שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ חֵלֶק בְּאוֹתוֹ דָבָר שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה בּוֹ הַמְּלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת:

English translation

And even if they are partners in something that requires melacha: if the Yid does not work a weekday opposite the Shabbos worked by the non-Jew, but all the weekdays they work together equally (or each a half-day, or each a full day), except the Shabbos when the non-Jew works alone — then the Yid may split all the profit equally according to all. For the Yid repays nothing to the non-Jew for his Shabbos work (since he works nothing opposite it); so what the non-Jew does on Shabbos, he intends for his own benefit alone, and he is not the Yid's agent, doing nothing at all for him. And even if the Yid then derives a benefit by splitting equally — that is no problem, for the non-Jew did not intend the Yid's benefit but his own.

Nonetheless, let him take care to split the Shabbos profit only b'havla'ah with the other days. Although, on the fundamental din, there is no schar Shabbos prohibition here — the Yid having no share in the non-Jew's Shabbos melacha, and the equal split being a gift (matanah) he gives him — it is nonetheless proper to forbid (the non-b'havla'ah split), because it has the appearance of taking schar Shabbos, since he has a share in the very thing in which the non-Jew does the melacha on Shabbos.

What is this Seif about?

A subtle case: there is indeed melacha, but the Yid never "makes up" the Shabbos with a weekday. The non-Jew covers the Shabbos without a counterpart. The decisive criterion: shaeino poreia — the Yid repays nothing.

אֵינוֹ פּוֹרֵעַ כְּלוּם = "he repays nothing". It was the "making up" (a weekday opposite the Shabbos) that made the non-Jew the Yid's agent. If that making-up does not exist, the non-Jew works for himself alone on Shabbos → no shelichus. The equal split is merely a gift.

The halachic question

Is there shelichus when the non-Jew works on Shabbos without the Yid "returning" a weekday to him? No: without repayment, the non-Jew's intent is purely self-interested. The din therefore permits לדברי הכל. There remains a rabbinic level of precaution: by appearance (k'ilu notel schar Shabbos), still require havla'ah.

The opinions

Mishnah BerurahThe criterion is repayment (poreia); absent it, no shelichus.
SHHaRav (245:8)Permitted לדברי הכל since shaeino poreia; but, having a share in the thing, let him take the Shabbos profit b'havla'ah (מפני מראית עין / appearance).

The pesak of the Rav

Two levels to distinguish:
  1. On the fundamental din (ikar hadin): the Yid repays nothing → no shelichus → equal split = gift, permitted לדברי הכל.
  2. As a precaution (maaras ayin): having a share in the asset, the split of the Shabbos profit must be done b'havla'ah with the other days.
  • A 50/50 company where both work together during the week, the non-Jewish partner covers Saturday alone without a counterpart: 🟢 split permitted לדברי הכל, b'havla'ah.
  • A restaurant partnership, Saturday service run by the non-Jewish partner alone, the Yid does not recoup another day: 🟢 shaeino poreia → permitted b'havla'ah.
  • But the Yid takes Saturday's figure alone, separately, naming it: 🔴 non-b'havla'ah → appearance of schar Shabbos.
  • A co-run practice, the non-Jewish partner covers Saturday emergencies for free: 🟢 global split b'havla'ah permitted.
  • A shared store, the Yid never works Sunday "in exchange" for Saturday: 🟢 no making-up → no shelichus.

  • But by appearance (having a share in the asset) → take the Shabbos profit b'havla'ah

15. Seif Yod — Bathhouse, mill, store: the day-against-Shabbos reckoning

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְכֵן הַדִּין בְּמֶרְחָץ וְרֵחַיִם וַחֲנוּת.

אִם אֵין הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל חוֹלֵק עִם הַנָּכְרִי שְׂכַר כָּל הַיָּמִים בְּשָׁוֶה, אֶלָּא כָּל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁל יוֹם אֶחָד לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל הֵן רַב הֵן מְעַט, וְכָל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁל יוֹם ב' לְהַנָּכְרִי הֵן רַב הֵן מְעַט — אָסוּר לוֹ לִטּוֹל שְׂכַר יוֹם אֶחָד כְּנֶגֶד שְׂכַר יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת שֶׁנָּטַל הַנָּכְרִי לְעַצְמוֹ, שֶׁאִם יִטּוֹל כָּל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁל יוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל כְּנֶגֶד יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת — נִמְצָא שֶׁטָּרַח הַנָּכְרִי לְהִתְעַסֵּק בַּתַּנּוּר בְּאוֹתוֹ הַיּוֹם שֶׁל חֹל בְּעַד שְׂכַר יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת שֶׁנָּטַל כֻּלּוֹ לְעַצְמוֹ, וַהֲרֵי נֶהֱנֶה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מִטֹּרַח זֶה שֶׁהוּא שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה:

And the same din applies to a bathhouse, a mill, and a store.

If the Yid does not split with the non-Jew the profit of all the days equally, but all the profit of one day goes to the Yid (whatever the amount) and all the profit of another day to the non-Jew (whatever the amount) — it is forbidden for him to take the profit of one day opposite the profit of the day of Shabbos that the non-Jew took for himself. What is this Seif about?

An extension to other assets (bathhouse, mill, store), then a return to the forbidden case: no longer an equal day-by-day split, but the assignment of whole days opposite one another. There, "my day against your Shabbos" reintroduces schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah — because the non-Jew "toils" a weekday in exchange for his Shabbos.

יוֹם כְּנֶגֶד יוֹם (without havla'ah) = "a whole day against a day". When each day is assigned in full to one or the other, taking "your day of Shabbos against my weekday" amounts to being paid for the non-Jew's Shabbos work → schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah.

The halachic question

Why does splitting by whole days (rather than equally each day) tip back into the prohibition? Because there is no longer any havla'ah: the Shabbos profit is isolated and exchanged for a weekday, so the Yid directly benefits from the non-Jew's Shabbos toil.

16. Seif Yod-Alef — The ineffective stipulation: one needs a חלק בגוף הדבר

English translation

And even if he stipulated this with him from the start, before becoming his partner in the oven's profit — it does not help at all. For the stipulation is effective only when the non-Jew has a share in the very thing (chelek b'guf hadavar) in which the melacha is done: then, by stipulating from the start that the Shabbos profit will be the non-Jew's alone and that of one day the Yid's alone, it is as if he transfers to him his share in the thing, so that the whole thing is acquired by the non-Jew alone on all the Shabbosos and, opposite, by the Yid alone on one day.

But here, where the whole body of the oven belongs to the Yid, even though he stipulated that the oven would be the non-Jew's alone on Shabbos — he did not transfer it to him by a full and absolute acquisition (kinyan gamur lachalutin) for all the Shabbosos, valid even if he does not work the oven during the week in exchange; he transferred it to him on condition (al tnai), provided that he work during the week opposite the Shabbos. This is therefore as if he rents it to him for the Shabbosos in exchange for his working during the week for him — and behold, he takes schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah.

What is this Seif about?

The Yid tries to "rescue" the forbidden case with a prior stipulation ("the oven is yours on Shabbos"). The Rav explains why this fails here: the stipulation is effective only if the non-Jew already holds a share in the asset. On an asset belonging entirely to the Yid, the "conditional" transfer is merely a disguised rental → schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah.

חֵלֶק בְּגוּף הַדָּבָר = "a share in the body of the thing". The stipulation that "assigns the Shabbos to the non-Jew" works only if it exchanges real shares between co-owners. If the asset is 100% the Yid's, giving "the Shabbos" to the non-Jew on condition of work = a rental (k'maskiro), not a transfer — hence schar Shabbos.

The halachic question

Does a prior stipulation always render the split permitted? No. It requires a true transfer of a share (kinyan). On an asset belonging to the Yid alone, "assigning the Shabbos to the non-Jew on condition" remains a conditional rental: the Yid rents out his oven for the Shabbos in exchange for the non-Jew's weekday work → schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah.

SHHaRav (245:11)The stipulation is ineffective on an asset belonging to the Yid alone: lacking a chelek b'guf hadavar, it is k'maskiro → schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah.

The pesak of the Rav

When the prior stipulation works (and when not):
  1. If the non-Jew already has a share in the asset (co-ownership) → the stipulation exchanges shares → permitted.
  2. If the asset is entirely the Yid's → "the Shabbos is yours on condition" = a rental → schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah → forbidden.
  3. Key distinction: kinyan gamur lachalutin (full transfer) vs kinyan al tnai (conditional transfer = rental).
  • An oven/workshop 100% the Yid's, a "contract" saying Saturday belongs to the non-Jew: 🔴 disguised rental → schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah.
  • A company where the non-Jew is a 50% co-owner and one stipulates "Saturday is yours": 🟢 a real exchange of shares → permitted (cf. Seif Gimmel/Daled).
  • The Yid's store rented to a non-Jew with a clause "Saturday for you, against your Tuesday work": 🔴 conditional transfer = rental.
  • A full and absolute transfer of a share in the asset (without a work condition): 🟢 kinyan gamur → no more shelichus.
  • An asset that is the Yid's own, a mere verbal promise to "assign the Shabbos": 🔴 ineffective — no share transferred.

Points to remember

  • The prior stipulation operates only if there is a chelek b'guf hadavar (a real share in the asset)
  • On an asset entirely the Yid's, "the Shabbos is yours on condition" = rental → schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah
  • Distinguish kinyan gamur lachalutin (full transfer, permitted) from kinyan al tnai (conditional = rental)

17. Seif Yod-Beis — Renting 2-3 days b'havla'ah with a share in the asset

Full text of the SHHaRav

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אֵין תְּנַאי זֶה מוֹעִיל אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁהִקְנָה לוֹ חֵלֶק בְּגוּף הַתַּנּוּר, דְּהַיְנוּ שֶׁבְּיָמִים אֵלּוּ שֶׁהַשַּׁבָּת בְּתוֹכָם גּוּף הַתַּנּוּר הוּא קָנוּי לְהַנָּכְרִי, שֶׁנִּמְצָא הוּא עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בַּתַּנּוּר שֶׁלּוֹ, וְאֵינוֹ נִרְאֶה כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל. אֲבָל אִם לֹא הִקְנָה לוֹ חֵלֶק בְּגוּף הַתַּנּוּר — אָסוּר לוֹמַר לוֹ שֶׁיִּטּוֹל לוֹ שְׂכַר יָמִים אֵלּוּ שֶׁהַשַּׁבָּת בְּתוֹכָם בְּעַד שֶׁיַּסִּיק לוֹ בְּחֹל, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁמַּסִּיק בְּשַׁבָּת בְּתַנּוּרוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל וְיֵשׁ רֶוַח לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּהֶסֵּק הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁאִם לֹא הָיָה מַסִּיק בְּשַׁבָּת לְעַצְמוֹ לֹא הָיָה מַסִּיק לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּחֹל — הֲרֵי נִרְאֶה כְּאִלּוּ מַסִּיק בְּשַׁבָּת בִּשְׁבִיל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל וּבִשְׁלִיחוּתוֹ שֶׁצִּוָּהוּ לַעֲסוֹק בְּשַׁבָּת:

English translation

But it is permitted for him to stipulate with the non-Jew (from the start) that he will take for himself the profit of 2 or 3 days and that opposite this he will give the Yid the profit of 2 or 3 other days — even if the Shabbos is among the days the non-Jew takes and the Yid takes opposite: there is no schar Shabbos prohibition in this, since he takes it b'havla'ah with another day.

Nonetheless, this stipulation is effective only if he transferred to him a share in the body of the oven (chelek b'guf hatanur): that is, during those days in which the Shabbos is included, the body of the oven is acquired by the non-Jew, so that he does the melacha in his own oven and does not appear to be the Yid's agent. But if he did not transfer to him a share in the body of the oven — it is forbidden to tell him to take the profit of those days in which the Shabbos is included in exchange for heating for him during the week. For since he heats on Shabbos the oven of the Yid and the Yid has a profit (revach) from the Shabbos heating — for if he did not heat on Shabbos for himself he would not heat during the week for the Yid — it appears as if he heats on Shabbos for the Yid, on his commission (bishlichuso).

What is this Seif about?

The permitted counterpart of the previous seif: one may group the profit into blocks of 2-3 days (Shabbos included) b'havla'ah, under two conditions: (1) havla'ah (the Shabbos folded into a block), and (2) a real share in the asset for those days, failing which the Yid's profit (revach) reveals shelichus.

רֶוַח = "actual profit". If "without the Shabbos heating, no weekday heating for the Yid," then the Yid draws a real gain from the Shabbos melacha → appearance of commission. Transferring a share in the asset neutralizes this: the non-Jew works in his oven.

The halachic question

How does one make permissible "the non-Jew takes the days with Shabbos, the Yid other days"? One must combine: havla'ah (the Shabbos in a block, never isolated) and kinyan b'guf (a real share in the asset). Without the share, the Yid's revach makes the non-Jew appear as his agent.

18. Seif Yod-Gimmel — Melacha resting also on the Yid: renting b'havla'ah

Full text of the SHHaRav

אֲפִלּוּ אִם הַמְּלָאכָה מֻטֶּלֶת גַּם עַל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, כְּגוֹן שֶׁהֵם שֻׁתָּפִים בְּתַנּוּר אוֹ בְּמֶרְחָץ אוֹ בְּרֵחַיִם אוֹ בִּמְלָאכָה אוֹ בִּסְחוֹרָה בַּחֲנוּת — אֵין אִסּוּר כְּשֶׁלֹּא הִתְנוּ מִתְּחִלָּה, אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁהוּא עוֹבֵד לְבַדּוֹ בְּיוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל כְּנֶגֶד הַשַּׁבָּת שֶׁעָבַד הַנָּכְרִי לְבַדּוֹ וְנוֹטֵל כָּל שְׂכַר יוֹם הַהוּא לְעַצְמוֹ, אֲבָל אִם כָּל יְמוֹת הַחֹל הֵם שָׁוִים בַּעֲבוֹדָה וּבְשָׂכָר יָכוֹל לְהַשְׂכִּיר כָּל הַשַּׁבָּתות לַנָּכְרִי בְּהַבְלָעָה, שֶׁיַּעֲבוֹד לְבַדּוֹ ב' אוֹ ג' יָמִים וְיִטּוֹל כָּל הַשָּׂכָר לְעַצְמוֹ הֵן רַב הֵן מְעַט, רַק שֶׁיִּתֵּן לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל דָּבָר קָצוּב בְּעַד ב' אוֹ ג' יָמִים אֵלּוּ, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהַשַּׁבָּת בִּכְלָלָם — אֵין בְּכָךְ כְּלוּם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁאֵינוֹ מְצַוֶּה לוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת, וְהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה מֵעַצְמוֹ לְטוֹבָתוֹ.

English translation

Even if the melacha rests also on the Yid — for example if they are partners in an oven, a bathhouse, a mill, a melacha, or store merchandise — there is no prohibition, absent a prior stipulation, except when he works alone one weekday opposite the Shabbos worked alone by the non-Jew and takes all that day's profit for himself. When the work rests also on the Yid, may one still "entrust" the Shabbos to the non-Jew? Yes, by renting b'havla'ah against a fixed amount, without an order to work on Shabbos. And maaras ayin, which elsewhere forbids public renting, falls away in a partnership with a non-Jew, where the non-Jew's share is well known.

19. Seif Yod-Daled — Hiring the non-Jewish partner בקבלנות

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְכֵן יָכוֹל לִשְׂכּוֹר אֶת הַנָּכְרִי שֻׁתָּפוֹ בְּקַבְּלָנוּת, דְּהַיְנוּ שֶׁיַּעֲבוֹד הַנָּכְרִי גַּם בְּעַד חֵלֶק הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וּבְעַד כָּךְ וְכָךְ שָׂכָר שֶׁיִּשְׂתַּכֵּר לְחֶלְקוֹ יִתֵּן לוֹ כָּךְ וְכָךְ, רַק שֶׁלֹּא יֹאמַר לוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת, אֶלָּא יֹאמַר לוֹ כָּל אֵימַת שֶׁתִּשְׂתַּכֵּר לְחֶלְקִי כָּךְ וְכָךְ אֶתֵּן לְךָ כָּךְ וְכָךְ, וְהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה לְעַצְמוֹ אֲפִלּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ד, שֶׁאֵין בְּזֶה אִסּוּר אֶלָּא מִשּׁוּם מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, וְכַאן שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי הוּא שֻׁתָּפוֹ — אֵין כַּאן חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית הָעַיִן:

English translation

Likewise, he may hire his non-Jewish partner b'kablanus (by contract): that is, the non-Jew will work also for the Yid's share, and for such-and-such a profit that he generates for that share, the Yid will give him such-and-such. Only, he must not tell him to work on Shabbos; rather he should say to him: "whenever you generate such-and-such a profit for my share, I will give you such-and-such," and the non-Jew works for himself even on Shabbos, as explained in siman רמ״ד. For there is no prohibition in this except for maaras ayin — and here, the non-Jew being his partner, there is no concern of maaras ayin.

What is this Seif about?

An application of the great principle of siman רמ״ד (contract work, kablanus) to the partner: the Yid may hire by contract his non-Jewish partner to work also his share, without ordering him the Shabbos. The non-Jew acts for his own benefit (adata d'nafshei). The sole obstacle of the kablan — maaras ayin — disappears in the partnership.

קַבְּלָנוּת = "contract work" (siman רמ״ד). Paid by task / by result, the non-Jew acts for his benefit, chooses his hours (Shabbos included), and is not the Yid's agent. The only reservation of רמ״ד is maaras ayin — lifted here because the partnership is well known.

The halachic question

May one apply the lenient regime of contract work (רמ״ד) to a non-Jewish partner? Yes: one hires him by contract for his own share, without amira to work on Shabbos. And the sole reservation of contract work (maaras ayin) falls away, since all know the non-Jew is a partner working for himself.

20. Seif Tes-Vav — Repairing a forgotten stipulation: dissolve and reform

It is permitted to give a non-Jew merchandise to sell, or money to do business with; and even though the non-Jew deals with it on Shabbos — that is no problem, since the Yid did not order him to deal with it on Shabbos. What is this Seif about?

Entrusting merchandise or capital to a non-Jew (trade, management). Two conditions: (1) no amira to work on Shabbos; (2) the non-Jew is invested in the result (a wage for his toil or a share of the profit), so that he acts for himself. The Yid is no longer the principal: he benefits passively from the Shabbos profit, taken b'havla'ah. And since the money is not identifiable, no maaras ayin.

Is it permitted to entrust assets to a non-Jew who works them on Shabbos? Yes, if one does not order him the Shabbos and he has a stake (wage/share), for then he acts for his own benefit. And maaras ayin, unlike the visible oven (Seif Tes), does not apply: the money is not recognizable as a Yid's.

English translation

In which cases was this said? When the melacha rests not at all on the Yid, but on the non-Jew alone: then the non-Jew is not his agent to do melacha on Shabbos, since it does not rest on the Yid at all. Or even if it rests also on the Yid, but he does not work alone one weekday opposite the Shabbos worked alone by the non-Jew: since the Yid repays nothing (poreia) to the non-Jew for his Shabbos work alone, the non-Jew intends in his Shabbos work only himself, not the Yid — and he is not his agent.

But if he works alone one weekday opposite the Shabbos worked alone by the non-Jew — then the non-Jew is his agent. And there is no remedy for this except to derive no benefit from the non-Jew's Shabbos act: that is, to leave all the Shabbos profit to the non-Jew alone — except in a case of great loss (hefsed gadol), where one may rely on the view of those who permit benefiting from the schar Shabbos b'havla'ah, in the manner explained.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav summarizes the central criterion of the siman with a "במה דברים אמורים": everything depends on whether the Yid repays (poreia) the non-Jew for his Shabbos. If he repays (a weekday opposite the Shabbos), the non-Jew is his agent (shelucho) → forbidden. Otherwise, the non-Jew acts for himself → permitted.

פּוֹרֵעַ → שְׁלוּחוֹ = "repaying → agency". This is the axis of the whole siman: "making up" a weekday opposite the Shabbos reveals that the non-Jew was working for the Yid on Shabbos → shelichus → forbidden. Without the making-up, the non-Jew intends his own benefit alone → permitted.

The halachic question

What is the unified criterion of the whole siman? Does the Yid repay the non-Jew's Shabbos? Yes → shelucho → forbidden; one must then leave all the Shabbos profit to the non-Jew (except in hefsed gadol, where one returns to havla'ah). No → the non-Jew acts for himself → permitted.

23. Seif Yod-Ches — The oven taken as collateral: the Shabbos fee even שלא בהבלעה

Full text of the SHHaRav

English translation

A Yid who took an oven as collateral (l'mashkon), and where the non-Jew undertook to give him all the oven's profit as interest on his money (b'ribbis me'osav) — it is permitted to receive from him even the Shabbos profit, even shelo b'havla'ah. For the body of the oven belongs to the non-Jew, and the Yid has no share in it; and the Yid does not tell him to work on Shabbos either — the non-Jew acts on his own for his benefit, in order to repay his debt, having obligated himself to give the oven's profit to the Yid as interest on his loan.

What is this Seif about?

A remarkable edge case: the oven belongs entirely to the non-Jew (given as collateral to the lending Yid). The non-Jew pays all the profit to the Yid as interest. Here, one may receive even the Shabbos profit without havla'ah — because the asset is not the Yid's, he orders nothing, and the non-Jew acts to repay his own debt.

מַשְׁכּוֹן בְּרִבִּית = "interest-bearing collateral". The oven is the non-Jew's; he pays its income to the Yid as interest on his loan. Since (a) the asset is not the Yid's, (b) no amira, (c) the non-Jew works for his debt — he is in no way the Yid's agent. Hence the heter even shelo b'havla'ah.

The halachic question

Why, exceptionally, is the Shabbos profit permitted even without havla'ah? Because all three anchors of the prohibition are missing: no share of the Yid in the asset, no amira, and no benefit of the Yid that the non-Jew would be serving. The non-Jew works for his own debt — the Yid merely collects interest.

RishonimAn asset entirely the non-Jew's + no amira → no schar Shabbos at all.
Mechaber (245:6)Oven held as collateral: receive the profit (Shabbos included) even shelo b'havla'ah.
Mishnah BerurahThe non-Jew repays his debt; he acts in no way for the Yid.
SHHaRav (245:18)Permitted even shelo b'havla'ah: the oven is the non-Jew's, no amira, and he works to repay his debt (interest).

The pesak of the Rav

Why even shelo b'havla'ah:
  1. The body of the oven is the non-Jew's: the Yid has no share in it.
  2. No amira: the Yid does not order the Shabbos.
  3. The non-Jew works to repay his debt; the Yid merely collects interest → permitted even without havla'ah.
  • A loan secured by the non-Jewish debtor's business, who pays its income as interest: 🟢 even shelo b'havla'ah.
  • A pledged asset belonging to the non-Jew, run by him on Saturday to repay: 🟢 he acts for his debt.
  • But the Yid holds a share in the pledged asset: 🟡 we fall back on the havla'ah requirement.
  • The Yid explicitly asks him to operate on Saturday: 🔴 amira → forbidden.
  • The rent of a non-Jew's asset paid to the creditor Yid, Shabbos included: 🟢 mere collection of interest.

Points to remember

  • An asset entirely the non-Jew's (collateral), no amira → permitted even shelo b'havla'ah
  • The non-Jew works to repay his debt; the Yid merely collects interest
  • None of the three anchors of the prohibition (share / amira / a benefit served) is present
Comprehension question: Why does this case permit the Shabbos profit even shelo b'havla'ah, whereas the Yid's oven (Seif Tes) required havla'ah?

24. Seif Yod-Tes — Forced baking in the Yid's oven: schar Shabbos forbidden forever

Full text of the SHHaRav

אִם אָפוּ נָכְרִים בְּשַׁבָּת בְּתַנּוּרוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּעַל כָּרְחוֹ וְנָתְנוּ לוֹ שְׂכַר הַתַּנּוּר — אָסוּר לֵהָנוֹת מִמֶּנּוּ עוֹלָמִית, בֵּין לוֹ בֵּין לַאֲחֵרִים, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהוּא שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת:

English translation

If non-Jews baked on Shabbos in a Yid's oven against his will (b'al korcho) and gave him a fee for the oven — it is forbidden to benefit from it forever (olamis), whether for himself or for others, because it is schar Shabbos.

What is this Seif about?

The siman closes with the most clear-cut case: an oven belonging to the Yid used on Shabbos by non-Jews by force (no partnership, no contract, no stipulation). The fee received is pure schar Shabbos — forbidden olamis (forever), for himself and for others. None of the permitting mechanisms (havla'ah, hisna, kablanus) operates here.

שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת אָסוּר עוֹלָמִית = "schar Shabbos forbidden forever". When the income is directly and exclusively a Shabbos fee (with no possible havla'ah and on a Yid's asset), it is struck with a permanent prohibition — assur b'hana'ah — including for third parties. This is the strongest sanction of the siman.

The halachic question

What becomes of income that is purely and exclusively schar Shabbos, with no absorption (havla'ah) or permitting structure? It is forbidden to benefit from forever, even for others — illustrating, by contrast, everything the previous seifim permitted through havla'ah, a stipulation, or contract work.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Gemara / RishonimPure, isolated schar Shabbos is assur b'hana'ah, even for third parties.
Mechaber (245:6)An oven fee taken for the Shabbos alone (without havla'ah): forbidden olamis.
Mishnah BerurahThe final contrast: without havla'ah, a stipulation, or contract work, nothing saves the schar Shabbos.
SHHaRav (245:19)The Yid's oven used by force on Shabbos: fee = schar Shabbos → forbidden olamis, for himself and for others.

The pesak of the Rav

Pure schar Shabbos:
  1. A Yid's asset, used on Shabbos by force, a fee received = pure schar Shabbos (no possible havla'ah).
  2. Forbidden olamis (forever) to benefit from — assur b'hana'ah.
  3. Forbidden even for others: one cannot "transfer" the benefit to a third party.
  • Squatting/forced use of the Yid's premises on Saturday, "rent" handed over afterwards: 🔴 schar Shabbos → forbidden olamis.
  • Takings corresponding exclusively to Saturday, isolated, on a Yid's asset: 🔴 without havla'ah → forbidden, even for others.
  • Giving this income to tzedaka to "get rid of it": 🔴 forbidden for others too — not a way out.
  • By contrast, a Shabbos fee folded in b'havla'ah (mixed days): 🟢 permitted (cf. previous seifim).
  • The non-Jew's asset used on Shabbos (Seif Yod-Ches): 🟢 the contrast — there, permitted even shelo b'havla'ah.

Points to remember

  • A fee purely and exclusively for the Shabbos (a Yid's asset, no havla'ah) = schar Shabbos
  • Forbidden olamis to benefit from — assur b'hana'ah, even for others
  • The siman's final contrast: without havla'ah / a stipulation / contract work, nothing redeems the schar Shabbos

Synthesis & questions

25. Practical synthesis of Siman רמ״ה

The 5 pillars of the siman in 5 sentences:
  1. מַעֲמִיד פּוֹעֵל בִּמְקוֹמוֹ — when the work rests on the Yid each day, "trading" his Shabbos for a weekday of the non-Jew makes the latter his agent (shelucho) → forbidden
  2. הִתְנָה מִתְּחִלָּה — a stipulation made at the beginning of the partnership (the Shabbos to the non-Jew alone) structurally assigns the Shabbos → permitted
  3. בְּהַבְלָעָה — the Shabbos profit "folded in" with other days (never isolated, never named) ceases to be forbidden schar Shabbos
  4. מַתִּירִין / אוֹסְרִין — for a spontaneous split without a stipulation, the Rav rules osrin (וכן עיקר), except in great loss (hefsed gadol)
  5. פּוֹרֵעַ → שְׁלוּחוֹ — the unifying criterion: does the Yid repay the non-Jew's Shabbos? Yes → agent → forbidden; no → the non-Jew acts for himself → permitted
The distinctive contributions of the SHHaRav (the 19 seifim):
  1. Seif Alef — A shared field: "trading" Shabbos/weekday = מעמיד פועל במקומו
  2. Seif Beis — Without a stipulation: the "weekday against Shabbos" reckoning forbidden; מתירין/אוסרין
  3. Seif Gimmel — התנה מתחילה: the prior stipulation permits (the Shabbos does not rest on the Yid)
  4. Seif Daled — Business/workshop: the explicit reckoning (הן רב הן מעט turned into a הערמה) forbidden
  5. Seif Heh — Unknown schar Shabbos: the equal split permitted because "one day equals another"
  6. Seif Vav — Melacha without a stipulation: מתירין/אוסרין; unknown profit → leave the שביעית
  7. Seif Zayin — Partnership without melacha (rented oven): permitted לדברי הכל, b'havla'ah
  8. Seif Ches — Melacha but שאינו פורע: permitted לדברי הכל; b'havla'ah by מראית עין
  9. Seif Tes — An oven entirely the Yid's: permitted b'havla'ah; מראית עין lechatchilah (רמ״ג)
  10. Seif Yod — Bathhouse/mill/store: whole days "opposite" → שלא בהבלעה → forbidden
  11. Seif Yod-Alef — A stipulation ineffective without a חלק בגוף הדבר (else = rental)
  12. Seif Yod-Beis — Renting 2-3 days b'havla'ah: also requires a קנין בגוף (else רֶוַח → shelichus)
  13. Seif Yod-Gimmel — Work also on the Yid: rent b'havla'ah against a fixed amount; no מראית עין (b'farhesya)
  14. Seif Yod-Daled — Hire the partner b'קבלנות (רמ״ד); no מראית עין in a partnership
  15. Seif Tes-Vav — Repair a forgotten stipulation: dissolve and reform the partnership
  16. Seif Tes-Zayin — Entrust merchandise/money (a wage or a share): permitted; no מראית עין (not identifiable)
  17. Seif Yod-Zayin — The summarizing criterion: פורע → שלוחו; hefsed gadol → b'havla'ah
  18. Seif Yod-Ches — An oven of the non-Jew as collateral: permitted even שלא בהבלעה
  19. Seif Yod-Tes — A Yid's oven used by force on Shabbos: schar Shabbos אסור עולמית

The levers of heter — a recap

  1. הַתְנָה מִתְּחִלָּה — make the stipulation at the beginning of the partnership (with a share in the asset if needed)
  2. בְּהַבְלָעָה — fold the Shabbos profit into the other days, never naming or isolating it
  3. קַבְּלָנוּת — hire by contract/by result, without amira to work on Shabbos (the non-Jew acts for himself)
  4. שֻׁתָּפוּת בְּלֹא מְלָאכָה — when there is no work (mere collection), no shelichus is possible
