📜 The text of the Shulhan Arukh — Seif Alef

אֲפִלּוּ מִי שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לַאֲחֵרִים, אִם יֵשׁ לוֹ מְעַט מִשֶּׁלּוֹ, צָרִיךְ לְזָרֵז עַצְמוֹ לְכַבֵּד אֶת הַשַּׁבָּת.

וְלֹא אָמְרוּ "עֲשֵׂה שַׁבַּתְּךָ חוֹל וְלֹא תִּצְטָרֵךְ לַבְּרִיּוֹת" אֶלָּא לְמִי שֶׁהַשָּׁעָה דְּחוּקָה לוֹ בְּיוֹתֵר. עַל כֵּן צָרִיךְ לְצַמְצֵם בִּשְׁאָר יָמִים כְּדֵי לְכַבֵּד אֶת הַשַּׁבָּת.

מִתַּקָּנַת עֶזְרָא שֶׁיִּהְיוּ מְכַבְּסִים בְּגָדִים בַּחֲמִישִׁי בְּשַׁבָּת מִפְּנֵי כְּבוֹד הַשַּׁבָּת.

הגה (רמ״א): נוֹהֲגִין לָלוּשׁ כְּדֵי שִׁעוּר חַלָּה בַּבַּיִת לַעֲשׂוֹת מֵהֶם לְחָמִים לִבְצֹעַ עֲלֵיהֶם בְּשַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב, וְהוּא מִכְּבוֹד שַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹם, וְאֵין לְשַׁנּוֹת.

Being mindful of Kavod Shabbat — a single Seif: Even one who depends on others, if he has something modest of his own, must hasten to honor Shabbat.

The Sages said "make your Shabbat like a weekday and do not depend on others" only with respect to one whose situation is extremely strained. Therefore one must restrict oneself on weekdays in order to honor Shabbat.

It is from the takkana of Ezra that one launders one's clothes on Thursday out of respect for Shabbat.

Hagahat ha-Rama: The minhag is to knead at home the shi'ur of challah and to make from it loaves for the betzi'a of Shabbat and Yom Tov; this is part of the kavod of Shabbat and one should not deviate from it.

1. Introduction to the sougya — the source and the beraita

The basic source

מצוות עונג שבת למדו חכמים (פסחים סח:) מהפסוק (ישעיה נח יג) "וקראת לשבת ענג", ומשום כן אמרו בגמרא (שם) שאף שלענין שמחת יום טוב נחלקו התנאים כיצד יש לנהוג, שלדעת רבי אליעזר יכול לנהוג כולו לכם [לאכול ולשתות ולשמוח], או כולו לה' [ללמוד ולשנות], ולדעת רבי יהושע צריך לחלקו, חציו לכם וחציו לה'. לענין שבת שיש בה חיוב עונג, לכולי עלמא צריך לנהוג גם לכם.
Fundamental remark: The sougya in Pesachim 68b clarifies that while regarding Yom Tov there is a mahloket whether it must be "half for you" or "all for you," concerning Shabbat — all agree that one must also act "for you." The head of the sougya in Shabbat is therefore: that the very essence of Shabbat contains an obligation of oneg — a matter not subject to the mahloket of R. Eliezer and R. Yehoshua.

Fundamental dispute among the Rishonim

The chakira: Is מצוות עונג שבת:
  • (a) déorayta — strictly from the Torah?
  • (b) dérabanan — a takkana of Chazal?
  • (c) divrei kabala — a takkana of the Nevi'im, having the status of déorayta or of dérabanan?
This is the central mahloket which the Acharonim had to address when interpreting the Rambam.

2. Shitat Rashi, Ramban and Yere'im — déorayta

The view of Rashi

Rashi (Shemot 20:8 s.v. Zachor):
"תנו לב לזכור תמיד את יום השבת, שאם נזדמן לך חפץ יפה תהא מזמינו לשבת." [Set your mind to constantly remember the day of Shabbat — that if you encounter a fine item, you shall designate it for Shabbat.]

It emerges that Oneg Shabbat is déorayta, for Rashi's derasha on "Zachor" encompasses the obligation to set aside the choice foods for Shabbat — and this mitzva is from the Torah.

The view of the Ramban

The Ramban (Vayikra 23:2, "Mikra'ei Kodesh"):
Explained that when the Torah says regarding Shabbat and the Mo'ed "Mikra'ei Kodesh," it means that on that day all are summoned and gathered to sanctify it, and also to make it a day of feasting, as it is said in the divrei kabala (Nechemia 8:10) "לכו אכלו משמנים" [Go, eat fat foods].

The view of the Yere'im

The Yere'im (siman 412):
Mitzvat Oneg Shabbat, according to "gemara gemirei la" — it is a halakha le-Moshe mi-Sinai, until Yesha'yahu came and provided an asmakhta from the verse.

Nafka mina: According to the Yere'im this is not a derivation from the verse but a halakha le-Moshe mi-Sinai, and there is here a new category of déorayta — the halakha le-Moshe mi-Sinai.

The view of the Rivash

Shu"t ha-Rivash (siman 513):
Oneg Shabbat is fully déorayta.

Targum Yonatan ben Uziel

Targum Yonatan ben Uziel (Shemot 31:16):
On the verse "ושמרו בני ישראל את השבת" he renders: "וינטרון בני ישראל ית שבתא למעבד תפנוקי שבתא" [And the children of Israel shall keep the Shabbat to make the delights of Shabbat].

The Rav Pe'alim writes (vol. III O.H. siman 22) that it emerges from the Targum that Oneg Shabbat is min ha-Torah, since he linked "ושמרו" — which is a Torah command — to the making of the delights of Shabbat (= oneg).

3. Shitat ha-Rambam — three readings

The language of the Rambam

הרמב״ם (הלכות שבת פ״ל ה״א):
"ארבעה דברים נאמרו בשבת — שנים מן התורה, ושנים מדברי סופרים והן מפורשין על ידי הנביאים. שבתורה — זכור ושמור. ושנתפרשו על ידי הנביאים — כבוד ועונג, שנאמר 'וקראת לשבת ענג' ולקדוש ה' מכובד."
On its face it appears from the Rambam that the mitzvot of kavod and oneg Shabbat are not min ha-Torah but rather mi-divrei Sofrim. So held the Nehepa ba-Kesef, the Shulchan Arukh ha-Rav (Kuntres Acharon siman 1), and the Rav Pe'alim (vol. III O.H. siman 22).

But if so, one must examine: why did other Rishonim (Rashi, Ramban, Yere'im) count Oneg Shabbat among the mitzvot of the Torah?

Three readings of the Rambam

CommentatorReading
The Chatam Sofer (Shu"t O.H. siman 165) The Rambam does not actually mean "dérabanan," but it is the Rambam's manner to label "divrei Sofrim" those matters which are not explicit in the Torah but are derived through a derasha of Chazal — yet their status is that of strict Torah law.
The Tzemach Tzedek (ha-Kadmon, siman 28) The Rambam too holds that Oneg Shabbat is min ha-Torah, and what he wrote "mi-divrei Sofrim" means that they were explicated by the Sofrim, but their status is full Torah law.
Nehepa ba-Kesef, Shela"h, Rav Pe'alim Indeed the Rambam holds it is dérabanan, and he genuinely disagrees with Rashi and Ramban.
The chakira at its yesod:
What is the meaning of "דברי סופרים" in the Rambam's language?

• Narrow reading — strictly dérabanan
• Intermediate reading — derived by derasha (hence "divrei Sofrim") but its status is déorayta
• Expansive reading — min ha-Torah, only that its interpretation was given by the Nevi'im

The pesak of the Shulchan Arukh ha-Rav and the Sha'ar ha-Tzion

השלחן ערוך הרב (סעיף א) והשער הציון (ס״ק א):
"אף להשיטות שכבוד ועונג שבת מדרבנן, מכל מקום צריך להיזהר בהם מאד, שדברי סופרים חמורים יותר מדברי תורה. וכל המעונג את השבת — שכרו מפורש בקבלה: 'אז תתענג על ה'' וגו'. ובדברי חכמים: שמוחלין לו על כל עוונותיו וניצול מדינה של גיהנם."

4. The sougya in Shabbat 118 — five contradictory statements

Regarding the obligation of Oneg Shabbat we find several ma'amarim of Chazal which appear to contradict one another. Let us survey them:

Ma'amar 1 — requirement of choice foods

שאילת משמיה דרב יהודה דרב שמואל בר שילת משמיה דרב אמר: "בתבשיל של תרדין ודגים גדולים וראשי שומין", ומבואר שצריך לענג את השבת במאכלים חשובים, וכל מקום ומקום לפי מנהגו יענגוהו במאכלים ומשקים החשובים להם עונג.

— Shabbat 118b

Ma'amar 2 — a small matter suffices

רב חייא בר אשי אמר רב: "אפילו דבר מועט, ולכבוד שבת זה עשאו — הרי זה עונג. מאי הוא? אמר רב פפא: כסא דהרסנא [small fish fried in their own oil with flour]." It is clear that one need not honor Shabbat with choice foods — any oneg, even minimal, suffices.

— Shabbat 118b

Ma'amar 3 — the statement of Rebbe Akiva

The statement of Rebbe Akiva who said: "עשה שבתך חול ואל תצטרך לבריות" [make your Shabbat like a weekday and do not depend on others]. It would appear that there is no need to take oneg on Shabbat at all, lest one come to poverty — and so the mitzva of Oneg Shabbat would be voided.

— Shabbat 118a and Pesachim 112a

Ma'amar 4 — Tana Devei Eliyahu

In the beraita: "תנא דבי אליהו: אף על פי שאמר רבי עקיבא 'עשה שבתך חול ואל תצטרך לבריות', אבל עושה הוא דבר מועט בתוך ביתו. מאי נינהו? אמר רב פפא: כסא דהרסנא."

— Pesachim 112a

Ma'amar 5 — Rebbe Yehuda ben Tema

"כדתנן רבי יהודה בן תימא אומר: הוי עז כנמר, וקל כנשר, רץ כצבי, וגבור כארי לעשות רצון אביך שבשמים."

Rashi explains (Pesachim 112a): "Exert oneself in the mitzva beyond one's actual capacity."

— Avot 5:20, Pesachim 112a
How do the five ma'amarim fit together? On the face of it:
  • Ma'amar 1 demands choice foods
  • Ma'amar 2 is satisfied with kasa de-harsana
  • Ma'amar 3 exempts altogether
  • Ma'amar 4 demands a small matter
  • Ma'amar 5 demands exertion beyond one's capacity
How is one to rule?

5. The pesak of the Tur — three categories

The words of the Tur

The Tur brings that Rebbe Akiva said his ruling only with respect to one whose situation is extremely strained, but a person must exert himself like a leopard and an eagle to honor the Shabbatot all the more. And this is the meaning of Rashi's words in Avot (5:20).

The Tur's proof: "That R. Akiva said 'even kasa de-harsana'" — that is, for the pauper for whom, though his situation is strained, he still has something modest of his own, but it is not enough for me, and I am dependent on others — is he in the category of "עשה שבתך חול" etc. or not?

The masqana of the Tur — three categories

The Tur concluded that there are three categories in the obligation of Oneg Shabbat:

CategorySituationObligationSource ma'amar
1. A man who earns enough for all his needs He honors Shabbat with choice foods according to his ability Ma'amar 1 — beets and fish
2. He has a little of his own and depends on others Obligated in three se'udot and kasa de-harsana Ma'amar 2 + 4 — a small matter
3. His situation is extremely strained — he has nothing of his own, all depends on tzedaka Not included in the obligation of oneg, obligated only in two se'udot Ma'amar 3 — "עשה שבתך חול"
The yesod of the Tur:
The test is "יש לו מעט משלו". One who has even a small amount of his own is obligated in three se'udot and kasa de-harsana. Only one who has nothing of his own is exempt.

Reinforcement of the Tur — the adjacency of the Mishnayot

The Tur proved his shita from the adjacency of the Mishnayot in Avot (5:20-21):

  1. First — "Rebbe Yehuda ben Tema says: Be bold as a leopard..." (Mishna 20)
  2. Then — "Rebbe Akiva says..." (Mishna 21)
From this adjacency, Rashi (Avot 5:20) proved that Rebbe Yehuda ben Tema's words were said with respect to Shabbat — that even one whose situation is extremely strained must exert himself "beyond his capacity" to honor Shabbat.

The Beit Yosef noted precisely: Rashi did not have the girsa "כדתנן" in the gemara but rather "תנן" — and therefore had to learn from the adjacency in Avot. But the Taz pushed back (see his commentary): on the contrary, Rashi did have the girsa "כדתנן," and his intent was to explain the rationale that we draw from the adjacency, which is only because perhaps they were not originally taught in juxtaposition.

6. Shitat ha-Rambam regarding a pauper who receives tzedaka

The Rambam in Peirush ha-Mishnayot

Rambam in Peirush ha-Mishnayot (Peah 8:7):
"דמי שיש לו מזון י״ד סעודות לא יאכל ויקדים סעודתו של מוצאי שבת, בשבת סמוך לחשכה, כדי לקיים סעודה שלישית בשבת."

That is, as the gemara initially resolves according to the Rabanan, who hold that one is obligated to eat three se'udot on Shabbat. The reason that one who has 14 se'udot should not take from the kupah is that he must move forward the motza'ei Shabbat meal and eat it during Shabbat.

Mahloket in interpreting the Rambam

Shita Understanding of the Rambam
The Bach, Be'er Sheva If he has only two se'udot — he does not take from tzedaka to eat a third se'uda, for since he has no way to move forward the motza'ei Shabbat meal, he is therefore exempt from the third se'uda, in keeping with Rebbe Akiva's "עשה שבתך חול."
The Chida, Rebbe Akiva Eiger (In disagreement with the Rambam) — a person is obligated to eat three se'udot on Shabbat even if only 14 remain to him, and he should not move forward. Some have answered that the Mishna only meant "moving forward" as a lekhatchila.

The view of the Taz on this matter

The Taz is in doubt regarding what the Rambam wrote in two places:

Place 1 (Peirush ha-Mishnayot, Peah 8:7): "One who has provisions for 14 se'udot…"
Place 2 (Hilkhot Shabbat): pesak of the obligation of the third se'uda.

And per his words one must say that the Rambam's view is:
  • One who has only 14 se'udot (= 2 per day) — is obligated to move forward
  • But one who has fewer than 14 — is not obligated to move forward, and is exempt
On this point the Rambam and the Rosh disagreed (in the Mishna and in the gemara).

7. Mahloket Rebbe Akiva Eiger and Olat Shabbat regarding the Arba Kosot

The view of the Gra

The Gra (s.k. 1): It is clarified that the obligation of kasa de-harsana is equivalent to the obligation of Arba Kosot. He is obligated to go around the doors [to obtain it].
The kashya of Rebbe Akiva Eiger:
But the shita of Rebbe Akiva Eiger contradicts this Mishna — for he wrote that a pauper who has nothing of his own is exempt from kasa de-harsana. But per the Gra he is obligated!
The essential ruling:
See in the Bei'ur Halakha where it is clarified that the shitot regarding whether one must go around the doors disagree about the pauper who has nothing of his own:
  • The Gra: Obligated to go around the doors even for kasa de-harsana (just as for Arba Kosot)
  • Rebbe Akiva Eiger: Entirely exempt, not obligated to go around
  • Olat Shabbat: Only obligated as a minhag chasidut

8. The sougya "Lavu Alai" (Beitza 15b)

The text of the sougya

The gemara in Beitza (15b):
"אמר להם הבורא לישראל בני, לוו עלי וקדשו קדושת היום, והאמינו בי ואני פורע." [The Creator said to Israel: My children, borrow against Me and sanctify the holy day, trust in Me and I will repay.]

Mahloket of the Rishonim in interpreting "Lavu Alai"

Interpretation Explanation
Tosafot (Beitza 15b s.v. lavu) This applies only when he has nothing to repay unless he takes from tzedaka — but here we speak of one who has pledges (mashkonot) to repay, and is only experiencing a strained moment. For such a one it says "lavu alai" and I will repay.
The Gra (girsa of the Maharshal) If he has only pledges — he may borrow. The concern that he might be in the category of "לוה רשע ולא ישלם" [the wicked borrower who does not repay] does not apply when he has pledges.
Tosafot in another girsa "Even though he has no pledges — if he is a respectable man, he may borrow and rely on the promise 'lavu alai'."
The Ateret Zekenim If he has no pledge, it is forbidden for him to borrow and rely on a miracle.
The Levush, Pnei Moshe For one who wishes to borrow for the needs of Shabbat and rely on the promise — it is permitted to borrow.

The sougya in the Yerushalmi versus the Bavli

The Yerushalmi (Peah 1:1, brought in the Beit Yosef):
"Shabbatot are not 'half for you' or 'all for Hashem'" — and the Beit Yosef writes precisely that the Yerushalmi too disagrees with Rebbe Yehoshua, that there is no need to keep "half for Hashem."

The Gra (Perek Kama, Ravlal): disagrees with the Yerushalmi.

The sougya per the Me'iri (Beitza 15b s.v. ve-lavu): the gemara's "lavu alai ve-ani pore'a" was said only regarding the mitzva of Kiddush ha-Yom, but regarding Oneg Shabbat they said "עשה שבתך חול ואל תצטרך לבריות."
Halakha lema'aseh:
  1. To borrow for the needs of Oneg Shabbat — permitted according to all, in a way that does not depend on a miracle
  2. To borrow at ribit from a non-Jew — through a heter, forbidden (Maharil), but where no other path exists — permitted
  3. To borrow with a pledge — always permitted
  4. To rely only on "lavu alai" without a pledge — mahloket between Maharshal and Ateret Zekenim

9. Takkanat Ezra — Thursday or Erev Shabbat?

The language of the Rama

Hagah (Rama): "תקנת עזרא שיהיו מכבסים בגדיהם בחמישי בשבת, מפני כבוד השבת."

The source — Bava Kama 82a

Takkanat Ezra: Ezra established ten takkanot, and one of them — that they would launder their clothes on Thursday.

Two reasons:
  1. For the sake of kavod Shabbat — so that he would have clean garments for Shabbat
  2. So that Erev Shabbat be free for the needs of Shabbat — so that he not be preoccupied with laundering

Mahloket of the Magen Avraham and the Eliyahu Raba

Shita Understanding of Takkanat Ezra Nafka mina
The Magen Avraham (s.k. 4) Takkanat Ezra is to launder specifically on Thursday so that Erev Shabbat will be free for the needs of Shabbat. But if he has no time on Thursday — there is no impediment to laundering on Erev Shabbat. On Erev Shabbat it is permitted to launder if there is no other way.
The Eliyahu Raba (s.k. 5) The essence of Takkanat Ezra is to launder on Thursday so that he will have clean garments for Shabbat — but he should not launder late on Erev Shabbat, since that is already the time for preparing the needs of Shabbat. And if he has no time on Thursday — he should not launder on Erev Shabbat but should wear clothes laundered earlier. On Erev Shabbat it is forbidden to launder if previously-laundered garments are available.
The Chut Shani The rationale of the takkana is that he not come to launder by hand on Erev Shabbat itself (= a forbidden melakha after the time of lighting candles) — and therefore if the laundry is done in a washing machine, then on Erev Shabbat it is permitted if the laundry will be finished well before Shabbat. Today with a washing machine — if it finishes before the time of bringing in Shabbat, it is permitted.
Halakha lema'aseh (Mishna Berura s.k. 5):
  1. Lekhatchila — one launders his clothes on Thursday
  2. If he did not manage on Thursday — it is permitted to launder on Erev Shabbat in the morning
  3. On Erev Shabbat in the afternoon — forbidden (because of "kavod Shabbat" + preoccupation)
  4. In a washing machine — permitted if the laundry will finish before Shabbat

10. The minhag of baking bread — five reasons

The language of the Rama

Hagah (Rama): "ויש שכתבו שבמקצת מקומות נהגו ללוש כדי שיעור חלה בבית, לעשות מהם לחמים לבצוע עליהם בשבת ויום טוב, והוא מכבוד שבת ויום טוב, ואין לשנות."

Five reasons for the minhag

  1. Fulfillment of the mitzva of hafrashat challah (Mas'at Binyamin):
    The mitzva is to knead at home the shi'ur of challah (= 5 kav, ~1.6 kg of flour) so that challah will be separated. If one goes to a non-Jewish baker — there is no hafrashat challah for Yisrael.
  2. Tikkun for the sin of Chava (Yerushalmi Shabbat 2:5):
    Adam Harishon was created on Erev Shabbat, and Chava caused him to sin on Erev Shabbat. It is fitting to make a tikkun for this through the woman separating challah on Erev Shabbat. This is a tikkun for the sin of the primordial blood.
  3. Fulfillment of pat Yisrael (a non-Jewish baker — perhaps the bread will not be kosher):
    If one goes to a non-Jewish baker, there is a concern regarding the kashrut of the bread — and there is a mitzva to make ha-motzi on pat Yisrael on Shabbat.
  4. For lechem mishneh (siman 274):
    There is a mitzva to make ha-motzi on two challot, which one prepares for the kavod of Shabbat. And if one bakes them specifically at home, there is in this a unique kavod Shabbat.
  5. The freshness of the bread (Pri Megadim):
    Fresh bread is more honorable than old bread, and honors Shabbat more.
The kashya of the Taz (on the words of the Magen Avraham): If the reason is only for pat Yisrael, how did the Rama write "אין לשנות" even when there is pat Yisrael in the market?
Terutz: The principal reason is hafrashat challah — and this is fulfilled only with bread of the home. Therefore, even if pat Yisrael is available in the market, one should not abandon the minhag, since by doing so one loses the hafrashat challah for kavod Shabbat.

11. Nafka mina lema'aseh

The obligation of Oneg Shabbat according to economic situation

Situation Halakha lema'aseh
Wealthy / middle class (has enough for all his needs) Obligated in choice foods — fish, meat, wine; and he should buy the finest according to his means.
If fish are expensive According to the Magen Avraham (takkana of Rashb"g) — it is proper for a beit din to enact that fish not be bought for several Shabbatot so that the price will drop for the poor.
A ba'al ha-bayit who has a little of his own and depends on others Obligated in three se'udot and kasa de-harsana at minimum.
A pauper who has nothing of his own Not obligated in kasa de-harsana — obligated only in two se'udot. But if he is a man of chasidut, he may fulfill it even so (Tana Devei Eliyahu).
To borrow for the needs of Shabbat Permitted and even proper, if it can be done through a heter (pledge, or a loan at ribit to a non-Jew through a heter).
Laundering clothes On Thursday lekhatchila. If one did not manage — on Erev Shabbat in the morning. In a washing machine — permitted if it will finish before Shabbat.
Baking bread at home It is a minhag of women to knead the shi'ur of challah at home — one should not deviate from it.
