1. The text of the Shulchan Aruch

Original text

אֲפִלּוּ מִי שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לַאֲחֵרִים, אִם יֵשׁ לוֹ מְעַט מִשֶּׁלּוֹ צָרִיךְ לְזָרֵז אֶת עַצְמוֹ לְכַבֵּד אֶת הַשַּׁבָּת. וְלֹא אָמְרוּ "עֲשֵׂה שַׁבַּתְּךָ חֹל וְאַל תִּצְטָרֵךְ לַבְּרִיּוֹת" אֶלָּא לְמִי שֶׁהַשָּׁעָה דְּחוּקָה לוֹ בְּיוֹתֵר.

English translation

Even one who needs help from others — provided he has at least a little of his own — must rouse himself to honor the Shabbos. And they only said "make your Shabbos a weekday and do not depend upon others" regarding one whose situation is truly extremely dire.
The central idea in one sentence: almost everyone is obligated to honor the Shabbos — the exemption applies only in cases of absolute extreme poverty.

2. First question — Min haTorah or miDivrei Sofrim?

Even before grasping the practical details, the Rishonim are involved in a fundamental machlokes:

The question: Is the mitzvah of עונג שבת (the oneg of Shabbos):
  • An obligation min haTorah (מן התורה)?
  • Or only an obligation miDivrei Kabbalah / miDerabbanan (מדברי קבלה / מדרבנן)?

The two opinions

Opinion Source Position
Min haTorah Rashi · Ramban · Yere'im · Rivash The pasuk "מקראי קודש" (Vayikra 23:2) includes the chiyuv to make Shabbos a day of mishteh (משתה) — a Torah obligation.
MiDerabbanan Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 30:1) Four things were said regarding Shabbos: two from the Torah (זכור and שמור), and two miDivrei Sofrim (כבוד and עונג) — expounded by the Nevi'im: "וקראת לשבת ענג".
Why this distinction matters:
  • If דאורייתא (min haTorah) → an absolute chiyuv, more stringent
  • If דרבנן (miDerabbanan) → a strong chiyuv but with greater flexibility
In practice: even according to those who hold it is miDerabbanan, one must be very vigilant. The Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe and the Shaar HaTziyun write that these obligations are more precious than Torah obligations — one who is mechabed Shabbos receives an extraordinary schar (az titaneg al Hashem) and is saved from Gehinnom.

3. The central dilemma

The Gemara (Shabbos 118b) presents two teachings that appear to contradict one another:

Teaching A — Honor generously

"בַּמֶּה מְעַנְּגוֹ? אָמַר רַב יְהוּדָה בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַב שְׁמוּאֵל בַּר שִׁילַת מִשְּׁמֵיהּ דְּרַב: בְּתַבְשִׁיל שֶׁל תְּרָדִין וְדָגִים גְּדוֹלִים וְרָאשֵׁי שׁוּמִין"

"With what does one honor Shabbos? With a dish of beets, large fish, and heads of garlic" — Rav Yehuda

→ One must honor Shabbos with important, refined foods.

Teaching B — Even a little suffices

"רַב חִיָּיא בַּר אַשִׁי אָמַר רַב: אֲפִלּוּ דָּבָר מוּעָט, וּלִכְבוֹד שַׁבָּת זֶה עֲשָׂאוֹ — הֲרֵי זֶה עוֹנֶג"

"Even a small thing, if it is done for the honor of Shabbos — this is already oneg"

→ Even a little is enough, such as a simple כסא דהרסנא (small fish fried in their own fat with flour).

Teaching C — "Make your Shabbos a weekday"

"אָמַר רַבִּי עֲקִיבָא: עֲשֵׂה שַׁבַּתְּךָ חֹל וְאַל תִּצְטָרֵךְ לַבְּרִיּוֹת"

"Rabbi Akiva said: make your Shabbos a weekday — and do not become dependent upon others"

→ Apparently, in certain cases, one is not obligated to honor Shabbos at all.

Teaching D — But still!

"תָּנָא דְּבֵי אֵלִיָּהוּ: אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאָמַר רַבִּי עֲקִיבָא 'עֲשֵׂה שַׁבַּתְּךָ חֹל', אֲבָל עוֹשֶׂה הוּא דָּבָר מוּעָט בְּתוֹךְ בֵּיתוֹ"

"Even according to Rabbi Akiva — who says 'make your Shabbos a weekday' — one must at least do some small thing in his home"
How is this tension resolved? This is precisely the great discussion of the Tur, the Beis Yosef and the Acharonim. The Shulchan Aruch rules by distinguishing three levels of wealth.

4. The three categories of people (Taz)

The Taz, after a lengthy analysis, concludes that the teachings of the Gemara address three different economic situations:

Category Situation Obligation
① The wealthy
(who has everything)		 Earns enough for his needs 🟢 Refined foods (meat, fish, etc.) according to his means
② The middling
(who has little but needs help)		 Has a little, but must receive from others to live 🟡 At minimum a כסא דהרסנא (a small preparation marking the honor)
③ The extreme aniy
(who has nothing of his own)		 Lives entirely on communal assistance 🔴 Exempt — may "make his Shabbos a weekday"
The classification criterion is the following: "does he have anything of his own?"
  • If he has everything → category 1
  • If he has a little (a small income, even if insufficient) → category 2
  • If he has absolutely nothing → category 3

The deeper teaching of the Tur

The Tur adds a spiritual dimension: each person should endeavor, during the week, to reduce his expenses in order to be able to honor Shabbos. "כי אדרבה אם יוסיף, מוסיפין לו" — on the contrary, one who spends for Shabbos will see Hashem add to him in return.

5. The aniy and tzedakah for Shabbos

The basic rule

"מִי שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ מְזוֹן אַרְבַּע עֶשְׂרֵה סְעוּדוֹת — לֹא יִטּוֹל מִן הַקֻּפָּה" (פאה פ״ח מ״ז)

"One who has food for 14 seudos (= 2 per day for 7 days) does not take from the kupah"

This raises a problem: 14 seudos = 2 per day. But on Shabbos, one must eat 3 seudos! Does the aniy then have no right to communal funds for the third seudah?

The opinions

Opinion Position
Bach The aniy who has nothing may receive from tzedakah what is needed for 3 seudos + a כסא דהרסנא
Rambam (according to one interpretation) If the aniy truly has nothing (category 3), no tzedakah for the 3rd seudah — he is exempt
Mishnah Berurah One gives to the aniy according to his dignity — if he is accustomed to better, one gives him more

6. Borrowing to honor Shabbos?

The Gemara (Beitzah 15b) recounts an extraordinary scene:

"אָמַר לָהֶם הַבּוֹרֵא לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בָּנַי, לְווּ עָלַי וְקַדְּשׁוּ קְדֻשַּׁת הַיּוֹם, וְהַאֲמִינוּ בִּי וַאֲנִי פּוֹרֵעַ"

"Hashem said to Israel: 'My children, borrow on My account and sanctify the holiness of the day — trust in Me and I will repay!'"

The debate — may one borrow with interest from a non-Jew?

Case Halakha
Borrow on credit (without interest) for Shabbos 🟢 Permitted and even encouraged (Maharil, Beis Yosef)
Borrow with a pledge (משכון) — without dependence on others 🟢 Permitted
Borrow relying on the divine promise ("לוו עלי") 🟢 Permitted (but Maharshal and Maharsha hesitate — risk of falling into "לוה רשע ולא ישלם")
Borrow with interest from a non-Jew 🟡 Mahlokes — see Yerushalmi, Hagra, Mahari Halevi
The spiritual lesson: one who spends to honor Shabbos — Hashem reimburses him. The Maharil and many Acharonim encourage borrowing (without interest) rather than diminishing the honor of Shabbos.

7. Tikkun Ezra — Laundering clothes on Thursday

The text of the Rama

הגה: תקנת עזרא שיהיו מכבסים בגדיהם בחמישי בשבת מפני כבוד השבת.

Translation

Gloss of the Rama: Ezra instituted that one should launder one's clothes on the 5th day of the week (= Thursday) in honor of Shabbos.

Why Thursday and not Friday?

For two reasons (Bava Kama 82a):
  1. So that clothes are ready and clean in time for Shabbos — Friday is already busy with preparations
  2. So that Friday is entirely devoted to Shabbos preparations (cooking, cleaning, shopping) without having to do laundry

Discussion among the Acharonim

Acharon Understanding of Tikkun Ezra
Magen Avraham The aim is to have clean clothes for Shabbos — therefore even laundering on Friday is technically permitted if necessary
Eliyahu Rabba · Mishnah Berurah The aim is for Friday to be free for the other preparations — therefore one should avoid laundering on Friday

8. The origin of the minhag of the Challos

The text of the Rama

ויש שכתבו שבמקצת מקומות נהגו ללוש כדי שיעור חלה בבית, לעשות מהם לחמים לבצוע עליהם בשבת ויום טוב, והוא מכבוד שבת ויום טוב, ואין לשנות.

Translation

Gloss of the Rama: In some places, the custom is to knead at home the amount of dough that requires the taking of challah, to make from it the loaves over which one will recite the blessing on Shabbos and Yom Tov — this is the honor of Shabbos and one should not change this custom.

Why knead at home?

Several reasons:
  1. Mitzvat Hafrashat Challah — the taking of challah, in memory of the terumah (תרומה) of the Temple
  2. Atoning for the sin of Chava — according to the Yerushalmi (Shabbos 2:6), Adam Harishon was the challah of the world (יצירה ראשונה), and Chava caused his fall by having him eat from the tree — the woman of the house thus rectifies this by taking challah on the eve of Shabbos
  3. Have a certainly kosher bread — avoiding bread of a non-Jew
  4. Honor of Shabbos — fresh bread made at home

9. Seif Bet — With what does one delight in Shabbos?

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ב)

בַּמֶּה מְעַנְּגוֹ? בִּימֵי חַכְמֵי הַגְּמָרָא הָיוּ מְעַנְּגִין בְּדָגִים גְּדוֹלִים וּבְתַבְשִׁיל שֶׁל תְּרָדִין, שֶׁמַּאֲכָלִים אֵלּוּ הָיוּ חֲשׁוּבִים עֹנֶג בִּימֵיהֶם. וְכָל מָקוֹם וּמָקוֹם לְפִי מִנְהָגוֹ יְעַנְּגוּהוּ בְּמַאֲכָלִים וּמַשְׁקִים הַחֲשׁוּבִים לָהֶם עֹנֶג.

וְאֵין חִיּוּב לֶאֱכוֹל בָּשָׂר וְלִשְׁתּוֹת יַיִן בְּשַׁבָּת, אֶלָּא לְפִי שֶׁמִּן הַסְּתָם יֵשׁ לְרֹב בְּנֵי אָדָם עֹנֶג בַּאֲכִילַת בָּשָׂר יוֹתֵר מִבִּשְׁאָר מַאֲכָלִים וּבִשְׁתִיַּת יַיִן יוֹתֵר מִבִּשְׁאָר מַשְׁקִים, לְכָךְ יֵשׁ לָהֶם לְהַרְבּוֹת בְּבָשָׂר וְיַיִן כְּפִי יְכָלְתָּם וְהַשָּׂגַת יָדָם.

English translation

With what does one delight in it? In the days of the Chachmei haGemara, they would be me'aneg Shabbos with large fish and a dish of beets, because these foods were considered oneg in their day. Every place, according to its minhag, will delight in Shabbos with the foods and drinks that are oneg for them.

And there is no chiyuv to eat meat or drink wine on Shabbos; rather, since most people generally have more oneg from eating meat than from other foods, and from drinking wine than from other beverages — it is therefore proper for them to increase meat and wine according to their means and their hasagas yad.

What does this Seif discuss?

After establishing (in Seif Alef) the chiyuv of oneg, the Rav now spells out what this chiyuv actually consists of in practice. The problem: the Gemara (Shabbos 118b) speaks of fish and beets — but these foods corresponded to the taste of the talmudic era. What do we do today?

The question: does the mitzvah of oneg Shabbos consist of:
  • Eating exactly what the Gemara mentions (fish, beets)?
  • Or eating what is delicious for oneself, according to the minhag of the place and time?

The halachic question

The Rav paskens that the criterion is subjective and cultural: what matters is actual pleasure. If in 18th-century Russia one delights in Shabbos with borscht and meat, this is just as valid as talmudic fish. But the Rav immediately adds a general presumption: for the majority of people, meat and wine are the universal delights — one should therefore increase them.

The opinions

Source Position
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 30:7-10) Mentions meat, wine, and refined foods without specifying that they are obligatory — the idea is personal pleasure.
Tur · Beis Yosef Follow the Gemara (Shabbos 118b): meat and wine are valued.
Mishnah Berurah (242:2) Explicitly cites Shabbos 118b — at least once at the daytime seudah, fish is recommended (the wordplay on the letters ענג / דג).
SHHaRav (242:2) Paskens clearly: no formal chiyuv of meat / wine — but a practical presumption that one should increase them according to one's means, because they are the universal delight.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

Three layers stacked together:
  1. Structural level: one delights with what is oneg for oneself (subjective and cultural)
  2. Presumptive level: meat and wine are generally the universal delights
  3. Practical level: one should therefore increase them according to one's means — without making it an absolute chiyuv

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — The vegetarian: can he be mekayem oneg Shabbos without meat? Answer: yes, because the criterion is personal delight. He must, however, be careful to have refined foods (fine fish, elegant dishes, quality wines).
  • Case 2 — One allergic to fish: exempt from fish — as the Rav writes explicitly in 242:7 (« אם הדגים מזיקים לו »). He must replace it with a dish he enjoys.
  • Case 3 — A member of a Yemenite community: the traditional dishes (jachnun, malawach, spicy soups) are fully mekayem oneg Shabbos — the local culture takes precedence over the Ashkenazi or Sefardi model.
  • Case 4 — One who enjoys beer more than wine: may consume what gives him more pleasure — subject to the chiyuv of kiddush, which requires wine.
  • Case 5 — The sick person: must eat what medicine permits and what gives him pleasure — without feeling guilty about "not having meat/wine".

Key points to remember

  • The criterion of oneg is actual pleasure, not a fixed list of foods
  • Each place and culture defines its own oneg
  • Meat and wine are a general presumption of delight — not a formal chiyuv
  • One must increase according to one's means in the direction of maximum pleasure
  • A personal exception (allergy, distaste, health) does not disqualify the kiyum of the mitzvah
Comprehension question: Why does the Rav specify that there is "no chiyuv" of meat / wine, yet then say one must "increase them according to one's means"? Isn't this a contradiction?

10. Seif Gimel — Increasing expenses & "לוו עלי"

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ג)

וְכָל הַמַּרְבֶּה בְּהוֹצָאַת שַׁבָּת וּבְתִקּוּן מַאֲכָלִים רַבִּים וְטוֹבִים — הֲרֵי זֶה מְשֻׁבָּח, וְהוּא שֶׁיָּדוֹ מַשֶּׂגֶת.

וְאַף אִם אֵין לוֹ עַכְשָׁו מָעוֹת מְזֻמָּנִים אֶלָּא חֲפָצִים — יְמַשְׁכְּנֵם וְיִלְוֶה עֲלֵיהֶם, וְהַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא יַמְצִיא לוֹ לִפְרוֹעַ. וְעַל זֶה אָמְרוּ רַזַ"ל: "אָמַר הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא לְווּ עָלַי וַאֲנִי פוֹרֵעַ", כְּלוֹמַר שֶׁאֵין הוֹצָאַת שַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב עוֹלֶה בְּחֶשְׁבּוֹן הַקִּצְבָה שֶׁנִּקְצַב לוֹ בְּרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה לִמְזוֹנוֹת כָּל הַשָּׁנָה וּשְׁאָר צְרָכָיו, שֶׁכָּךְ אָמְרוּ חֲכָמִים: כָּל מְזוֹנוֹתָיו וְהוֹצָאוֹתָיו שֶׁל אָדָם קְצוּבִין לוֹ מֵרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה, שֶׁקּוֹצְבִין לוֹ כַּמָּה יִשְׂתַּכֵּר בְּשָׁנָה זוֹ לְצֹרֶךְ מְזוֹנוֹתָיו וּשְׁאָר צְרָכָיו שֶׁל כָּל יְמוֹת הַשָּׁנָה, חוּץ מֵהוֹצָאוֹת שַׁבָּתוֹת וְיָמִים טוֹבִים, שֶׁאֵין קוֹצְבִין עָלֶיהָ כְּלוּם, אֶלָּא אִם מוֹסִיף בְּהוֹצָאַת שַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב — מוֹסִיפִין לוֹ.

אֲבָל אִם אֵין לוֹ חֲפָצִים לְמַשְׁכְּנָם וְלִפְרוֹעַ בָּהֶם — לֹא יִלְוֶה וְשֶׁהַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא יִפְרַע, כִּי מֵאַחַר שֶׁאֵין לוֹ מִשֶּׁלּוֹ — אֵין עָלָיו חִיּוּב כְּלָל לְהַרְבּוֹת בְּהוֹצָאַת שַׁבָּת יוֹתֵר מֵהַשָּׂגַת יָדוֹ.

English translation

Anyone who increases the expenses of Shabbos and the preparation of many fine dishes — is praiseworthy, provided his hand reaches that far.

And even if he has no cash on hand but only belongings — let him pawn them and borrow against them, and HaKadosh Baruch Hu will provide him with the means to repay. About this Chazal said: "Borrow on My account and I will repay" — meaning that the expenses of Shabbos and Yom Tov do not enter the cheshbon of the budget set for him at Rosh Hashanah for the year. All of a person's income and expenses are fixed at Rosh Hashanah — except the expenses of Shabbos and Yom Tov, for which nothing is fixed: if he adds to the expenses of Shabbos, they add to him.

But if he has no belongings to pawn — let him not borrow relying on HaKadosh Baruch Hu to repay, for since he has nothing of his own, there is no chiyuv at all upon him to increase expenses beyond his hasagas yad.

What does this Seif discuss?

The Rav unfolds here a remarkable economic and theological logic. The Gemara (Beitzah 15b) cites the promise of Hashem: "Borrow on My account". But how exactly does this promise work? The Rav explains: Hashem sets the year's income at Rosh Hashanah — except for the "Shabbos / Yom Tov / Talmud Torah" account, which is off-budget. Investing in Shabbos therefore does not create a debt but triggers a compensatory flow.

The halachic question

Three nested questions:
  1. May one go into debt for Shabbos?
  2. For whom does the divine promise of repayment apply?
  3. What should one do if he has absolutely nothing?

The opinions

Source Position
Tur · Beis Yosef Cite the promise "לוו עלי" as a general encouragement to invest for Shabbos.
Mechaber (OC 242:1) States the principle without specifying conditions of application.
Mishnah Berurah (242:3-4) Specifies: (1) one who has an item to pawn may borrow; (2) one who has nothing and will not be able to repay should not borrow; (3) one borrows for חיובים, not for הידורים.
SHHaRav (242:3) Provides the structural conceptual framework: Shabbos is off the annual budget — the promise is mechanical, not miraculous. Sharp distinction: only one who has belongings to pawn may borrow; with nothing, one refrains.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

The Rav's logic in 4 stages:
  1. One who has the means must increase (משובח)
  2. One who has no cash but has belongings must pawn and borrow — the divine promise applies
  3. Why the promise works: Shabbos is off the annual budget — the Master of the world compensates structurally
  4. One who has nothing: no borrowing, no chiyuv — the promise does not extend to nothingness

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — Average salaried person with a car: may take a loan against it (in the broad sense) or use a credit card (to be repaid from upcoming income) — the promise applies, since he has assets and an income.
  • Case 2 — Student with no fixed income but with savings: may draw on his savings for Shabbos — this is an investment, not an impoverishment.
  • Case 3 — Homeless person with no assets at all: exempt from borrowing — the promise does not apply. He is mekayem oneg with what the kehillah provides.
  • Case 4 — Family in debt with heavy monthly payments: may invest in Shabbos as long as there remains an asset to pledge or a reasonably expected income — the promise does not require the absence of all other debt.
  • Case 5 — Person in personal bankruptcy: must consult a Rav — the kavod of Shabbos must be preserved, but without falling into the trap of "לוה רשע ולא ישלם" (the wicked one borrows and does not repay).

Key points to remember

  • Increasing Shabbos expenses is praiseworthy within one's means
  • The promise "לוו עלי" is structural, not miraculous: Shabbos is off the annual budget
  • Conditions for application: having an asset to pawn or a reasonably expected income
  • With absolutely nothing: no chiyuv — the promise does not extend to nothingness
  • The principle also applies to Yom Tov and the Torah chinuch of children (per the same Gemara)
Comprehension question: Why does the promise "לוו עלי" not apply to one who has nothing? Isn't he precisely the one who would need this help the most?

11. Seif Dalet — The aniy who has 14 seudos

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ד)

וְאַף אִם אֵין יָדוֹ מַשֶּׂגֶת כְּלוּם וְרוֹצֶה לִטּוֹל מִן הַקֻּפָּה שֶׁל צְדָקָה הַמִּתְחַלֶּקֶת בְּכָל עֶרֶב שַׁבָּת — לֹא יִטּוֹל אִם אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לִטּוֹל לְצֹרֶךְ מְזוֹנוֹתָיו שֶׁל יְמוֹת הַחֹל שֶׁל שָׁבוּעַ הַבָּא, וּבִשְׁבִיל מְזוֹנוֹת יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת גַּם כֵּן לֹא הָיָה צָרִיךְ לִטּוֹל אִם הָיָה נוֹהֵג כִּבְחֹל מַמָּשׁ לֶאֱכוֹל בּוֹ שְׁתֵּי סְעֻדּוֹת בִּלְבָד פַּת וְקִטְנִיּוֹת כִּסְעֻדּוֹתָיו בְּחֹל, כְּגוֹן שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ מְזוֹן י"ד סְעֻדּוֹת לְשִׁבְעָה יָמִים, שֶׁעַד עֶרֶב שַׁבָּת הַבָּא סְעֻדָּה אַחַת לְכָל יוֹם וּסְעֻדָּה אַחַת לְכָל לַיְלָה, אֶלָּא שֶׁרוֹצֶה לִטּוֹל כְּדֵי לֶאֱכוֹל שְׁלֹשָׁה סְעֻדּוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת.

וְכָל שֶׁכֵּן אִם רוֹצֶה לִטּוֹל כְּדֵי לְהִתְעַנֵּג קְצָת יוֹתֵר מִימוֹת הַחֹל, שֶׁאָסוּר לוֹ לִטּוֹל מִן הַקֻּפָּה בְּשָׁבוּעַ זוֹ, שֶׁכָּךְ אָמְרוּ חֲכָמִים: "עֲשֵׂה שַׁבַּתְּךָ חֹל וְאַל תִּצְטָרֵךְ לַבְּרִיּוֹת".

English translation

And even if his hand reaches nothing, and he wishes to take from the kupah shel tzedakah distributed every erev Shabbos — he does not take if he has no need for the upcoming weekdays. And for the meals of Shabbos day itself, he would also not need to take if he conducted himself as on a weekday and ate only two seudos — bread and legumes, as his weekday meals — for instance if he has food for 14 seudos for 7 days (one meal each day and one each night until the following erev Shabbos); but he wishes to take in order to eat 3 seudos on Shabbos.

And all the more so if he wishes to take in order to have a bit more oneg than weekdays — he is forbidden to take from the kupah that week, for so said the Chachamim: "Make your Shabbos like a weekday and do not become dependent on others".

What does this Seif discuss?

The Rav addresses a fundamental case in communal policy: the aniy who has just enough for 14 seudos in the week (= 2 meals a day × 7 days) — may he go to the kupah to ask for a 3rd seudah on Shabbos or for some refinement for the holy day?

The historical context: in the time of the Alter Rebbe (late 18th century), every kehillah had a kupah shel tzedakah that distributed food to the needy every erev Shabbos. The question is: who is entitled, and for what.

The halachic question

There is an apparent contradiction with the mitzvah of oneg Shabbos: if one must honor Shabbos at all costs, why may an aniy not ask for a little more for Shabbos? The Rav explains: the dignity of autonomy ("not being dependent on creatures") takes precedence over the embellishment of Shabbos. One who can manage on 14 seudos must do so — even if it means eating his 3 seudos of Shabbos more modestly.

The opinions

Source Position
Mishnah Pe'ah 8:7 "One who has food for 14 seudos does not take from the kupah" — talmudic basis.
Rambam (Matnos Aniyim 9:13) Codifies the rule of 14 seudos.
Tur · Mechaber (YD 253:1) Take up the rule — no explicit extension to Shabbos.
Taz · Bach Mahlokes: should the rule be extended to Shabbos (15 seudos including the 3rd) or kept at 14?
SHHaRav (242:4) Paskens: for one who has 14 seudos and is not yet enrolled on the kupah, "עשה שבתך חול" is applied strictly — he may not request the 3rd seudah nor refinement. Critical distinction with Seif Heh (who is already on the kupah).

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

The structuring rule of Seif Dalet:
  1. The criterion is 14 seudos for 7 days (2 per day)
  2. With this, one manages with a modest 3rd seudah drawn from his own reserves (for example by eating slightly less during the week)
  3. One does not go to the kupah for the 3rd seudah nor for refinement
  4. Governing principle: the dignity of autonomy ≻ embellishment of Shabbos

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — Family with sufficient food stock: does not go to the aid system to ask for Shabbos chicken. Eats what it has, simply.
  • Case 2 — Retiree whose pension covers 14 seudos: may cut back during the week to add to Shabbos — not request more from the kehillah.
  • Case 3 — Modest family that would like refinement: the desire is legitimate but not a communal right. Must draw on its own resources or accept simplicity.
  • Case 4 — Yeshivah bochur with a stipend covering the minimum: same logic — no right to additional kupah money for Shabbos comfort.
  • Case 5 — Traveler passing through with 14 seudos for the week: does not apply to local organizations.

Key points to remember

  • The rule of 14 seudos = critical dividing line for the kupah
  • "עשה שבתך חול" applies to one who has just enough and wants more
  • Autonomy takes precedence over embellishment
  • The 3rd seudah can be done modestly from one's own reserves
  • Do not confuse this case with Seif Heh (already on the kupah)
Comprehension question: Why does the Rav require an aniy with 14 seudos not to touch the kupah, when he insists so much on the importance of honoring Shabbos? Isn't there a tension between kavod Shabbos and autonomy?

12. Seif Heh — The aniy already on the kupah

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ה)

אֲבָל בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת הַבָּא נוֹתְנִין לוֹ מְזוֹן ט"ו סְעֻדּוֹת לְאוֹתָהּ שָׁבוּעַ, כְּדֵי שֶׁיִּהְיֶה לוֹ שְׁלֹשָׁה סְעֻדּוֹת לְאוֹתוֹ שַׁבָּת, וְגַם נוֹתְנִים לוֹ דָּגִים וְיָרָק לְאוֹתוֹ שַׁבָּת, שֶׁכֵּיוָן שֶׁכְּבָר נִצְרָךְ לַבְּרִיּוֹת נוֹתְנִים לוֹ כָּל צָרְכּוֹ, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר "דֵּי מַחְסֹרוֹ אֲשֶׁר יֶחְסַר לוֹ".

וְכֵן מִי שֶׁנּוֹתְנִים לוֹ מִדֵּי שָׁבוּעַ בְּשָׁבוּעַ מִצְּדָקָה אַחֶרֶת, שֶׁמֻּתָּר לוֹ לְקַבֵּל מִמֶּנָּה הַרְבֵּה כָּל שֶׁאֵין לוֹ שִׁעוּר הַמְפֹרָשׁ בְּיוֹרֶה דֵעָה סִמָּן רנ"ג — הֲרֵי זֶה יָכוֹל לְהַרְבּוֹת בְּהוֹצָאַת שַׁבָּת כְּפִי עֶרֶךְ מַה שֶּׁנּוֹתְנִים לוֹ.

(וּבִלְבָד שֶׁלֹּא יוֹצִיא כָּל כָּךְ לְשַׁבָּתוֹת עַד שֶׁתֶּחְסַר פַּרְנָסָתוֹ בְּחֹל וְיִצְטָרְכוּ לְהוֹסִיף לוֹ מִן הַצְּדָקָה מֵחֲמַת הוֹצָאוֹתָיו לְשַׁבָּתוֹת, שֶׁנִּמְצָא מַטִּיל כְּבוֹד שַׁבְּתוֹתָיו עַל הַבְּרִיּוֹת, וּכְבָר אָמְרוּ "עֲשֵׂה שַׁבַּתְּךָ חֹל וְאַל תִּצְטָרֵךְ לַבְּרִיּוֹת").

English translation

But on the following erev Shabbos, one gives him food for 15 seudos for that week, so that he will have 3 seudos for that Shabbos, and one also gives him fish and vegetable for that Shabbos — for since he is already dependent on the creatures, one gives him all his needs, as it is said: "Sufficient for his lack which is lacking to him" (Devarim 15:8).

Similarly, one to whom another tzedakah gives week after week — from which he is permitted to receive much, as long as he has not the amount specified in Yoreh Deah 253 — may increase his Shabbos expenses in proportion to what is given to him.

(Provided that he does not spend so much on Shabbos that his weekday parnassah is diminished and they have to add to him from the kupah on account of his Shabbos expenses — for then he casts the kavod of his Shabbosos upon the creatures, and it has already been said: "make your Shabbos like a weekday and do not be dependent on creatures").

What does this Seif discuss?

A direct continuation of Seif Dalet. The Rav makes the distinction: one who is already enrolled on the kupah (because he truly has nothing) no longer falls under the rule of "עשה שבתך חול". On the contrary: the kehillah must give him 15 seudos (= 14 + the 3rd of Shabbos) plus fish and vegetable — that is, a dignified Shabbos.

The halachic question

The decisive transition: at what point do we move from "do not depend on creatures" to "we give you all your lack"? The Rav's answer: as soon as one is already a dependent — dignity is already lost in the autonomy realm, so we switch to the logic of "די מחסורו".

The opinions

Source Position
Mishnah Pe'ah 8:7 "One gives to the aniy [who takes from the kupah] 15 seudos for the week, including fish and vegetable for Shabbos" — talmudic basis.
Rambam · Tur Codify the rule.
Bach Clarifies that the kehillah must give — this is a right of the aniy, not a favor.
SHHaRav (242:5) Articulates the double requirement: (1) one must give 15 seudos + fish + vegetable; (2) but the aniy must not spend so much on Shabbos that the week suffers — otherwise he abusively casts the kavod onto the kehillah.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

Three principles of Seif Heh:
  1. The aniy already enrolled on the kupah is entitled to 15 seudos/week + fish + vegetable on Shabbos
  2. For other forms of tzedakah (beyond the official kupah), he may accept according to what is permitted in Yoreh Deah 253
  3. Ethical limit: not to spend so much on Shabbos that the week is unbalanced — he would be casting the luxury of Shabbos onto the kehillah

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — Family on weekly aid lists: entitled to fish + vegetable + 3rd seudah. The kehillah must provide for this.
  • Case 2 — Recipient of welfare + Shabbos package: may accept both, as long as the total remains within the norms of YD 253.
  • Case 3 — Family that spends the weekly budget on fine foods on Friday night: contrary to the Rav's spirit. The kupah is for 15 balanced seudos, not for a lavish Shabbos followed by weekday privations.
  • Case 4 — Lonely elderly person on the kupah: entitled to fish + vegetable — this is a standard, not a luxury.
  • Case 5 — Indigent traveler: the local kehillah must provide him with 3 Shabbos seudos with fish/vegetable — a right of the aniy, not optional charity.

Key points to remember

  • The aniy already on the kupah is entitled to 15 seudos + fish + vegetable
  • Basis: "די מחסורו" (Devarim 15:8) — sufficient for what he lacks
  • The transition happens at the moment when autonomy is already lost
  • The aniy must not overinvest in Shabbos at the expense of his week
  • For other tzedakos: refer to Yoreh Deah 253
Comprehension question: Why does an aniy already enrolled on the kupah have a right to more than an aniy who manages with 14 seudos? Isn't this paradoxical: the one who strives to remain autonomous receives less?

13. Seif Vav — "Doing something small" and cutting back during the week

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ו)

וְאִם מַה שֶּׁנּוֹתְנִים לוֹ אֵינוֹ מַסְפִּיק לוֹ לְהַרְבּוֹת בְּהוֹצָאַת שַׁבָּת וְשֶׁיּוֹתִיר מִזֶּה לְפַרְנָסָתוֹ לְחֹל בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁיִּצְטָרְכוּ לְהוֹסִיף לוֹ, מִכָּל מָקוֹם אִם יֵשׁ לוֹ מְעַט מִשֶּׁלּוֹ — יְזָרֵז אֶת עַצְמוֹ לְהוֹצִיא מָמוֹן בִּכְבוֹד הַשַּׁבָּת בְּכָל מַה שֶּׁאֶפְשָׁר.

אֲבָל אִם אֵין לוֹ כְּלוּם מִשֶּׁלּוֹ — אֵין צָרִיךְ לְבַקֵּשׁ מֵאֲחֵרִים שֶׁיִּתְּנוּ לוֹ כְּדֵי לְכַבֵּד אֶת הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁכְּבָר אָמְרוּ "עֲשֵׂה שַׁבַּתְּךָ כוּ'". וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, צָרִיךְ הוּא לַעֲשׂוֹת דָּבָר מוּעָט בְּתוֹךְ בֵּיתוֹ לְקַיֵּם מִצְוַת עֹנֶג.

וְאִם מַה שֶּׁנּוֹתְנִים לוֹ אֵינוֹ מַסְפִּיק לוֹ אֲפִלּוּ לְדָבָר מוּעָט — צָרִיךְ הוּא לְצַמְצֵם מְעַט בִּשְׁאָר הַיָּמִים בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁיּוּכַל לַעֲשׂוֹת דָּבָר מוּעָט לְעֹנֶג שַׁבָּת.

וְכָל שֶׁכֵּן מִי שֶׁאֵינוֹ נוֹטֵל כְּלוּם מֵהַצְּדָקָה אֶלָּא מִסְתַּפֵּק בַּמּוּעָט שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ מִשֶּׁלּוֹ, שֶׁאֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לְבַקֵּשׁ מֵאֲחֵרִים שֶׁיִּתְּנוּ לוֹ כְּדֵי לְכַבֵּד אֶת הַשַּׁבָּת, אֶלָּא יְצַמְצֵם מְעַט בִּשְׁאָר הַיָּמִים בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה דָּבָר מוּעָט לְעֹנֶג שַׁבָּת, וְזֶה טוֹב יוֹתֵר מִשֶּׁיִּצְטָרֵךְ לַבְּרִיּוֹת.

English translation

And if what is given to him is not enough for him to increase Shabbos expenses with something left over for weekday parnassah such that they would have to add to him [from the kupah] — nevertheless, if he has a little of his own, let him hasten to spend money on kavod Shabbos as much as he can.

But if he has nothing of his own — he is not obligated to ask others to give him in order to honor Shabbos, for it has already been said "make your Shabbos like a weekday". Nevertheless, he must do a small thing in his home to be mekayem the mitzvah of oneg.

And if what is given to him is not enough even for a small thing — he must cut back a little on the other days so that he can do a small thing for oneg Shabbos.

All the more so one who takes nothing from tzedakah and makes do with the little he has of his own — he need not ask others; he cuts back a little on the other days so as to do a small thing for oneg Shabbos — and this is better than being dependent on creatures.

What does this Seif discuss?

The Rav lays out the conduct of intermediate nuances: the aniy whose kupah is not enough for both the week and Shabbos. Four sub-cases are distinguished, forming a true decision algorithm for the aniy.

The halachic question

Four nested situations:
  1. Has a little of his own + insufficient kupah → hastens to spend his own
  2. Has nothing of his own → not obligated to ask, but must do the minimum
  3. Kupah insufficient even for the minimum → cuts back during the week to free up a little
  4. Takes nothing and makes do with little → cuts back during the week, this is better than being dependent

The opinions

Source Position
Pesachim 112a (R. Akiva) "עשה שבתך חול ואל תצטרך לבריות" — talmudic basis for the exemption.
Tana Devei Eliyahu (Rabbah 26) Even one who says "עשה שבתך חול" must do something small — basis of "דבר מועט".
Mechaber (OC 242:1) "Even one who needs others — if he has a little of his own — must hasten for kavod Shabbos".
Mishnah Berurah (242:1) Follows the Mechaber and the Tana Devei Eliyahu — explains R. Akiva's exemption as limited to absolute indigence.
SHHaRav (242:6) Articulates the complete algorithm on 4 levels — proposes cutting back during the week as a preferable solution to dependence.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

The Rav's algorithm:
  1. Has a little → spends his own resources for Shabbos
  2. Has nothing → not obligated to beg, but must do something small (the bare minimum of oneg)
  3. The kupah is not enough even for the minimum → cuts back on weekday meals to free up something
  4. Refuses tzedakah by choice → cuts back on the week — this is better than being dependent on others

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — Bochur on a stipend with small savings: must draw on his savings for kavod Shabbos — not wait for additional aid.
  • Case 2 — Modest family that asks for nothing: cuts back a little during the week (a little less meat, no festive dishes) so as to be able to set out a bottle of wine + homemade challah on Shabbos. The Rav values this approach.
  • Case 3 — Widow who is helped but wants a little more: must cut back during the week rather than going beyond what the kehillah offers.
  • Case 4 — Precarious worker without savings: may make do with a "דבר מועט" on Shabbos (a simple dish set aside for Shabbos) — is mekayem the mitzvah.
  • Case 5 — Person emerging from poverty, refusing aid: a valued conduct — cutting back during the week to have a little for Shabbos is better than being dependent.

Key points to remember

  • The Rav articulates an algorithm on 4 levels according to the economic situation
  • Having a little of one's own = chiyuv to spend for Shabbos
  • Having nothing = exempt, but "דבר מועט" remains obligatory
  • If the kupah is insufficient: cutting back during the week is the recommended solution
  • Refusing aid and cutting back is better than being dependent — מעלת ההסתפקות

14. Seif Zayin — Two cooked dishes minimum, fish, and communal boycott

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ז)

אַף מִי שֶׁאֵין יָדוֹ מַשֶּׂגֶת לְהַרְבּוֹת בְּמַאֲכָלֵי שַׁבָּת — טוֹב לוֹ לִזָּהֵר שֶׁלֹּא יִפְחוֹת מִשְּׁנֵי תַבְשִׁילִין. וְטוֹב לִזָּהֵר לֶאֱכוֹל דָּגִים בְּכָל סְעֻדָּה, אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן הַדָּגִים מַזִּיקִים לוֹ לְפִי טִבְעוֹ, אוֹ שֶׁשּׂוֹנְאָם, שֶׁאֵין לוֹ מֵהֶם עֹנֶג אֶלָּא צַעַר, וְהַשַּׁבָּת לְעֹנֶג נִתַּן, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רפ"ח.

What does this Seif discuss?

Three interwoven topics: (1) the minimum floor of Shabbos — two cooked dishes; (2) the status of fish — recommended except in special cases; (3) a rare communal halachah: the temporary boycott against price gouging.

The halachic question

Three questions:
  1. How many cooked dishes minimum for Shabbos?
  2. Is fish obligatory?
  3. What does one do when the market prevents the aniyim from being mekayem oneg?

15. Seif Ches — Food sent for Shabbos

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ח)

אִם שָׁלְחוּ לְאָדָם אֵיזֶה דְבַר מַאֲכָל שֶׁיֹּאכְלֶנּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת — אַל יֹאכְלֶנּוּ בְּחֹל (מִמִּדַּת חֲסִידוּת, אֲבָל מִן הַדִּין אֵין אִסּוּר בַּדָּבָר, עַיֵּן סִמָּן תרצ"ד).

English translation

If they sent someone a food item for him to eat on Shabbos — let him not eat it on a weekday (out of midas chassidus — a quality of piety; but according to strict halachah, there is no issur in the matter; see siman תרצ״ד regarding Purim).

What does this Seif discuss?

A common practical case: someone offers a food gift saying "this is for your Shabbos". Can it be eaten before? The Rav distinguishes between midas chassidus (a quality of piety, a preferable conduct) and strict din (a legal prohibition).

The halachic question

The question: are foods sent specifically for Shabbos legally consecrated to Shabbos? If yes, they cannot be diverted. If no, one may, but it is less ideal.

The opinions

Source Position
Beis Yosef Distinction between the sender's intent (lichvod Shabbos) and the recipient's rights.
Magen Avraham Midas chassidus — without absolute issur.
Mishnah Berurah Takes up the same distinction and refers to the rules of Purim (mishloach manos).
SHHaRav (242:8) Paskens cleanly: from the din, there is no issur; from midas chassidus, one refrains. The Rav distinguishes precisely between the two orders.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

Two levels:
  1. Strict din: no issur — one may technically consume it during the week
  2. Midas chassidus: one respects the sender's intent and keeps it for Shabbos — this is in line with kavod Shabbos

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — A neighbor brings a dish on Tuesday "for your Shabbos": keep it for Shabbos (midas chassidus). If the fridge breaks, one may eat it earlier — no issur.
  • Case 2 — A gift of wine offered "special for Shabbos": set it aside for kiddush or the seudah.
  • Case 3 — Leftovers from the previous Shabbos: not subject to this rule (this is about new sendings, not surplus).
  • Case 4 — Mishloach manos on Purim: see siman תרצ״ד — parallel rule for Purim.
  • Case 5 — Food that will not last until Shabbos: midas chassidus does not require wasting — one consumes it during the week and prepares something else for Shabbos.

Key points to remember

  • No strict issur to eat during the week a food "designated for Shabbos"
  • Midas chassidus: one respects the sender's intent
  • Distinction din vs. chassidus by the Rav — very illuminating
  • Exception in case of risk of waste
  • See also siman תרצ״ד for mishloach manos of Purim

16. Seif Tes — Borrowing with rabbinic ribbis

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ט)

מֻתָּר לִלְווֹת בְּרִבִּית שֶׁל דִּבְרֵיהֶם לְצֹרֶךְ סְעֻדַּת שַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב, אוֹ לְצֹרֶךְ שְׁאָר סְעֻדַּת מִצְוָה, אִם אִי אֶפְשָׁר לִלְווֹת בְּלֹא רִבִּית.

English translation

It is permitted to borrow with rabbinic ribbis (ribbis de'rabbanan = indirect / non-fixed interest forbidden only by the Chachamim) for the seudas Shabbos or Yom Tov, or for other seudos mitzvah, if it is impossible to borrow without ribbis.

What does this Seif discuss?

A crucial technical clarification: is the issur of ribbis (interest) loosened for Shabbos? The Rav paskens yes, but only for rabbinic ribbis (not biblical), and only if no other way is possible.

The halachic question

The hierarchy of ribbis:
  • Ribbis de'oraisa = ribbis ketzutzah, fixed interest agreed upon between Yidden — strictly forbidden
  • Ribbis de'rabbanan = avak ribbis (indirect cases, deposits, unbalanced exchanges) — forbidden by the Chachamim, may be relaxed in cases of mitzvah

The opinions

Source Position
Yerushalmi (cited by the Maharil 37) Talmudic source for the heter for a seudas mitzvah.
Maharil Permits a loan with ribbis de'rabbanan for a seudas Shabbos / mitzvah.
Tur · Mechaber (YD 160) Discuss the conditions.
Taz · Shach (YD 172) Discuss the limits — no ribbis ketzutzah, even for a mitzvah.
SHHaRav (242:9) Codifies: permitted only for ribbis de'rabbanan, and only if no other way exists. The Rav specifies in the KA (אות ה) the exact conditions.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

Cumulative conditions:
  1. It must be for a seudas Shabbos / Yom Tov / mitzvah
  2. It must be that only a loan with ribbis de'rabbanan is available
  3. It must be that it is impossible to borrow without any ribbis at all
  4. Ribbis de'oraisa (ketzutzah) remains forbidden — no heter

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — Family without cash on Friday afternoon: may use a credit card (deferred interest, considered de'rabbanan in certain configurations according to the poskim) to buy Shabbos needs — if truly no other option.
  • Case 2 — Loan between Yidden with fixed interest: ribbis de'oraisa — forbidden even for Shabbos. The Rav is clear.
  • Case 3 — Bank heter iska: standard way around ribbis — applicable even outside a mitzvah context, but particularly valid for Shabbos needs.
  • Case 4 — Hot meal ordered for delivery on credit: if the surcharge constitutes ribbis de'rabbanan, it may be justified for a seudas Shabbos.
  • Case 5 — Bank overdraft with a non-Jew: generally permitted (no ribbis applies between a Yid and a non-Jew on this level).

Key points to remember

  • The Rav permits only ribbis de'rabbanan — not de'oraisa
  • Conditions: seudas mitzvah + impossibility of other ways
  • Do not confuse with "לוו עלי" of Seif Gimel (which speaks of borrowing in general, not specifically of ribbis)
  • The Rav specifies the nuances in the KA אות ה
  • Consult a Rav for modern configurations (credit cards, overdrafts, etc.)
Comprehension question: Why do the Chachamim permit transgressing their own issur (ribbis de'rabbanan) for a seudas Shabbos? Can the governing principle be extended to other mitzvos?

17. Seif Yod — "Zachor": Beis Shammai vs. Beis Hillel

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif י)

"זָכוֹר אֶת יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת לְקַדְּשׁוֹ וְגוֹ'", דָּרְשׁוּ בֵּית שַׁמַּאי שֶׁתְּהֵא זוֹכְרוֹ מֵאֶחָד בַּשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁאִם נִזְדַּמֵּן לְךָ חֵלֶק יָפֶה תְּהֵא מְתַקְּנוֹ לְשַׁבָּת.

וְאָמְרוּ עָלָיו עַל שַׁמַּאי הַזָּקֵן שֶׁכָּל יָמָיו הָיָה אוֹכֵל לִכְבוֹד שַׁבָּת, כֵּיצַד? מָצָא בְּהֵמָה נָאָה — לוֹקְחָהּ וְאוֹמֵר "זוֹ לְשַׁבָּת", מָצָא אַחֶרֶת נָאָה הֵימֶנָּה — לוֹקְחָהּ וּמַנִּיחָהּ לְשַׁבָּת, וְאוֹכֵל אֶת הָרִאשׁוֹנָה בְּחֹל, נִמְצָא אוֹכְלָהּ לְזוֹ כְּדֵי שֶׁהַיָּפָה תֵּאָכֵל בְּשַׁבָּת.

אֲבָל הִלֵּל הַזָּקֵן מִדָּה אַחֶרֶת הָיְתָה בּוֹ, שֶׁהָיָה אוֹמֵר "בָּרוּךְ ה' יוֹם יוֹם יַעֲמָס לָנוּ צְרָכֵינוּ". וְאַף הִלֵּל מוֹדֶה שֶׁכְּדִבְרֵי בֵּית שַׁמַּאי יוֹתֵר נָכוֹן לַעֲשׂוֹת, אֶלָּא שֶׁהָיָה בּוֹטֵחַ בַּה' שֶׁבְּוַדַּאי יַזְמִין לוֹ לְשַׁבָּת מָנָה יָפָה מִכָּל הַיָּמִים.

English translation

"Zachor es yom haShabbos lekadsho" — Beis Shammai expounded: you should remember it from Sunday onward, so that if a fine piece comes your way, you prepare it for Shabbos.

They said about Shammai HaZaken that all his days he ate for the kavod of Shabbos. How so? If he found a fine animal, he would take it and say: "this one for Shabbos". If he found another finer one, he would take this one and set it aside for Shabbos, and eat the first one during the week — he ate this one in order that the finer one would be eaten on Shabbos.

But Hillel HaZaken had a different conduct: he would say "Boruch Hashem yom yom, ya'amos lanu" (Blessed is Hashem day by day, He provides for our needs). And even Hillel concedes that it is more correct to act according to Beis Shammai, but he had bitachon in Hashem that He would surely send him for Shabbos a finer portion than all the [other] days.

What does this Seif discuss?

A magnificent philosophical-practical topic: when does Shabbos begin? Beis Shammai says "from Sunday" — the entire week is oriented toward Shabbos. Beis Hillel says "each day in its time" — bitachon that Hashem will provide at the right moment. The Rav paskens: the conduct of Beis Shammai is more correct, even Hillel acknowledges this.

The halachic question

The question: should one set aside the best as soon as it is found during the week, or trust that the best will come at the right time?

The opinions

Source Position
Beitzah 16a The story of Shammai vs. Hillel — Hillel concedes the superiority of the conduct of Beis Shammai.
Mechilta (Yisro) Derashah on "זכור" as a constant reminder throughout the week.
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 30:7) Mentions the conduct of Shammai as exemplary.
Mechaber (OC 250:1) Follows the same line on preparations.
SHHaRav (242:10) Codifies the story in the siman and specifies: the conduct of Beis Shammai is more correct; Hillel acted out of exceptional bitachon, not as a model for everyone.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

Three teachings:
  1. The commandment "זכור" implies an active memory throughout the week
  2. The conduct of Beis Shammai (setting aside the best as soon as one finds it) is more correct
  3. The conduct of Beis Hillel (bitachon that the best will come) is exceptional — reserved for those of a particular level of bitachon

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — At the supermarket mid-week: finding a fine piece of meat or a beautiful fish — set it aside mentally for Shabbos, even buy it and put it in the freezer.
  • Case 2 — At the market on Tuesday: seeing beautiful fruit — thinking "this is for Shabbos".
  • Case 3 — Receiving a quality food gift: set it aside "for Shabbos" (cf. Seif Ches).
  • Case 4 — Cook at a restaurant: note which dishes are particularly successful in order to make them again for Shabbos.
  • Case 5 — The chassid at a high level of bitachon: may adopt Hillel's conduct — bitachon that Hashem will send. For the majority, Beis Shammai is the standard.

Key points to remember

  • "זכור" = active memory of Shabbos throughout the week
  • Conduct of Beis Shammai = normative standard
  • Conduct of Beis Hillel = exceptional, for those of a particular bitachon
  • Even Hillel acknowledges the superiority of Beis Shammai on this point
  • Concrete application: set aside the best as soon as you encounter it during the week
Comprehension question: Why does the halachah normally follow Beis Hillel, but on this specific point Hillel himself concedes the superiority of Beis Shammai? What does this reveal about the nature of Shabbos?

18. Seif Yod-Alef — Tikkun Ezra: laundering on Thursday

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif יא)

עֶזְרָא תִּקֵּן שֶׁיִּהְיוּ מְכַבְּסִים בְּגָדִים בַּחֲמִישִׁי בַּשַּׁבָּת, מִפְּנֵי כְּבוֹד הַשַּׁבָּת, כְּדֵי לִלְבּוֹשׁ לְבָנִים בְּשַׁבָּת, אֲבָל בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת אֵין פְּנַאי לְכַבֵּס, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְהִתְעַסֵּק בְּצָרְכֵי שַׁבָּת.

English translation

Ezra instituted that one should launder clothes on the 5th day of the week (= Thursday) for kavod Shabbos, in order to wear clean garments on Shabbos; but on erev Shabbos, there is no leisure to launder, because one must be occupied with the needs of Shabbos.

What does this Seif discuss?

The Rav takes up, in his own text, the historical takkanah attributed to Ezra HaSofer (5th century BCE): laundering on Thursday. Why Thursday specifically, and not Friday? The Rav connects the two reasons: (1) having clean clothes for Shabbos, (2) freeing up Friday for the other preparations.

The halachic question

The question: is the takkanah just an advice for good organization, or a real rabbinic decree that forbids (or discourages) laundering on Friday?

The opinions

Source Position
Bava Kama 82a Source of Ezra's 10 takkanos, including that of Thursday laundering.
Rambam Codifies the takkanah without making it an absolute chiyuv.
Rama (OC 242:1) Brief mention of the takkanah in a hagahah.
Magen Avraham The aim is to have clean clothes — therefore even Friday laundering is acceptable if necessary.
Mishnah Berurah (242:5) The aim is also to free up Friday for Shabbos preparations — therefore one avoids laundering on Friday.
SHHaRav (242:11) Articulates the two reasons: (1) "כדי ללבוש לבנים בשבת", (2) "אין פנאי לכבס מפני שצריך להתעסק בצרכי שבת". A more complete structural position.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

The Rav unifies the two te'amim:
  1. Positive purpose: wearing clean garments on Shabbos (active kavod)
  2. Organizational purpose: freeing up Friday for the preparations of Shabbos (logic of hierarchy of activities)

Consequence: one prefers Thursday; Friday remains technically possible but should be avoided.

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — Family that usually launders on Friday: the Rav's advice: shift to Thursday.
  • Case 2 — Stained garment Friday afternoon: may be laundered urgently — the issur is not absolute.
  • Case 3 — Family without a home washing machine (shared laundromat): schedule the Thursday slot.
  • Case 4 — Special Shabbos clothes: particularly important to prepare on Thursday.
  • Case 5 — Emergency (uniform, work clothes): may launder on Friday without transgressing.

Key points to remember

  • Clothes are laundered in principle on Thursday, not Friday
  • Two reasons unified by the Rav: clean clothes for Shabbos + Friday free
  • Not an absolute issur — a tikkun of organization
  • The positive purpose of Shabbos structures the week
  • Extendable logic: all major chores are done during the week, not Friday
Comprehension question: Why did Ezra choose the 5th day (Thursday) rather than the 4th (Wednesday) or the 3rd (Tuesday)? What halachic principle is at stake?

19. Seif Yod-Bet — Kneading the challos at home

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif יב)

נוֹהֲגִין לָלוּשׁ כְּדֵי שִׁעוּר חַלָּה כָּל אֶחָד בְּבֵיתוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת מֵהֶם לְחָמִים לִבְצוֹעַ עֲלֵיהֶם בְּשַׁבָּת, וְלֹא לִקַּח לֶחֶם מִן הַשּׁוּק כְּמוֹ בִּשְׁאָר יָמִים, וְדָבָר זֶה מִכְּבוֹד שַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב, וְאֵין לְשַׁנּוֹת הַמִּנְהָג.

English translation

The minhag is for each one to knead at home the shiur that requires hafrashas challah, to make from it loaves over which one will be botzeia on Shabbos — and not to take bread from the marketplace as on weekdays. This is from kavod Shabbos and Yom Tov, and one should not change this minhag.

What does this Seif discuss?

The Rav codifies in the very body of his Shulchan Aruch (not in a hagahah like the Rama) the minhag of kneading the challos at home. Three interwoven functions: (1) being mekayem mitzvas challah; (2) having fresh lechem mishneh; (3) kavod Shabbos.

The halachic question

The question: may one buy commercial bread for Shabbos, or must one absolutely knead at home? Is the minhag optional or binding?

The opinions

Source Position
Mechaber Silent — not codified in the body of the SA (Ashkenazi minhag).
Rama (hagahah OC 242:1) Codifies the minhag in a hagahah: "ואין לשנות".
Magen Avraham · Mishnah Berurah Three reasons: (1) mitzvas challah; (2) fresh lechem mishneh; (3) kavod Shabbos.
SHHaRav (242:12) Codifies the minhag in the body of the Shulchan Aruch (not in a hagahah). "ואין לשנות המנהג" — a stronger structural position.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

Three functions of home kneading:
  1. Mitzvas hafrashas challah — elevating the dough through the separation
  2. Fresh lechem mishneh — two loaves for the seudos
  3. Kavod Shabbos and Yom Tov — affirmation of the sacred character of the table

Strength of the pesak: "ואין לשנות המנהג" — not just a custom, but a structural kavod.

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — Baalas habayis who kneads each week: fully fulfills the minhag — this is the valued standard.
  • Case 2 — Family that buys challos from a Jewish baker: fulfills the kavod Shabbos function, but loses mitzvas challah at home. Acceptable but below the standard.
  • Case 3 — Single bochur: may forgo personal kneading — less relevant for a single person, but valued if he does it.
  • Case 4 — Shabbos travel without a kitchen: one buys from a Yid, makes a brachah, fulfills the mitzvah without the kneading minhag.
  • Case 5 — Family with young children: involving the children in the kneading = active transmission of kavod Shabbos — recommended.

Key points to remember

  • Kneading at home = valued and structural minhag
  • Three functions: mitzvas challah, fresh lechem mishneh, kavod Shabbos
  • Strength of the pesak: "ואין לשנות המנהג" — a minhag to preserve
  • Codified in the body of the Shulchan Aruch by the Rav (not only in a hagahah)
  • Failing that, buying Jewish bread remains valid

20. Seif Yod-Gimel — Pas akum and Shabbos challos

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif יג)

(וּבִמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁאוֹכְלִים פַּת שֶׁל נָכְרִים כָּל יְמוֹת הַחֹל — טוֹב לִזָּהֵר שֶׁלֹּא לֶאֱכוֹל בְּשַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב כִּי אִם מִלְּחָמִים הַכְּשֵׁרִים שֶׁנִּלּוֹשׁוּ בְּבֵיתוֹ, שֶׁזֶּהוּ כְּבוֹד שַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב).

English translation

(And in the places where pas akum is eaten [pas akum, considered kosher but lacking the quality of pas Yisrael] on all weekdays — it is good to be careful not to eat on Shabbos or Yom Tov anything but kosher loaves kneaded at home [or by a Yid], for this is the kavod of Shabbos and Yom Tov.)

What does this Seif discuss?

A precision of application: what does one do in regions where it is customary — for practical reasons — to consume pas akum (bread baked by a non-Jewish baker, halachically permitted but less ideal) during the week? The Rav paskens: for Shabbos and Yom Tov, one strives to eat kosher bread kneaded at home or by a Yid — this is kavod Shabbos.

The halachic question

The question: is pas akum (permitted during the week in certain configurations) also permitted for Shabbos, or is there an additional kavod requirement?

The opinions

Source Position
Yoreh Deah 112 General rule of pas akum vs. pas Yisrael — for Shabbos, one is more stringent.
Beis Yosef Recommends pas Yisrael for Shabbos.
Mishnah Berurah Codifies the requirement for Shabbos.
SHHaRav (242:13) Explicitly extends: even in places where pas akum is customary during the week, one refrains on Shabbos and Yom Tov. A structural position that widens the scope of the minhag of Seif Yod-Bet.

The Alter Rebbe's pesak

The principle:
  1. During the week, pas akum may be halachically permitted in certain regions
  2. For Shabbos and Yom Tov, one raises the standard: bread kneaded at home or by a Yid
  3. Reason: kavod Shabbos and Yom Tov — not an issur, but a positive requirement of spiritual quality

Practical cases

  • Case 1 — Family in a region where Jewish bread is scarce: during the week, pas akum if necessary; on Shabbos, one strives to knead at home or buy from a Yid (even at a distance).
  • Case 2 — Travel where only non-Jewish bread is available: in case of force majeure, may be consumed — the Rav speaks of "טוב לזהר", not of an absolute issur.
  • Case 3 — Supermarket bread certified kosher but produced by a non-Jew: between the two categories — check the exact status (pas palter, pas Yisrael, etc.). For Shabbos, prefer pas Yisrael.
  • Case 4 — Tourist in a country without a Jewish kehillah: may consume pas akum if no alternative, even for Shabbos — the mitzvah of oneg must not be blocked.
  • Case 5 — Strict chassidishe family: only buys Jewish bread (pas Yisrael) including during the week — all the more so on Shabbos.

Key points to remember

  • For Shabbos and Yom Tov, one raises the standard of the bread
  • Even where pas akum is accepted during the week
  • Reason: structural kavod Shabbos, not an additional issur
  • Bread kneaded at home remains the ideal (cf. Seif Yod-Bet)
  • Pas Yisrael bought from a Yid = acceptable standard
  • Force majeure (travel, etc.): the mitzvah of oneg must not be blocked

21. Practical synthesis of Siman רמ״ב

The 5 teachings of Seif Alef in 5 sentences:
  1. Everyone must honor Shabbos — except the absolute extreme aniy
  2. The kavod is measured by one's means: refinement for the wealthy, the symbolic for the middling, nothing for one who has nothing
  3. It is preferable to borrow without ribbis than to diminish the kavod of Shabbos — Hashem promises to repay
  4. Launder clothes on Thursday, not Friday — Tikkun Ezra
  5. Knead bread at home in the shiur of challah — a minhag to preserve
The Alter Rebbe's own contributions (Seifim Bet to Yod-Gimel):
  1. Seif Bet — Oneg is subjective and cultural; meat and wine are a general presumption, not an absolute chiyuv
  2. Seif Gimel — "לוו עלי": Shabbos is off the annual budget; the promise is structural, on condition of having an asset to pawn
  3. Seif Dalet — The aniy who has 14 seudos does not go to the kupah, even for the 3rd seudah
  4. Seif Heh — The aniy already on the kupah receives 15 seudos + fish + vegetable — "די מחסורו"
  5. Seif Vav — A 4-level algorithm for intermediate nuances; cutting back during the week is better than being dependent
  6. Seif Zayin — Floor: 2 cooked dishes; fish recommended except for health/distaste; communal boycott against price gouging
  7. Seif Ches — Foods sent "for Shabbos": midas chassidus not to consume them during the week
  8. Seif Tes — Ribbis de'rabbanan permitted for seudas Shabbos if no other way; de'oraisa always forbidden
  9. Seif Yod — "זכור": Beis Shammai's conduct (setting aside the best from Sunday) is more correct
  10. Seif Yod-Alef — Tikkun Ezra: laundering on Thursday for clean clothes + free Friday
  11. Seif Yod-Bet — Kneading the challos at home: ואין לשנות המנהג
  12. Seif Yod-Gimel — Even where pas akum is accepted during the week, one raises the standard for Shabbos

Practical decision table

Situation Conduct
I earn a good living Refined foods, meat, fish, wine according to my means
I earn just what I need At minimum 3 seudos, and a dish marking the kavod
I earn little and receive aid At minimum a כסא דהרסנא + 3 seudos
I am in extreme distress Exempt — 2 seudos suffice
I lack money for Shabbos Borrow without ribbis (best), or with a pledge
