1. The central axiom

Siman רצ"ו in one sentence.
Havdalah on the cup follows the order YBN"H (Yayin · Besamim · Ner · Havdalah), on a non-pagum cup of wine (or chamar medinah in default, never on bread or water). Priority: Chanukah > havdalah > Kiddush. Sefardim seated, Ashkenazim standing. Women obligated (Mechaber) but listen from men (Rama).

4. Decision tree — With which cup to make havdalah?

Q1: Do I have a cup of wine (or kosher grape juice) non-pagum?
↓ Yes → use it
↓ No, pagum wine → repair + use
↓ No wine at all → next
Q2: Do I have chamar medinah (beer, whisky)?
↓ Yes → use it ("she-ha-kol nihiyah bi-dvaro" instead of Borei pri ha-gafen)
↓ No → next
Q3: Hope of having wine tomorrow?
↓ Yes → wait until tomorrow, do not eat
↓ No → eat + BHM with cup + havdalah on it

5. Chamar medinah and the havdalah priority

The heart of the siman is the question: on what does one make havdalah when there is no wine? The siman determines a strict hierarchy — wine (ideal) > chamar medinah ("wine of the country") > nothing — and absolutely refuses water and bread. To understand the notion of chamar medinah is to understand the whole practical lever of the siman.

What is chamar medinah? A non-aqueous beverage, sufficiently esteemed to be served to a distinguished guest in the country where one is: beer, whisky, grape juice. Its berachah is not borei pri ha-gafen but its own.
↓
Why never water or bread? Water is not an "honored beverage". Bread is excluded — unlike Kiddush where it can replace wine — because havdalah is not a meal: it closes Shabbos, it does not introduce it.
↓
Edge case — the pagum cup. Even when one has wine, if it is pagum (used), one does not make havdalah on it as is: one must "repair" it by adding fresh wine. The rule of honoring the cup tolerates no negligence.

From this hierarchy flows also the central budget priority rule of the siman: among Chanukah, havdalah and Kiddush, the order is Chanukah > havdalah > Kiddush. Havdalah comes before Kiddush precisely because it has no alternative — Kiddush, itself, can be done on bread. Chanukah precedes it in the name of pirsumei nissa. The whole siman reads thus through a single question: what to do when means are lacking?

6. Mnemonic — "יבנ"ה"

י — יין (Yayin): Borei pri ha-gafen

ב — בשמים (Besamim): Borei minei besamim

נ — נר (Ner): Borei me'orei ha-eish

ה — הבדלה (Havdalah): Ha-Mavdil

Yavneh (city of the Sages) = easy mnemonic.

Hierarchy of priorities:

1 — Chanukah (pirsumei nissa)

2 — Havdalah (no alternative)

3 — Kiddush (alternative possible: bread)

7. Pitfalls to avoid

Pitfall 1 — Making havdalah on water: absolutely forbidden (seif ב).
Pitfall 2 — Making havdalah on bread: forbidden (unlike Kiddush where bread is possible).
Pitfall 3 — Drinking from a pagum cup without repairing: add a little fresh wine first, otherwise potential berachah le-vatalah.
Pitfall 4 — Reversing the YBN"H order: order fixed by the Gemara. Do not bless N before B, or H before N.
Pitfall 5 — Considering havdalah as a detail: it is as important as Kiddush. Possibly de'oraisa according to the Rambam.
Pitfall 6 — Sephardi who stands / Ashkenazi who sits: strong minhag of the community. Respect local custom.

10. The 6 practical commandments of Siman רצ"ו

For havdalah at home

  1. Prepare a full cup of wine (or chamar medinah in default) — non-pagum.
  2. Follow the YBN"H order — Yayin, Besamim, Ner, Havdalah.
  3. Posture: seated (Sephardi) or standing (Ashkenazi).
  4. Drink at least a revi'is (≈ 86 ml) after the last berachah.
  5. Spill, extinguish, wash eyes — Ashkenazi minhag of chibuv mitzvah (Rama).
  6. Women: listen from a man or do it themselves if alone.
