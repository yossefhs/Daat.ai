1. The central axiom

Siman שפ״ה in one sentence.
Among those who live with Jews, the siman says who is treated like a Jew, through renouncing the domain, and who like a non-Jew, through renting. The Tzeduki renounces his domain, but the eruv does not help with him (seif א); the Kuti: « כותי הרי הוא כשאר עכו״ם ואין לו תקנה אלא בשכירות » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ה:ב); the mumar for idolatry, or for desecrating Shabbos in public, even through a rabbinic prohibition, is like a non-Jew, and one who desecrates only in private, even through a Torah prohibition, is like a Jew (seif ג); one who changed his religion, when the Jews' neighborhood had made the eruv for the whole year, forbids on every Shabbos that falls afterwards, but if he has another entrance to the neighborhood of the non-Jews, even a small one, we push him off to it (seif ד).

3. Structure of the siman — from the Tzeduki to one who changed his religion

Seif א — the Tzeduki: he is like a Jew and renounces his domain; but the eruv does not help with him, since he does not acknowledge the eruv.
↓
Seif ב — the Kuti: he is like other non-Jews; his only remedy is renting.
↓
Seif ג — the mumar: for idolatry, or for desecrating Shabbos in public, even if he desecrates only through a rabbinic prohibition, he is like a non-Jew; if he desecrates only in private, even through a Torah prohibition, he is like a Jew and renounces his domain.
↓
Seif ד — one who changed his religion: he had houses in the Jews' neighborhood, which had made the eruv for the whole year; if he has no other entrance at all, he forbids on every Shabbos that falls after he changed his religion; if he has another entrance to the neighborhood of the non-Jews, even a small one that he was not accustomed to at first, we push him off to it.

4. Decision tree — renouncing the domain, or renting?

Q1: Who is the one living with them? (seifim א, ב and ג)
↓ A Tzeduki → he renounces his domain, but the eruv does not help with him (seif א); on a Tzeduki who desecrates Shabbos in public, the views differ (section 5). A Kuti → only renting (seif ב). A mumar → Q2.
Q2: For what is he a mumar, and does he desecrate in public or in private? (seif ג)
↓ For idolatry (according to the Mishnah Berurah, even in private, ס״ק ג), or for desecrating Shabbos in public, even through a rabbinic prohibition → like a non-Jew; according to the Mishnah Berurah, renouncing no longer helps, only renting (ס״ק ה), and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes that one must rent from him (seif 3). Only in private, even through a Torah prohibition → like a Jew, he renounces his domain. Who is called a mumar: the views differ (section 5).
Q3: A Jew who had houses in the Jews' neighborhood, which had made the eruv for the whole year, changed his religion: does he have another entrance to the neighborhood of the non-Jews? (seif ד)
↓ NO → he forbids on every Shabbos that falls after he changed his religion; according to the Mishnah Berurah, until one rents from him (ס״ק ז), and if he changed his religion on that very Shabbos, that Shabbos remains permitted (ס״ק ח). YES, even a small one → we push him off to it; according to the Mishnah Berurah, it must be at least four tefachim by four (ס״ק י׳), and if he opened it after changing his religion, it refers to siman 383 (ס״ק ט).
→ At the slightest doubt: consult your Rav.

6. Mnemonic — one word per seif

Four seifim, four words taken from their text, in order:

א — צדוקי: he renounces his domain, but the eruv does not help with him.

ב — כותי: like other non-Jews, his only remedy is renting.

ג — מומר: for idolatry, or for desecrating Shabbos in public even through a rabbinic prohibition, like a non-Jew; only in private, even through a Torah prohibition, like a Jew.

ד — שהמיר: when the neighborhood had made the eruv for the whole year, with no other entrance, he forbids on every Shabbos that follows; with another entrance to the neighborhood of the non-Jews, even a small one, we push him off to it.

7. Pitfalls to avoid

Pitfall 1: Thinking that the Tzeduki, since he renounces his domain, may also join the eruv with the others. Seif א: « צדוקי הרי הוא כישראל ומבטל רשות אבל עירוב אינו מועיל כיון שאינו מודה בעירוב » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ה:א). And according to the Mishnah Berurah, even if he gave his share in the eruv: « אף אם נתן חלק בעירוב אינו מועיל ואוסר עליהן עד שיבטל רשותו להן » (משנה ברורה שפ״ה ס״ק ב).
Pitfall 2: Thinking that the Kuti renounces his domain like the Tzeduki. Seif ב: « כותי הרי הוא כשאר עכו״ם ואין לו תקנה אלא בשכירות » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ה:ב).
Pitfall 3: Thinking that only desecration through a Torah prohibition makes the mumar like a non-Jew, or that it always does. Seif ג: « ישראל מומר לעבודת אלילים או לחלל שבתות בפרהסיא אפילו אינו מחללו אלא באיסור דרבנן הרי הוא כעכו״ם » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ה:ג); then « ואם אינו מחלל אלא בצינע׳ אפילו מחללו באיסור דאוריית׳ הרי הוא כישראל ומבטל רשות » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ה:ג). For idolatry, the Mishnah Berurah writes « אפילו בצינעה » (משנה ברורה שפ״ה ס״ק ג).
Pitfall 4: Thinking that the seif decides who is called a mumar, and for which questions he is like a non-Jew. Seif ג does not define the public mumar; the Mishnah Berurah brings two views on a single desecration (ס״ק ד) and, on desecration through a rabbinic prohibition, writes that some Acharonim hold otherwise for other matters (ס״ק ה); the Shulchan Aruch HaRav gives its own definition and writes in all respects (seif 3). See section 5; consult your Rav.
Pitfall 5: Thinking that an eruv made for the whole year also covers the Shabbosos after the change of religion. Seif ד: « ישראל שהמיר והיו לו בתים בשכונת ישראל שעירבו לכל השנה אם אין לו פתח אחר כלל הוא אוסר בכל שבת שחל לאחר שהמיר » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ה:ד). The Mishnah Berurah: « דכל שבת ושבת מילתא בפני עצמה היא » (משנה ברורה שפ״ה ס״ק ז); and if he changed his religion on that very Shabbos: « ואם המיר ביום השבת גופא מותר על אותו השבת דכיון שהותר מקצת שבת הותר לכל השבת » (משנה ברורה שפ״ה ס״ק ח).
Pitfall 6: Thinking that any entrance, however small, is enough to push one who changed his religion off to it. Seif ד: « אבל אם יש לו פתח אחר לשכונת העכו״ם אפילו הוא פתח קטן שלא היה רגיל בו מתחיל׳ דוחין אותו אצל פתח הפתוח לשכונת העכו״ם » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ה:ד); the Mishnah Berurah sets a measure for it: « ועכ״פ בעינן שיהיה בו ד״ט על ד״ט דבפחות מזה לא חשיב פתח » (משנה ברורה שפ״ה ס״ק י׳). The Shulchan Aruch HaRav counts even a window: « וַאֲפִלּוּ אֵינוֹ פֶּתַח גָּמוּר אֶלָּא חַלּוֹן ד׳ עַל ד׳ » (שו״ע הרב שפ״ה:ד).

10. The practical commandments of Siman שפ״ה

For daily conduct

  1. The Tzeduki renounces his domain; the eruv does not help with him, since he does not acknowledge the eruv (seif א). On a Tzeduki who desecrates Shabbos in public, the views differ (section 5).
  2. The Kuti is like other non-Jews: his only remedy is renting (seif ב).
  3. The mumar for idolatry, or for desecrating Shabbos in public, even through a rabbinic prohibition, is like a non-Jew; if he desecrates only in private, even through a Torah prohibition, he is like a Jew and renounces his domain (seif ג). Who is called a mumar: the views differ (section 5).
  4. One who changed his religion and had houses in the Jews' neighborhood, which had made the eruv for the whole year: if he has no other entrance at all, he forbids on every Shabbos that falls after he changed his religion; if he has another entrance to the neighborhood of the non-Jews, even a small one, we push him off to it (seif ד).
  5. In case of doubt — consult your Rav. In-depth pilpul — Level 2; Chabad shitah — Level 4.
