The text of the Shulchan Aruch — excerpts from the 22 seifim

אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאָסוּר לְהַנִּיחַ לְנָכְרִי שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה מְלָאכָה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּשַׁבָּת... אֲבָל אִם הוּא עוֹשֶׂה בְּתוֹרַת קַבְּלָנוּת — מֻתָּר. בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בְּדָבָר הַתָּלוּשׁ... אֲבָל בִּמְחֻבָּר — אָסוּר.

1. Introduction to the sougya — Avoda Zara 21b

"תָּנוּ רַבָּנָן: בְּעִיר שֶׁיֵּשׁ בָּהּ גוֹיִים וְיִשְׂרָאֵל וְהָיוּ בָהּ מֶרְחֲצָאוֹת הַמַּרְחִיצִין בְּשַׁבָּת — אִם רֹב גּוֹיִים מֻתָּר... אִם רֹב יִשְׂרָאֵל אָסוּר".

(Avoda Zara 21b)

Root of the sougya: The beraisa establishes the main principle: everything depends on the intent of the act. If the non-Jew intends for himself (majority gentiles = majority gentile customers), it is permitted. If he intends for the Jew (majority Jews = majority Jewish customers), it is forbidden. The kablan is precisely this case: he labors on Shabbos for his own sake (to complete the job and collect his wages), not for the Jew.

2. Chakira in the gader of amira le-akum — shelichus or gezeira?

Chakira: What is the root of the issur of amira le-akum?

  • Tzad 1 — shelichus: "Shelucho shel adam kemoso" — the non-Jew acting for the Jew is his agent. The Jew acts through the shaliach. DeOraisa according to some shitos.
  • Tzad 2 — rabbinic gezeira: Chazal decreed against amira le-akum lest the Jew come to do it himself. DeRabanan.
  • Tzad 3 — extension of a mitzvah: "Shevisas Yisrael" includes that his property and matters not be done on Shabbos by others as his agents.
The Rambam (ch. 6 hal. 1): Tzad 2 — gezeira. "Amira le-nochri be-Shabbos asura mi-divrei sofrim".

The Yereim (siman 304): Tzad 1 — shelichus. Therefore some shitos hold the issur amira is deOraisa.

The Admur HaZaken: Combines tzedadim 1 and 2 — essentially deRabanan, but with an aspect of shelichus.

3. Root of the distinction sechir yom / kablan

"לְפִיכָךְ הֲרֵי זֶה כְּעוֹשֶׂה שְׁלִיחוּת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, אֲבָל הַקַּבְּלָן הַמְּלָאכָה הִיא שֶׁלּוֹ, שֶׁכַּוָּנָתוֹ בַּעֲבוֹדָתוֹ הִיא לְהַשְׁלִים הַמְּלָאכָה כְּדֵי לִטּוֹל שְׂכָרוֹ, וְאֵינוֹ כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל" (Admur HaZaken seif 1).

Yesod: The sechir yom is "as one performing the shelichus of the Jew". The kablan — "the melakha is his". The chiluk lies in the question: whose act is it?. In the sechir yom — the Jew's. In the kablan — the non-Jew's own.

4. Talush vs. mechubar — reality or siman?

Chakira: The issur of mechubar — is it intrinsic, or because of maaras ayin?

  • Tzad 1 — intrinsic: That which is attached to the ground creates a tighter connection to the owner, and kablanus on it does not release one from the shelichus.
  • Tzad 2 — maaras ayin: Mechubar is a public and visible thing. It is forbidden solely because of maaras ayin. Without maaras ayin, mechubar would be like talush.
The Beis Yosef cites seif 2 of the Admur HaZaken: "nikar ha-davar le-chol ro'eh she-gam ha-melakha hi shel ha-Yisrael" — tzad 2, maaras ayin.

The Admur HaZaken in seif 2 paskens clearly like tzad 2.

5. Chakira on maaras ayin — independent issur or extension?

Chakira: Maaras ayin — an independent halachic issur, or merely an extension of an existing issur?

  • Tzad 1 — independent: Chazal decreed maaras ayin as a stand-alone issur. DeRabanan.
  • Tzad 2 — extension: Maaras ayin extends the underlying issur (amira le-akum) — not an independent issur, only an extension.
The Shaar HaTziyun (siman 244 s.k. 7): Maaras ayin is an independent issur. It therefore applies even to a kablan (in which there is no amira).

The Beis Yosef unifies: maaras ayin here is an extension of the issur amira (we are concerned the public will think the non-Jew is a sechir yom).

7. The kablan on the land — seif 2

Kashya: If the kablan comes to the Jew's land within the city — he appears as a sechir yom. But if he comes outside the techum — he does not appear so. What is the limit?

Terutz: Three layers:

  1. Inside the city: all the people of the city see. Forbidden.
  2. Outside the techum and without Jews nearby: no one sees. Permitted.
  3. Outside the techum but with Jews in the vicinity: passers-by might come — we are machmir.

8. Inside the techum and outside the techum — seif 3

The Beis Yosef explains: the techum Shabbos is the criterion. Within the techum, the people of the city can come and see. Outside the techum they are forbidden to come (unless there is an eruv), so the townspeople are not at the location.

9. Explicit amira — absolute prohibition

"וְשֶׁלֹּא יֹאמַר לוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת" (Admur HaZaken seif 1).

Yesod: Even in permitted kablanus — it is forbidden to tell the non-Jew explicitly to work on Shabbos. The amira is explicit shelichus, and it makes no difference whether he is a kablan or a sechir yom. Only the conclusion drawn by the non-Jew himself (= "I will work on Shabbos in order to finish faster") is what permits the matter.

10. Modern practical nafka minos

1. A non-Jewish print shop printing a Jewish book: kablan + talush + at the non-Jew's premises + no explicit "work on Shabbos" — permitted.

2. Construction of a Jew's home: attached to the ground, visible to the public — forbidden even for a kablan. The contract must include a Shabbos pause.

3. Delivery services (Amazon, FedEx): a global kablan, talush, not at the Jew's premises — permitted. But one may not order on Erev Shabbos with the knowledge that the delivery will take place on a specific Shabbos for him.

4. A non-Jewish cleaner working at my home: if paid per task — kablan. But in a Jew's home = maaras ayin. Most poskim are machmir — not on Shabbos.

5. A non-Jewish driver (Uber): a kablan per trip. But "ordering a ride on Shabbos" = explicit amira. Forbidden.

6. A website that updates on Shabbos: if the servers are under kablanus (Cloud), the upgrade is automatic, no explicit amira — permitted.

7. A non-Jewish restaurant providing catering for an event: permitted if paid as a kablan and one did not demand a specific Shabbos.

8. A live-in domestic worker (au pair): if paid by the hour = sechir yom, forbidden on Shabbos. If by task = kablan; but in the home — maaras ayin. As a rule, we are machmir.
