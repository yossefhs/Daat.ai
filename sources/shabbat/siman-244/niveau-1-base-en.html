Siman 244 · 22 Seifim — Having a non-Jew do work via contract. The great siman of kablanus (contract work done by a non-Jew). 22 seifim that codify when a non-Jew may do melacha for a Yid on Shabbos. The key distinction: שכיר יום (sechir yom — a day laborer, the Yid's shaliach, forbidden) vs קבלן (kablan — a contractor paid by the job, acting for his own interest, permitted). The limits: in his own place and not at the Yid's, talush (a movable object) and not mechubar (attached to the ground), and no מראית עין.

General framework & key concepts

1. General context

What is this siman about?

Siman רמ״ד is the great siman of kablanus — the densest of the halachos of אמירה לעכו״ם (amira l'akum). It lays out the conceptual structure that will govern all interactions between a Yid and a non-Jew on Shabbos.

QuestionAnswer
Under what kind of contract does the non-Jew work?By the day (forbidden) or by the job (permitted)
On what does he work?Movable/talush (permitted) or real estate/mechubar (forbidden)
Where?In his own place (permitted) or at the Yid's (forbidden)
Visible to the public?No marit ayin
The foundational sugya — Avodah Zarah 21b and Moed Katan 12a: "נכרי שעוסק במלאכת ישראל בקבלנות — מותר" (a non-Jew engaged in a Yid's work via kablanus is permitted). The discussion turns on the limits: where, what, and how. The conclusion: 4 cumulative conditions.

2. Key concept 1 — שכיר יום vs קבלן

שְׂכִיר יוֹם (sechir yom) = a day laborer. Paid by time. קַבְּלָן (kablan) = a contractor working by the job. Paid by the result. The halacha distinguishes these two modes by agency (שליחות, shelichus):
  • Sechir yom: the Yid rents the non-Jew's time → the non-Jew is his shaliach
  • Kablan: the Yid pays for a result → the non-Jew acts for his own interest (finishing fast = getting paid)

3. Key concept 2 — תלוש vs מחובר

תָּלוּשׁ (talush) = "detached" — a movable object (cloth, paper, a portable machine). מְחֻבָּר לַקַּרְקַע (mechubar lakarka) = "attached to the ground" — land, a building, plantings. The talush can be taken to the non-Jew's place (= invisible). The mechubar is necessarily public (= visible).
The logic: the danger is not the work itself (already resolved by kablanus) but marit ayin — the public image. If passersby see a non-Jew building the Yid's house on Shabbos, they will assume he is a sechir yom (an employee) → a bad example.

4. Key concept 3 — מראית עין and publicity

מַרְאִית עַיִן (marit ayin) = "the appearance to the eye." The halacha forbids certain acts even when permitted in themselves if they give a false impression to the public. Here: a non-Jew working for a Yid on Shabbos in a public place may give the impression he is a direct employee.

5. The 4 cumulative conditions of permitted kablanus

For a non-Jew to be allowed to work on Shabbos for a Yid:
  1. קַבְּלָנוּת — a contract by the job, not by the day
  2. בְּבֵיתוֹ — at the non-Jew's place (not at the Yid's)
  3. תָּלוּשׁ — a movable object (not land or a visible building)
  4. בְּלִי אֲמִירָה — without explicitly telling him to work on Shabbos; no marit ayin
+ The conditions of siman רנ״ב: for work that began on erev Shabbos.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, צָרִיךְ לִזָּהֵר שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשֶׂה בְּבֵית הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְשֶׁלֹּא יֹאמַר לוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת, וְגַם שֶׁיִּקְצוֹץ לוֹ דָּמִים קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת, וְגַם שֶׁלֹּא יִתֵּן לוֹ אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁיּוּכַל הַנָּכְרִי לַעֲשׂוֹת כָּל הַמְּלָאכָה בְּחֹל אִם יִרְצֶה קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת אוֹ לְאַחֲרָיו, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רנ"ב, עַיֵּן שָׁם כָּל פְּרָטֵי דִינִים אֵלּוּ:

English translation

Which melachos a non-Jew may do for the Yid — contains 22 seifim:

Although it is forbidden to allow a non-Jew to do melacha for a Yid on Shabbos — even for pay — when he acts as a sechir yom (a day laborer), for example if the Yid hired him before Shabbos saying "for each day you work for me I will give you such-and-such," even without telling him to work on Shabbos but he works on his own — one must protest (mechaah) and stop him (see siman רמ״ג). But if he acts via kablanus (contract work), having undertaken to do such-and-such for such-and-such a price — whether he works with his own material or with the Yid's (a garment to sew, threads to weave into cloth, whether he undertook the whole garment or by the amah) — it is permitted to let him do all his work on Shabbos.

It is even permitted lechatchilah to hand him the work on erev Shabbos near nightfall (siman רנ״ב), even knowing the non-Jew will do it on Shabbos — and even desiring this — because the non-Jew is not acting for the Yid's benefit but for himself, to finish fast and collect his pay.

This is unlike a sechir yom, whose work is not his own and who therefore acts as the Yid's shaliach. The kablan, however — the work is his own, so he is not like the Yid's shaliach.

Nonetheless, one must take care of four things: (1) that he not work in the Yid's house; (2) that one not tell him to do it on Shabbos; (3) that the price be fixed before Shabbos (ketzitzah); (4) that one give him the work only in a way that he could do all of it during the week — see siman רנ״ב.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav opens the Siman with the foundation of kablanus. The whole question of amira l'akum is: is the non-Jew the Yid's shaliach (shelichus)? The seif contrasts two structures:

The two modes of payment:
  • שְׂכִיר יוֹם — paid by time; the work is not his → the Yid's shaliach → forbidden.
  • קַבְּלָן — paid by result; the work is his own → acts for himself → permitted.

The halachic question

Why does the kablan escape the issur, even though the work benefits the Yid and the Yid desires it? Because the issur of amira rests on shelichus; and the kablan acts אדעתא דנפשיה (adata d'nafshei — on his own initiative). But then why keep four conditions?

The pesak of the Rav

The criterion: "to whom does the work belong?"
  1. By the day: the work is not his → for the Yid → shelichus → forbidden.
  2. Kablanus: the work is his → for himself (adata d'nafshei) → no shelichus → permitted.
  3. Safeguards: not at the Yid's, no amira, ketzitzah before Shabbos, doable during the week.

Modern practical cases

  • A printer paid per print run: 🟢 kablanus → permitted (at his place, price fixed in advance, without saying "Shabbos").
  • A freelancer paid per project: 🟢 kablanus — do not say "deliver on Shabbos" nor demand work doable only on Shabbos.
  • A cleaning lady paid by the hour: 🔴 a sechir yom — my shaliach; forbidden on Shabbos.
  • A translator paid per document: 🟢 kablanus — permitted if the price is fixed in advance.
  • A driver paid per ride: kablanus, but getting in on Shabbos = amira → 🔴 forbidden in practice.

Points to remember

  • The criterion = shelichus: by the day = shaliach → forbidden; by the job = for himself → permitted
  • Permitted even if the Yid desires it to be done on Shabbos
  • Four conditions: not at the Yid's, no amira, ketzitzah before Shabbos, doable during the week
Comprehension question: Why does the Yid's desire (ובכך הוא חפץ) not forbid the matter under kablanus?

7. Seif Beis — תלוש vs מחובר; the gezeirah of מראית עין

Full text of the SHHaRav

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים שֶׁמֻּתָּר לְהַנִּיחַ לְקַבְּלָן נָכְרִי לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת? כְּשֶׁעוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בְּדָבָר הַתָּלוּשׁ מִן הַקַּרְקַע, שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי יָכוֹל לַעֲשׂוֹתָהּ בְּבֵיתוֹ אוֹ בְּמָקוֹם אַחֵר שֶׁאֵינוֹ רְשׁוּת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְאֵין הַדָּבָר נִכָּר שֶׁהִיא מְלֶאכֶת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְאֵין כַּאן חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית הָעַיִן. אֲבָל כְּשֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בִּמְחֻבָּר לַקַּרְקַע, כְּגוֹן שֶׁקִּבֵּל עָלָיו לִבְנוֹת בַּיִת לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, אוֹ לִקְצוֹר שָׂדֶה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהַקַּרְקַע גְּלוּיָה וּמְפֻרְסֶמֶת לַכֹּל שֶׁהִיא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל — נִכָּר הַדָּבָר לְכָל רוֹאֶה שֶׁגַּם הַמְּלָאכָה הִיא שֶׁל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, לְפִיכָךְ אִם הוּא בְּתוֹךְ הָעִיר אוֹ בְּתוֹךְ תְּחוּמָהּ — אָסוּר לְהַנִּיחוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת, מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, שֶׁהָרוֹאֶה יֹאמַר שֶׁהוּא שְׂכִיר יוֹם אֵצֶל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל.

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם אֵין הַדָּבָר מְפֻרְסָם לַכֹּל שֶׁקַּרְקַע זוֹ הִיא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם יֵשׁ לָחוּשׁ לְמַרְאִית הָעַיִן שֶׁל שְׁכֵנָיו, שֶׁהֵם יוֹדְעִים שֶׁקַּרְקַע זוֹ הִיא שֶׁלּוֹ, וְאֵינָן יוֹדְעִים שֶׁהַנָּכְרִִי הָעוֹסֵק בִּמְלָאכָה הוּא קַבְּלָן, אֶלָּא יֹאמְרוּ שֶׁהוּא שְׂכִיר יוֹם.

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם הוּא דָר בִּכְפָר בֵּין הַנָּכְרִים שֶׁאֵין לוֹ שָׁם שְׁכֵנִים יִשְׂרָאֵל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם אִם עוֹשֶׂה בְּתוֹךְ תְּחוּם הַכְּפָר — יֵשׁ לָחוּשׁ שֶׁמָּא יִזְדַּמְּנוּ לוֹ שָׁם אוֹרְחִים שֶׁיִּשְׁבְּתוּ אֶצְלוֹ וְיִרְאוּ אֶת הַנָּכְרִי עוֹסֵק בְּשַׁבָּת בְּקַרְקַע הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְיַחְשְׁדוּהוּ שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי שְׂכִיר יוֹם אֶצְלוֹ. וְגַם יֵשׁ לָחוּשׁ לִבְנֵי בֵיתוֹ, שֶׁהֵם יוֹדְעִים שֶׁהַמְּלָאכָה הִיא שֶׁלּוֹ, וְיַחְשְׁדוּ אוֹתוֹ שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי הוּא שְׂכִיר יוֹם אֶצְלוֹ.

אֲבָל כְּשֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בְּדָבָר תָּלוּשׁ, אַף שֶׁמִּקְצָת יִשְׂרָאֵל יוֹדְעִים שֶׁהַמְּלָאכָה הִיא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל — לֹא גָזְרוּ חֲכָמִים, כֵּיוָן שֶׁרֹב הַפְּעָמִים אֵין הַדָּבָר נוֹדָע לַאֲחֵרִים כְּלָל, שֶׁמְּלֶאכֶת תָּלוּשׁ אֵין לָהּ קוֹל כְּלָל, מַה שֶּׁאֵין כֵּן כְּשֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בִּמְחֻבָּר שֶׁהוּא בְּמָקוֹם גָּלוּי וּמְפֻרְסָם, וְרֹב הַפְּעָמִים הַמְּלָאכָה גְּלוּיָה וּמְפֻרְסֶמֶת לָרַבִּים, שֶׁמְּלֶאכֶת מְחֻבָּר יֵשׁ לָהּ קוֹל, לְפִיכָךְ אִם לִפְעָמִים אֵין חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית הָעַיִן אֶלָּא מִמִּקְצָת יִשְׂרָאֵל גָּזְרוּ חֲכָמִים:

English translation

In which cases is it permitted to let a non-Jewish kablan work on Shabbos? When he works on something detached from the ground (talush), which the non-Jew can do in his own place or somewhere that is not the Yid's domain, where it is not recognizable that it is a Yid's work — there is then no concern of marit ayin.

But when he works on something attached to the ground (mechubar) — for example if he undertook to build a house or to harvest a field for the Yid — since the land is openly and publicly known to belong to the Yid, every passerby understands that the work too is the Yid's. If it is within the city or its techum, it is forbidden on Shabbos, because of marit ayin: people will say he is a sechir yom. And even if the ownership is not widely known, we are concerned for the marit ayin of his neighbors, who know the land is his and do not know the non-Jew is a kablan. Even living in a village with no Jewish neighbors, we are concerned for chance guests or the members of his own household.

For the talush, however, even if some Yidden know the work is a Yid's, Chazal did not decree, because most of the time detached work "has no kol" (אין לה קול — no public resonance), unlike the attached, which is in an open and well-known place.

What is this Seif about?

After the foundation (Seif Alef), the Rav lays down the first great limit of kablanus: everything depends on the nature of the object being worked.

תָּלוּשׁ vs מְחֻבָּר:
  • תָּלוּשׁ (detached) — a movable object (cloth, a sefer, a portable machine). Discreet, "אין לה קול" → permitted.
  • מְחֻבָּר לַקַּרְקַע (attached) — a house, a field, a building site. Public, the land betrays the Jewish owner → marit ayin → forbidden.

The halachic question

If kablanus is already permitted in essence (Seif Alef), why re-forbid the mechubar? The answer: no longer because of shelichus, but because of marit ayin — the appearance: the public will think the non-Jew is a sechir yom of the Yid.

The opinions

SourcePosition
YerushalmiOnly forbade the mechubar — hence the basic distinction.
Tosafos / RanThe detached "לא מיפרסמא מילתא" (is not publicized); the attached, being on the Yid's land, is known as his.
Mechaber (244:1)Permits the detached outside the Yid's domain; forbids the attached within the city/techum.
Mishnah Berurah (244:8-12)Marit ayin applies even to neighbors and guests; details the degrees of notoriety.
SHHaRav (244:2)The theory of "קול": the attached has public resonance, the detached does not; Chazal decreed only where marit ayin is usual.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's rule:
  1. Detached outside the Yid's domain → permitted (no kol, no marit ayin).
  2. Attached within the city or techum → forbidden (the land betrays the owner).
  3. Marit ayin applies even to some Yidden (neighbors, guests, household members).

Modern practical cases

  • A non-Jewish firm builds my house/building: 🔴 visible mechubar → forbidden on Shabbos; the contract must provide for stopping on Shabbos.
  • A non-Jewish workshop sews my clothes at its place: 🟢 discreet talush → permitted.
  • A non-Jewish landscaper mows my garden/field: 🔴 mechubar, land known as mine → forbidden.
  • A print shop runs my sefer in its workshop: 🟢 talush with no kol → permitted.
  • Renovating my store downtown: 🔴 public place, well-known ownership → forbidden even via kablanus.

Points to remember

  • תלוש (movable, discreet) → permitted; מחובר (land, public) → forbidden
  • The driver of the issur is no longer shelichus but marit ayin
  • The attached "has a קול" (public resonance); the detached does not
  • Marit ayin applies even to neighbors and chance guests
Comprehension question: Why does the mechubar remain forbidden even when the non-Jew is a kablan acting for himself?

8. Seif Gimmel — חוץ לתחום: the heter beyond the techum

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְכָל זֶה כְּשֶׁעוֹשֶׂה הַמְּלָאכָה בְּתוֹךְ הָעִיר אוֹ בְּתוֹךְ תְּחוּם הָעִיר, שֶׁיְּכוֹלִין אַנְשֵׁי הָעִיר לֵילֵךְ לְשָׁם בְּשַׁבָּת, אֲבָל כְּשֶׁעוֹשֶׂה הַמְּלָאכָה חוּץ לַתְּחוּם, וְגַם אֵין עִיר אַחֶרֶת שֶׁיִּשְׂרָאֵל דָּרִים בָּהּ תּוֹךְ תְּחוּמָיו שֶׁל מְקוֹם הַמְּלָאכָה — מֻתָּר לְהַנִּיחוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת:

English translation

And all this is only when he does the work within the city or its techum, where the townspeople can come on Shabbos. But if he does the work outside the techum, and there is also no other city where Yidden live within the techum of the work site — it is permitted to let him work on Shabbos.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav clarifies the geographic scope of the gezeirah of Seif Beis. Marit ayin exists only where Yidden could see. Hence the exception: outside the techum.

The condition is twofold: (1) outside the techum of the Yid's city, AND (2) no other Jewish city within the techum of the work site. If no Yid can come there on Shabbos, there is no witness → no marit ayin → permitted, even for mechubar.

The halachic question

The mechubar is forbidden by marit ayin. But marit ayin presupposes a potential Jewish observer. What happens when the work site is too far for any Yid to come there on Shabbos?

The opinions

SourcePosition
Gemara / RishonimThe gezeirah of marit ayin applies only where there is a public liable to see.
Mechaber (244:1)Permits outside the techum, when no Yidden are nearby.
Mishnah Berurah (244:13-15)Requires the two cumulative conditions: outside the Yid's techum and no nearby Jewish city.
SHHaRav (244:3)Formulates the double condition precisely: the criterion of "can come there on Shabbos."

The pesak of the Rav

The heter beyond the techum:
  1. Outside the techum of the Yid's city (≈ 1 km beyond the limits).
  2. No Jewish city within the techum of the work site.
  3. Then even mechubar (a building, a field) becomes permitted on Shabbos.

Modern practical cases

  • A non-Jewish plant in an isolated industrial zone: 🟢 if no Yid can come there on Shabbos → permitted.
  • A warehouse build site far from any community: 🟢 outside the techum + no Yidden → permitted (mechubar).
  • Drilling/extraction in open countryside: 🟢 conditions met → permitted.
  • Construction in a suburb 500 m from a Jewish neighborhood: 🔴 within the techum → forbidden.
  • The Yid's farm worked by non-Jews near a Jewish village: 🔴 a Jewish city within the techum → forbidden.

Points to remember

  • Marit ayin presupposes a potential Jewish observer
  • Double condition: outside the techum of the Yid AND no nearby Jewish city
  • Together, they permit even mechubar on Shabbos
Comprehension question: Why are both conditions needed (outside the Yid's techum and no nearby Jewish city) rather than just one?

9. Seif Daled — Public, well-known talush: the ship (ספינה)

Full text of the SHHaRav

אֲפִלּוּ מְלֶאכֶת תָּלוּשׁ, אִם הִיא מְפֻרְסֶמֶת וִידוּעָה לָרַבִּים שֶׁהִיא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְהִיא נַעֲשֵׂית בְּמָקוֹם גָּלוּי וּמְפֻרְסָם, כְּגוֹן בִּנְיַן הַסְּפִינָה, שֶׁדֶּרֶךְ לִבְנוֹתָהּ עַל שְׂפַת הַנָּהָר שֶׁהוּא מְקוֹם פַּרְהֶסְיָא — אָסוּר לְהַנִּיחוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת, מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן:

English translation

Even detached work (talush), if it is well-known to belong to the Yid and is done in an open, public place — for example building a ship, which is customarily built on the riverbank, a place of full publicity (parhesya) — it is forbidden to let him work on Shabbos, because of marit ayin.

What is this Seif about?

Seif Beis established: detached = permitted, attached = forbidden. The Rav here introduces an exception to the detached: if the detached itself becomes public and identified with the Yid, it joins the regime of the attached.

The case of the ship (ספינה): an object detached by nature, yet built in full view on the riverbank and publicly known as "so-and-so's ship." The notoriety re-creates marit ayin.

The halachic question

If the detached is permitted because it "has no kol" (Seif Beis), what happens when a particular detached item acquires a kol — done in public and attributed by name to the Yid?

The opinions

SourcePosition
Magen Avraham (244)If done in parhesya, forbidden even if only some people know.
Mechaber (244:2)Forbids the public, well-known detached (the ship type) by marit ayin.
Mishnah Berurah (244:16-18)Requires two features: a public place AND the Yid's name attached to the object.
SHHaRav (244:4)Crystallizes the criterion: "ששם הישראל נקרא עליו" — the Yid's name is attached to the thing.

The pesak of the Rav

The exception of the public detached:
  1. Ordinary detached (at the non-Jew's, discreet) → permitted.
  2. Detached in public AND identified by name with the Yid → forbidden (marit ayin).
  3. The Rav's key criterion: the Yid's name is attached to the object (שם הישראל נקרא עליו).

Modern practical cases

  • Building a yacht in the Yid's name at the marina: 🔴 public + named → forbidden.
  • The Yid's car repaired outside, with a known plate: 🔴 if publicly identified as his → forbidden.
  • A lit "House of X" sign mounted on a facade: 🔴 a detached object but public and named → forbidden.
  • Furniture made in the non-Jew's closed workshop: 🟢 discreet → permitted.
  • The Yid's stand set up in the public square: 🔴 public + in his name → forbidden on Shabbos.

Points to remember

  • The detached can become forbidden again if it is public AND named
  • Criterion: שם הישראל נקרא עליו (the Yid's name is attached to the object)
  • The ship on the riverbank is the Talmudic archetype
Comprehension question: What distinguishes the ship (forbidden) from furniture made in the non-Jew's workshop (permitted), both being detached?

10. Seif Heh — דבר שאין שם הישראל נקרא עליו

Full text of the SHHaRav

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? בִּסְפִינָה וְכַיּוֹצֵא בָהּ מִדְּבָרִים שֶׁשֵּׁם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל נִקְרָא עֲלֵיהֶם, שֶׁאוֹמְרִים דָּבָר זֶה הוּא שֶׁל פְּלוֹנִי. אֲבָל דָּבָר שֶׁאֵין שֵׁם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל נִקְרָא עָלָיו, רַק שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בְּפַרְהֶסְיָא, וְנִתְפַּרְסֵם לָרַבִּים שֶׁדָּבָר זֶה נַעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת לְצֹרֶךְ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, כְּגוֹן יִשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁשָּׂכַר לוֹ נָכְרִי בְּקַבְּלָנוּת לַעֲשׂוֹת לוֹ כֵּלִים חֲדָשִׁים וְעָשָׂה אוֹתָם בְּשַׁבָּת בְּפַרְהֶסְיָא, שֶׁאַף שֶׁיָּצָא הַקּוֹל וְנִתְפַּרְסֵם לָרַבִּים שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בִּשְׁבִיל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם עֲדַיִן לֹא נִקְרָא שְׁמוֹ עֲלֵיהֶם לִהְיוֹת נִקְרָאִים כֵּלִים שֶׁל פְּלוֹנִי, כֵּיוָן שֶׁעֲדַיִן לֹא בָאוּ לִרְשׁוּתוֹ מֵעוֹלָם, לְפִיכָךְ אַף שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בְּמָקוֹם גָּלוּי וּמְפֻרְסָם — אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדוֹ מֵעִקַּר הַדִּין. וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, טוֹב לְהַחֲמִיר וְלִמְחוֹת בְּיָדוֹ לָחוּשׁ לְרִנּוּן שָׁעָה, שֶׁלֹּא יָרֹנּוּ אַחֲרָיו לוֹמַר שֶׁנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת בִּשְׁלִיחוּתוֹ:

English translation

In which case does the previous issur apply? To a ship and similar things whose name belongs to the Yid, about which people say "this thing is so-and-so's." But a thing whose name is not attached to the Yid, only that it is done in public and it is well-known that it is done for the Yid on Shabbos — for example a Yid who hired a non-Jew via kablanus to make him new vessels which he made on Shabbos in public: even though the kol spread that it is for the Yid, the Yid's name is not yet attached to them (they never yet entered his domain) — therefore, by the strict din, he is not obligated to protest. Nonetheless, it is good to be machmir and protest, to guard against passing slander (rinun).

What is this Seif about?

The Rav refines the criterion of Seif Daled: the trigger is not mere publicity, but the attachment of the Yid's name to the object.

Two distinct situations:
• The object already belongs to the Yid (name attached) → forbidden (pure din).
• The object is new, not yet in the Yid's possession, simply made for him in public → permitted by din, though it is praiseworthy to protest (rinun).

The halachic question

If notoriety re-creates marit ayin (Seif Daled), is it enough that "the kol spreads" that the work is for the Yid? Or must the object be already identified as his?

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's line:
  1. An object already the Yid's (name attached) in public → forbidden.
  2. A new object, not yet possessed, made for the Yid → permitted by din.

Modern practical cases

English translation

Even doing detached work, if it is for the sake of the attached — for example chiseling stones and dressing beams to set them into a building — one must protest if it is within the techum, even if he works far from the site, even if he works in his own house where it is not recognizable that it is for this building of the Yid: since their ultimate purpose is to be set into the building, their status is that of the building itself (הרי דינם כהבנין עצמו).

What is this Seif about?

The Rav treats a borderline case: detached work (chiseling stone) that is destined for the attached (the building). Does it follow the lenient regime of the detached or the strict regime of the attached?

The answer: the final purpose prevails. Chiseling stones to set into a wall is already "building" → forbidden, even when done far away and discreetly.

The halachic question

The detached is permitted because it is discreet (Seif Beis). But if this detached is merely a step toward a forbidden attached, does its apparent discretion suffice to permit it?

The opinions

Mechaber (244:3)Rules with the majority: forbidden, because "סופן לשקעם בבנין."
Mishnah BerurahConfirms: chiseling stones destined for the wall is treated as building.

The pesak of the Rav

The rule of destination:
  1. Detached for its own sake → permitted (Seif Beis).
  2. Detached destined for the attached (stones, beams) → status of the attached → forbidden.
  3. Applies even far from the site and in the non-Jew's workshop.

Modern practical cases

  • Prefabricating beams for my house, in a factory: 🔴 destined for the building → forbidden on Shabbos.
  • Cutting marble for my floor/countertop to be set in: 🔴 destined for the mechubar → forbidden.
  • Fabricating a steel frame for my building: 🔴 status of construction → forbidden.
  • Producing generic tiles not destined for a named site: 🟢 ordinary detached → permitted.
  • Cloth cut for a garment (unrelated to building): 🟢 pure detached → permitted.

Points to remember

  • Detached destined for the attached = status of the attached → forbidden
  • Applies even far from the site and in the non-Jew's workshop

12. Seif Zayin — Bediavad: living in a house built on Shabbos

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְאִם לֹא מִחָה בְּיָדוֹ וְעָשָׂה בְּשַׁבָּת בְּקַבְּלָנוּת — יֵשׁ לְהַתִּיר לְשַׁקְּעָם בַּבִּנְיָן, וְכֵן הַבַּיִת שֶׁנִּבְנָה בְּשַׁבָּת בְּקַבְּלָנוּת — מֻתָּר לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לִכָּנֵס לָדוּר בּוֹ, לְפִי שֶׁיֵּשׁ מִי שֶׁמַּתִּיר קַבְּלָנוּת נָכְרִי וַאֲפִלּוּ בִּמְחֻבָּר, וּכְדַאי הוּא לִסְמוֹךְ עָלָיו בְּדִיעֲבַד.

אֲבָל נָכְרִי שְׂכִיר יוֹם שֶׁבָּנָה בְּשַׁבָּת — נָכוֹן לְהַחֲמִיר שֶׁלֹּא יִכָּנֵס בּוֹ שׁוּם יִשְׂרָאֵל לְעוֹלָם. וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם כְּשֶׁשָּׂכַר אֶת הַנָּכְרִי לְיָמִים הִתְנָה עִמּוֹ שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשֶׂה בְּיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת וְהַנָּכְרִי עָשָׂה מֵעַצְמוֹ, (וְ)אִם עָשָׂה לְטוֹבַת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל — נָכוֹן שֶׁלֹּא יִכָּנֵס בּוֹ שׁוּם יִשְׂרָאֵל לְעוֹלָם. אֲבָל אִם נִתְכַּוֵּן לְטוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ — מֻתָּר אֲפִלּוּ לְבַעַל הַבַּיִת לָדוּר בּוֹ, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהִתְנָה עִמּוֹ שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת:

English translation

If he did not protest and the non-Jew worked on Shabbos via kablanus — there is room to permit setting the stones into the building, and likewise a house built on Shabbos via kablanus: the Yid is permitted to enter and live in it, since there is an opinion that permits a non-Jew's kablanus even in the attached, and it is worth relying upon it bediavad.

But a sechir yom non-Jew who built on Shabbos — it is proper to be machmir: that no Yid ever enter it. And even if, when hiring him, the Yid stipulated that he not work on Shabbos and the non-Jew did so on his own: if he did it for the Yid's benefit, no Yid should ever enter it; but if he intended his own benefit — it is permitted even for the owner to live in it, since he had stipulated abstention on Shabbos.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav moves from lechatchilah (preventive prohibition) to bediavad (what to do once the fact is accomplished?). A decisive distinction between kablanus and a sechir yom.

• Built via kablanus on Shabbos → one may rely on the lenient opinion → habitable bediavad.
• Built via sechir yom → chumra: forbidden to ever enter — except if there was a stipulation of abstention + the non-Jew acted for himself.

The halachic question

The mechubar via kablanus is forbidden lechatchilah (marit ayin). But once the house is built on Shabbos, may one benefit from it? Does the bediavad status depend on the mode of payment?

The opinions

SourcePosition
The lenient opinion (יש מתירין)Permits kablanus even in the attached — the basis for the bediavad.
Rema (244:3)Bediavad, one relies on the isolated view in cases of loss/pressing need; distinguishes kablanus and by-the-day.
Magen Avraham / TazBediavad we follow the lenient opinion (Rabbeinu Tam); the sechir yom remains strict.
SHHaRav (244:7)Permits bediavad for kablanus; for the sechir yom: forbidden except for his own benefit + stipulation.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's bediavad:
  1. Built via kablanus on Shabbos → habitable (relying on the lenient opinion).
  2. Built via sechir yom on Shabbos → forbidden to ever enter.
  3. Exception: a sechir yom who was given a stipulation of abstention + acted for himself → permitted to the owner.

Modern practical cases

  • A building finished on a Shabbos by a kablanus firm: 🟢 habitable bediavad.
  • A house built by workers paid by the day on Shabbos: 🔴 do not enter it (chumra).
  • Renovation done on Shabbos by an hourly worker, against instructions: 🟡 examine whether he acted for himself + the stipulation.
  • Stones chiseled on Shabbos then delivered: 🟢 kablanus → one may set them in bediavad.
  • A day laborer who "does a favor" on Shabbos for the Yid: 🔴 for the Yid's benefit → forbidden to benefit from it.

Points to remember

  • Kablanus built on Shabbos → habitable bediavad
  • Sechir yom built on Shabbos → forbidden to ever enter
  • Sechir yom exception: stipulation + his own benefit → permitted
Comprehension question: Why is a house built via kablanus habitable bediavad, whereas one built by a sechir yom is forbidden forever?

Full text of the SHHaRav

וּמִטַּעַם זֶה יֵשׁ לְהַתִּיר לְהַנִּיחַ לְנָכְרִים לִבְנוֹת בֵּית הַכְּנֶסֶת בְּשַׁבָּת, אִם הוּא מָקוֹם שֶׁמְּפֻרְסָם בּוֹ דֶּרֶךְ בִּנְיַן הַבָּתִּים עַל יְדֵי אֻמָּנִים קַבְּלָנִים, וְלֹא עַל יְדֵי שְׂכִירֵי יוֹם כְּמוֹ שֶׁהוּא בְּרֹב הַמְּקוֹמוֹת, שֶׁהָאַדְרִיכָל לְבַדּוֹ הוּא קַבְּלָן וְהָאֻמָּנִים עוֹשֵׂי מְלָאכָה שֶׁתַּחְתָּיו הֵם שְׂכִירֵי יוֹם, שֶׁשָּׁם יֵשׁ חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית הָעַיִן.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אֵין לְהַתִּיר אֶלָּא בִּשְׁעַת הַדְּחַק, כְּגוֹן שֶׁיֵּשׁ חֲשַׁשׁ שֶׁמָּא יִתְבַּטֵּל בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ הַזְּמַן בִּנְיַן בֵּית הַכְּנֶסֶת לְגַמְרֵי מֵחֲמַת רִשְׁעַת הַנָּכְרִים. אֲבָל שֶׁלֹּא בִּשְׁעַת הַדְּחַק — אֵין לְהַתִּיר, מִפְּנֵי חִלּוּל הַשֵּׁם בְּעֵינֵי הַנָּכְרִים, שֶׁהֵם אֵינָם מַנִּיחִים לְשׁוּם אָדָם לַעֲשׂוֹת מְלָאכָה בְּפַרְהֶסְיָא בְּיוֹם אֵידָם, וְאָנוּ מַנִּיחִים לַעֲשׂוֹת מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת בִּשְׁבִילֵנוּ בְּפַרְהֶסְיָא.

אֲבָל כִּבּוּד הָרְחוֹב אֵינוֹ נִקְרָא עַל שֵׁם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל כָּל כָּךְ, וְאֵין בּוֹ חִלּוּל הַשֵּׁם, לָכֵן נוֹהֲגִין לְהַתִּירוֹ אַף שֶׁלֹּא בִּשְׁעַת הַדְּחַק.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁאֵין שָׁם מִנְהָג — אֵין לְהָקֵל אַף בְּכִבּוּד הָרְחוֹב:

English translation

In some places the custom is to permit the kehillah to hire non-Jews via kablanus to clear the refuse from the street, the non-Jew working for himself even on Shabbos, with no concern for marit ayin — for it is already well-known that the custom is to hire via kablanus, not by the day.

For this reason, there is room to permit letting non-Jews build a shul on Shabbos, in a place where the custom is to build houses through contractor craftsmen (not by the day). But this is permitted only in a sha'as hadechak (pressing circumstance) — for example, if the building is liable to be halted entirely over time through the wickedness of the non-Jews. Outside a sha'as hadechak it is not permitted, because of chillul Hashem in the eyes of the non-Jews (who themselves let no one work in public on their festivals). Clearing the street, however, is not attached to the Yid's name and involves no chillul Hashem; it is permitted even outside a sha'as hadechak. But where there is no custom, one is not lenient even for clearing the street.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav introduces the role of minhag, which can neutralize marit ayin, and the additional factor of chillul Hashem.

• Street cleaning via kablanus: permitted if there is an established minhag (not attached to the Yid, no chillul Hashem).
• A shul: permitted only in a sha'as hadechak (pressing need), because of chillul Hashem when in public.

The halachic question

Can minhag dissolve marit ayin (if "everyone knows" it is kablanus)? And does a visible religious edifice introduce a new risk: chillul Hashem?

The opinions

SourcePosition
Local minhagWhen kablanus is well-known, there is no more marit ayin (cf. siman רמ״ג, bathhouse/oven/mill).
Rema (244)Permits the shul in a sha'as hadechak; stresses the concern for chillul Hashem in public.
Magen AvrahamDistinguishes street cleaning (anonymous) from the shul (in the community's name).
SHHaRav (244:8)Articulates three drivers: minhag, sha'as hadechak, chillul Hashem; and the absence of minhag as a limit.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's three drivers:
  1. The minhag of kablanus dissolves marit ayin.
  2. A shul: permitted only in a sha'as hadechak (otherwise chillul Hashem).
  3. Without a local minhag → no leniency, even for street cleaning.

Modern practical cases

  • A non-Jewish municipal refuse service on Shabbos: 🟢 well-known kablanus → permitted.
  • Road maintenance/snow removal by a contractor firm: 🟢 if there is an established minhag → permitted.
  • Building a shul by a non-Jewish firm, in public: 🟡 permitted only in a sha'as hadechak.
  • Public works in the Jewish community's name, with no urgency: 🔴 outside a sha'as hadechak → forbidden (chillul Hashem).
  • Cleaning where no kablanus custom exists: 🔴 no minhag → forbidden.

Points to remember

  • The minhag of kablanus can neutralize marit ayin
  • A shul in public → only in a sha'as hadechak (chillul Hashem)
  • Without a local minhag, no leniency even for street cleaning
Comprehension question: Why is street cleaning permitted outside a sha'as hadechak, but building a shul only in a sha'as hadechak?

מִי שֶׁשָּׂכַר לוֹ נָכְרִי לְשָׁנָה אוֹ לִשְׁתַּיִם שֶׁיִּכְתּוֹב לוֹ אוֹ שֶׁיֶּאֱרוֹג לוֹ בְּכָל עֵת שֶׁיִּצְטָרֵךְ בְּתוֹךְ זְמַן מֶשֶׁךְ שְׂכִירוּתוֹ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁהוּא דֶרֶךְ הַשָּׂרִים שֶׁיֵּשׁ לָהֶם סוֹפֵר מְיֻחָד שֶׁכּוֹתֵב לוֹ כָּל עֵת שֶׁצָּרִיךְ, וּכְשֶׁאֵין צָרִיךְ הוּא יוֹשֵׁב בָּטֵל — מֻתָּר לְהַנִּיחוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת מַה שֶּׁצִּוָּה לוֹ קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת (רַק שֶׁיִּזָּהֵר שֶׁלֹּא יֹאמַר לוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת) אִם אֵינוֹ עוֹשֶׂה בְּבֵית הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, לְפִי שֶׁאֵין לְיִשְׂרָאֵל רֶוַח בְּמַה שֶּׁעוֹשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁאִם לֹא יַעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת יַעֲשֶׂה אַחַר הַשַּׁבָּת. וְאַף אִם הַמְּלָאכָה צְרִיכָה לוֹ בְּמוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת מִיָּד, מִכָּל מָקוֹם אֵין זֶה נִקְרָא רֶוַח אֶלָּא הֲנָאָה בְּעָלְמָא, וְרֶוַח הוּא כְּגוֹן שֶׁהַמְּלָאכָה הָיְתָה מִתְבַּטֶּלֶת לְגַמְרֵי אִם לֹא הָיְתָה נַעֲשֵׂית בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁאָז הוּא מַרְוִיחַ הַמְּלָאכָה עַל יְדֵי שֶׁנַּעֲשֵׂית בְּשַׁבָּת, וּכְמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רנ"ב, וְכֵן אִם יֵשׁ לוֹ רֶוַח אַחֵר שֶׁרְכוּשׁוֹ מִתְרַוֵּחַ וּמִתְרַבֶּה עַל יְדֵי שֶׁנַּעֲשֵׂית הַמְּלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ג:

English translation

One who hired a non-Jew for a year or two to write or weave for him whenever needed during the hire — as is the way of noblemen who keep a dedicated scribe writing on demand and idle when not needed — it is permitted to let him do on Shabbos what was ordered before Shabbos (provided one is careful not to tell him to do it on Shabbos), if he does not work in the Yid's house: because the Yid has no revach (gain) in its being done on Shabbos — if he does not do it on Shabbos he will do it afterward. And even if the work is needed immediately at the close of Shabbos, this is only a hana'ah (mere benefit), not a revach (gain). A "revach" would be: that the work would be lost entirely if not done on Shabbos, or that the Yid's wealth increases through the Shabbos work (cf. siman רמ״ג).

What is this Seif about?

The Rav introduces the decisive distinction between הנאה (mere benefit) and רֶוַח (actual gain). The idle yearly scribe is permitted because the Yid gains nothing from its being done on Shabbos.

הנאה vs רֶוַח:
  • הנאה (benefit) — mere convenience (having it ready Sunday morning). Does not forbid.
  • רֶוַח (gain) — the work would otherwise be lost, or his wealth increases. Forbids.

The halachic question

The non-Jew is a contractor/retained worker acting for his pay. But does the Yid derive a gain from the work being done specifically on Shabbos? If so, we approach a forbidden shelichus; if it is only a benefit, it is permitted.

The opinions

SourcePosition
RambamPermits the idle retained worker; it is only a benefit, not a gain.
Rosh / TurThe criterion of actual gain (משתרשא ליה) determines the issur.
Mechaber (244:5)Permits what was ordered before Shabbos, outside the Yid's house, without amira.
Mishnah BerurahDistinguishes benefit (permitted) from a loss averted / enrichment (forbidden).
SHHaRav (244:9)Defines רֶוַח precisely: work lost otherwise, or an increase of wealth; mere "ready by Sunday" is only a benefit.

The pesak of the Rav

The rule of gain:
  1. An idle retained worker, order placed before Shabbos, outside the Yid's house, without amira → permitted.
  2. Condition: no רֶוַח (gain) — only a benefit.
  3. If there is gain (work saved, wealth increased) → forbidden.

Modern practical cases

  • An IT provider on a yearly retainer performs a task on Shabbos: 🟢 if mere convenience, off my premises, without amira → permitted.
  • A retained accountant prepares a document useful by Sunday: 🟢 benefit, not gain → permitted.
  • A task that would be lost if not done on Shabbos: 🔴 that is a revach → forbidden.
  • A rented server that enriches the Yid by running on Shabbos: 🔴 increase of wealth → forbidden.
  • Explicitly saying "do it on Shabbos": 🔴 amira → forbidden in all cases.

Points to remember

  • Distinguish הנאה (benefit, permitted) from רֶוַח (gain, forbidden)
  • "Ready by Sunday" is only a benefit
  • Conditions: order before Shabbos, outside the Yid's house, without amira
Comprehension question: What is the difference between having a document "ready Sunday morning" (permitted) and work "that would be lost otherwise" (forbidden)?

15. Seif Yod — קפידא: the criterion of the Yid's insistence

Full text of the SHHaRav

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁאֵין הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מַקְפִּיד עִמּוֹ כְּלָל אִם הוּא יוֹשֵׁב וּבָטֵל לְאַחַר שֶׁצִּוָּה לוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת הַמְּלָאכָה, אֶלָּא בְּיָדוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹתָהּ בְּכָל עֵת שֶׁיִּרְצֶה, לְפִיכָךְ כְּשֶׁהוּא עוֹשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת — לְדַעַת עַצְמוֹ הוּא עוֹשֶׂה וְלֹא מֵחֲמַת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל. אֲבָל אִם הוּא מַקְפִּיד עִמּוֹ עַל בִּטּוּלוֹ — הֲרֵי הוּא עוֹשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת מֵחֲמַת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, לְפִיכָךְ צָרִיךְ הוּא לוֹמַר לוֹ שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת. וְאִם אַף עַל פִּי כֵן הוּא עוֹשֶׂה — אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדוֹ, שֶׁלְּדַעַת עַצְמוֹ הוּא עוֹשֶׂה:

English translation

In which case does this apply? When the Yid is not at all particular (eino makpid) that the non-Jew sit idle after being given the work — the non-Jew being free to do it whenever he wishes. Then, when he does it on Shabbos, he does so on his own initiative (ledaas atzmo), not because of the Yid. But if the Yid is particular (makpid) about his idleness — then the non-Jew does it on Shabbos because of the Yid; one must therefore tell him not to do it on Shabbos. And if he does it anyway — one need not protest, since he is acting on his own initiative.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav adds a psychological-objective criterion: the קפידא (insistence) of the Yid. It determines whether the non-Jew works "for himself" or "because of the Yid."

• A Yid indifferent to when it is done → the non-Jew acts ledaas atzmo → permitted.
• A Yid insistent (pressing, controlling) → the non-Jew acts because of the Yid → one must say "not on Shabbos" (but need not protest afterward).

The halachic question

Even a retained worker can slip into shelichus if the Yid insists on it. The line is therefore not only contractual (kablanus/by-the-day) but also in the Yid's attitude. When does insistence turn the work into work "for the Yid"?

The opinions

SourcePosition
RoshThe Yid's insistence (kpeidah) makes him an active beneficiary → assimilated to shelichus.
Mechaber (244:5)Permits when the Yid is not particular; requires saying "not on Shabbos" if he is.
Mishnah BerurahClarifies that it suffices to say "not on Shabbos"; no obligation to protest afterward.
SHHaRav (244:10)Sets the criterion לדעת עצמו vs מחמת הישראל according to the kpeidah; nuances the duty of protest.

The pesak of the Rav

The criterion of insistence:
  1. Yid not particular → non-Jew ledaas atzmo → permitted.
  2. Yid particular → non-Jew mei-chamas haYid → say "not on Shabbos."
  3. If he continues anyway → no obligation to protest (he acts for himself).

Modern practical cases

  • A provider free to set his own schedule: 🟢 acts for himself → permitted for him to work on Shabbos.
  • I press a supplier "I absolutely must have it": 🔴 my kpeidah → tell him not to do it on Shabbos.
  • An on-call arrangement where I demand immediate response: 🔴 insistence → work because of me.
  • A loose SLA with no strict deadline: 🟢 no kpeidah → permitted.
  • I told him "not on Shabbos" but he does it anyway: 🟡 no need to protest (he acts for himself).

Points to remember

  • The קפידא (the Yid's insistence) can turn the work into shelichus
  • Not particular → לדעת עצמו → permitted; particular → say "not on Shabbos"
  • If he persists despite the instruction → no obligation to protest
Comprehension question: How can the Yid's insistence (kpeidah) turn permitted work into forbidden work, without changing anything in the contract?

16. Seif Yod-Alef — Hired for all tasks: משתרשא ליה

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְכָל זֶה כְּשֶׁאֵין הַנָּכְרִי מֻשְׂכָּר לוֹ אֶלָּא לִמְלָאכוֹת מְיֻחָדוֹת, כְּגוֹן לִכְתּוֹב אוֹ לֶאֱרוֹג בְּכָל עֵת שֶׁיִּצְטָרֵךְ, וְאַחַר שֶׁגָּמַר הַמְּלָאכָה הוּא יוֹשֵׁב וּבָטֵל, אֲבָל אִם שְׂכָרוֹ לְכָל הַמְּלָאכוֹת שֶׁיִּצְטָרֵךְ בְּתוֹךְ מֶשֶׁךְ זְמַן שְׂכִירָתוֹ — אָסוּר לְהַנִּיחוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת, לְפִי שֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מַרְוִיחַ מְלָאכָה זוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁהֲרֵי כְּשֶׁיִּהְיֶה פָּנוּי אַחַר הַשַּׁבָּת יִצְטָרֵךְ לַעֲשׂוֹת מְלָאכוֹת אֲחֵרוֹת. וְאֵין לְהַתִּיר מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה לְטוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ כְּדֵי לְקַבֵּל כָּל שְׂכָרוֹ, שֶׁהֲרֵי אַף אִם לֹא יַעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת לֹא יְנַכֶּה לוֹ כְּלוּם, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ג.

וְיֵשׁ מַתִּירִים גַּם בָּזֶה, לְפִי שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי אֵינוֹ מַעֲלֶה עַל דַּעְתּוֹ שֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל אֵינוֹ מַכְרִיחוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת וְשֶׁלֹּא יְנַכֶּה לוֹ כְּלוּם מִשְּׂכָרוֹ אַף אִם לֹא יַעֲשֶׂה כְּלוּם בְּשַׁבָּת, אֶלָּא הוּא עוֹשֶׂה מֵאֵלָיו לְפִי תֻמּוֹ לְהַשְׁלִים קַבְּלָנוּתוֹ שֶׁקִּבֵּל עָלָיו בִּתְחִלַּת שְׂכִירוּתוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת כָּל הַמְּלָאכוֹת שֶׁיִּצְטָרְכוּ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּכָל מֶשֶׁךְ זְמַן שְׂכִירוּתוֹ, כְּדֵי שֶׁיְּשַׁלֵּם לוֹ כָּל שְׂכָרוֹ מִשָּׁלֵם וְלֹא יִהְיֶה לוֹ עָלָיו שׁוּם תַּרְעֹמֶת.

וְאֵין לִסְמוֹךְ עַל סְבָרָא זוֹ (מִטַּעַם שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר שָׁם), כִּי אִם לְעִנְיַן דְּלֵקָה מִשּׁוּם הֶפְסֵד, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן של"ד, וּלְעִנְיַן שְׁלִיחוּת אִגֶּרֶת, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ז, עַיֵּן שָׁם הַטַּעַם:

English translation

All this applies only when the non-Jew is hired solely for specific tasks (melachos meyuchados) — writing, weaving — after which he sits idle. But if he is hired for all the tasks that arise during the period — it is forbidden to let him work on Shabbos, because the Yid gains this Shabbos work (משתרשא ליה, mishtarsha lei): free after Shabbos, the non-Jew would otherwise have had to do other tasks. We do not permit on the grounds that he acts to collect his full pay, since nothing would be deducted if he rested on Shabbos (cf. siman רמ״ג).

Some permit this case too, since the non-Jew does not imagine he will not be compelled, and works on his own to fulfill his undertaking. But we do not rely on this opinion, except for a fire (loss, siman של״ד) and sending a letter (siman רמ״ז).

What is this Seif about?

The Rav distinguishes the worker hired for specific tasks (permitted, Seif Tes) from the worker hired for all tasks (forbidden), via the concept of משתרשא ליה — "it profits him."

Hired "to do everything": whatever he does on Shabbos, he will no longer have to do afterward → the Yid saves work-time → that is a gain → forbidden. The lenient opinion is accepted only in two extreme cases (fire, letter).

The halachic question

When a worker does on Shabbos a task he would have had to do anyway (since hired "for everything"), does the Yid gain? Yes: he saves the post-Shabbos slot. Does this gain (mishtarsha lei) suffice to forbid despite the global contract?

The opinions

SourcePosition
Sefer haTerumah / Hagahos MordechaiThe lenient opinion (יש מתירין): the non-Jew works to fulfill his undertaking.
RoshForbidden: the Yid gains (mishtarsha lei) the Shabbos work.
Mechaber (244:6)Forbids "for all tasks"; permits only specific tasks.
Mishnah BerurahAccepts the lenient opinion only for a fire and sending a letter.
SHHaRav (244:11)Rules to forbid; permits the lenient opinion only for a fire (של״ד) and a letter (רמ״ז).

The pesak of the Rav

The rule of work-gain:
  1. Hired for specific tasks + idle afterward → permitted.
  2. Hired for all tasks → forbidden (mishtarsha lei).
  3. The lenient opinion is reserved for a fire and a letter.

Modern practical cases

  • A non-Jewish full-time "catch-all" assistant on a permanent contract: 🔴 mishtarsha lei → forbidden on Shabbos.
  • A provider on an unlimited global retainer: 🔴 the Shabbos work saves me time → forbidden.
  • A specialist hired for a single task, then idle: 🟢 permitted (Seif Tes).
  • Sending a letter/package via a non-Jew in a special case: 🟡 reliance possible (siman רמ״ז).
  • Intervention in a fire/major loss: 🟡 the lenient opinion is accepted (siman של״ד).

Points to remember

  • Specific tasks + idle → permitted; all tasks → forbidden
  • The key concept: משתרשא ליה (the Shabbos work profits the Yid)
  • The lenient opinion is accepted only for a fire and a letter
Comprehension question: Why is a worker hired "for all tasks" forbidden on Shabbos, whereas a worker hired for a specific task is permitted?

17. Seif Yod-Beis — The toll-farmer (מכס): שבות דשבות

Full text of the SHHaRav

יִשְׂרָאֵל הַקּוֹנֶה מֶכֶס — אָסוּר לוֹ לְקַבֵּל מֶכֶס בְּשַׁבָּת, אֲפִלּוּ הֱבִיאוֹ דְּבַר מַאֲכָל שֶׁאֵינוֹ מֻקְצֶה וְרָאוּי לַאֲכִילָה, כְּגוֹן שֶׁהוּבָא מִתּוֹךְ הַתְּחוּם, וְהוּא דָּבָר שֶׁאֵין בּוֹ חֲשָׁשׁ שֶׁמָּא נִלְקַט הַיּוֹם מֵהַמְחֻבָּר אוֹ שֶׁמָּא נִצַּד הַיּוֹם — אַף עַל פִּי כֵן אָסוּר לוֹ לְקַבֵּל בְּעַצְמוֹ, מִשּׁוּם שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר "מִמְּצֹא חֶפְצְךָ", חֲפָצֶיךָ אֲסוּרִים, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן ש"ו.

אֶלָּא שׂוֹכֵר לוֹ נָכְרִי בְּקַבְּלָנוּת, דְּהַיְנוּ שֶׁאוֹמֵר לוֹ לִכְשֶׁתִּגְבֶּה מֵאָה דִינָרִים אֶתֵּן לְךָ כָּךְ וְכָךְ. אֲבָל לֹא יִשְׂכּוֹר לוֹ נָכְרִי לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁכְּשֶׁהַנָּכְרִי הוּא שְׂכִיר יוֹם — הֲרֵי הוּא כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, מַה שֶּׁאֵין כֵּן בְּקַבְּלָנוּת שֶׁאֵינוֹ כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶלָּא לְעַצְמוֹ הוּא טוֹרֵחַ כְּדֵי לְהַרְבּוֹת שְׂכָרוֹ.

וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל קוֹבֵעַ לוֹ מְלַאכְתּוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת — אֵין בָּזֶה מִשּׁוּם אֲמִירָה לְנָכְרִי שְׁבוּת, לְפִי שֶׁקַּבָּלַת הַמֶּכֶס אֵין בָּהּ אִסּוּר אֲפִלּוּ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל אֶלָּא מִשּׁוּם שְׁבוּת, וַהֲרֵי זֶה שְׁבוּת דִּשְׁבוּת, שֶׁיֵּשׁ מַתִּירִין אוֹתוֹ בִּמְקוֹם הֶפְסֵד, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן ש"ז. וְאַף שֶׁיֵּשׁ אוֹסְרִין אַף בִּמְקוֹם הֶפְסֵד אִם לֹא לְצֹרֶךְ מִצְוָה כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר שָׁם, מִכָּל מָקוֹם כַּאן חָשׁוּב דְּבַר מִצְוָה, שֶׁאִם לֹא יִטּוֹל הַמֶּכֶס מֵהַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת לֹא יוּכַל לִגְבּוֹתוֹ אַחַר הַשַּׁבָּת, וְיִשְׁתַּקֵּעַ הַמָּמוֹן בְּיַד הַנָּכְרִי, וַהֲרֵי הוּא עוֹשֶׂה מִצְוָה בְּמַה שֶּׁמַּצִּיל הַמָּמוֹן מִיָּדָם, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּיוֹרֶה דֵעָה סִמָּן קמ"ט וְשע"ב:

English translation

A Yid who is a toll-farmer (mochesh): he may not collect the toll himself on Shabbos, even non-muktzeh food, because it is said "מִמְּצֹא חֶפְצְךָ" — your affairs are forbidden (siman ש״ו). Rather he hires a non-Jew via kablanus — telling him "when you collect a hundred dinars I will give you such-and-such." He should not hire him as a sechir yom for the day of Shabbos, for then he would be his shaliach; via kablanus he is not his shaliach but toils for himself.

And although the Yid sets his Shabbos work, there is no forbidden amira here, because collecting the toll is, for the Yid himself, only a shevus (a light rabbinic issur) — this is therefore a שבות דשבות (a double rabbinic issur), permitted in a case of loss and for a mitzvah: saving the money from the hands of the non-Jews.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav applies kablanus to a commercial case: the toll-farmer. He introduces the lever of שבות דשבות (a double rabbinic issur).

שבות דשבות: when the act is doubly "light" — (1) amira to a non-Jew (rabbinic) regarding (2) a melacha that is itself rabbinic (receiving the toll) — it is permitted in a case of loss or a mitzvah (here: recovering the money from the non-Jews).

The halachic question

The Yid may not collect himself on Shabbos (muktzeh / מִמְּצֹא חֶפְצְךָ). Via kablanus, the non-Jew acts for himself → no shelichus. But the Yid sets his Shabbos work — is that not amira? The answer: shevus d'shevus, permitted here.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Gemara / RishonimKablanus neutralizes shelichus even when the work is set for Shabbos.
Mechaber (244:6)Permits kablanus for the toll; forbids paying by the day of Shabbos.
Mishnah BerurahGrounds the heter on shevus d'shevus in a case of loss/mitzvah.
SHHaRav (244:12)Spells out the mechanism: collection = a shevus for the Yid → amira on it = שבות דשבות → permitted (loss + the mitzvah of saving the money).

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's heter:
  1. Kablanus (not pay by the day) → no shelichus.
  2. Collection is only a shevus for the Yid → amira on it = שבות דשבות.
  3. Permitted in a case of loss and for the mitzvah of saving the money.

Modern practical cases

  • A non-Jewish collection agency on kablanus collects on Shabbos: 🟢 shevus d'shevus + loss → permitted.
  • Automated/delegated collection to avert a loss: 🟢 if kablanus and a real loss.
  • Paying an agent by the day of Shabbos to collect: 🔴 a Shabbos sechir yom = shaliach → forbidden.
  • Leasing a public concession run on Shabbos by a non-Jewish kablan: 🟢 the framework of the מכס.
  • Collection with no loss and no mitzvah at all: 🔴 shevus d'shevus does not apply.

Points to remember

  • Kablanus neutralizes shelichus even if the Yid sets the Shabbos work
  • Collection is only a shevus → amira on it = שבות דשבות
  • Permitted for loss + the mitzvah of saving the money

18. Seif Yod-Gimmel — A toll requiring a melacha gemurah

Full text of the SHHaRav

אֲפִלּוּ אִם הוּא מֶכֶס שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לַעֲשׂוֹת בּוֹ מְלָאכָה גְמוּרָה, כְּגוֹן שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לִכְתּוֹב — אַף עַל פִּי כֵן מֻתָּר לוֹמַר לְנָכְרִי שֶׁיִּתְעַסֵּק בּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת בְּקַבְּלָנוּת, לְפִי שֶׁאָדָם בָּהוּל עַל מָמוֹנוֹ, וְאִם לֹא נַתִּיר לוֹ אֲמִירָה לְנָכְרִי יָבֹא לְהִתְעַסֵּק בְּעַצְמוֹ, וְשֶׁמָּא יִשְׁכַּח וְיִכְתּוֹב, לְפִיכָךְ מַתִּירִים לוֹ אִסּוּר אֲמִירָה לְנָכְרִי שֶׁהוּא מִדִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יָבֹא לִידֵי כְתִיבָה הָאֲסוּרָה מִן הַתּוֹרָה:

English translation

Even if it is a toll that requires a full melacha (melacha gemurah) — such as writing — it is nonetheless permitted to tell the non-Jew to handle it on Shabbos via kablanus. For a person is frantic over his money (אדם בהול על ממונו): if we do not permit him the amira, he will come to handle it himself and may forget and write (a biblical issur). We therefore permit him the amira (rabbinic) so that he should not come to forbidden writing of the Torah.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav extends the toll heter even when a biblical melacha (writing) is required. The lever changes: no longer shevus d'shevus but the preventive principle אדם בהול על ממונו.

We prefer to permit the amira (a rabbinic issur) rather than risk the Yid, frantic over his money, writing himself (a biblical issur). The logic of the lesser evil.

The halachic question

If collection requires writing (a melacha gemurah), shevus d'shevus no longer holds (the act is not light). How then permit the amira? Through the preventive concern: averting a worse, biblical transgression.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Gemara ("אדם בהול על ממונו")The principle: financial panic can lead to a grave transgression; we prevent it with a light leniency.
Mechaber (244:6)Permits amira via kablanus even for a full melacha, out of this concern.
Mishnah BerurahConfirms: better the rabbinic amira than the Yid's biblical writing.
SHHaRav (244:13)Explicitly formulates the logic of the lesser evil: permit the divrei sofrim to avert the d'oraisa.

The pesak of the Rav

The principle of the lesser evil:
  1. A toll requiring writing = a biblical melacha.
  2. Risk: the frantic (bahul) Yid will do it himself.
  3. We therefore permit the amira (rabbinic) via kablanus to avert the worse.

Modern practical cases

  • Customs documents to be drawn up, handled by a non-Jewish kablan: 🟢 permitted (to avert my doing it).
  • Urgent data entry of a debt by a non-Jewish third party: 🟢 if kablanus + the risk that I would handle it.
  • Blocking tax formalities entrusted to a non-Jew: 🟢 the framework of בהול על ממונו.
  • A writing task with no financial panic at all: 🔴 the principle does not apply.
  • The Yid handles it himself on Shabbos: 🔴 risk of biblical writing — precisely what we seek to avert.

Points to remember

  • Even a biblical melacha (writing) may be entrusted to the non-Jew via kablanus
  • Lever: אדם בהול על ממונו (financial panic)
  • The logic of the lesser evil: permit the rabbinic to avert the biblical

19. Seif Yod-Daled — Leasing out the toll; marit ayin and הפסד

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְכֵן מֻתָּר לוֹ לְהַשְׂכִּיר לְנָכְרִי אֶת הַמֶּכֶס שֶׁיִּטּוֹל לְעַצְמוֹ מַה שֶּׁיִּגְבֶּה בְּשַׁבָּת הֵן רַב הֵן מְעַט וְהוּא יִתֵּן לְיִשְׂרָאֵל דָּבָר קָצוּב, שֶׁנִּמְצָא שֶׁהוּא טוֹרֵחַ לְעַצְמוֹ, וְאֵינוֹ כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל.

וּכְשֶׁמַּשְׂכִּיר הַמֶּכֶס לְנָכְרִי, וְכֵן כְּשֶׁשּׂוֹכֵר לוֹ הַנָּכְרִי בְּקַבְּלָנוּת — אֵין חוֹשְׁשִׁין לְמַרְאִית הָעַיִן שֶׁהָרוֹאֶה יֹאמַר שְׂכִיר יוֹם הוּא אֶצְלוֹ, לְפִי שֶׁבִּמְקוֹם הֶפְסֵד גָּדוֹל לֹא גָזְרוּ עַל מַרְאִית הָעַיִן. וְאַף אִם לִפְעָמִים הוּא הֶפְסֵד מוּעָט, מִכָּל מָקוֹם כֵּיוָן שֶׁעַל הָרֹב דֶּרֶךְ לִהְיוֹת הֶפְסֵד גָּדוֹל בִּמְנִיעַת קַבָּלַת הַמֶּכֶס בְּשַׁבָּתוֹת — לֹא חִלְּקוּ חֲכָמִים, וְהִתִּירוּ לְגַמְרֵי גְּזֵרָה זוֹ שֶׁל מַרְאִית הָעַיִן בְּמֶכֶס וּבְכָל כַּיּוֹצֵא בָזֶה שֶׁדֶּרֶךְ לִהְיוֹת שָׁם הֶפְסֵד גָּדוֹל עַל הָרֹב:

English translation

Likewise it is permitted to lease the toll to a non-Jew — that he take for himself whatever he collects on Shabbos, much or little, and give the Yid a fixed sum — so that he toils for himself and is not the Yid's shaliach.

And when one leases it out thus, or hires via kablanus, we are not concerned for marit ayin ("people will say he is a sechir yom"), because in a case of great loss Chazal did not decree marit ayin. And even if the loss is sometimes small, since in general there is great loss in failing to collect the toll on Shabbasos, Chazal did not draw distinctions and lifted this gezeirah entirely for the toll and every similar case where there is usually great loss.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav adds a second mode (leasing out) and a major rule: marit ayin yields before great loss (הפסד גדול).

When a field is usually a source of great loss (the toll), Chazal did not decree marit ayin — and this across the board, even the particular cases with small loss (לא חילקו חכמים).

The halachic question

Marit ayin normally forbids (people will think it is a sechir yom). But when refusing to work on Shabbos would cause great loss, does this gezeirah hold? And must one evaluate each case, or rule by category?

The opinions

SourcePosition
Rishonim"במקום הפסד לא גזרו על מראית העין" — no marit ayin where there is loss.
Mechaber (244:6)Permits leasing out and kablanus of the toll without concern for marit ayin.
Mishnah BerurahStresses the rule of usual great loss.
SHHaRav (244:14)Rules by category: since the toll usually involves great loss, the gezeirah is lifted for all cases (לא חילקו).

The pesak of the Rav

The rule of great loss:
  1. Leasing out (fixed sum to the Yid) → non-Jew for himself → no shelichus.
  2. Great loss (הפסד גדול) → no gezeirah of marit ayin.
  3. Status by category: even the small-loss cases are covered.

Modern practical cases

  • A concession leased to a non-Jewish operator (fixed sum): 🟢 he acts for himself → permitted.
  • An activity with heavy loss if closed on Shabbos (toll, market): 🟢 marit ayin lifted.
  • Renting out a business with fixed rent, run on Shabbos by a non-Jew: 🟢 the framework of leasing out.
  • An activity with no real risk of loss: 🔴 marit ayin remains.
  • An isolated small-loss case within a great-loss category: 🟢 covered (לא חילקו).

Points to remember

  • Leasing out (fixed rent) makes the non-Jew an actor for himself
  • Great loss (הפסד גדול) lifts the gezeirah of marit ayin
  • The Rav rules by category, not case by case
Comprehension question: Why did Chazal "not distinguish" (לא חילקו), lifting marit ayin even for the small-loss toll cases?

20. Seif Tes-Vav — Leasing the toll for Shabbos alone (without הבלעה)

Full text of the SHHaRav

וּכְשֶׁמַּשְׂכִּיר הַמֶּכֶס לְנָכְרִי — יָכוֹל לְהַשְׂכִּיר לוֹ לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בִּלְבָד שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה עִם שְׁאָר הַיָּמִים. וְאֵינוֹ דוֹמֶה לְמֶרְחָץ שֶׁאָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירוֹ לְשַׁבָּת בִּלְבָד כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יִטּוֹל שָׂכָר בְּשַׁבָּת שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה, לְפִי שֶׁהַמֶּרְחָץ גּוּפוֹ קָנוּי לוֹ אַף בְּיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, וּכְשֶׁהַנָּכְרִי נוֹתֵן לוֹ שָׂכָר בְּעַד מֶרְחָץ שֶׁלּוֹ שֶׁהָיָה מֻשְׂכָּר לוֹ לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת — הֲרֵי מִשְׂתַּכֵּר הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בְּמֶרְחָץ שֶׁלּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, וַהֲרֵי זֶה נִקְרָא שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת, וְאָסוּר לִטְּלוֹ שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה, מַה שֶּׁאֵין כֵּן בְּמֶכֶס וְכַיּוֹצֵא בוֹ שֶׁכְּשֶׁהִשְׂכִּירוֹ לְנָכְרִי לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת הֲרֵי מְכָרוֹ לוֹ לְגַמְרֵי לְיוֹם זֶה, וְאֵין כַּאן שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת כְּלָל, שֶׁאֵין הַנָּכְרִי נוֹתֵן לוֹ שָׂכָר כְּלָל, אֶלָּא קָנָה מִמֶּנּוּ לְגַמְרֵי לְיוֹם זֶה, וַהֲרֵי זֶה דוֹמֶה לְמִי שֶׁיּוֹדֵעַ שֶׁיָּבִיאוּ לוֹ סְחוֹרָה בְּשַׁבָּת וּמוֹכְרָהּ לְנָכְרִי מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת שֶׁיְּקַבְּלֶנָּה לְמָחָר לְעַצְמוֹ, שֶׁאֵין בְּזֶה מִשּׁוּם שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת:

English translation

When one leases the toll to a non-Jew, he may lease it for the day of Shabbos alone, without havla'ah (absorption) with the other days. This is unlike a bathhouse (merchatz), which one may not lease for Shabbos alone, so as not to take schar Shabbos (Shabbos wages) without havla'ah — because the bathhouse itself remains the Yid's on Shabbos, and the non-Jew's payment is schar Shabbos. For the toll, however, leasing him the day of Shabbos amounts to selling it to him entirely for that day: there is no schar Shabbos, for the non-Jew does not pay rent but buys the right for that day. It is comparable to one who, knowing that merchandise will be delivered on Shabbos, sells it in advance to a non-Jew on erev Shabbos.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav distinguishes two regimes of שכר שבת (Shabbos wages): the bathhouse (forbidden without havla'ah) and the toll (permitted for Shabbos alone).

הבלעה (havla'ah, absorption): folding the Shabbos payment into a wider period to avoid "Shabbos wages." Required for the bathhouse (which stays the Yid's), unnecessary for the toll — because leasing it on Shabbos amounts to selling it for that day (not renting).

The halachic question

Schar Shabbos (gain drawn from Shabbos) is forbidden. Why then may one lease the toll for Shabbos alone, without havla'ah, while the bathhouse requires it? It all depends on the legal nature: rent (forbidden) vs sale of the right for that day (permitted).

Mechaber (244:6)Permits leasing the toll for Shabbos alone, without absorption.
Mishnah BerurahExplains the rent/sale distinction regarding Shabbos wages.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's distinction:
  1. Bathhouse: stays the Yid's property → rent = שכר שבת → requires havla'ah.
  2. Toll: leasing it on Shabbos = sale of the right for that day → no Shabbos wages.
  3. Comparable to selling in advance merchandise delivered on Shabbos.

Modern practical cases

יִשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁשָּׂכַר הַמֶּלַח מֵהַשָּׂר לְשָׁנָה, שֶׁכַּמָּה מֶלַח שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּתוֹךְ הַשָּׁנָה הוּא שֶׁלּוֹ, וְהוּא שׂוֹכֵר לוֹ פּוֹעֲלִים נָכְרִים לְשָׁנָה אוֹ לְחֹדֶשׁ, וְקָצַץ לָהֶם שְׂכָרָם לְפִי רִבּוּי מְלַאכְתָּם, דְּהַיְנוּ מִכָּל כָּךְ וְכָךְ מֶלַח שֶׁיְּבַשְּׁלוּ נוֹתֵן לָהֶם כָּךְ וְכָךְ, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמְּבַשְּׁלִים בְּכֵלִים שֶׁל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל — מֻתָּר לְהַנִּיחָם שֶׁיַּעֲשׂוּ בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁאֵין אָדָם מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתַת כֵּלָיו, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ו, וְהַנָּכְרִים הֵם כְּקַבְּלָנִים שֶׁעוֹשִׂים לְטוֹבַת עַצְמָן כְּדֵי לְהַרְבּוֹת בִּשְׂכָרָן, שֶׁהֲרֵי אֵין שְׂכָרָם מִשְׁתַּלֵּם לְפִי הַיָּמִים אוֹ חֳדָשִׁים, אֶלָּא לְפִי סְכוּם הַמְּלָאכָה שֶׁגּוֹמְרִין, וְכָךְ מְשַׁלֵּם לָהֶם, נִמְצָא שֶׁהֵם מִתְכַּוְּנִים בַּעֲשִׂיָּתָם בְּשַׁבָּת לְטוֹבַת עַצְמָן.

(וְאַף שֶׁאִם לֹא הָיוּ עוֹשִׂים בְּשַׁבָּת הָיָה מַגִּיעַ הֶפְסֵד לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, שֶׁהֲרֵי הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל שָׂכַר הַמֶּלַח שֶׁיְּבַשֵּׁל בְּתוֹךְ שְׁנָתוֹ, וְהַיָּמִים שֶׁהֶחְסִיר וְלֹא בִשֵּׁל מֵהֶם אֵין לָהֶם תַּשְׁלוּמִים, שֶׁלֹּא יְנַכֶּה לוֹ הַשָּׂר כְּלוּם מִשְּׂכָרוֹ, וְגַם לֹא יַנִּיחֶנּוּ לְבַשֵּׁל כְּנֶגְדָּם בְּשָׁנָה הָאַחֶרֶת, וּבִשְׁבִיל כָּךְ כְּשֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל שׂוֹכֵר פּוֹעֲלִים לְחֹדֶשׁ אוֹ לְשָׁנָה הוּא מַקְפִּיד עֲלֵיהֶם מְאֹד שֶׁלֹּא יְבַטְּלוּ אֲפִלּוּ יוֹם אֶחָד, וְהֵם מֻכְרָחִים לַעֲשׂוֹת גַּם בְּשַׁבָּת מֵחֲמַת קְפֵדָתוֹ, מִכָּל מָקוֹם בִּשְׁבִיל כָּךְ אֵין צָרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדָם שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשׂוּ בְּשַׁבָּת, אֶלָּא דַּי שֶׁיֹּאמַר לָהֶם שֶׁאֵינָם מֻכְרָחִים לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת בִּשְׁבִילוֹ, וְאִם יִרְצוּ שֶׁלֹּא לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת הָרְשׁוּת בְּיָדָם, וְאִם אַף עַל פִּי כֵן הֵם עוֹשִׂים מֵאֲלֵיהֶם כְּדֵי לְהַרְבּוֹת בִּשְׂכָרָם — אֵין צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר לָהֶם כְּלוּם, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמַּגִּיעַ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל הֲנָאָה גְדוֹלָה מִזֶּה — אֵין בְּכָךְ כְּלוּם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהַנָּכְרִים מִתְכַּוְּנִים לְטוֹבַת עַצְמָן[)].

וְאֵין לֶאֱסוֹר מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, לְפִי שֶׁהָרוֹאֶה אֵינוֹ יוֹדֵעַ שֶׁבִּשּׁוּל זֶה הוּא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, שֶׁהַכֵּלִים שֶׁמְּבַשְּׁלִים בָּהֶם, וְכֵן הָעֵצִים שֶׁמַּבְעִירִים בָּהֶם — אֵין שֵׁם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל נִקְרָא עֲלֵיהֶם, דְּהַיְנוּ שֶׁאֵין מְפֻרְסָם לָרַבִּים שֶׁהֵן שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל.

וּבִלְבָד שֶׁלֹּא יָבִיאוּ עֵצִים מֵרְשׁוּת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁכָּל קַבְּלָנוּת אֵינָהּ מֻתֶּרֶת אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן מָסַר לוֹ מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת כָּל מַה שֶּׁרוֹצֶה לִמְסוֹר לוֹ לְצֹרֶךְ הַמְּלָאכָה.

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם מָסַר לָהֶם הָעֵצִים מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, אֶלָּא שֶׁלֹּא הוֹצִיאוּם מֵרְשׁוּתוֹ עַד שֶׁחָשְׁכָה — אָסוּר לְהַנִּיחָם שֶׁיּוֹצִיאוּ בְּשַׁבָּת, מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, שֶׁיֹּאמְרוּ שֶׁבְּשַׁבָּת מָסַר לָהֶם, וּכְמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ו, עַיֵּן שָׁם:

English translation

A Yid who leased the salt-works from a nobleman for a year — all the salt produced in the year is his — and who hires non-Jewish workers for a year or a month, fixing their wage by quantity (so much per such-and-such amount of salt): even if they cook in the Yid's vessels, it is permitted to let them work on Shabbos — for a person is not commanded about the resting of his vessels (אין אדם מצווה על שביתת כליו, siman רמ״ו), and the non-Jews are like kablanim working for their own profit (paid by quantity, not by time).

One does not forbid it by marit ayin, for no one knows that this cooking is a Yid's (neither the vessels nor the wood bear his name). Provided that the wood not be brought from the Yid's domain on Shabbos — for all kablanus is permitted only if all the material was handed over before Shabbos. And even if handed over before Shabbos but not taken out of the domain before nightfall, one may not let it be taken out on Shabbos (marit ayin, siman רמ״ו).

What is this Seif about?

The Rav here gathers several principles: pay by quantity (= kablanus), שביתת כלים (the resting of vessels), anonymity, and the condition of handing over the material before Shabbos.

Pay "so much per quantity of salt" = kablanus (not by time) → the workers are for themselves. Even with the Yid's vessels, it is permitted (a person is not commanded about the resting of his vessels). Strict condition: all material handed over before Shabbos.

The halachic question

The workers use the Yid's vessels — is that not a problem (שביתת כלים)? And is pay by quantity kablanus? And must one fear marit ayin? The Rav answers each point.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Gemara (שביתת כלים)"אין אדם מצווה על שביתת כליו" — no obligation regarding the resting of one's vessels (siman רמ״ו).
Rosh / RishonimPay by quantity = kablanus → the workers are for their own profit.
Mechaber (244:6)Permits this arrangement; requires handing over the material before Shabbos.
Mishnah BerurahConfirms the anonymity (neither vessels nor wood named) and the handover condition.
SHHaRav (244:20)A synthesis: kablanus by quantity + שביתת כלים + anonymity; material handed over before Shabbos, not taken out of the domain on Shabbos.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's permitted arrangement:
  1. Pay by quantity = kablanus → the workers are for themselves.
  2. The Yid's vessels → permitted (שביתת כלים).
  3. Anonymity → no marit ayin. Condition: material handed over before Shabbos.

Modern practical cases

  • The Yid's factory, non-Jewish workers paid by output, runs on Shabbos: 🟢 kablanus + שביתת כלים → permitted (materials handed over before Shabbos).
  • The Yid's machines used by a provider paid by output: 🟢 permitted (anonymous).
  • Bringing raw materials from the Yid's warehouse on Shabbos: 🔴 must be done before Shabbos.
  • Materials handed over Friday but taken out only on Shabbos: 🔴 marit ayin → do not take out on Shabbos.
  • Workers paid by the hour in the Yid's factory: 🔴 sechir yom → forbidden.

Points to remember

  • Pay by quantity = kablanus → permitted even with the Yid's vessels
  • שביתת כלים: a person is not commanded about the resting of his vessels
  • Strict condition: all material handed over before Shabbos

26. Seif Khaf-Alef — מראית עין vs loss of profit alone

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְאִם הוּא בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁיֵּשׁ חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית הָעַיִן — אֵין לְהַתִּיר מֵחֲמַת הֶפְסֵד מְרֻבֶּה שֶׁיַּפְסִיד הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בְּכָל שַׁבְּתוֹת הַשָּׁנָה, אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן הוּא בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁמַּפְסִיד הַרְבֵּה מִכִּיסוֹ מִמַּה שֶּׁצָּרִיךְ לְשַׁלֵּם לְהַשָּׂר וְלַפּוֹעֲלִים וְיֶתֶר הוֹצָאוֹתָיו, אֲבָל אִם הוּא בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁלֹּא יַפְסִיד כְּלוּם מִכִּיסוֹ, אֶלָּא שֶׁמַּפְסִיד הָרֶוַח שֶׁהָיָה יָכוֹל לְהַרְוִיחַ הַרְבֵּה בַּעֲשִׂיַּת הַמֶּלַח גַּם בְּשַׁבָּתוֹת — אֵין מַתִּירִין לוֹ בִּשְׁבִיל כָּךְ אִסּוּר שֶׁל מַרְאִית הָעַיִן.

וְאֵינוֹ דוֹמֶה לְמֶכֶס וּלְמַטְבֵּעַ שֶׁאַף אִם הוּא בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁלֹּא יַפְסִיד כְּלוּם מִכִּיסוֹ, אֶלָּא מִשּׁוּם שֶׁלֹּא יַפְסִיד הָרֶוַח שֶׁל הַשַּׁבָּתוֹת הִתִּירוּ לוֹ אִסּוּר מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, לְפִי שֶׁהַמֶּכֶס כְּבָר נִתְחַיְּבוּ בּוֹ הַנָּכְרִים הַסּוֹחֲרִים הָעוֹבְרִים בְּשַׁבָּת לִתְּנוֹ לְהַמֶּלֶךְ, וְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל כְּבָר קָנָה חִיּוּב זֶה מֵהַמֶּלֶךְ, נִמְצָא שֶׁאִם לֹא יִטְּלֶנּוּ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מֵהַסּוֹחֲרִים — הֲרֵי זֶה כְּמַפְסִיד הָרֶוַח שֶׁבָּא כְּבָר לְכִיסוֹ. וְכֵן הַמַּטְבֵּעוֹת שֶׁיַּעֲשׂוּ עוֹשֵׂי הַמְּלָאכָה אֲשֶׁר לַמֶּלֶךְ בְּעַל כָּרְחוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, כְּבָר הֵם קְנוּיִים לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, וְאִם לֹא יִטְּלֵם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל — הֲרֵי זֶה כְּמַפְסִיד הָרֶוַח שֶׁבָּא כְּבָר לְכִיסוֹ:

English translation

If the situation involves a concern of marit ayin — one does not permit it on account of the great loss the Yid would suffer on all the Shabbasos of the year, unless he would lose much out of his own pocket (what he must pay the nobleman, the workers, his expenses). But if he loses nothing out of pocket, losing only the profit he could have made by producing also on Shabbos — one does not permit him the issur of marit ayin for that.

This is unlike the toll and coinage, where even for the mere loss of the Shabbos profit the marit ayin was permitted — because there the non-Jews already owed that toll to the king, and the Yid already acquired it: not collecting it is losing a profit already come into his pocket. So too the coins struck by the king's workers are already acquired by the Yid.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav refines the rule "great loss lifts marit ayin" (Seif Yod-Daled): one must distinguish an actual loss of capital (lifts the gezeirah) from a mere loss of expected profit (does not lift it).

• Loss out of pocket (expenses already incurred) → marit ayin yields.
• Mere loss of profit (unrealized gain) → marit ayin remains.
Exception (toll/coinage): the profit was already acquired → treated as a real loss.

The halachic question

"Great loss" lifts marit ayin (Seif Yod-Daled). But what is a "loss"? An out-of-pocket expense, or also a gain one could not make? The Rav rules: only an actual loss (or already-acquired profit) counts.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Raavad / RoshDistinguishes loss of capital (חסרון כיס) from loss of profit (מניעת ריוח).
Mechaber / Beis YosefThe toll/coinage: profit already acquired → treated as a real loss.
Mishnah BerurahConfirms: a mere loss of profit does not lift marit ayin.
SHHaRav (244:21)A fine distinction: חסרון כיס (lifts the gezeirah) vs מניעת ריוח (does not lift it); toll/coinage = profit already in pocket.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's distinction:
  1. Out-of-pocket loss (expenses paid to the nobleman, the workers) → marit ayin lifted.
  2. Loss of profit alone → marit ayin upheld.
  3. Toll/coinage = profit already acquired → treated as a real loss.

Modern practical cases

  • An activity halted on Shabbos with fixed charges paid (rent, salaries): 🟢 out-of-pocket loss → marit ayin lifted.
  • Pure loss of profit (sales not made on Shabbos): 🔴 no lifting of marit ayin.
  • A debt already acquired not collected on Shabbos (toll type): 🟢 profit already in → permitted.
  • A factory idle losing mainly potential turnover: 🔴 loss of profit → forbidden.
  • Fixed charges owed regardless: 🟢 actual loss → lifting possible.

Points to remember

  • Distinguish out-of-pocket loss (lifts marit ayin) from loss of profit (does not lift it)
  • The toll/coinage: profit already acquired → treated as a real loss
  • Loss of profit alone does not justify overriding marit ayin
Comprehension question: What is the difference between an "out-of-pocket loss" and a "loss of profit" regarding marit ayin?

27. Seif Khaf-Beis — Iron and glass works: the general rule

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְיִשְׂרְאֵלִים שֶׁשּׂוֹכְרִים מֵהַשָּׂר שֶׁיִּהְיֶה רְשׁוּת בְּיָדָם לַחְפּוֹר בֶּהָרִים לִמְצוֹא הֶעָפָר שֶׁעוֹשִׂים מִמֶּנּוּ בַּרְזֶל, וְשׂוֹרְפִים וְצוֹרְפִים הֶעָפָר יוֹם וְלַיְלָה לֹא יִשְׁבֹּתוּ עַד כְּלוֹת מֶשֶׁךְ זְמַן שְׂכִירוּתָם, וְשׂוֹכְרִים לָהֶם פּוֹעֲלִים לְכָךְ לְשָׁנָה אוֹ לְחֹדֶשׁ, וְקוֹצְצִים לָהֶם שָׂכָר כָּךְ וְכָךְ בְּעַד כָּךְ וְכָךְ בַּרְזֶל — הֲרֵי דִינָם כְּדִין שׂוֹכֵר אֶת הַמֶּלַח לְכָל דָּבָר, אִם הוּא בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁאֵין לָחוּשׁ לְמַרְאִית הָעַיִן, עַל דֶּרֶךְ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ג עַיֵּן שָׁם. וְכֵן הַדִּין בְּשׂוֹכְרֵי הִתּוּךְ הַזְּכוּכִית מֵהַשָּׂרִים, וְכֵן בְּכָל כַּיּוֹצֵא בָזֶה:

English translation

Yidden who lease from a nobleman the right to dig the mountains for the ore from which iron is made, burning and refining the ore day and night without rest until the end of their lease, and who hire workers for this by the year or the month, fixing their wage by quantity of iron: their din is in every respect that of the salt lease — provided there is no concern of marit ayin (cf. siman רמ״ג). And likewise for those who lease the smelting of glass from noblemen, and every similar case.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav closes the Siman with a generalization: the salt regime (Seif Khaf) applies to any leased industry paid by quantity — iron, glass, "and every similar case."

The principle is now transferable: kablanus by quantity + anonymity (no marit ayin) → permitted, whatever the industry. The Rav formulates a general rule rather than a closed list.

The halachic question

Must one redo the whole analysis for each new industry (iron, glass, etc.)? No: the Rav establishes that all these cases follow the salt regime, once the conditions (kablanus, anonymity) are met.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's general rule:
  1. Any industry leased with pay by quantity = the salt regime.
  2. Condition: no marit ayin (anonymity, cf. רמ״ג).
  3. An open rule: "and every similar case" (iron, glass, etc.).

Modern practical cases

  • A leased steel mill, workers paid by tonnage, runs on Shabbos: 🟢 the salt regime → permitted (anonymous).
  • A glassworks/foundry on production kablanus: 🟢 permitted under the same conditions.
  • A refinery running continuously, paid by quantity: 🟢 if no marit ayin.
  • Any new industry on anonymous kablanus: 🟢 covered by the general rule.
  • The same industry but publicly identified with the Yid: 🔴 marit ayin → forbidden.

Points to remember

  • The salt regime generalizes to any industry leased by quantity
  • The single condition: no marit ayin (anonymity)
  • An open rule: "וכן בכל כיוצא בזה" (iron, glass, and every similar case)

Synthesis & questions

28. Practical synthesis of Siman רמ״ד

The 5 pillars of the Siman in 5 sentences:
  1. קבלנות vs שכיר יום — the kablan acts for himself (אדעתא דנפשיה) → permitted; the sechir yom is the Yid's shaliach → forbidden
  2. תלוש vs מחובר — the discreet detached is permitted; the attached (land, building site) is forbidden by marit ayin
  3. מראית עין — blocks even the detached if it is public AND named (שם הישראל נקרא עליו); yields before great loss
  4. הנאה vs רֶוַח — a mere benefit is permitted; an actual gain (משתרשא ליה) is forbidden
  5. שבות דשבות, שביתת כלים, אדם בהול — levers that ease kablanus in commercial cases (toll, coinage, salt, iron)
The SHHaRav's own contributions (the 22 seifim):
  1. Seif Alef — Foundation: ownership of the work + intent; the 4 safeguards
  2. Seif Beis — תלוש/מחובר; the theory of "קול" (public resonance)
  3. Seif Gimmel — חוץ לתחום: the double condition of the heter beyond the techum
  4. Seif Daled — The public and named detached (ספינה) becomes forbidden again
  5. Seif Heh — The line: "of the Yid" (forbidden) vs "for the Yid" (permitted, רינון)
  6. Seif Vav — תלוש לצורך מחובר: the destination determines the status
  7. Seif Zayin — Bediavad: kablanus habitable, sechir yom forbidden
  8. Seif Ches — מנהג, שעת הדחק, chillul Hashem (cleaning / shul)
  9. Seif Tes — The idle yearly scribe: הנאה (permitted) vs רֶוַח (forbidden)
  10. Seif Yod — The קפידא (the Yid's insistence) tips into shelichus
  11. Seif Yod-Alef — Hired "for everything": משתרשא ליה → forbidden
  12. Seif Yod-Beis — Toll via kablanus: שבות דשבות (loss + mitzvah)
  13. Seif Yod-Gimmel — A biblical melacha: אדם בהול על ממונו
  14. Seif Yod-Daled — Leasing out; great loss lifts marit ayin (by category)
  15. Seif Tes-Vav — Toll for Shabbos alone: sale ≠ rent (no שכר שבת)
  16. Seif Tes-Zayin — Coinage: the king's franchise; the noise does not forbid
  17. Seif Yod-Zayin — Do not sit beside him (except for great loss)
  18. Seif Yod-Ches — Watching yes, doing the accounts no (הכנה)
  19. Seif Yod-Tes — Noisy but anonymous detached → permitted
  20. Seif Khaf — Salt: kablanus by quantity + שביתת כלים + material handed over before Shabbos
  21. Seif Khaf-Alef — חסרון כיס (lifts) vs מניעת ריוח (does not lift) marit ayin
  22. Seif Khaf-Beis — Iron, glass: the generalization ("and every similar case")

The 4 cumulative conditions — a reminder

  1. קַבְּלָנוּת — by the job, never by the day
  2. בְּבֵיתוֹ — at the non-Jew's, not at the Yid's
  3. תָּלוּשׁ — a movable object, no visible attached
  4. בְּלִי אֲמִירָה — without saying "do it on Shabbos"; ketzitzah (price) fixed before Shabbos; doable during the week
