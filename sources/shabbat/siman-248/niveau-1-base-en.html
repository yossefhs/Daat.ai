1. The text of the Shulchan Aruch

Siman רמ״ח contains 4 seifim of the Mechaber which codify the halachos relating to traveling by ship and caravan in the days preceding Shabbat.

Seif Alef — The basic rule

מֻתָּר לְהַפְלִיג בִּסְפִינָה אֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת אִם הוֹלֵךְ לִדְבַר מִצְוָה, וּפוֹסֵק עִמּוֹ שֶׁיִּשְׁבּוֹת. וְאִם אַחַר כָּךְ לֹא יִשְׁבּוֹת — אֵין בְּכָךְ כְּלוּם. אֲבָל לִדְבַר הָרְשׁוּת אֵין מַפְלִיגִין בִּסְפִינָה בְּפָחוֹת מִג' יָמִים קוֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת. הגה: וְאִם הוּא דֶּרֶךְ מוּעָט כְּמוֹ מִצּוֹר לְצִידוֹן, שֶׁאֵין בֵּינֵיהֶם כִּי אִם מַהֲלַךְ יוֹם אֶחָד — מֻתָּר לְהַפְלִיג בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת בַּבֹּקֶר.
It is permitted to board a ship (sefina) even on Friday if one is traveling for a devar mitzvah, and one stipulates with the captain that the ship will halt for Shabbat. And if afterwards it does not halt — there is nothing wrong. But for a devar reshus (mundane travel), one does not board a ship less than three days before Shabbat. Hagahah of the Rema: If the journey is short (like Tsour to Tsidon, a one-day distance) — one may board on Friday morning, because it is possible to arrive before Shabbat.
The central idea of Seif Alef: "one does not board within the three days preceding Shabbat" — with two major exceptions: (1) if it is for a mitzvah (with a stipulation to pause on Shabbat); (2) if the distance is short and one can arrive before Shabbat.

Seif Beis — Why three days? Sea/river distinction

הָא דְּאֵין מַפְלִיגִין בִּסְפִינָה בְּפָחוֹת מִשְּׁלֹשָׁה יָמִים קוֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת — הַטַּעַם מִשּׁוּם עוֹנֶג שַׁבָּת, שֶׁכָּל שְׁלֹשָׁה יָמִים הָרִאשׁוֹנִים יֵשׁ לָהֶם צַעַר וּבִלְבּוּל. וְדַוְקָא לְמַפְלִיגִים בְּמַיִם הַמְּלוּחִים. אֲבָל בִּנְהָרוֹת אֵין שׁוּם צַעַר — וּלְפִיכָךְ מֻתָּר לְהַפְלִיג בָּהֶם אֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת.
The "three day" rule is due to oneg Shabbat: during the first three days at sea, one suffers from seasickness (צער ובלבול). This applies only to salt water seas. On rivers there is no suffering — one may board even on Friday.
Two distinct reasons emerge: עונג שבת (the first three days are difficult) and איסור תחומין (in shallow water, there is a tchum problem).

Seif Gimel — קנה שביתה: boarding before Shabbat

הֵיכָא דְּמֻתָּר לְהַפְלִיג מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, אִם נִכְנַס בַּסְּפִינָה מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת וְקָנָה בָהּ שְׁבִיתָה — אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמַּפְלֶגֶת בְּשַׁבָּת מֻתָּר.
Wherever it is permitted to embark on Friday, if one boards the ship before Shabbat and "acquires shevisa" there, even if the ship sets sail on Shabbat — it is permitted (provided one does not disembark).
קנה שביתה (kanah shevisa): by boarding the ship before Shabbat (with a meaningful act such as Kiddush), one "anchors his place of Shabbat rest there." The ship becomes an extension of his home. The Rema broadens this permission.

Seif Daled — Caravan in the desert + Aliyah

הַיּוֹצְאִים בְּשַׁיָּרָא בַּמִּדְבָּר וְהַכֹּל יוֹדְעִים שֶׁהֵם צְרִיכִים לְחַלֵּל שַׁבָּת — ג' יָמִים קוֹדֶם שַׁבָּת אֲסוּרִים לָצֵאת. וּבְיוֹם רִאשׁוֹן וּבַשֵּׁנִי וּבַשְּׁלִישִׁי מֻתָּר. וְהָעוֹלֶה לְאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל, אִם נִזְדַּמְּנָה לוֹ שַׁיָּרָא אֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת — כֵּיוָן דִּדְבַר מִצְוָה הִיא יָכוֹל לִפְרֹשׁ.
For those who set out in a caravan (shayara) in the desert and it is known that they will have to violate Shabbat — three days before Shabbat it is prohibited to leave. But on Sunday, Monday and Tuesday (days 1-2-3) it is permitted. For one who goes up (oleh) to Eretz Yisrael, even on Friday — since it is a devar mitzvah, he may set out.
Seif Daled extends the rule to overland caravans and introduces two essential concepts: פיקוח נפש (which overrides all prohibitions) and עליה לארץ ישראל = דבר מצוה (mitzvah par excellence).

Hagahah of the Rema — extension of דבר מצוה (Rabbeinu Tam)

הגה: יֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁכָּל מָקוֹם שֶׁאָדָם הוֹלֵךְ לִסְחוֹרָה אוֹ לִרְאוֹת פְּנֵי חֲבֵרוֹ — חָשׁוּב הַכֹּל דְּבַר מִצְוָה, וְאֵינוֹ חָשׁוּב דְּבַר הָרְשׁוּת רַק כְּשֶׁהוֹלֵךְ לְטַיֵּל.
Hagahah of the Rema (citing Rabbeinu Tam): Anywhere a person travels — for business or to visit a friend — is considered devar mitzvah. Only pure pleasure travel is devar reshus.
Rabbeinu Tam's extension is massive in practice: it considerably broadens the category of devar mitzvah. Business travel, visiting a friend, family travel — all become mitzvah. Only pure tourism remains devar reshus.

2. The general context

What does this siman discuss?

Our siman addresses a very practical situation: when may one undertake a journey that will spill over into Shabbat — whether by ship, caravan, or in our modern applications (airplane, train, cruise)?

The fundamental question: knowing that the journey will continue on Shabbat, may one undertake it? Or does this violate the prohibition of "לא תעשה כל מלאכה" by being in motion on Shabbat?

The Talmudic origin — the beraisa of Shabbos 19a

The entire siman derives from a classical beraisa cited in Shabbos 19a:

תָּנוּ רַבָּנָן: אֵין מַפְלִיגִין בִּסְפִינָה פָּחוֹת מִג' יָמִים קוֹדֶם לְשַׁבָּת. בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? לִדְבַר הָרְשׁוּת. אֲבָל לִדְבַר מִצְוָה — שַׁפִּיר דָּמֵי, וּפוֹסֵק עִמּוֹ עַל מְנָת לִשְׁבּוֹת. רַבָּן שִׁמְעוֹן בֶּן גַּמְלִיאֵל אוֹמֵר: אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ.
The Rabbanan taught: one does not board a ship less than three days before Shabbat. To what case does this apply? To devar reshus. But for a devar mitzvah — shapir dami (it is fine), and one stipulates with him on condition that he will rest. Rashbag says: it is not even necessary to make such a stipulation.

The halachah follows Rashbag according to the Rosh. But the Mechaber maintains the stipulation as lechatchilah.

3. First key concept — the three-day rule

Rule: one does not embark on a ship (nor on a caravan) less than three days before Shabbat. Concretely: Thursday, Friday, and Shabbat itself = prohibited. Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday = permitted (according to the majority).

How to count the "three days"?

The Mishnah Berurah clarifies (ס״ק ה): according to the Magen Avraham and the majority, Wednesday is prohibited. But the Gra cites Rishonim who count the three days including Shabbat, and so Wednesday would be permitted.

Rabbeinu Tam's extension

Tosafos (Shabbos 19a): any travel that is not pure tourism falls into devar mitzvah:
  • Business travel (לסחורה)
  • Visiting a friend / relative (לראות פני חבירו)
  • Aliyah, study, hachnasas orchim, etc.

5. Third key concept — פוסק עמו שישבות

פוסק עמו שישבות (posek imo she-yishbos): an explicit stipulation with the captain that "the ship will halt on Shabbat" — even if in actuality the ship does not stop.

The Jewish traveler is not requesting the ship's journey on Shabbat. If the ship continues, it does so against his request. Therefore there is no violation.

PositionStipulation required?
Tanna Kamma (Rebbi)YES
Rashbag — halachahNO, not required
Today, one can almost never "stipulate" with a flight captain or a cruise ship captain. The stipulation is structurally impossible with a commercial service. So in practice: either the travel is devar mitzvah (R. Tam: most travel) → one may depart; or it is pure tourism → strict three-day rule applies.

6. Why three days? The three classical pirushim

Pirush A — Rashi and Rif: משום עונג שבת

Rashi and Rif: for the first three days at sea, one is physically suffering (seasickness, fatigue, disorientation). No oneg Shabbat is possible. The rule preserves oneg.

→ Consequence: for rivers, no seasickness → the rule does not apply (Mechaber Seif Beis).

Pirush B — Yereim and Ramban: משום איסור תחומין

Yereim and Ramban: when the ship is less than 10 tefachim above the bottom, one is within the terrestrial tchum. One risks leaving the 2000 amos on Shabbat. The rule protects the prohibition of tchum.

→ Consequence: deep water (>10 tefachim) → no problem; shallow water → problem.

Pirush C — Riba/Mahari: משום סכנה (pikuach nefesh)

Riba and Mahari: in a storm or peril, the Jewish traveler will be obligated to violate Shabbat for pikuach nefesh. In order not to put oneself voluntarily into such a situation, one does not embark so close to Shabbat.

→ Consequence: guaranteed safety (modern airplane) → this explanation weakens.

The three pirushim coexist and are cumulative. Each one illuminates a different borderline case. To understand a modern situation, analyze all three.

7. The detail of the 4 seifim — one by one

Seif Alef — The general rule

Three cumulative conditions to embark on Friday:
  1. It is for a mitzvah (R. Tam broadens widely)
  2. One stipulates the Shabbat pause (posek imo she-yishbos) — lechatchilah
  3. If in the end it does not stop → ein bechach klum (no issue)
For short distance: only the condition of arriving before Shabbat suffices.

Seif Beis — Sea/river distinction + tchumin

The practical application:
  • Lake, river, fresh water → no three-day rule, except tchum
  • Salt water sea → three-day rule applies
  • Deep water (>10 tefachim) → no tchum problem
  • Shallow water → potential tchum problem

7b. The additional seifim of the SHHaRav (ה–יד)

The Admur HaZakein (Shulchan Aruch HaRav) spreads the siman across 14 seifim. Seifim 1 through 4 of the SHHaRav roughly cover the Mechaber's seif Alef and Beis. Here is a concise read of seifim 5–14, indispensable to grasping the full sugya.

Seif Heh (5) — Boarding before the three days and pikuach nefesh

וְאִם נִכְנַס לַסְּפִינָה קֹדֶם ג' יָמִים לִפְנֵי הַשַּׁבָּת, דְּהַיְנוּ בַּשְּׁלִישִׁי בַּשַּׁבָּת אוֹ קֹדֶם לְכֵן — מֻתָּר לֵילֵךְ בָּהּ בְּשַׁבָּת חוּץ לַתְּחוּם… וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם הוּא בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁאֶפְשָׁר שֶׁיִּצְטָרֵךְ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְחַלֵּל שַׁבָּת… מִפְּנֵי פִּקּוּחַ נֶפֶשׁ — אַף עַל פִּי כֵן מֻתָּר לוֹ לִכָּנֵס בְּתוֹכָהּ קֹדֶם ג' יָמִים.
If one boards the ship before the three days preceding Shabbos (i.e., Sunday, Monday or Tuesday), it is permitted to continue on Shabbos outside the tchum — even if we know he may have to be mechalel Shabbos for pikuach nefesh. But within the three days, if one knows in advance he will need to be mechalel Shabbos (even for a derabbanan), it is forbidden — since it appears as a deliberate act.
Fundamental temporal chiluk: the first three days of the week are linked to the past Shabbos, the last three to the upcoming Shabbos. That is why boarding the ship Sunday-Tuesday, even knowing one will be mechalel Shabbos for pikuach nefesh, is permitted. From Wednesday onward (according to the majority), putting oneself voluntarily into a situation of future chillul looks like an intentional act.

This idea — "yamim shel achar haShabbos" vs "yamim shelifnei haShabbos" — is the conceptual key of the entire sugya. It explains why the number 3 is not arbitrary: it is half of the week. From midweek on, one is ontologically "turned toward Shabbos."

Seif Vav (6) — Ship that is mostly Jewish: forbidden even without chillul

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם יָדוּעַ לוֹ שֶׁלֹּא יִצְטָרֵךְ לְחַלֵּל הַשַּׁבָּת כְּלָל, שֶׁהַנָּכְרִים עוֹשִׂים הַכֹּל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם אִם עִקַּר הַהֲלִיכָה שֶׁל הַסְּפִינָה הוּא בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל, כְּגוֹן שֶׁרֻבָּהּ יִשְׂרָאֵל — אָסוּר לִכָּנֵס בְּתוֹכָהּ בְּתוֹךְ ג' יָמִים שֶׁלִּפְנֵי הַשַּׁבָּת.
Even if one knows he will not need to be mechalel Shabbos at all (the goyim do everything), if the ship sails primarily for the benefit of Jews (the majority of passengers are Jewish), it is assur to board within the last three days, since the work done by the goy on Shabbos benefits the Jewish passengers directly — it is considered as done for them.
Decisive modern application: on a charter flight booked by a Jewish kehillah, or a cruise with a predominantly Jewish clientele, the three-day rule applies strictly — even if all piloting is done by a non-Jewish crew. The mere fact that the service benefits Jews on Shabbos suffices to forbid it.

Seif Zayin (7) — Devar mitzvah: permitted even erev Shabbos

וְכָל זֶה כְּשֶׁהוֹלֵךְ לִדְבַר הָרְשׁוּת, אֲבָל לִדְבַר מִצְוָה מֻתָּר לְהַפְלִיג אֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, אֲפִלּוּ בִּסְפִינָה הַמְהַלֶּכֶת לְמַטָּה מִי' טְפָחִים… דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁעַכְשָׁו מַפְלִיג בְּהֶתֵּר — לֹא גָזְרוּ חֲכָמִים כְּשֶׁהוֹלֵךְ לִדְבַר מִצְוָה.
All this applies to a leisure trip. But for a devar mitzvah, it is mutar to set out even on erev Shabbos, even on a ship traveling below 10 tefachim, even if one will need to perform melachos d'oraysa for pikuach nefesh. Lechatchilah one stipulates with the navigator that he will rest on Shabbos; but if he does not stop — ein bechach klum.
The SHHaRav's sevara: "keivan she-achshav maflig be-heter" — since one is now boarding within a heter (mitzvah), Chazal did not extend their gezerah. The three-day rule is a גזרה; gezeiros never apply when one is already in a legal framework of a mitzvah.

Seif Ches (8) — Active chillul Shabbos for a mitzvah: not permitted

אֲבָל לְחַלֵּל שַׁבָּת לְצֹרֶךְ מִצְוָה, אֲפִלּוּ בְּדָבָר שֶׁאֵין בּוֹ אֶלָּא אִסּוּר שְׁבוּת מִדִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים — לֹא הִתִּירוּ, אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן הוּא שְׁבוּת דִּשְׁבוּת.
However, being mechalel Shabbos even for a mitzvah — even a simple issur shvus (rabbinic prohibition) — is not permitted, except in a case of shvus deshvus (double-rabbinic). So if one knows he will need to disembark outside the tchum without any danger involved, boarding is assur even for a mitzvah — unless one boarded before the three days.
Crucial nuance: the heter of Seif Zayin (mitzvah) does not cover a deliberate and planned chillul Shabbos. It only covers violations rendered necessary by pikuach nefesh. For avoidable violations, we revert to the strict rule.

Seif Tes (9) — Boarding the ship on Shabbos itself: forbidden

וְכֵן אָסוּר לוֹ לִכָּנֵס בַּסְּפִינָה בְּשַׁבָּת עַצְמָהּ אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהוֹלֵךְ לִדְבַר מִצְוָה… לְפִי שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כְּאִלּוּ הוּא עַצְמוֹ צָף וְשָׁט עַל פְּנֵי הַמַּיִם… וְיֵשׁ מַתִּירִין לִכָּנֵס בְּתוֹכָהּ וּלְהַפְלִיג בְּשַׁבָּת אִם קָנָה בָּהּ שְׁבִיתָה מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת.
It is assur to board the ship on Shabbos itself, even for a mitzvah, on account of the gezeirah of shayit (swimming). But if one boarded on Friday and remained through bein hashemashos, one may continue on Shabbos (קנה שביתה). Minhag: making Kiddush on the ship to publicize that one was koneh shevisah.
Three levels to distinguish: (1) boarding on Shabbos itself → absolutely assur (gezeiras shayit); (2) boarding Friday night before Shabbos → disputed; (3) boarding earlier with kinyan shevisah → mutar by all opinions. Practice adopts the lenient position with Kiddush on the ship.

Seif Yud (10) — Yom Tov on erev Shabbos and kinyan shevisah

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם חָל יוֹם טוֹב בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת יָכוֹל לִקְנוֹת בּוֹ שְׁבִיתָה, וְאֵין בְּזֶה מִשּׁוּם הֲכָנָה.
Even if Yom Tov falls on Friday, one may be koneh his shevisah on the ship that day for Shabbos — there is no issur of hachanah. But a shevisah acquired on erev Yom Tov is not effective for the Shabbos that follows, and vice versa. For the two days of Yom Tov of galuyos, one must renew; for Rosh Hashanah, one kinyan suffices for both days.
Application: today this seif sheds light on travel by ship (or plane) where Yom Tov is followed immediately by Shabbos. The technical rule of kinyan shevisah must be renewed for each distinct sacred period.

Seif Yud-Alef (11) — River: ship pulled by animals on the bank

וְאַף אִם מוֹלִיכִים אוֹתָהּ עַל יְדֵי בְּהֵמוֹת הַמְהַלְּכִים בִּשְׂפַת הַנָּהָר — אֵין בְּכָךְ כְּלוּם, וְאֵינוֹ דוֹמֶה לְקָרוֹן… שֶׁהַיּוֹשֵׁב בִּסְפִינָה רָחוֹק הוּא מִבְּהֵמוֹת הַמְהַלְּכוֹת בִּשְׂפַת הַנָּהָר.
Even if the ship is pulled by animals walking on the riverbank — no problem, and it is not comparable to a wagon (karon) pulled by animals, which is assur even within the tchum (gezeirah lest one cut a branch to drive the animal). On the ship, one is far from the animals (long ropes), so one will not come to drive them.

Seif Yud-Beis (12) — Minhag: trade and visiting a friend = devar mitzvah

וּבְמָקוֹם שֶׁנָּהֲגוּ לְהַפְלִיג אַף בְּיַמִּים הַמְּלוּחִים… אִם אֵינָם הוֹלְכִים לְטִיּוּל אֶלָּא לִסְחוֹרָה, אוֹ לִרְאוֹת פְּנֵי חֲבֵרֵיהֶם — אֵין לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדָם, לְפִי שֶׁיֵּשׁ מִי שֶׁאוֹמֵר שֶׁכָּל מַה שֶּׁהָאָדָם הוֹלֵךְ לִסְחוֹרָה… חָשׁוּב הַכֹּל דְּבַר מִצְוָה.
In a place where the minhag is to set sail even in salt seas, for trade or to visit a friend (but not for pure pleasure) — one does not stop them, since some hold that all this is devar mitzvah. Only purely touristic travel remains devar reshus.
The SHHaRav adopts here the extension of R. Tam / Rema as a valid minhag not to be contested. The practical consequence: almost all modern travel falls into devar mitzvah. Only pure tourism remains under the strict issur.

Seif Yud-Gimel (13) — Caravan in the wilderness + Aliyah

הַיּוֹצְאִים בְּשַׁיָּרָא בַּמִּדְבָּר וְהַכֹּל יוֹדְעִים שֶׁהֵם צְרִיכִים לְחַלֵּל שַׁבָּת… אֲסוּרִים לָצֵאת תּוֹךְ ג' יָמִים לִפְנֵי הַשַּׁבָּת… אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן הוֹלְכִים לִדְבַר מִצְוָה, כְּגוֹן לַעֲלוֹת לְאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל.
Those who set out in a caravan into the wilderness, knowing they will need to be mechalel Shabbos due to the danger of being left alone — it is assur to depart within the last three days. Except for a mitzvah such as aliyah: one may depart even on Friday. Lechatchilah one stipulates with the group that they will stop on Shabbos; if they refuse once in the wilderness, one may continue even beyond 12 mil if one fears wild beasts, robbers, theft of goods or of clothing in the cold season — all these are pikuach nefesh.
The SHHaRav brings a major nuance here: some (yesh omrim) forbid even on Sunday for a caravan where chillul is certain — they restrict the mitzvah heter to cases where chillul is only probable. The SHHaRav recommends stringency (יש להחמיר) but does not censure the lenient ones. Aliyah for trade alone: this too is devar mitzvah according to some (4 amos in Eretz Yisrael = ben olam haba).

Seif Yud-Daled (14) — Bedieved: already departed, what to do?

וְכָל זֶה לְכַתְּחִלָּה, אֲבָל בְּדִיעֲבַד שֶׁכְּבָר יָצָא בְּשַׁיָּרָא אֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, וַאֲפִלּוּ לְטַיֵּל, וְהִגִּיעַ לְמָקוֹם שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ חֲשַׁשׁ סַכָּנָה לְהִפָּרֵד מֵהַשַּׁיָּרָא — יָכוֹל לָלֶכֶת עִמָּהֶם בְּשַׁבָּת.
All this is lechatchilah. But bedieved, if one already departed in a caravan (even on Friday, even for pleasure) and arrived at a place where parting from the group would be dangerous — one may continue with them on Shabbos until a safe place. There he will rest. Even close to a city, he has only 4 amos (law of one who went outside the tchum). But if he departed out of halachic ignorance (thinking it was permitted), he is granted 2000 amos in every direction — like one who goes out to save lives.
Three levels of bedieved: (1) departed willfully in violation → 4 amos only; (2) departed out of halachic ignorance → 2000 amos in all directions; (3) departed within a heter (mitzvah or before three days) → full 2000 amos, no limit. The halachah distinguishes between will and mistake.
Summary of the additional seifim (ה–יד): they elaborate the fine mechanics of the three-day rule — who can enter, when, into what ship, with what potential chillul, and what to do in case of error. They transform a general principle into a complete halachic code covering all cases of travel before and on Shabbos.

8. The Rema's position — what changes in practice

The Rema adds 4 crucial elements:

Element 1 — Animal-powered ships before three days

The Rema permits boarding before the three days on a ship pulled by animals, even in shallow water, and even if the Jew will have to physically help on Shabbat.

Element 2 — Rivers within the three days

On rivers: no problem for the three days, provided the Jew has no melacha to perform.

Element 3 — Custom of Kiddush on the ship

Making Kiddush on the ship, sleeping on land, returning to set sail on Shabbat. "One should not protest, as they have whom to rely upon."

Element 4 — R. Tam's extension (the most important)

Practical consequence: in mainstream Ashkenazic practice, almost all travel is devar mitzvah. The three-day rule applies strictly only to pure tourism.

9. Modern practical cases

Case 1 — Long-haul flight Friday into Saturday

Case: Paris → New York flight Friday 14:00, landing NY 17:00 local (but Shabbat in Paris at 18:00, so the plane flies during Shabbat Paris time).
Conduct: permitted if devar mitzvah. For halachah lema'aseh, consult one's Rav, especially if the flight involves Shabbat violations onboard.

Case 2 — Mediterranean cruise Thursday-Sunday

Case: 4-day cruise (Thursday → Sunday), boarding Thursday evening.
Conduct: problematic. The leisure cruise falls precisely into the case the siman intends to prohibit. Board at least 3 days earlier (Tuesday) or consult a Rav.

Case 3 — Overnight train Thursday evening → Friday morning

Case: overnight train Thursday 22:00 → Friday 08:00.
Conduct: permitted without concern. The overnight train is not within the scope of siman רמ״ח.

Case 4 — Business travel abroad during Shabbat

Case: businessman traveling to see a client, flight Friday 16:00, return Sunday.
Conduct: per the Rema, permitted (parnassah = mitzvah). Per strict Mechaber, more problematic.

Case 5 — Aliyah to Eretz Yisrael on a Friday

Case: aliyah, flight Friday 11:00, landing Tel Aviv 17:00.
Conduct: permitted without any doubt. Model case of Mechaber Seif Daled.

10. Practical synthesis of the Siman

The 4 teachings of Siman רמ״ח:
  1. Basic rule: do not embark less than three days before Shabbat for leisure travel
  2. Mitzvah exception: permitted even on Friday with stipulation (Rema broadens "mitzvah" widely)
  3. Short distance: if one can arrive before Shabbat → permitted
  4. קנה שביתה: boarding the ship before Shabbat with a meaningful act → permitted thereafter

The 5 practical commandments of Siman רמ״ח

  1. Distinguish mitzvah / reshus — per Rema, almost all travel is mitzvah except pure tourism
  2. Observe the three-day rule for clearly non-mitzvah travel
  3. Honor Shabbat onboard: kiddush, davening, dignified meals
  4. Do not put oneself into a position of Shabbat violation voluntarily (except pikuach nefesh)
  5. Consult one's Rav for borderline cases
