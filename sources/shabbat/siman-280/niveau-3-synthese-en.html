1. The central axiom

Siman 280 in one sentence.
Marital relations are part of the taanugei Shabbos (delights of Shabbos) — not merely permitted but encouraged. The onah of talmidei chachamim is ideally Shabbos night. No issue of choveil (bi'as besulah = pesach sasum) and no issue of tza'ar (natural pain, not caused).

3. Hierarchy of rules

Encouraged: tashmish ha-mittah on Shabbos night — mitzvah + oneg.
↓
Standard: healthy couple. Onah of a talmid chacham = ideally Shabbos night.
↓
Adaptation: couples with fragile health — according to their strength.
↓
Permitted even in borderline cases: bi'as besulah on Shabbos — no choveil, no tza'ar (s.2).

5. Bi'as besulah on Shabbos — פֶּתַח סָתוּם — the heart of the siman

The first seif — that tashmish ha-mittah is part of oneg — raises no difficulty. The real crux of Siman 280 is seif 2: why does the first union of a couple, on Shabbos night, raise no problem? Two melachos seem to be at issue, and the siman explains why neither applies.

First objection — חוֹבֵל. Bi'as besulah is accompanied by bleeding. Causing bleeding falls in principle under חוֹבֵל, a melachah of Shabbos. The siman's response: it is a פֶּתַח סָתוּם — the opening of a natural passage, already existing though closed. Nothing is "torn off" and no wound is "created": one opens what was meant to be opened. This is therefore not the חוֹבֵל the Torah forbade.
↓
Second objection — צַעַר. The union may be painful. But the pain here is not caused as a deliberate act: it is a צַעַר נַעֲשֶׂה, a natural pain that accompanies a permitted and desired act. Suffering that is not intentional, an inevitable consequence of a mitzvah, is not a "forbidden tza'ar."
The borderline case not to confuse. The central pitfall of the siman: to think the Mechaber only "tolerates bediavad" bi'as besulah on Shabbos night. False — he permits it לְכַתְּחִילָּה, fully and at the outset, since both objections fall away on analysis. Conversely, do not confuse the prohibitions of this siman with those of נִדָּה: a niddah remains forbidden on Shabbos night — but that is a Yoreh Deah prohibition, foreign to Shabbos, and in no way contradicts the principle of Siman 280.
In practice. Tashmish ha-mittah is a תַּעֲנוּג of Shabbos, encouraged — to defer out of false "reverence" for the day is to miss the mitzvah. The עוֹנָה of talmidei chachamim is ideally Shabbos night. The first union of a young couple on Shabbos night is permitted without reservation. For the SA HaRav and Chabad practice: like the Mechaber, with a kabbalistic dimension — see Level 4.

6. Mnemonic — T.O.P.

T — Tashmish ha-mittah = oneg Shabbat (s.1).

O — Onah of a talmid chacham = Shabbos night (Kesubos 62b).

P — Pesach sasum: bi'as besulah = natural opening, not choveil (s.2).

7. The 3 pitfalls to avoid

Pitfall 1 — Deferring out of "respect" for Shabbos. Error. Tashmish is part of the oneg. Deferring = missing the mitzvah.
Pitfall 2 — Hesitating with a besulah on Shabbos night (wedding case). Error. The Mechaber permits it "lechatchilah" (s.2).
Pitfall 3 — Confusing niddah prohibitions with Shabbos prohibitions. Siman 280 does NOT concern niddah questions — these follow their own framework (Yoreh Deah).

10. The 3 practical directives of Siman 280

💍 The rule "tashmish ha-mittah on Shabbos" — in 3 directives

  1. Tashmish = oneg Shabbat — encouraged for healthy couples.
  2. Onah of a talmid chacham: Shabbos night ideal.
  3. Bi'as besulah on Shabbos permitted — no choveil, no tza'ar (s.2).

→ Niddah, contraception, etc.: their own simanim (Yoreh Deah).
