1. The central axiom

Siman שפ״ב in one sentence.
The non-Jew who lives in the courtyard forbids the Jews to carry only when two Jews live in two houses and forbid each other: « הדר עם העכו״ם בחצר אינו אוסר עליו עד שיהיו שני ישראלים דרים בשני בתים ואוסרים זה על זה אז העכו״ם אוסר עליהם » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ב:א); and then his renunciation does not help, his domain must be rented from him: « ואינו מועיל שיבטל העכו״ם רשותו אלא צריך שישכירו ממנו » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ב:א). The reason, according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק א): by strict law his dwelling is not a dwelling; the Sages decreed that it forbid, so that one not live with him and learn from his deeds, and only in the common case, where several Jews live. The Shulchan Aruch HaRav opens the siman with « דִּירַת נָכְרִי כְּדִירַת בְּהֵמָה » (שו״ע הרב שפ״ב:א) and gives the same concern, that one might learn from his deeds (seif 1). The rest of the siman says how the rental is made, how long it holds and from whom it is taken, and then which arrangements of courtyards and houses make him forbid.

3. The course of the siman — from the non-Jew's dwelling to the eruv through windows

Seifim א and ב — when he forbids: two Jews in two houses who forbid each other; his renunciation does not help, one must rent (seif א, with three cases in the Rema's gloss); renouncing in favor of one of the Jews, so that he be alone with him, does not help (seif ב).
↓
Seif ג — without a right of passage: he does not forbid; two courtyards open to each other, the non-Jew in one, two Jews or more in the other: one carries through the window between them, without renting; when both courtyards open onto one alley where the Jews forbid one another (two courtyards of Jews), the commentators diverge (section 5).
↓
Seifim ד to ט — the rental: unspecified and without writing (ד); even for less than a perutah, and, in small print, on Shabbos (ה); as long as the non-Jew does not retract (ו; section 5); for a fixed period, when it ends one rents again and makes the eruv again (ז); if he rents his dwelling to another within the period, the first rental suffices (ח); one rents on behalf of all (ט).
↓
Seifim י to יד — from whom: not against his will (י); from his wife, his hired or seasonal worker, even if he protests (יא; on renting despite the householder's protest, the commentators diverge: section 5); if he refuses, two views (יב; section 5); an assigned place does not help (יג); the dismissed hired worker, and, in the Rema's gloss, the king's treasurer (יד).
↓
Seifim טו and טז — one house: a Jew and a non-Jew in one house (טו, with the Rema's gloss; section 5); Jewish hired workers living in the non-Jew's house (טז).
↓
Seifim יז and יח — the courtyards and the non-Jew who rented out: two courtyards, one inside the other (יז; section 5); a non-Jew who rented his house to another non-Jew (יח, with the Rema's gloss).
↓
Seifim יט and כ — windows and doorway: the house of another Jew, adjoining, not opening onto the courtyard where a Jew and a non-Jew live: an eruv through the window to take one's utensils out through the neighbor's house (יט; section 5); in a courtyard where Jews and a non-Jew live, an eruv made through the windows does not permit carrying through the doorways, because of the non-Jew, until there is a rental (כ); the ship, in the Rema's gloss.

4. Decision tree — must one rent his domain, and from whom?

Q1: Do two Jews live in two houses and forbid each other? (seif א)
↓ No → the non-Jew does not forbid them (seif א); but, according to the Rama's gloss on seif א, a hold (tefisah) in a house lent or rented to another Jew does not exempt from renting. Yes → Q2. For one house, see seif טו; for two courtyards one inside the other, seif יז and section 5.
Q2: Does the non-Jew have a right of passage over them? (seif ג)
↓ No → he does not forbid (seif ג); two courtyards on one alley where the Jews forbid one another (two courtyards of Jews): section 5. Yes → his domain must be rented, except for the Jew who rented or lent him his house: he does not forbid him (the Rama's gloss on seif א); his renunciation does not help (seif א), nor does the Jews' renunciation in favor of one of them (seif ב).
Q3: How does one rent, and for how long? (seifim ד to ט)
↓ An unspecified rental suffices (seif ד), even for less than a perutah (seif ה); it works as long as the non-Jew does not retract (seif ו; section 5); for a fixed period, when it ends, rent again and make the eruv again (seif ז); if he rents his dwelling to another within the period, the first rental suffices (seif ח; for his death or a sale, section 5); one rents on behalf of all (seif ט).
Q4: Does the non-Jew refuse to rent it out? (seifim י to יג)
↓ Yes → not against his will (seif י); from his wife, his hired or seasonal worker, even if he protests (seif יא; on renting despite the householder's protest, the commentators diverge: section 5); or through being lent the right to put something there, according to the two views of seif יב (section 5); a place assigned in the house he lends does not help (seif יג).
→ At the slightest doubt: consult your Rav.

7. Pitfalls to avoid

Pitfall 1: Thinking that the non-Jew forbids as soon as a Jew lives in the courtyard with him. Seif א: « הדר עם העכו״ם בחצר אינו אוסר עליו עד שיהיו שני ישראלים דרים בשני בתים ואוסרים זה על זה אז העכו״ם אוסר עליהם » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ב:א). For two courtyards one inside the other, see seif יז and section 5.
Pitfall 2: Thinking that renunciation is enough. The non-Jew's does not help: « ואינו מועיל שיבטל העכו״ם רשותו אלא צריך שישכירו ממנו » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ב:א); nor does the Jews' renunciation in favor of one of them: « אם בטלו הישראלים רשותם לאחד מהם כדי שיחשב כיחידי אצל העכו״ם אינו מועיל » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ב:ב).
Pitfall 3: Thinking that, since one may rent on Shabbos, everything is done then as on a weekday. The small-print passage of seif ה permits renting on Shabbos; according to the Mishnah Berurah, one gives him food or an object that may be moved, and money may not be moved; referring to siman 383, it writes that the eruv is then void, and that the residents must renounce their domain in favor of one of them (ס״ק כ״ב). The Shulchan Aruch HaRav, which also permits renting on Shabbos, writes: « אֲבָל לִקְנוֹת רְשׁוּת בְּשַׁבָּת — אָסוּר, אַף שֶׁאֵינוֹ קוֹנֶה אֶלָּא לְהַתִּיר טִלְטוּל, וַאֲפִלּוּ בְּמַתְּנַת חִנָּם » (שו״ע הרב שפ״ב:ח).
Pitfall 4: Thinking that at the end of a rental for a fixed period it is enough to rent again. Seif ז also requires making the eruv again: « אם שכרו מהעכו״ם לזמן ידוע לכשיכלה הזמן צריך לחזור ולשכור שנית וצריך לחזור ולערב דאין עירוב ראשון חוזר וניעור » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ב:ז).
Pitfall 5: Thinking that one may rent against his will. Seif י: « אם שכרו ממנו בעל כרחו אינו מועיל אף ע״פ שהיה רגיל להשכיר מקודם » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ב:י). But from his wife, his hired or seasonal worker, one rents even if he protests (seif יא; on renting despite the householder's protest, the commentators diverge: section 5).
Pitfall 6: Thinking that an eruv made through the windows permits carrying through the doorways. Seif כ: « אף ע״פ שהם מותרים להוציא מבית לבית דרך חלונות הרי הם אסורים להוציא מבית לבית דרך פתחים מפני העכו״ם עד שישכיר » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ב:כ).
Pitfall 7: Mixing the two numberings. Levels 1 to 3 follow the Shulchan Aruch of the Mechaber (20 seifim); Level 4 follows the Shulchan Aruch HaRav (23 seifim in the text Sefaria serves). The Rav presents the Mechaber's seif טו before seif יד, and seif יח before seif יז (Level 4, introduction).

10. The practical commandments of Siman שפ״ב

For daily conduct

  1. When he forbids: the non-Jew who lives in the courtyard forbids only if two Jews live in two houses and forbid each other; his renunciation does not help, his domain must be rented (seif א). The Rema's gloss: a Jew who rented or lent his house to a non-Jew — the non-Jew does not forbid him; a house belonging to the non-Jew which a Jew rented from him, the non-Jew living in it with him — that rental does not count as the rental for the eruv; a hold in a house lent or rented to one's fellow does not exempt from renting from the non-Jew. Renouncing in favor of one of the Jews, so that he be alone with him, does not help (seif ב).
  2. Without a right of passage over the Jews, he does not forbid: two courtyards open to each other, the non-Jew in one, two Jews or more in the other — one carries from courtyard to courtyard through the window between them, without renting (seif ג; according to the Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ז, this concerns objects that spent Shabbos in the houses, and the Jews made an eruv among themselves). The Shulchan Aruch HaRav excepts the case where they wish to partner in the alley (seif 5; Level 4, point א). When both courtyards open onto one alley where the Jews forbid one another (two courtyards of Jews), the commentators diverge (section 5).
  3. The rental: unspecified and without writing (seif ד); even for less than a perutah, and, in small print, it is permitted to rent on Shabbos (seif ה; pitfall 3); one rents on behalf of all the residents (seif ט).
  4. Its duration: it works as long as the non-Jew does not retract (seif ו; section 5); for a fixed period, when it ends, rent again and make the eruv again (seif ז); if he rents his dwelling to another within the period, the first rental suffices (seif ח; for his death or a sale, section 5).
  5. From whom: not against his will (seif י); from his wife, his hired or seasonal worker, even if he protests (seif יא; section 5); if he refuses, the two views of seif יב (section 5); a place assigned in the house he lends does not help (seif יג); from his hired worker, a rental for a period holds until its end even if he is dismissed, an unspecified rental is void once he is, and likewise, in the Rema's gloss, for the king's treasurer removed entirely; removed only from the treasury, if he still eats the king's allowance, he is still his hired worker (seif יד).
  6. One house: a Jew and a non-Jew — rent from the non-Jew and make the eruv with the Jew, and the Rema's gloss (seif טו; section 5); Jewish hired or seasonal workers living in the non-Jew's house do not forbid one another (seif טז).
  7. Two courtyards, one inside the other: he forbids in the cases of the seif, and one view goes further (seif יז; section 5).
  8. The non-Jew who rented his house to another non-Jew: one rents from him if he keeps a hold in the house, or if he can remove the lessee within his term; otherwise, from the lessee (seif יח). The Rema's gloss: a person who has a hold in the house — one may rent from him (the Mishnah Berurah reads איש אחר, ס״ק ס״ג).
  9. Windows and doorway: the house of another Jew, adjoining, not opening onto the courtyard where a Jew and a non-Jew live, and connected by windows — no eruv through the window to take one's utensils out through the neighbor's house; with a doorway open between them, it is permitted, and one view forbids even then (seif יט; section 5). In a courtyard where Jews and a non-Jew live, an eruv made through the windows permits carrying through the windows, not through the doorways, because of the non-Jew, until there is a rental (seif כ). The Rema's gloss: the ship on which Jews have separate quarters — make an eruv and rent from the non-Jew who owns the ship, or include it in the fare; the Shulchan Aruch HaRav adds a condition and requires that it be included explicitly (seif 23; Level 4, point ח).
  10. In case of doubt — consult your Rav. In-depth pilpul — Level 2; Chabad shitah — Level 4.
