דעת הרב

Daat HaRav — The Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe on Siman ש"ה

The shittah of the Alter Rebbe on his 38 seifim codifying what an animal (behemah) may go out with on Shabbos — distinction between adequate guarding and excessive guarding, milking by a non-Jew, entrusting to a non-Jewish shepherd, tza'ar ba'alei chaim.

Why a Level 4 dedicated to the Alter Rebbe? The Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe is not a commentary on the Mechaber — it is a complete, freestanding Shulchan Aruch, written by the Alter Rebbe. Its singularity: it combines halachah + ta'amei hamitzvos + inner dimension in one work, and it rules with unique talmudic rigor.

For Chabad, the Alter Rebbe is the final posek. This page gathers, for siman ש"ה, the full text of the Rav, his chiddush, and the divrei haRebbe that illuminate the topic.

שלחן ערוך הרב — סימן ש"ה

The Full Text of the Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe (שולחן ערוך הרב)

סימן ש"ה — בַּמֶּה בְּהֵמָה יוֹצֵאת בְּשַׁבָּת — וּבוֹ לח סְעִיפִים
Source: Kehot edition, as reproduced on Sefaria. 38 seifim + 2 entries of Kuntres Acharon.

סעיף א בַּמֶּה בְּהֵמָה יוֹצֵאת בְּשַׁבָּת וּבוֹ ל"ח סְעִיפִים כְּבָר נִתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ו…

בַּמֶּה בְּהֵמָה יוֹצֵאת בְּשַׁבָּת וּבוֹ ל"ח סְעִיפִים

כְּבָר נִתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ו שֶׁאָדם מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתַת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ מִן הַתּוֹרָה, וְאִם בְּהֶמְתּוֹ הוֹצִיאָה אֵיזֶה דָבָר לִרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים אַף שֶׁיָּצָאת מֵאֵלֶיהָ — עָבַר הוּא עַל מִצְוַת עֲשֵׂה שֶׁל תּוֹרָה.

אֶלָּא שֶׁיֵּשׁ מִקְצָת דְּבָרִים שֶׁדֶּרֶךְ הַבְּהֵמָה לָצֵאת בָּהֶם בְּחֹל אַף כְּשֶׁאֵינָהּ נוֹשֵׂאת מַשּׂוֹי, וּדְבָרִים אֵלּוּ חֲשׁוּבִים לָהּ תַּכְשִׁיט, וּמֻתָּר שֶׁתֵּצֵא בָּהֶם לִרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים בְּשַׁבָּת. וְאֵלּוּ הֵם: כָּל דָּבָר שֶׁהַבְּהֵמָה מִשְׁתַּמֶּרֶת עַל יָדוֹ שֶׁלֹּא תִבְרַח כְּשֶׁהוֹלֶכֶת בִּרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים, כְּגוֹן רֶסֶן וְכַיּוֹצֵא בוֹ. וְאַף שֶׁאֶפְשָׁר לְשָׁמְרָהּ בִּשְׁמִירָה פְּחוּתָה מִזּוֹ מְעַט — מֻתָּר שֶׁתֵּצֵא בִּשְׁמִירָה זוֹ. אֲבָל אִם דַּי לָהּ בִּשְׁמִירָה פְּחוּתָה מִזּוֹ הַרְבֵּה, שֶׁנִּמְצֵאת שְׁמִירָה זוֹ יְתֵרָה הַרְבֵּה — הֲרֵי הִיא מַשּׂוֹי לָהּ.

With what may an animal go out on Shabbos — containing 38 seifim:

It has already been explained in siman 246 (רמ"ו) that a person is commanded regarding shevisas beheimto (the rest of his animal) from the Torah; and if his animal carried out some item to a reshus harabim (public domain) even if it went out on its own — he has transgressed a positive mitzvah of the Torah.

However, there are certain items in which it is the way of the animal to go out during the week even when not carrying a load, and these items are considered for it a tachshit (adornment) — and it is permitted for the animal to go out with them to a reshus harabim on Shabbos. These are: any item by which the animal is guarded so that it not flee while walking in the reshus harabim — such as a bridle (resen) and the like. And even though it could be guarded with slightly lesser guarding, it is permitted that it go out with this guarding. But if much lesser guarding would suffice, so that this guarding is far in excess — then it constitutes a load (masoy) for it.

סעיף ב לְפִיכָךְ, הַנָּאקָה, וְהִיא גַמְלָא נְקֵבָה גְּדוֹלָה וּלְבָנָה, יוֹצֵאת בַּחֲטָם…

לְפִיכָךְ, הַנָּאקָה, וְהִיא גַמְלָא נְקֵבָה גְּדוֹלָה וּלְבָנָה, יוֹצֵאת בַּחֲטָם, וְהוּא טַבַּעַת שֶׁל בַּרְזֶל שֶׁנּוֹקְבִין חָטְמָהּ וּמַכְנִיסִין לָהּ הַטַּבַּעַת, אֲבָל אֵינָהּ יוֹצֵאת בְּאַפְסָר. וְהַסּוּס שֶׁלָּנוּ — יוֹצֵא בֵּין בְּאַפְסָר בֵּין בִּפְרֻמְבְּיָא. וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, לֹא יֵצְאוּ בִּשְׁנֵיהֶם יַחַד. אֲבָל הַפֶּרֶד וְהַגָּמָל וְהַחֲמוֹר יוֹצְאִין בְּאַפְסָר אֲבָל לֹא בִּפְרֻמְבְּיָא.

Therefore, the nakah — which is a large white female camel — goes out with a chatam (nose-ring of iron driven through its nostril), but does not go out with a halter (afsar), since it is not properly restrained by it and the halter would constitute a load. Our horse goes out with either an afsar or a prumbiya (iron bit). However, the two shall not be used together — only one at a time. But a mule, a camel, and a donkey go out with an afsar, but not with a prumbiya (because for them the prumbiya is far more guarding than they need, and is not their usual way).

סעיף ג-לח Continuation of all seifim — wrapping the halter rope, carrying the halter, rope in the horse's mouth, rebellious animal, ba'alei hashir, rams/ewes/goats, mardaas and saddle, taraskal, fox tail and zehoris, bell, seal, metulteles, akud and ragul, tying camels, leading with rope, neck board and leg strap, kameia mumcheh, courtyard and karmelis, riding, sides of an animal, fallen animal, milking by non-Jew, cheese by maids, non-Jewish boy, non-Jewish shepherd, animal techumin…

סעיפים ג–לח של שולחן ערוך הרב על סימן ש"ה ממשיכים את הניתוח של במה בהמה יוצאה — דרך הסעיפים העוסקים בכריכת חבל האפסר לנוי (ג), טלטול האפסר (ד), חבל בפי הסוס (ה), בהמה שעסקיה רעים (ו), בעלי השיר עם אצעדה (ז), אלים לבובים ורחלות שחוזות ועזים צרורות (ח), מרדעת לחמור (ט), אוכף (י), הנחת מרדעת בחצר (יא), טרסקל (יב), זנב שועל וזהורית ודינים נלווים (יג), זוג פקוק (יד), חותם (טו), מטולטלת לגמל (טז), עקוד ורגול (יז), קשירת גמלים זה אחר זה (יח), משיכה בחבל ראוי (יט), סולם ורצועה ברגל (כ), קמיע מומחה לבהמה (כא), חצר וכרמלית והבדלי דין מאדם (כב), רכיבה וצידי בהמה ובהמה נושאה את עצמה (כג), פריקת משא משום צער בעלי חיים (כד), זהירות בפריקה (כה), בהמה שנפלה לאמת המים (כו), המראת אווזים (כז), מלאכת דש והחולב (כח), אמירה לגוי לחלוב (כט), גוי שחלב מעצמו (ל), עבדו ושפחתו של ישראל (לא), אכילת החלב בשבת (לב), [סעיף לג חסר], גבינות השפחות (לד), נער עכו"ם הרוכב (לה), מסירה לרועה גוי (לו), תחומי בהמה (לז), עד לסעיף לח החותם במסירה לרועה ושביתת הבהמה.

Seifim 3–38 of the Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe on siman ש"ה continue the analysis of "with what may an animal go out" — through seifim treating: winding the halter rope around the neck as adornment (3); carrying (tiltul) the halter (4); a rope tied to the horse's mouth as a load (5); an animal with asakeha ra'im (a tendency to flee) permitted extra guarding (6); the ba'alei hashir — hunting dogs and small wild animals with a collar-ring (7); rams going out levuvim, ewes shechuzos / kevulos / kevunos, and goats tzeruros (8); the donkey going out with a mardaas (saddle-blanket for warmth) since it is by nature cold-bodied (9); not with an uchaf (saddle) which does not warm (10); placing a mardaas on the donkey in a courtyard against the cold (11); not hanging a traskal (feedbag) on the donkey for mere pleasure (12); not the horse with a fox-tail or zehoris (red thread between the eyes against the evil eye), nor goats with udder-bag, nor a cow with a mouth-muzzle, nor any animal with a foot-sandal — yet permitted with a wound-bandage or with splints over a broken bone (13); a bell stuffed with wool so it does not ring — permitted in a courtyard but not in reshus harabim because of mar'is ayin (it appears one is leading the animal to market) (14); not with a chosam (seal), whether on the neck or woven into the garment (15); not the camel with a metulteles (small cushion under the tail) unless tied to both tail and hump (16); not akud (foreleg tied to hindleg) nor ragul (one leg bent and tied up) (17); not tying camels one after the other and leading them as a string (it appears as bringing them to market) (18); one who leads his animal by the halter rope must take care that the end of the rope not extend below his hand a tefach, and the rope between his hand and the animal must be a tefach above the ground (19); not the donkey with a sulam (neck-board), nor with a leg-strap, nor chickens with foot-cords or short straps tying their legs together, nor rams with a small wagon under their tail, nor goats with a stick in the nose, nor a calf with a small yoke, nor a calf with a zemam (mouth-piece preventing it from suckling), nor a cow with a shrew-hide on the udders, nor a cow with a strap between its horns (whether for restraint or for adornment, since it is not the universal practice) — and similarly oxen and cows shall not go out with a rope on their necks, but calves may (because they are easily rebellious and need guarding) (20); the animal goes out with a kameia mumcheh (an amulet proven effective for animals — not merely for people, since people have a guiding angel whereas animals do not) (21); anything forbidden in reshus harabim because it is a load is forbidden in a karmelis according to all opinions, yet a courtyard is more lenient (22); one does not ride upon an animal, nor lean against it, nor make any use of ba'alei chayim — the Sages included all use of living creatures under the decree against riding (lest one cut a branch to drive the animal) — even from the sides of an animal, even from "sides of sides" only by way of an intermediate object (23); one who climbed onto an animal on Shabbos, even purposefully, must descend immediately because of tza'ar ba'alei chayim (the suffering of living creatures); and one unloads burdens from upon an animal because of tza'ar ba'alei chayim — even when the load is muktzeh — by inserting one's head beneath the load and tilting it so it falls of itself (24); one must take care when unloading not to lean on the animal's flanks (25); an animal that fell into a watercourse — if one can supply it with food in its place, one does so until the issue of Shabbos; if the water is too deep, one brings pillows and cushions and places them beneath it (the rule of bitul keli mei-heichano is set aside for tza'ar ba'alei chayim, which is from the Torah); but one may not lift the animal out with one's hands since it is muktzeh (although some permit this in case of great loss) — however, all agree one may instruct a non-Jew to lift it out (26); likewise it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to force-feed geese once a day because of tza'ar ba'alei chayim (27); dosh (threshing) is one of the avos melachos, and milking — separating milk from the udder — is a tolada of dosh, liable only when milking into an empty vessel; but milking into a vessel containing food is exempt biblically, though forbidden mi-divrei sofrim (28); it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to milk an animal on Shabbos because of tza'ar ba'alei chayim, provided the non-Jew take the milk for himself or sell it back for a small sum (29); a non-Jew who milked of his own accord — one may take the milk from him for free (30); however, one's own non-Jewish servant or maidservant hired for a year or two need not "buy" the milk back, since they act for their own benefit (their wages) (31); all this concerns drinking the milk after Shabbos — on Shabbos itself, milk produced on Shabbos is muktzeh under the decree of mashkin she-zavu (liquids that flowed) (32); [seif 33 missing]; cheeses made by maidservants on Shabbos from a Jew's milk in the Jew's house — even if of their own initiative, one must protest because of mar'is ayin (34); one whose non-Jewish lad rides his animal to water it need not prevent it, since "the living carries itself" and the Sages did not extend their decree to one's own animal — but one must prevent him from placing his garments on it (35); it is permitted to hand over a horse or donkey to a non-Jewish shepherd, and one need not fear he will use it for melachah on Shabbos, since he would be considered a thief if he did (36); the techum (Shabbos boundary) of one's animal: an animal that acquired Shabbos rest by the shepherd outside the city techum, and on Shabbos came inside the owner's techum — the owner may not lead it home with his hands, but may call it (37); one may entrust an animal to a shepherd even on Shabbos itself, although it had acquired Shabbos rest with its Jewish owner — since a person is not commanded regarding the techum of his animal but only its melachah-rest; some forbid this and it is good to be stringent (38).

קונטרס אחרון, אות א

במה דברים אמורים כשאמר כו'. אף שלפי דעת המג"א בסי' רע"ו שפסק כר' שמחה דבעושה משל ישראל צריך למחות אף שעושה מאליו, יש לומר דהכא נמי עכ"פ צריך לקנות החלב כי היכי דליעבד אדעתא דנפשיה. מכל מקום כיון שהמג"א מיקל כאן לגמרי, וכן משמע מלשון הש"ע וב"י שדעתו להקל, אין להחמיר כלל, כי בלאו הכי רבים החולקים על ר' שמחה כמו שנתבאר בסי' רנ"ב.

"In what does this apply — when he said etc." Even though, according to the Magen Avraham in siman 276 who rules like R. Simchah that when [a non-Jew] does work with Jewish property one must protest even when he acts on his own initiative — one might argue that here too at least one must "buy" the milk so that the non-Jew acts adata de-nafsheih (for his own benefit). Nevertheless, since the Magen Avraham is fully lenient here, and so it appears from the language of the Shulchan Aruch and Beis Yosef, who tend to leniency, there is no reason to be stringent at all — for in any case many disagree with R. Simchah, as explained in siman 252.

קונטרס אחרון, אות ב

בשבת עצמה כו'. מה שכתב המג"א כיון דאינה ראויה לאכילה הוי מוקצה צע"ג, דבהדיא אמרינן בריש ביצה שבת דעלמא תשתרי אי לא משום גזירה, וכן פסקו הרי"ף ורא"ש ושאר פוסקים. ומאי שנא חלב מביצה. ומשום משקין שזבו אין לאסור בלתוך האוכלין.

"On Shabbos itself, etc." What the Magen Avraham wrote — that since [the animal] is not fit for eating [on Shabbos] the milk is muktzeh — is highly questionable (tzarich iyun gadol): for it is stated explicitly at the beginning of Beitzah that a weekday Shabbos would be permitted were it not for the gezeirah, and so ruled the Rif, Rosh, and other poskim. What difference is there between milk and an egg? And as for the decree of mashkin she-zavu (liquids that flowed), one should not forbid milk drawn into food — for if so, even on Yom Tov it should be forbidden like an egg laid on Yom Tov according to R. Yehudah.

Source: Sefaria — Shulchan Arukh HaRav, Orach Chayim ש"ה.

The test of control vs load
