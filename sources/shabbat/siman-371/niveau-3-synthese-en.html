The seif numbers on this page are those of the Shulchan Aruch of the Mechaber (6 seifim). The Shulchan Aruch HaRav, studied in Level 4, also has 6 seifim, but divided differently: when it is cited, its name is given next to the number.

1. The central axiom

Siman שע״א in one sentence.
Does the house of a resident of the courtyard still forbid carrying to the other residents when he has left, died or is dying? The Mishnah Berurah gives the reason for seif א: « והטעם דדירה בלא בעלים לא שמה דירה ואינה אוסרת בחצר אף שלא נתנה חלק בעירוב » (משנה ברורה שע״א ס״ק א); the Shulchan Aruch HaRav opens its seif 1 with this principle: « דִּירָה בְּלֹא בְעָלִים אֵינָהּ דִּירָה וְאֵינָהּ אוֹסֶרֶת בְּחָצֵר » (שו״ע הרב שע״א:א). The siman then sets out the cases, each in its own seif: a resident who went to spend Shabbos elsewhere, Jew or non-Jew (seif א), one who changes his mind and returns (seif ב), a house inherited from someone from the marketplace (seif ג), a resident who died on Shabbos (seif ד), the convert (seif ה), and then the dying man, the minor and the guest (seif ו).

3. Structure of the siman — from leaving to death

Seif א — a resident who went to spend Shabbos elsewhere: a Jew who went to another courtyard, even an adjacent one, without intending to return home on Shabbos, does not forbid; a non-Jew, even if he went to another city, forbids until his place is rented from him, if it is within a day's walk — farther, he does not forbid. One view (וי״א): a non-Jew who went to another courtyard does not forbid; the notes in square brackets read « [וכן נראה עיקר] » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:א) and refer to siman 383 for a non-Jew who came on Shabbos.
↓
Seif ב — one who changes his mind: a Jew who left without intending to return, and changed his mind and returned on Shabbos, does not forbid.
↓
Seif ג — a house inherited from someone from the marketplace: if he died by day, the one who acquires it does not forbid, his eruv also permitting what he inherited; if he died after nightfall, he forbids, even though he made the eruv with them — the case, according to the Mishnah Berurah, is one where the deceased had not made the eruv for that house (ס״ק ט).
↓
Seif ד — a resident who died on Shabbos: if he had made the eruv, he does not forbid, even if someone from the marketplace inherits from him; otherwise, it depends on the heir. If he died by day, an heir from the marketplace forbids, provided he came to the house on Shabbos.
↓
Seif ה — the convert: if he died by day, another Jew who takes possession of his property forbids, even if he did so only after nightfall, until he renounces his domain; if he died after nightfall, he does not forbid — the case, according to the Mishnah Berurah, is one where they had made the eruv together (ס״ק כ״ו).
↓
Seif ו — the dying man, the minor, the guest: the dying man forbids until bread is transferred to him and the eruv is made on his behalf; likewise the minor, even though he cannot eat a kezayis; but the guest does not forbid (siman 370).

4. Decision tree — does the resident's house forbid?

Q1: Did the resident go to spend Shabbos elsewhere? (seifim א and ב)
↓ A Jew, without intending to return on Shabbos → he does not forbid (seif א) — according to the Mishnah Berurah, having left with his whole household while it was still day (ס״ק ב); if he changes his mind and returns on Shabbos, he does not forbid (seif ב), and the Biur Halachah concludes on this case that one should be stringent, except in a pressing situation; the Shulchan Aruch HaRav (seif 1) writes that one who left after nightfall forbids when he returns. A non-Jew → according to the first view of the seif, he forbids until his place is rented, if the city is within a day's walk; farther, he does not forbid; according to the view introduced by וי״א, which the brackets adopt, even if he went to another courtyard he does not forbid; the Mishnah Berurah refers to the Biur Halachah (ס״ק ו), which writes in the name of several poskim that one is lenient for a non-Jew only if he went to another city, not to another courtyard; for another city as for another courtyard, consult your Rav (seif א).
Q2: Did he die? (seifim ג, ד and ה)
↓ YES → the ruling depends, among other things, on the time of death, on the deceased's eruv and on who inherits or takes possession: see section 5.
Q3: Is he dying, or a minor? (seif ו)
↓ YES → the dying man forbids until bread is transferred to him and the eruv is made on his behalf; likewise the minor. A guest, however, does not forbid (seif ו; siman 370).
→ At the slightest doubt: consult your Rav.

5. By day or after nightfall — seifim ג, ד and ה

Seifim ג, ד and ה distinguish a death that occurred while it was still day (מבעוד יום) from a death after nightfall (משחשיכה) or on Shabbos. summary: the table condenses these seifim; the full text and its translation are in Level 1.

SeifCaseDied while it was still dayDied after nightfall
גThe house of someone from the marketplace, left to a resident of the courtyard; according to the Mishnah Berurah, he had not made the eruv for that house (ס״ק ט)The one who acquires it does not forbid: his eruv also permits what he inherited, even if he made it before the death (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״א)He forbids, even though he made the eruv with them: at twilight the house was not his (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ד)
דA resident of the courtyard (the seif first deals with death on Shabbos; death by day comes only at its end)An heir from the marketplace forbids, provided he came to the house on Shabbos; here the seif does not distinguish whether the deceased had made the eruv: when he dies by day, his eruv is annulled (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ג)Died on Shabbos: if he had made the eruv, he does not forbid, even if someone from the marketplace inherits. Otherwise, an heir from the marketplace forbids if he comes to live in the house on Shabbos (in these two cases, according to the Mishnah Berurah, the deceased had not left any household members in the house, ס״ק ט״ז, י״ז); an heir from the courtyard who had made the eruv does not forbid if he lived with the deceased, and forbids if he lived in another house in the courtyard
הThe convert, who lived in the courtyard with a Jew; according to the Mishnah Berurah, they had made the eruv together (ס״ק כ״ו)Another Jew who takes possession of his property forbids, even if he did so only after nightfall, until he renounces his domain; if it is the Jew living there who took possession, it is permitted in every case, since he remains alone in his courtyard (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ז)The one who takes possession does not forbid: « שבהיתר הראשון עומד » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:ה)
What the Shulchan Aruch HaRav adds on these cases (its own numbering, Level 4):
  • Seif 2 (the Mechaber's seif ג): if he died after nightfall, the heir himself may not carry either, « וְגַם הוּא אָסוּר לְטַלְטֵל בֶּחָצֵר, בֵּין מִבַּיִת שֶׁלּוֹ בֵּין מִבַּיִת זֶה » (שו״ע הרב שע״א:ב), and he forbids even if he has not yet entered the house — which the Mishnah Berurah, later than the Rav, also writes for this seif (ס״ק י״ב); the Rosh, reported by the Beis Yosef, writes it with the same reason for an heir who lives in another house in the courtyard, the case of the Mechaber's seif ד (where the Mishnah Berurah writes it too, ס״ק כ״א).
  • Seifim 3 and 4 (the Mechaber's seifim ד and ה): the Rav opens his seif 3 by specifying that the deceased left no members of his household in his house, « אֶחָד מִבְּנֵי הֶחָצֵר שֶׁמֵּת בְּשַׁבָּת וְלֹא הִנִּיחַ בְּנֵי בַיִת בְּבֵיתוֹ » (שו״ע הרב שע״א:ג) — which the Mishnah Berurah, later than the Rav, also writes on the Mechaber's seif ד (ס״ק ט״ז, י״ז); and where Shabbos remains permitted through the deceased's eruv, the Rav sets a condition, « וּבִלְבָד שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה הַמּוֹרִישׁ גּוֹסֵס מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם » (שו״ע הרב שע״א:ג), and the same for the convert (seif 4); according to the Magen Avraham on seif ו (ס״ק ח), writes the Biur Halachah, the Mechaber's seif ד must be speaking of a deceased who was not yet dying as Shabbos began; according to the Atzei Almogim, which it cites, the Shulchan Aruch there speaks of every case.
  • Seif 5 (the Mechaber's seif ו): if the dying man dies on Shabbos, one does not say that what was permitted remains permitted, « כֵּיוָן שֶׁאֵין הָעֵרוּב רָאוּי לְהִתְקַיֵּם כָּל הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁרֹב גּוֹסְסִין לְמִיתָה » (שו״ע הרב שע״א:ה). The Magen Avraham writes this before him (ס״ק ח); the Biur Halachah reports that the Atzei Almogim disputes this rule, and writes: « ובשעת הדחק נראה דיש לסמוך ע״ז » (ביאור הלכה שע״א). Consult your Rav.

6. Mnemonic דירה

The siman turns on the dwelling: a dwelling without its owner does not forbid (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק א). Four letters of the word for four reminders, given here in the order of the seifim:

ד — דירה בלא בעלים: the house of a Jew who left without intending to return on Shabbos does not forbid (seif א).

ה — הסיח מלבו: a Jew who put [his dwelling] out of his mind does not forbid (seif א), even if he changes his mind and returns (seif ב; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ח) — for the non-Jew, see pitfall 1; for one who returns, the reservation in pitfall 2.

י — יורש: the heir — the ruling depends, among other things, on the time of death and on where the heir lives (seifim ג and ד).

ר — רשותו: another Jew who takes possession of the property of a convert who died by day forbids until he renounces his domain (seif ה).

7. Pitfalls to avoid

Pitfall 1: Judging the non-Jew like the Jew. A Jew who left without intending to return does not forbid; but, according to the first view of the seif, « אבל בעכו״ם אפי׳ הלך לשבו׳ בעיר אחר׳ אוסר עליהם עד שישכרו ממנו מקומו » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:א), if the city is within a day's walk. For another courtyard, the seif reports a view that he does not forbid, which the brackets adopt; the Mishnah Berurah writes: « עיין בה״ל שביארנו בשם כמה פוסקים דאין להקל כ״א בעיר אחרת ולא בחצר אחרת » (משנה ברורה שע״א ס״ק ו).
Pitfall 2: Thinking that one who changes his mind and returns on Shabbos forbids — or, conversely, making it a rule without reservation. The Mechaber: « ואח״כ נמלך בשב׳ וחזר אינו אוסר » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:ב), and the Mishnah Berurah gives the reason: « והו״ל שבת שהותרה ושוב לא תאסר » (משנה ברורה שע״א ס״ק ח), and adds that a non-Jew who comes on Shabbos does forbid: « אבל גבי עכו״ם קי״ל דאם בא בשבת אוסר » (משנה ברורה שע״א ס״ק ח). But the Biur Halachah concludes: « וע״כ יש להחמיר אם לא בשעת הדחק » (ביאור הלכה שע״א); and according to the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, one who left after nightfall forbids when he returns: « אֲבָל אִם הָלַךְ מִשֶּׁחָשֵׁכָה — אוֹסֵר כְּשֶׁחוֹזֵר » (שו״ע הרב שע״א:א).
Pitfall 3: Thinking that the heir's eruv always covers the house he inherits. In seif ג: « ואם מת משחשיכה אוסר אע״פ שעירב הזוכה עמהם אינו מועיל למה שירש אח״כ בשב׳ » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:ג).
Pitfall 4: Thinking that a death by day is always the lenient case. In seif ג, if he died by day, the heir does not forbid, the eruv he makes for his house also permitting what he inherited, and if he died after nightfall, he forbids; in seif ד, one who dies on Shabbos after making the eruv does not forbid, « א׳ מן החצר שמת בשב׳ אם עירב אפי׳ ירשו א׳ מבני השוק אינו אוסר » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:ד) (for a deceased already dying as Shabbos began, see section 5), and, if he died by day, the heir from the marketplace forbids if he comes into the house on Shabbos, his eruv being void (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ג); in seif ה too it is the reverse of seif ג: if the convert died by day, another Jew who takes possession of his property forbids until he renounces his domain; if he died after nightfall, he does not forbid, « שבהיתר הראשון עומד » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:ה) (for a convert already dying while it was still day, see section 5). The cases are not the same: in seif ג, according to the Mishnah Berurah, the deceased had not made the eruv for that house (ס״ק ט); seif ד itself distinguishes whether or not the deceased had made the eruv, and, if he died by day, his eruv is void (ס״ק כ״ג); in seif ה, according to the Mishnah Berurah, the Jew and the convert had made it together (ס״ק כ״ו).
Pitfall 5: Thinking that a dying man, who will not survive the day, does not forbid. Seif ו: « אף על פי שאינו יכול לחיות בו ביום אוסר עד שיזכו לו בפת ויערבו עליו » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:ו); and « וכן קטן אוסר אע״פ שאינו יכול לאכול כזית אבל האורח אינו אוסר כמו שנתבאר בסי׳ ש״ע » (שו״ע או״ח שע״א:ו).
Pitfall 6: Mixing the two numberings. Levels 1 to 3 follow the Shulchan Aruch of the Mechaber; Level 4 follows the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, which also has 6 seifim, divided differently: its seif 1 deals with the Jew who left, of whom the Mechaber speaks in seifim א and ב; its seifim 2, 3 and 4 correspond to seifim ג, ד and ה; its seifim 5 and 6, to seif ו.

10. The practical commandments of Siman שע״א

For daily conduct

  1. A Jew who left to spend Shabbos in another courtyard, even an adjacent one, without intending to return home on Shabbos, does not forbid (seif א) — according to the Mishnah Berurah, having left with his whole household while it was still day (ס״ק ב); for one who leaves on Shabbos, the Biur Halachah is not lenient, except perhaps, in a pressing situation, relying on the Magen Avraham, when he explicitly put his dwelling out of his mind.
  2. A non-Jew: according to the first view of the seif, even if he went to another city, he forbids until his place is rented from him, if it is within a day's walk; farther, he does not forbid. According to the view introduced by וי״א, which the brackets adopt, even if he went to another courtyard he does not forbid (seif א). The Mishnah Berurah refers to the Biur Halachah (ס״ק ו), which writes, in the name of several poskim, that one is lenient for a non-Jew only if he went to another city, not to another courtyard, and that the view permitting even another courtyard is an isolated opinion. For another city as for another courtyard, consult your Rav.
  3. A Jew who changes his mind and returns on Shabbos, having left without intending to return on Shabbos, does not forbid (seif ב; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ח); according to the Shulchan Aruch HaRav (seif 1), this applies to one who left while it was still day: one who left after nightfall forbids when he returns; the Biur Halachah writes that one should be stringent, except in a pressing situation. A non-Jew who comes on Shabbos does forbid, writes the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק ח).
  4. A house inherited from someone from the marketplace: if he died by day, the one who acquires it does not forbid, the eruv he makes for his house also permitting what he inherited; if he died after nightfall, he forbids, even though he made the eruv (seif ג) — the case, according to the Mishnah Berurah, is one where the deceased had not made the eruv for that house (ס״ק ט), as the Shulchan Aruch HaRav also writes (seif 2 in its numbering; Level 4).
  5. A resident who died on Shabbos does not forbid if he had made the eruv — provided, according to the Magen Avraham as the Biur Halachah reads him, that he was not already dying as Shabbos began, a condition the Shulchan Aruch HaRav also sets (seif 3 in its numbering); the Biur Halachah reports that the Atzei Almogim disputes it (section 5); otherwise, it depends on the heir, on where he lives and on his coming to the house; if he died by day, an heir from the marketplace forbids if he came to the house on Shabbos (seif ד).
  6. A convert who died by day: another Jew who takes possession of his property forbids, even if he did so only after nightfall, until he renounces his domain; if he died after nightfall, he does not forbid (seif ה) — the case, according to the Mishnah Berurah, is one where they had made the eruv together (ס״ק כ״ו); the Shulchan Aruch HaRav adds: provided the convert was not dying while it was still day (seif 4 in its numbering; section 5).
  7. The dying man forbids until bread is transferred to him and the eruv is made on his behalf; likewise the minor (seif ו; for the minor, until the eruv is made on his behalf, Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל״ד); the guest does not forbid (seif ו).
  8. In case of doubt — consult your Rav. In-depth pilpul — Level 2; Chabad shitah — Level 4.
