The seif number on this page is that of the Shulchan Aruch of the Mechaber (a single seif, with no gloss of the Rema). The Shulchan Aruch HaRav, studied in Level 4, also writes this siman in one seif: when it is cited, its name is given next to the number.

1. The central axiom

Siman שפ״ט in one sentence.
A non-Jew lives in the alley and has, behind his house, a window onto a valley or an enclosure: « עכו״ם הדר במבוי ויש לו חלון אחורי ביתו פתוח לבקעה או לקרפף » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ט:א) — he does not forbid [carrying] to the residents of the alley, even if the window is only four tefachim by four, because he cares more for the opening at the back; provided the valley or the enclosure has more than two se'ah (seif א). The Jew who forgot the eruv and has, behind his house, a door to an enclosure: if it has more than two se'ah, the enclosure is not usable for him, and he forbids; if it was enclosed for dwelling, he does not forbid, even beyond two se'ah — and the seif reports a contrary opinion (seif א; section 5).

2. The condensed key concepts

ConceptDefinitionIn the seif
פתוח לבקעה או לקרפףThe opening, behind the non-Jew's house, onto a valley or an enclosure: even a window of four tefachim by four, « אפי׳ אין בו אלא ד״ט על ד״ט » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ט:א); the Mishnah Berurah: « וכ״ש אם הוא פתח גמור » (משנה ברורה שפ״ט ס״ק א) — all the more so a full door. The Biur Halachah writes that it seems to him (נ״ל) that an enclosure was permitted only if, like the valley, it is open to another place, and he cites the Ritva (Level 1, section 3)Seif א: he does not forbid the residents of the alley, even if he brings camels and wagons in and out through the alley all day
כמו שאינו רגילThe reason: « שחפץ יותר באותו שפתוח לו מאחוריו לבדו שיש לו אויר והוי פתחו למבוי כמו שאינו רגיל שאינו אוסר » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ט:א) — he cares more for the opening at the back, where he has space, and his door to the alley is like a door one does not usually use. 3. The course of the seif — from the non-Jew's window to the enclosure enclosed for dwelling

Seif א, first clause — the non-Jew with the window: he lives in the alley and has, behind his house, a window onto a valley or an enclosure, even of four tefachim by four: he does not forbid the residents of the alley, even if he brings camels and wagons in and out through the alley all day.
↓
Seif א, second clause — the reason: he cares more for the opening at the back, for himself alone, where he has space; and his door to the alley is like a door one does not usually use, which does not forbid.
↓
Seif א, third clause — the condition: the valley or the enclosure has more than two se'ah; if it has only two se'ah, it is small, and he does not care for it.
↓
Seif א, fourth clause — the Jew who forgot the eruv: he has a door to the alley and, at the back, a door to an enclosure; if the enclosure has more than two se'ah, he forbids, because the enclosure is not usable for him, and he does not withdraw from the alley.
↓
Seif א, fifth clause — enclosed for dwelling: even with more than two se'ah, he does not forbid, since it is usable for him.
↓
Seif א, last clause — "יש מי שאומר": on the contrary, enclosed for dwelling, even with two se'ah, he forbids, because there is no space (section 5).

The Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes this siman in one seif (389:1). Level 4 shows where its text differs from the Mechaber's seif: the reason for the Jew's prohibition (point א); the enclosure of two se'ah, written in square brackets in the text Sefaria serves, and the objects that rested in the house (point ב); the opinion "יש מי שאומר", which its seif does not report (point ג).

4. Decision tree — does the resident with an exit behind his house forbid the residents of the alley?

Q1: Who has, behind his house, the exit onto a valley or an enclosure? (seif א)
↓ A non-Jew → Q2. A Jew who forgot the eruv, with a door to an enclosure → Q3.
Q2: Does the valley or the enclosure have more than two se'ah? (seif א)
↓ YES → he does not forbid the residents of the alley, even if the window is only four tefachim by four, and even if he passes through the alley all day (seif א). Only two se'ah → the seif says: it is small, and he does not care for it (seif א).
Q3: Was the enclosure enclosed for dwelling? (seif א)
↓ NO, and it has more than two se'ah → he forbids, because the enclosure is not usable for him (seif א); the enclosure of two se'ah, the seif does not state outright (Level 1, section 3; Level 4, point ב). YES → according to the rule the seif writes without a name, he does not forbid, even beyond two se'ah; the seif reports a contrary opinion (section 5).
→ At the slightest doubt: consult your Rav.

5. The enclosure enclosed for dwelling — the two opinions of the seif

The seif first writes the rule without a name: « ואם הוקף לדירה אפי׳ יותר מסאתי׳ אינו אוסר כיון שראוי לו » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ט:א); then it reports: « ויש מי שאומר דאדרבה אם הוקף לדירה אפי׳ בית סאתים אוסר שכיון שהוקף לדירה אין בו אויר ואינו חפץ בו להסתלק בשבילו מן המבוי » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ט:א).

6. Mnemonic ח-ס-ש-ה-ד

Five words of the seif, in its order:

ח — חלון: the non-Jew who has, behind his house, a window onto a valley or an enclosure does not forbid the residents of the alley — on the condition of the next letter.

ס — סאתים: provided the valley or the enclosure has more than two se'ah; with two se'ah, it is small.

ש — שכח: the Jew who forgot the eruv, with an enclosure of more than two se'ah, forbids.

ה — הוקף: enclosed for dwelling, the enclosure does not forbid, even beyond two se'ah.

ד — דאדרבה: the contrary opinion the seif reports (section 5).

7. Pitfalls to avoid

Pitfall 1: Confusing the measures. The non-Jew's window: « אפי׳ אין בו אלא ד״ט על ד״ט » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ט:א) — four tefachim by four. In the enclosure of more than two se'ah of the Jew who forgot the eruv, one carries only within four amos (Shulchan Aruch HaRav 389:1); the Mishnah Berurah: it is a karmelis (ס״ק ה).
Pitfall 2: Forgetting the condition of the non-Jew's rule: « והוא שיש בבקעה או בקרפף יותר מסאתים אבל אם אין בו אלא סאתים קטן הוא ולא ניחא ליה ביה » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ט:א).
Pitfall 3: Judging the Jew like the non-Jew. For the non-Jew, an opening onto a valley or an enclosure of more than two se'ah means he does not forbid; for the Jew who forgot the eruv, an enclosure of more than two se'ah, not enclosed for dwelling, means he forbids: « אם הקרפף יותר מסאתים אוסר לפי שאינו ראוי לו ואינו מסתלק מן המבוי » (שו״ע או״ח שפ״ט:א); for the enclosure enclosed for dwelling, see pitfall 5.
Pitfall 4: Thinking that the Rav's seif allows, without reservation, taking the house's objects out into the enclosure. After the sentence in square brackets on the unenclosed enclosure of two se'ah, the Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes: « אֲבָל כֵּלִים שֶׁשָּׁבְתוּ בַּבַּיִת — אָסוּר לְהוֹצִיאָם לְתוֹכוֹ » (שו״ע הרב שפ״ט:א), and it refers to simanim 358 and 372; the Magen Avraham refers to siman 372 for the prohibition of taking [an object] out of the house into the enclosure (ס״ק א). To which enclosure this limit applies, the page does not decide: consult your Rav.
Pitfall 5: Giving only one rule for the enclosure enclosed for dwelling. The seif reports a contrary opinion, under "יש מי שאומר": see section 5. Consult your Rav.
Pitfall 6: Writing that the Shulchan Aruch HaRav rejects the opinion "יש מי שאומר". Its seif writes the rule of the enclosure enclosed for dwelling and does not report that opinion; Level 4 does not say why (point ג). Consult your Rav.

10. The practical commandments of Siman שפ״ט

For daily conduct

  1. The non-Jew of the alley who has, behind his house, a window — even of four tefachim by four — onto a valley or an enclosure of more than two se'ah does not forbid the residents of the alley, even if he passes through the alley all day (seif א).
  2. The Jew who forgot the eruv and has, behind his house, a door to an enclosure of more than two se'ah, not enclosed for dwelling, forbids, because the enclosure is not usable for him (seif א); the enclosure of two se'ah, the seif does not state outright: see Level 1 (section 3) and Level 4 (point ב).
  3. The enclosure enclosed for dwelling: according to the rule the seif writes without a name, he does not forbid, even beyond two se'ah; the seif reports a contrary opinion (section 5): consult your Rav.
  4. Objects that rested in the house: the Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes that they may not be taken out into the enclosure (389:1); to which enclosure, the page does not decide (pitfall 4).
  5. When in doubt — consult your Rav. In-depth pilpul — Level 2; Chabad shitah — Level 4.
