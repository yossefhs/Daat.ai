1. The Shulchan Aruch text

Siman שצ״ז contains 18 seifim of the Mechaber (Rabbi Yosef Karo); the Rema (Rabbi Moshe Isserles) adds a gloss, introduced by הגה, in seif ג. In seif ב, a parenthesis in small type, without «הגה», reads (בינוניות) (טור). Topic of the siman, according to its title: the law of the place of rest (שביתה) of the individual and his belongings, and of walking two thousand amos. The Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe has 20 seifim for this siman: its numbering is that of Level 4, not that of this page.

Seif א

דין שביתת היחיד וכליו ומהלך אלפים אמה. ובו יח סעיפים:
כל אדם יש לו אלפים אמה לכל רוח חוץ מארבע אמותיו או מהמקום ששבת בו:
Translation: Every person has two thousand amos in every direction, beyond his four amos, or from the place where he took his rest (shavas bo).
What this seif says:
  • Two thousand amos in every direction, beyond his four amos: כל אדם יש לו אלפים אמה לכל רוח חוץ מארבע אמותיו. A person's place, wherever he settles, is four amos, and from there he has two thousand in every direction: so he may walk, from where his feet stand, two thousand amos and four (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ב). The Mishnah Berurah also writes that this is an enactment of the Sages, and reports that the poskim differ on what the Torah forbids (ס״ק א).
  • Or from the place where he took his rest: או מהמקום ששבת בו. If he took his rest in a place enclosed by partitions, the whole of it counts as four amos, and he has two thousand around that place (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ג).

Seif ב

קדש עליו היום בבקעה ואינו יודע תחום שבת מהלך אלפי' פסיעו' (בינוניות) (טור) שהם תחום שבת:
Translation: If the day became holy upon him in a valley (bik'ah) and he does not know [the limits of] the Shabbos boundary (techum), he walks two thousand paces — [in parentheses, in small type, without «הגה»: (בינוניות), “average”, and (טור), a reference to the Tur] —, which are the Shabbos boundary.
What this seif says:
  • The day became holy upon him in a valley, and he does not know the techum: קדש עליו היום בבקעה ואינו יודע תחום שבת.
  • He walks two thousand paces, which are the techum: מהלך אלפי׳ פסיעו׳ (בינוניות) (טור) שהם תחום שבת. Besides the four amos (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ד). The average pace, according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק ה): half an amah for the place of the foot and half an amah between one foot and the other; it writes that the case is of one who needs to walk for some Shabbos need, and that this is a measurement for a mitzvah, which is permitted (reference to siman 306, seif ז).

Seif ג

כשם שאין אדם רשאי להלך בשבת וביום טוב אלא אלפי' אמה לכל רוח כך כליו ובהמתו אין יכול שום אדם להוליכ' חוץ לאלפים אמה של בעליה' ואם עירבו בעליה' לרוח א' אין שום אדם יכול להוליכם לרוח האחרת אפי' פסיעה אחת במקו' שאין הבעלי' יכולי' לילך: הגה ועיין לעיל סוף סימן ש״ה אם מותר למסור בהמה לעכו״ם במקום שיש לחוש שיוציאנה חוץ לתחום:
Translation: Just as a person is permitted to walk, on Shabbos and on Yom Tov, only two thousand amos in every direction, so too his belongings and his animal: no one may take them beyond the two thousand amos of their owners; and if their owners made an eruv toward one direction, no one may take them toward the other direction, even one step, to a place where the owners cannot go. Gloss of the Rema: and see above, at the end of siman 305, whether it is permitted to hand over an animal to a non-Jew (akum) where there is reason to fear that he will take it out beyond the techum.
What this seif says:
  • Belongings and animal do not go beyond the two thousand amos of their owners: כשם שאין אדם רשאי להלך בשבת וביום טוב אלא אלפי׳ אמה לכל רוח כך כליו ובהמתו אין יכול שום אדם להוליכ׳ חוץ לאלפים אמה של בעליה׳. Where a person acquires his place of rest (shevisah), his belongings and his animals acquire it too, for they are drawn after their owner (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ו). The same, it adds, applies to his produce and to anything of his; and the case is that of Yom Tov, where there is no prohibition of carrying but only that of techumin — for on Shabbos it is forbidden to carry beyond four amos —, or that of a garment one wears (ס״ק ז, in the name of the Magen Avraham). No one: even if the place is within the techum of the one taking them (ס״ק ח).
  • If the owners made an eruv toward one direction: ואם עירבו בעליה׳ לרוח א׳ אין שום אדם יכול להוליכם לרוח האחרת אפי׳ פסיעה אחת במקו׳ שאין הבעלי׳ יכולי׳ לילך.
  • Gloss of the Rema: reference to the end of siman 305: ועיין לעיל סוף סימן ש״ה אם מותר למסור בהמה לעכו״ם במקום שיש לחוש שיוציאנה חוץ לתחום. At the end of siman 305, the Rema writes that all the more so there is no need to fear that the shepherd will take [the animals] beyond the techum, since techumin are rabbinic (section 6). The Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק ט) writes that there it is explained as permitted; it refers to what it wrote there — some are stringent, and the general custom is to permit — and adds that to instruct him to take it beyond the techum is forbidden.

Seif ד

המוסר בהמתו לבנו הרי היא כרגלי האב:
Translation: One who hands his animal over to his son: it is like the feet (k'raglei) of the father.
What this seif says:
  • The animal handed over to the son follows the father's techum: המוסר בהמתו לבנו הרי היא כרגלי האב. The reason, according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק י, in the name of the Magen Avraham citing the Maggid Mishneh): people are accustomed to deposit their belongings in the hands of their sons, without intending to put them in their possession. On this seif, the poskim differ (same ס״ק; Level 2).

Seif ה

מסרה לרועה אפי' נתנה לו בי״ט הרי הוא כרגלי הרועה ואם מסרה לשני רועים הרי הוא כרגלי בעליה מפני שלא קנה א' מהם:
Translation: If he handed it over to a shepherd, even if he gave it to him on Yom Tov, it is like the feet of the shepherd; and if he handed it over to two shepherds, it is like the feet of its owner, because neither of them acquired it.
What this seif says:
  • Handed over to a shepherd, even on Yom Tov: like the shepherd's feet: מסרה לרועה אפי׳ נתנה לו בי״ט הרי הוא כרגלי הרועה. For already on the eve of Yom Tov his intention was that it should acquire its place of rest not with him but with the shepherd to whom he would hand it over the next day (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ב).
  • Handed over to two shepherds: like the owner's feet: ואם מסרה לשני רועים הרי הוא כרגלי בעליה מפני שלא קנה א׳ מהם. On the scope of this seif — several shepherds in the town, an animal handed over to two shepherds from the eve or on the holy day itself —, the poskim differ (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ב and י״ג; Level 2).

Seif ו

שור של פטם כרגלי מי שלקחו לשחטו ביו״ט וכן אם שחטו בעליו בי״ט ומכר בשרו כל א' מהלוקחי' מוליך מנתו למקום שהוא הולך:
Translation: The ox of a fattener (pattam) is like the feet of the one who bought it to slaughter it on Yom Tov; and likewise, if its owner slaughtered it on Yom Tov and sold its meat, each of the buyers takes his portion to the place where he goes.
What this seif says:
  • The fattener's ox follows the one who bought it to slaughter it on Yom Tov: שור של פטם כרגלי מי שלקחו לשחטו ביו״ט. Pattam: one who fattens oxen to sell them (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ד). Even a buyer from another town who came here by means of an eruv takes it to his place: such an ox, being fattened, is bought from other towns as well, and its owner's intention from the day before was to place it in the possession of whoever would buy it (ס״ק ט״ו).
  • Slaughtered and sold on Yom Tov: each takes his portion where he goes: וכן אם שחטו בעליו בי״ט ומכר בשרו כל א׳ מהלוקחי׳ מוליך מנתו למקום שהוא הולך. For its owner's intention was to divide it among whoever would come, even from other towns (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ט״ז).

Seif ז

שור של רועה כרגלי אנשי אותה העיר:
Translation: The ox of a herdsman (ro'eh) is like the feet of the people of that town.
What this seif says:
  • The herdsman's ox follows the people of the town: שור של רועה כרגלי אנשי אותה העיר. The ro'eh here is a man who raises animals and sometimes sells one to his neighbours and acquaintances (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ז, citing Rashi); it explains that, not being fattened, such an ox is not bought from other towns, so that its owner has in mind only the people of his town.

Seif ח

כלים המיוחדי' לא' מהאחי' שבבית הרי הם כרגליו ושאינם מיוחדי' אין יכולי' להוליכם אלא למקום שכולם יכולים לילך:
Translation: Belongings set aside for one of the brothers of the household are like his feet; and those not set aside may be taken only to a place where all of them can go.
What this seif says:
  • Belongings set aside for one brother follow his feet: כלים המיוחדי׳ לא׳ מהאחי׳ שבבית הרי הם כרגליו. That is, the brothers do not use them: although they have a share in them, the place of rest of the one who uses them applies to them (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ט).
  • Belongings not set aside: only where all can go: ושאינם מיוחדי׳ אין יכולי׳ להוליכם אלא למקום שכולם יכולים לילך. If one made an eruv a thousand amos to the east and the other a thousand amos to the west, the belongings may be taken only a thousand amos this way and a thousand amos that way, since all are partners in them and each restrains the other (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ׳).

Seif ט

שנים ששאלו חלוק זה לילך בו שחרית וזה לילך בו ערבית לא יוליכו אלא למקום ששניה' יכולים לילך ואם עירב זה לסוף אלפים למזרח וזה לסוף אלפים למערב לא יזיזוהו ממקומו:
Translation: Two who borrowed a garment, one to go out in it in the morning (shacharis) and the other to go out in it in the evening (arvis): they may take it only to a place where both can go; and if one made an eruv at the end of two thousand [amos] to the east and the other at the end of two thousand to the west, they may not move it from its place.
What this seif says:
  • A garment borrowed by two: only where both can go: שנים ששאלו חלוק זה לילך בו שחרית וזה לילך בו ערבית לא יוליכו אלא למקום ששניה׳ יכולים לילך. They borrowed it while it was still day, so that it be in the possession of both (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״א). The “evening” is not that of the second day of Yom Tov — two distinct sanctities, in which each may take it, at the time of his borrowing, as far as he made his eruv —, but the evening that precedes the morning (ס״ק כ״ב).
  • One made an eruv to the east, the other to the west: it is not moved: ואם עירב זה לסוף אלפים למזרח וזה לסוף אלפים למערב לא יזיזוהו ממקומו. For one cannot go east at all, nor the other west; and this applies to the preceding seif as well (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ג).

Seif י

ב' שלקחו בהמה בשותפות ושחטוה ביום טוב אע״פ שלקח כל א' מנתו הרי כל הבשר כרגלי שניהם אבל אם לקחו חבית של יין וחלקוה ביום טוב חלקו של כל א' מהם כרגליו:
Translation: Two who bought an animal in partnership and slaughtered it on Yom Tov: although each took his portion, all the meat is like the feet of both; but if they bought a barrel of wine and divided it on Yom Tov, each one's share is like his own feet.
What this seif says:
  • An animal bought in partnership and slaughtered on Yom Tov: all the meat follows both: ב׳ שלקחו בהמה בשותפות ושחטוה ביום טוב אע״פ שלקח כל א׳ מנתו הרי כל הבשר כרגלי שניהם. The Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק כ״ד) explains: even if one were to say that each one's share was determined from the eve of Yom Tov, each share draws nourishment from the other's during twilight (bein hashmashos), the time when the eruv is acquired, and every limb is mixed of both shares; so it is, stringently, like the feet of both, as if they had not divided. On the animal, the commentators differ: see Level 2, section 5.
  • A barrel of wine divided on Yom Tov: each one's share follows his feet: אבל אם לקחו חבית של יין וחלקוה ביום טוב חלקו של כל א׳ מהם כרגליו. For in a rabbinic matter there is bereirah: when they divided, we say that each share was retroactively determined from the eve of Yom Tov, and acquired the place of rest of its owner (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ד and כ״ה).

Seif יא

השואל כלי מחבירו מערב יום טוב אפי' לא לקחו עד הלילה הוא כרגלי השואל ואם שאלו ממנו ביום טוב אע״פ שדרכו לשאלו ממנו בכל יום טוב הרי הוא כרגלי המשאיל:
Translation: One who borrows an article from his fellow on the eve of Yom Tov: even if he did not take it until night, it is like the feet of the borrower; and if he borrowed it from him on Yom Tov, even though he is accustomed to borrow it from him every Yom Tov, it is like the feet of the lender.
What this seif says:
  • Borrowed on the eve, even if taken at night: like the borrower's feet: השואל כלי מחבירו מערב יום טוב אפי׳ לא לקחו עד הלילה הוא כרגלי השואל. Even though he did not draw the article to himself before Yom Tov, so that by law it is not yet in his possession: since [the lender] had promised him on the eve to lend it to him, he placed it in his possession at twilight as regards the acquisition of eruv (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ו).
  • Borrowed on Yom Tov, even habitually: like the lender's feet: ואם שאלו ממנו ביום טוב אע״פ שדרכו לשאלו ממנו בכל יום טוב הרי הוא כרגלי המשאיל. Since he did not come to ask for it while it was still day, [the lender] thought that perhaps he had found someone else to borrow from (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ז, following the Gemara).

Seif יב

האשה ששאלה מחברתה מים ומלח לעיסתה ותבלין לקדירתה בי״ט הרי העיסה ותבשיל כרגלי שתיהן:
Translation: A woman who borrowed from her friend water and salt for her dough, and spices for her pot, on Yom Tov: the dough and the cooked dish are like the feet of both of them.
What this seif says:
  • What the woman borrowed on Yom Tov: the dough and dish follow both: האשה ששאלה מחברתה מים ומלח לעיסתה ותבלין לקדירתה בי״ט הרי העיסה ותבשיל כרגלי שתיהן. The same applies to water and salt for her pot (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ח). She may take the dough and the dish only to a place where both are permitted to go, since something that acquired its place of rest with another has been mixed into it (ס״ק כ״ט).

Seif יג

לקח מחבירו בי״ט גחלת לא יוליכנה אלא כרגלי הנותן אבל אם הדליק נר או עץ משלהבת חבירו הרי הוא כרגלי זה שהדליק:
Translation: One who took a coal from his fellow on Yom Tov may take it only as the feet of the giver; but if he lit a lamp or wood from his fellow's flame, it is like the feet of the one who lit.
What this seif says:
  • A coal follows the giver: לקח מחבירו בי״ט גחלת לא יוליכנה אלא כרגלי הנותן. Since it has substance (yesh bah mamash), it is like other articles (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל׳).
  • What one lights from the other's flame follows the one who lights: אבל אם הדליק נר או עץ משלהבת חבירו הרי הוא כרגלי זה שהדליק. Since a flame has no substance, the place of rest of its owner does not apply to it (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל״א).

Seif יד

בור של יחיד הרי הוא כרגלי בעליו ושל אותה העיר כרגלי אנשי אותה העיר ושל הפקר כרגלי הממלא:
Translation: The cistern of an individual is like the feet of its owner; that of the town, like the feet of the people of that town; and an ownerless one (hefker), like the feet of the one who draws.
What this seif says:
  • An individual's cistern follows its owner: בור של יחיד הרי הוא כרגלי בעליו. The case, according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק ל״ב), is that of collected rain or snow water; a well of springing water, even of an individual, is like the feet of the one who draws, as the next seif says.
  • The town's cistern follows the people of the town: ושל אותה העיר כרגלי אנשי אותה העיר.
  • An ownerless one follows the one who draws: ושל הפקר כרגלי הממלא. For anything ownerless, whoever lifts it acquires it, and we say it was retroactively determined at twilight that it was fit for this man and in his possession; and even if the one who drew gives [the water] to others after drawing it on Yom Tov for himself, it is like his feet (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל״ה).

Seif טו

נהרות המושכי' ומעיינו' הנובעים הרי הם כרגלי הממלא היו באים מחוץ לתחום לתוך התחום ממלאים מהם בשבת ואין צריך לומר ביום טוב:
Translation: Flowing rivers and springing fountains are like the feet of the one who draws; if they came from outside the techum into the techum, one may draw from them on Shabbos, and needless to say on Yom Tov.
What this seif says:
  • Rivers and springs follow the one who draws: נהרות המושכי׳ ומעיינו׳ הנובעים הרי הם כרגלי הממלא. Even if they are not ownerless, belonging to a town or to an individual: since they move, they did not acquire a place of rest with their owners (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל״ז).
  • Coming from outside the techum: one draws from them on Shabbos, and needless to say on Yom Tov: היו באים מחוץ לתחום לתוך התחום ממלאים מהם בשבת ואין צריך לומר ביום טוב. Since they acquired no place of rest at all outside the techum, because they move (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל״ח).

Seif טז

מילא מים מבור של הפקר לצורך חבירו הרי הם כרגלי הממלא:
Translation: One who drew water from an ownerless cistern for his fellow: it is like the feet of the one who drew.
What this seif says:
  • Drawn for a fellow from an ownerless cistern: like the feet of the one who drew: מילא מים מבור של הפקר לצורך חבירו הרי הם כרגלי הממלא. Even after he handed it to him, explains the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק ל״ט); it refers to the Biur Halachah, and writes that many dispute this rule: the poskim differ (Level 2).

Seif יז

מי שהיו לו פירות מופקדים בעיר אחרת רחוקה ממנו ועירבו בני אותה העיר לבא אצלו לא יביאו לו מפירותיו שפירותיו כמוהו במה דברים אמורים כשייחד להם קרן זויות אבל אם לא ייחד להם הם כרגלי זה שהם מופקדים אצלו:
Translation: One who had produce deposited in another town far from him, and the people of that town made an eruv to come to him: they may not bring him any of his produce, for his produce is like him. When is this said? When he set aside a corner (keren zavis) for them; but if he did not set one aside for them, they are like the feet of the one with whom they are deposited.
What this seif says:
  • Produce deposited in a distant town is like its owner: מי שהיו לו פירות מופקדים בעיר אחרת רחוקה ממנו ועירבו בני אותה העיר לבא אצלו לא יביאו לו מפירותיו שפירותיו כמוהו. And he did not place an eruv (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק מ׳).
  • When a corner was set aside for them: במה דברים אמורים כשייחד להם קרן זויות. According to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק מ״א), he lent him a room and said to him “here is the house before you”, so that there is no acceptance of safekeeping: therefore the produce is in its owner's possession. On what decides this case, the commentators differ: see Level 2, section 8.
  • Otherwise, they follow the depositary: אבל אם לא ייחד להם הם כרגלי זה שהם מופקדים אצלו. According to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק מ״ב), if he received them under his authority and responsibility, they are like his feet. All this, it further writes, is when the produce was deposited without specification and he later decided to take some on Yom Tov; but if it was meant from the start to be taken on Yom Tov, it is in its owner's possession in any case (ס״ק מ״ג).

Seif יח

מי שזימן אצלו אורחים בי״ט לא יוליכו בידם מנות למקום שאין בעל הסעודה יכול לילך בו אא״כ זיכה להם מערב יו״ט ע״י אחר במנות אלו:
Translation: One who invited guests to his home on Yom Tov: they may not carry portions in their hands to a place where the host of the meal cannot go, unless he gave them title to these portions on the eve of Yom Tov through another person.
What this seif says:
  • Guests do not carry portions where the host cannot go, unless he gave them title on the eve through another: מי שזימן אצלו אורחים בי״ט לא יוליכו בידם מנות למקום שאין בעל הסעודה יכול לילך בו אא״כ זיכה להם מערב יו״ט ע״י אחר במנות אלו. The same applies if he invited them while it was still day: as long as he did not give them title, [the portions] are in the host's possession and acquired their place of rest with him (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק מ״ד).

2. General context

What does this siman discuss?

Siman שצ״ז sets the measure of the techum: two thousand amos in every direction, beyond a person's four amos or the place where he took his rest (seif א), and what one does who does not know the techum (seif ב). It then extends the rule to belongings and the animal: no one takes them beyond the two thousand amos of their owners (seif ג). The following seifim say, case by case, whose techum a thing follows — for example the father (seif ד), the shepherd (seif ה), the borrower or the lender (seif יא), the one who draws (seifim יד to טז), the depositary (seif יז), the host of the meal (seif יח).

The mishnah in tractate Beitzah sets the rule: « הַבְּהֵמָה וְהַכֵּלִים כְּרַגְלֵי הַבְּעָלִים. הַמּוֹסֵר בְּהֶמְתּוֹ לִבְנוֹ אוֹ לָרוֹעֶה — הֲרֵי אֵלּוּ כְּרַגְלֵי הַבְּעָלִים » (ביצה ל״ז ע״א). The Gemara first says that this mishnah is not according to Rabbi Dosa — or, according to another version, Abba Shaul —, for whom an animal handed over to a shepherd, even if only on Yom Tov, is like the feet of the shepherd; then it answers: « אֲפִילּוּ תֵּימָא רַבִּי דּוֹסָא, וְלָא קַשְׁיָא: כָּאן בְּרוֹעֶה אֶחָד, כָּאן בִּשְׁנֵי רוֹעִים » (ביצה ל״ז ע״ב). Seif ה distinguishes likewise between one shepherd and two shepherds; on its scope, the poskim differ (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ב and י״ג; Level 2).

The fundamental question: how far may a person go on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and how far may what belongs to him be taken? The siman answers with the two thousand amos (seifim א and ב), then with the rule of belongings and the animal, which go where their owners can go (seif ג); seifim ד to יח say, case by case, whose techum they follow.

Place in Hilchos Techumin

In Sefaria's index, the section הלכות תחומין of the Shulchan Aruch runs from siman 396 to siman 407. Siman שצ״ז follows siman שצ״ו · 396, whose title, at the head of its seif א, is « דין ארבע אמו׳ שיש לכל אדם בשבת » (שו״ע או״ח שצ״ו:א), and precedes siman שצ״ח · 398, whose title, at the head of its seif א, is « דין היאך מודדין אלפים אמה » (שו״ע או״ח שצ״ח:א). The gloss of the Rema refers to the end of siman 305 (section 6); the Mishnah Berurah refers to siman 306, seif ז (ס״ק ה).

3. Key halachic concepts

A few notions structure this siman:

The essential notions of siman שצ״ז:
  • תחום שבת — the Shabbos boundary: two thousand amos in every direction, beyond a person's four amos or the place where he took his rest (seif א). The Mishnah Berurah writes: « והוא תקנת חכמים » (משנה ברורה שצ״ז ס״ק א), and reports that the poskim differ on what the Torah forbids.
  • כרגלי — “like the feet of”: a thing that follows a person's techum may be taken only where that person can go. Seif ג states it for belongings and the animal: « כך כליו ובהמתו אין יכול שום אדם להוליכ׳ חוץ לאלפים אמה של בעליה׳ » (שו״ע או״ח שצ״ז:ג).
  • שביתה — the place of rest a person acquires, and with him what is his: « דבמקום שאדם קונה שם שביתתו קונים שם כליו ובהמותיו דנגררים אחריו » (משנה ברורה שצ״ז ס״ק ו).
  • מיוחד — an article set aside for one of the brothers follows his feet; one not set aside goes only where all can go (seif ח).
  • ברירה — retroactive determination: for the meat of an animal bought in partnership and slaughtered on Yom Tov, all the meat follows both, and the Mishnah Berurah explains that each share drew nourishment from the other at twilight (seif י; ס״ק כ״ד); for the wine of a barrel divided on Yom Tov, each one's share follows his feet (same seif; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ד and כ״ה); on the animal, the commentators differ: see Level 2, section 5.
  • יש בה ממש — what has substance: the coal follows the giver, « דהואיל ויש בה ממש הרי היא כשאר הכלים » (משנה ברורה שצ״ז ס״ק ל׳); the flame does not: « דהואיל ששלהבת אין בה ממש לא חל עליה שביתת בעליה » (משנה ברורה שצ״ז ס״ק ל״א) (seif יג).
  • כרגלי הממלא — like the feet of the one who draws: water from an ownerless cistern (seifim יד and טז), and that of rivers and springs (seif טו).
  • זיכה על ידי אחר — giving title through another: the host who gave his guests title to the portions on the eve of Yom Tov, through another, allows them to carry them (seif יח).

To know whose techum a thing follows, the seifim look, for example, at where the thing acquired its place of rest — as the Mishnah Berurah explains for seifim ה, יא and יח (ס״ק י״ב, כ״ו and מ״ד) — and at whether the thing has substance (seif יג; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל׳ and ל״א). On several seifim, the poskim differ (section 6).

6. Position of the Rema

In this siman, the Rema (Rabbi Moshe Isserles, הגה) adds a gloss, introduced by הגה, in seif ג. The commentators also add reservations to the seifim of the Mechaber; here are some, seif by seif.

The gloss of the Rema, and some reservations of the commentators:
  • Seif ב, the parenthesis — it is printed in small type, without «הגה»: (בינוניות) (טור). On the measure of the average pace, see the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק ה), on seif ב.
  • Seif ג, the gloss of the Rema — « ועיין לעיל סוף סימן ש״ה אם מותר למסור בהמה לעכו״ם במקום שיש לחוש שיוציאנה חוץ לתחום » (רמ״א או״ח שצ״ז:ג). At the end of siman 305, the Rema writes: « וכ״ש דאין לחוש שהרועה יוציא אותם חוץ לתחום דהא תחומין דרבנן » (רמ״א או״ח ש״ה:כ״ג). The Mishnah Berurah writes that there it is explained as permitted; it refers to what it wrote there — some are stringent, and the general custom is to permit — and adds: « אכן לצוותו שיוליכנו חוץ לתחום אסור » (משנה ברורה שצ״ז ס״ק ט).
  • Seif ד — on the animal handed over to the son, the poskim differ (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י; Level 2).
  • Seif ה — on the scope of the seif (several shepherds in the town; two shepherds, from the eve or on the holy day itself), the poskim differ (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ב and י״ג; Level 2).
  • Seif י — on the animal bought in partnership, the commentators differ (Level 2, section 5).
  • Seif טז — on water drawn for a fellow, the poskim differ (Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל״ט, which refers to the Biur Halachah; Level 2).
  • Seif יז — on what decides the case of deposited produce, the commentators differ (Level 2, section 8).
  • Chabad → Level 4 — Daat HaRav studies this siman in the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, siman שצ״ז, under its own numbering, in 20 seifim.

8. Practical synthesis of the Siman

The teachings of Siman שצ״ז:
  1. Two thousand amos in every direction, beyond one's four amos or the place where one took one's rest (seif א); one who does not know the techum, in a valley, walks two thousand paces (seif ב).
  2. Belongings and the animal do not go beyond the two thousand amos of their owners; if these made an eruv toward one direction, they are not taken to the other, even one step, where the owners cannot go (seif ג).
  3. An animal handed over to the son follows the father (seif ד); to a shepherd, even on Yom Tov, the shepherd; to two shepherds, the owner (seif ה) — on these two seifim, the poskim differ (section 6).
  4. The ox of a fattener follows the one who bought it to slaughter it on Yom Tov; slaughtered and sold by its owner on Yom Tov, each buyer takes his portion where he goes (seif ו); that of a herdsman follows the people of the town (seif ז).
  5. Shared things: set aside for one of the brothers, they follow him, otherwise they go only where all can go (seif ח); a garment borrowed by two goes only where both can go, and is not moved if they made eruvin in opposite directions at the end of two thousand amos (seif ט); the meat of an animal slaughtered in partnership follows both (on the animal, the commentators differ: section 6), divided wine follows each (seif י).
  6. Borrowing: on the eve, it follows the borrower; on Yom Tov, the lender (seif יא); the dough and dish of a woman who borrowed on Yom Tov follow both (seif יב); a coal follows the giver, a flame the one who lights (seif יג).
  7. Water: an individual's cistern follows its owner, the town's the people of the town, an ownerless one the one who draws (seif יד); rivers and springs follow the one who draws, and one draws from them on Shabbos if they come from outside the techum (seif טו); water drawn for a fellow from an ownerless cistern follows the one who draws — the poskim differ (seif טז).
  8. The deposit: the produce is like its owner when he set aside a corner for it, otherwise it follows the depositary (seif יז); on what decides this case, the commentators differ (section 6).
  9. Guests do not carry portions where the host cannot go, unless he gave them title on the eve, through another (seif יח).
  10. For halachah lema'aseh, consult your Rav.

9. Comprehension questions

Check your understanding:
  1. From where are the two thousand amos measured: from where one stands, or from the place where one took one's rest (seif א; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ב and ג)?
  2. Why are a person's belongings and animal subject to his techum (seif ג; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ו)?
  3. Why does an animal handed over to a shepherd follow the shepherd, and one handed over to two shepherds the owner (seif ה; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק י״ב and י״ג)?
  4. According to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק כ״ד and כ״ה), why does the meat of an animal bought in partnership follow both, while divided wine follows each (seif י)? On the animal, the commentators differ: see Level 2, section 5.
  5. Why does an article borrowed on Yom Tov follow the lender, even when one usually borrows it (seif יא; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק כ״ז)?
  6. Why do the coal and the flame not follow the same person (seif יג; Mishnah Berurah, ס״ק ל׳ and ל״א)?
  7. When is deposited produce like its owner, and when does it follow the depositary (seif יז)? What decides it according to the Mishnah Berurah (ס״ק מ״א and מ״ב)? On this point, the commentators differ: see Level 2, section 8.
