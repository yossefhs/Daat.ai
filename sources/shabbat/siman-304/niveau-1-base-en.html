1. The Shulchan Aruch text

Siman ש״ד contains 3 seifim of the Mechaber (Rabbi Yosef Karo). Subject of the siman: the rest of the servant and of the slave on Shabbos.

Seif א

על איזה עבד מצווה על שביתתו. ובו ג סעיפים:
אדם מצווה על שביתת עבדו שמל וטבל לשם עבדות וקיבל עליו מצות הנוהגות בעבד אבל אם לא מל וטבל אלא קבל עליו שבע מצות בני נח הרי הוא כגר תושב ומותר לעשות מלאכה בשבת לעצמו אבל לא לרבו ואסור לכל ישראל לומר לו לעשות מלאכה בשבת לצורך ישראל אפילו מי שאינו רבו ואם לא קבל עליו שום מצוה אלא עדיין הוא עכו״ם גמור דינו שוה לקבל עליו שבע מצות ולפי זה צרכי חולה שאין בו סכנה דקיי״ל אומר לעכו״ם ועושה וכן מת ביום טוב ראשון דקיי״ל יתעסקו בו עממין אסור לומר לעבד ישראל אפי' הוא ע״א דכיון דמלאכת העבד אסור מן התורה לא הותרה בדבר שאין בו פיקוח נפש ויש חולקים ומתירים בזה ומ״מ אם היה עושה מלאכת רבו שלא מדעתו וניכר שאינו עושה לדעתו מותר ואינו צריך להפרישו (אפי' קבל עליו שבע מצות) ולישראל אחר שאינו רבו אפי' עושה לדעת ישראל מותר כל שאין שם אמירת ישראל ובלבד שלא יהנה ישראל בשבת מאותה מלאכה וי״א שכל שלא קבל עליו שבע מצות בני נח כיון דעכו״ם גמור הוא אין רבו מוזהר עליו ולפי זה צרכי חולה שאין בו סכנה וכן צרכי מתים ביום טוב ראשון מותר לומר להם לעשותו: הגה וכל עבד שמצווה על שביתתו אסור לצאת בחותם שעשה לו רבו להראות בו שהוא עבדו ואם הוא של טיט מותר לצאת בו כשתלוי בצוארו אבל לא בכסותו ובשל מתכת בכל ענין אסור (טור) ואם העבד עשה החותם לעצמו אפי' בשל טיט בכל ענין אסור (המגיד פי״ט):
Translation: A person is bound to the rest (shevisa) of his slave who was circumcised and immersed for the sake of servitude and accepted the mitzvos incumbent on the kena'ani slave. But if he was neither circumcised nor immersed, and accepted only the seven Noahide laws, he has the status of a גר תושב (ger toshav): he is permitted to do a melacha on Shabbos for himself, but not for his master; and it is forbidden for any Jew to tell him to do a melacha for the needs of a Jew, even a Jew who is not his master. If he accepted no mitzva at all and remains a complete idolater, his status is the same as one who accepted the seven laws. Consequently, for the needs of a sick person not in mortal danger (חולה שאין בו סכנה) — where the rule is "one tells a non-Jew and he does it" — or for a deceased person on the first day of Yom Tov, it is forbidden to ask this of a Jew's slave, since the slave's melacha is forbidden by Torah law and was not permitted where there is no pikuach nefesh. Some permit it nonetheless. In any case, if the slave was doing his master's melacha without his knowledge, and it is evident that he acts on his own initiative, it is permitted and the master need not stop him. For another Jew who is not his master, it is permitted so long as there is no amira (instruction) and the Jew derives no benefit on Shabbos from that melacha. Gloss of the Rema: see section 6 below (going out with a seal of servitude).

Seif ב

והיכא דמותר העבד לעשות מלאכה לעצמו אם אמר ליה האדון שיעשה לעצמו ויזון עצמו ביום השבת כיון שהתנה עמו מבע״י עושה הוא לצרכי מזונותיו ובלבד בצנעה שלא יהא בדבר חשש רואים:
Translation: Where the slave is permitted to do a melacha for himself: if the master told him to work for himself and to provide his own sustenance on Shabbos — since he made that stipulation before Shabbos (מבעוד יום, mibe'od yom) — he may act for the needs of his food, provided it is done discreetly (betzin'a), so that there be no suspicion on the part of onlookers (חשש רואים).

Seif ג

עכו״ם גמור שהוא שכיר אין רבו מצווה על שביתתו:
Translation: A complete idolater (עכו״ם גמור) who is a hired worker (sachir) — his employer is not bound to his rest. (A mere non-Jewish employee does not fall under the obligation of shevisas eved: only the owned kena'ani slave, who has accepted the mitzvos, is subject to it.)
Complete text: these 3 seifim constitute the whole of the Mechaber's codification on this subject. Each one specifies a case, a condition, or an exception.
What this seif says:
  • One is bound by his servant’s rest — one circumcised and immersed for servitude who accepted upon himself the mitzvot incumbent on a servant.
  • But if neither circumcised nor immersed, having accepted only the seven Noahide commandments: he is as a ger toshav — permitted to work on Shabbat for himself, not for his master.
  • And it is forbidden for any Jew to tell him to work on Shabbat for a Jew’s need — even one who is not his master.
What this seif says:
  • Where the servant may work for himself: if the master told him to work for his own account and feed himself on Shabbat,
  • having stipulated with him while it was still day — he works for his needs. But discreetly, that there be no concern of onlookers.
What this seif says:
  • A full non-Jew who is a hired man:
  • his master is not bound by his rest.

2. The general context

What is this siman about?

Our siman deals with the rest of the servant and of the slave on Shabbos. It belongs to the great corpus of הלכות שבת (the laws of Shabbos) in the Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim.

The fundamental question: what are the halachic conditions, permissions and prohibitions bearing on the rest of the servant and of the slave on Shabbos? Which structuring principles allow one to decide the borderline cases?

Place within Hilchos Shabbos

Siman ש״ד is one of simanim 242-365 which cover Hilchos Shabbos. It has its own conceptual scope, but it works together with the neighbouring simanim.

3. The key halachic concepts

To understand this siman one must master a few structuring halachic concepts — which appear across the 3 seifim and the commentary of the Mishnah Berurah:

Recurring concepts in Hilchos Shabbos (worth knowing):
  • מלאכה (melacha) — the act of work forbidden on Shabbos
  • דאורייתא / דרבנן — forbidden by the Torah or by the Sages
  • פסיק רישא — an unavoidable consequence
  • דבר שאינו מתכוון — an unintended act
  • שבות — a secondary rabbinic prohibition
  • פיקוח נפש — mortal danger, which suspends the prohibition
Siman ש״ד deploys some of these concepts in the context of the rest of the servant and of the slave on Shabbos.

4. The seifim in detail — one by one

Let us take each seif of the Mechaber in turn and set out its practical import. For the in-depth pilpul (Talmudic sources, מחלוקות of the Rishonim, נפקא מינות), see Level 2 — Lamdan.

Seif א — who is bound by the obligation of rest?

The Mechaber distinguishes three statuses of a non-Jew in the service of a Jew, following the verse למען ינוח עבדך ואמתך ("so that your servant and your maidservant may rest", Devarim 5:14):

StatutDéfinitionTravail le Chabbat
Kena'ani slaveCircumcised, immersed, having accepted the mitzvos of a slaveForbidden — the master is metzuveh al shevisaso (bound to his rest), even for work the slave does for himself
Ger toshavHas accepted the 7 Noahide laws, without conversion into servitudePermitted for himself; forbidden for his master; and no Jew may instruct him to work for a Jew
Complete idolaterHas accepted no mitzvaDisputed: per the Mechaber, the same as a ger toshav; per the yesh omrim, the master is not bound to his rest
נפקא מינה (the practical consequence): may one ask such a slave to do a melacha for a sick person not in mortal danger, or for a deceased person on Yom Tov (where amira l'akum is normally permitted)? For the slave subject to the obligation of rest, the Mechaber forbids it (his melacha is d'oraisa); the yesh cholkin permit it.

Seif ב — the slave who works for his own sustenance

Where the slave has the right to work for himself, the master may — by stipulating before Shabbos — let him provide his own food on Shabbos, provided it is discreet, so as to avoid maris ayin (the appearance that he is working for the master).

Seif ג — the non-Jewish hired worker

A non-Jew who is merely employed (sachir), and is not the Jew's property, does not fall under the obligation of shevisas eved at all. The employer is not bound to his rest.

6. The position of the Rema

The Rema (Rabbi Moshe Isserles) adds here a הגהה (gloss) on a concrete point: going out on Shabbos with the seal of servitude (חותם, chosam) which the master affixed to mark that this man is his slave.

The gloss of the Rema — the seal (chosam):
  • A seal made by the master, of clay: one may go out with it if it is hung around the neck — but not if fastened to the garment.
  • A seal of metal: forbidden in every case (Tur).
  • A seal the slave made for himself: forbidden in every case, even of clay (HaMaggid, ch. 19).
The logic: a sign worn around the neck is akin to an ornament or identifier; fastened to the garment, or made of metal, it counts as masui (a carried burden) — hence the prohibition of hotza'ah on Shabbos.

Sephardi / Ashkenazi marker: Mechaber → Sephardim (Beis Yosef); Rema → Ashkenazim (Magen Avraham, Mishnah Berurah). This siman has no Level 4 Daat HaRav: the Alter Rebbe did not write it (the subject of the slave had become rare in his day).

7. Modern practical cases

The institution of the kena'ani slave no longer exists today, so this siman is largely historical. Its principles nonetheless shed light on several present-day questions:

SituationAnalysis
Non-Jewish employee (sachir)A mere hired worker is not subject to shevisas eved (seif ג). The rules on what he may do on Shabbos belong to amira l'akum and to contract work (kablanus) — see simanim 244, 247, 252.
Non-Jewish caregiverFor a sick person not in mortal danger, the Mechaber forbids asking the melacha of a slave subject to the obligation of rest; for an ordinary non-Jewish employee, amira l'akum is permitted in this setting (siman 328).
The notion of ger toshavThe siman is one of the classic sources on the status of the ger toshav (a non-Jew who has accepted the 7 Noahide laws) and its implications.
A sign of belonging worn on ShabbosThe Rema's gloss on the chosam feeds the broader discussion on badges, medals and objects worn around the neck versus fastened to the garment (hotza'ah / tachshit).
For halacha lema'aseh — in particular on employing a non-Jew on Shabbos — consult your Rav and the simanim devoted to amira l'akum.

8. Practical synthesis of the siman

What Siman ש״ד teaches, in 3 seifim:
  1. The Mechaber codifies 3 cases around the subject: the rest of the servant and of the slave on Shabbos
  2. The Mishnah Berurah adds 26 entries of clarification and sources
  3. The Rema, where applicable, states the Ashkenazi nuances
  4. Practical application requires staying attentive to the underlying halachic concepts
  5. For halacha lema'aseh, consult your local Rav
