1. The Shulchan Aruch text

Siman 312 contains 10 se'ifim from the Mechaber (Rabbi Yosef Karo) codifying the halachos of personal hygiene on Shabbos.

Se'if 1 — Stones for wiping: kevod habriyos

הנצרך לנקביו במה מקנח בשבת. ובו י סעיפים:
משום כבוד הבריות התירו לטלטל אבנים לקנח אפי' להעלותם לגג עמו דהוי טירחא יתירא מותר ומי שיש לו מקום מיוחד לבית הכסא יכול להכניס עמו אבנים לקנח מלא היד ואם אין לו מקום קבוע מכניס עמו כשיעור בוכנא קטנה ואם ניכר באבן שקנחו בו מותר להכניסו אפי' הוא גדול הרבה או אפי' הם הרבה ממלא היד מותר ליטול כולן דכיון שקנחו בהן הוכנו לכך: הגה י"א דוקא בחצר מותר לטלטל אבנים (רש"י ס"פ המוציא יין ור' ירוחם ני"ב חי"ו ור"ן) וי"א דאפי' מכרמלית לרה"י נמי שרי דהא נמי אינו רק איסור דרבנן ומשום כבוד הבריות התירוהו. (תוספות פרק לולב הגזול והגהות מרדכי פרק הזורק):
Translation: By virtue of kevod habriyos (human dignity), Chazal permitted moving stones for wiping (stones are in principle muktzeh) — even bringing them up to a roof, which is excessive effort, is permitted. One who has a fixed makom for the beis hakisei may bring in a handful of stones. Without a fixed place — he brings in the size of a small pestle. If a stone bears a visible mark of previous use, one may bring it even if very large, or many — since use has made them muchan for that purpose. Rama's gloss: Some hold moving the stones is only permitted in a courtyard; others hold even from karmelis to reshus hayachid — since this too is only a derabbanan, lifted for kevod habriyos.

Se'if 2 — Stones sunken by rain

אם ירדו גשמים על האבנים ונטבעו אם רישומן ניכר מותר ליטלן כדי לקנח ואין בזה משום סותר ולא משום טוחן:
Translation: If rain fell on the stones and they sank into mud: as long as their imprint is visible (one can identify them), one may take them for wiping — and there is no issur of סותר (demolishing) nor of טוחן (grinding/crushing the ground).

2. General context — kevod habriyos in the beis hakisei

What does this siman address?

Siman 312 addresses a humble but essential topic: how to wipe on Shabbos after relieving oneself. In the time of the Gemara, people used stones (which are muktzeh) or natural materials. The siman shows how halachah navigates the tension between the issur of muktzeh and human dignity.

The framing principle: כבוד הבריות (kevod habriyos) — "dignity of the creations." This is one of the great principles of halachah: human dignity is so great that it pushes aside derabbanan prohibitions. Berachos 19b: "גדול כבוד הבריות שדוחה את לא תעשה שבתורה" — for the details (= derabbanan), kevod habriyos prevails.
Why a whole siman on this topic? Because it illustrates a major halachic mechanism: a derabbanan (moving a muktzeh stone) can be set aside in the face of human dignity. But a de'oraisa (cutting, smoothing, writing) is never set aside. The siman traces this line with precision.

Place in Hilchos Shabbos

Siman 312 naturally follows the muktzeh block (308-311): it is a concrete application of muktzeh to bodily hygiene. It also prepares for siman 313 (boneh/soser) by introducing the melachos of mechatech (cutting to size) and memachek (smoothing).

3. Key halachic concepts

1. כבוד הבריות (kevod habriyos) — human dignity. Sets aside derabbanan prohibitions (like muktzeh). Source: Berachos 19b. Never sets aside a de'oraisa.
2. מוקצה applied to stones. A stone is muktzeh machmas gufo. But a stone already used for wiping (with a visible imprint) is "prepared" for that function → it becomes a "k'li" and is no longer muktzeh.
3. מחתך (mechatech) — cutting to a precise dimension. A de'oraisa melachah. Cutting paper to a specific size = assur. This is the crux of the modern toilet paper issue.
4. דבר שאינו מתכוון — unintended action. In se'if 3: wiping with a clump from which grasses protrude — one is not aiming to detach them → muttar (Rabbi Shimon). Unless it is a pesik reisha (inevitable consequence).
5. סכנה and רפואה — danger and healing. The siman also touches health: holding back from going to the beis hakisei is dangerous (bal teshaktzu). One never delays a natural need because of Shabbos.

Method of reading:
  1. Identify the material used (stone, clump, paper, grass)
  2. Check the issur involved: muktzeh (derabbanan, can be lifted) or melachah (de'oraisa, not lifted)
  3. If derabbanan → kevod habriyos may lift it
  4. If de'oraisa melachah → assur, find an alternative

6. The Rama's position

The Rama adds a gloss on se'if 1, regarding the scope of permitted movement:

Rama's gloss (se'if 1):
  • First opinion — moving the stones is permitted only in a chatzer (extended reshus hayachid).
  • Second opinion — permitted even from karmelis to reshus hayachid, since that transport is also only a derabbanan, and kevod habriyos lifts it (Tosafos, Hagahos Mordechai).
Mechaber vs. Rama difference:
  • Mechaber → kevod habriyos lifts the muktzeh of stones.
  • Rama → spells out how far the lifting extends (karmelis → reshus hayachid).
  • Chabad → Shulchan Aruch HaRav siman 312.

7. Modern practical cases

SituationHalachic analysis
Pre-cut toilet paper (separate sheets)Muttar — each sheet is already at its size, no mechatech.
Toilet paper on a roll (to tear)Problematic — tearing along the perforation = potential mechatech / kore'a. Most poskim assur tearing on Shabbos; prepare separate sheets before Shabbos.
Tearing off the perforation, without intent to sizeEdge case — some are meikil in shaas hadchak (no other option) since one does not aim at a precise size (derech kilkul). But the ideal is to prepare in advance.
Tissues / wipes pre-cutMuttar (no cutting). Watch out: wet wipes raise an issue of sechitah (squeezing) — avoid pressing.
Bidet / water sprayCold unheated water = OK. Water heated by a Shabbos boiler = problem.
Holding back from the beis hakiseiAssur / strongly discouraged — bal teshaktzu. Health takes priority; one never delays a natural need for Shabbos.
For halachah l'maaseh: The major modern issue is toilet paper on a roll. Standard solution: prepare separate sheets before Shabbos (boxed tissues, or pre-torn sheets). For everything else, kevod habriyos guarantees one is never left in an undignified situation — but it lifts only derabbanan, never a de'oraisa melachah.

8. Practical summary of the siman

Key teachings of Siman 312:
  1. Kevod habriyos (human dignity) sets aside derabbanan — like muktzeh of stones.
  2. But never a de'oraisa melachah — cutting paper to size (mechatech) remains assur.
  3. Already-used stone (with imprint) = "muchan," no longer muktzeh.
  4. Davar she-eino mis'kavein: wiping with a clump from which grasses protrude — muttar (one does not aim to detach them).
  5. Toilet paper on a roll: prepare separate sheets before Shabbos.
  6. Bal teshaktzu: never hold back a natural need — health takes priority.
