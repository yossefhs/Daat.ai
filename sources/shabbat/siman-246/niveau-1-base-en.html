Siman רמ״ו · Level 1 — Lending and Renting Kelim to a Non-Jew for Shabbat

Accessible version — text, translation and pedagogical explanation
Section 1
The text of the Shulchan Aruch
שולחן ערוך, אורח חיים, סימן רמ״ו, סעיף א׳ — הטקסט המלא
מֻתָּר לְהַשְׁאִיל וּלְהַשְׂכִּיר כֵּלִים לְגוֹי, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בָּהֶם מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁאֵין אָנוּ מְצֻוִּים עַל שְׁבִיתַת כֵּלִים. בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁהִשְׁאִילָן וְהִשְׂכִּירָן לוֹ מִקֹּדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת. אֲבָל בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה, אָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָן אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִילָן לוֹ, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כְּאִלּוּ הוּא שְׁלוּחוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת מְלָאכָה. וְאָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָן לוֹ אֲפִלּוּ מִקֹּדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת בִּסְכַר יוֹם, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כְּמַשְׂתַּכֵּר בְּשַׁבָּת, אֲבָל בְּהַבְלָעָה — מֻתָּר.
הגה׃ וְהַשְׁאָלָה מֻתֶּרֶת אֲפִלּוּ עֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה, רַק שֶׁלֹּא יְקַבֵּל שָׂכָר אִם הוּא כְּלִי שֶׁמְּלַאכְתּוֹ לְאִסּוּר. וְכָל זֶה בְּשֶׁאֵין הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מַצְוֵהוּ עַל הַמְּלָאכָה.
Full English translation
It is permitted to lend (lehash'il / השאלה) and to rent out (lehaskir / השכרה) kelim to a non-Jew, even though he does melakha with them on Shabbat, because we are not commanded regarding the resting of kelim (shvitat kelim / שביתת כלים).

When does this apply? When they were lent or rented out to him before Shabbat. But on erev Shabbat, close to nightfall (samukh la-chasheikha), it is forbidden to rent or lend them out, because it would appear as though he is the Yid's shaliach (שלוחו) doing the melakha for him.

And it is forbidden to rent them out even before Shabbat at a daily rate (sekhar yom / סכר יום), because it appears as earning wages on Shabbat (mishtaker be-Shabbat / sekhar Shabbat). But through הבלעה (havla'ah — bundling several days into one combined fee) — it is permitted.
Hagahah of the Rema (for Ashkenazim): And lending (sheilah / השאלה) is permitted even on erev Shabbat close to nightfall, provided he does not receive payment, if it is a kli she-melakhto le-issur (כלי שמלאכתו לאיסור — a kli whose typical use is something forbidden on Shabbat). And all of this applies only when the Yid does not instruct the non-Jew to do the melakha.
⚖
Two sources of the issur — must be distinguished
① Samukh la-chasheikha (close to nightfall) — gezeira of nireh ki-shlucho (נראה כשלוחו, appearance of agency): it looks like the non-Jew is the Yid's agent.
② Sekhar yom (daily rate) — gezeira of sekhar Shabbat (Shabbat wages): the Yid earns money from melakha done on Shabbat.
Havla'ah (bundling) dissolves the second problem: when the rental fee covers several days and is not broken down day-by-day, the "Shabbat wages" disappear.
Section 2
Understanding the halakha

What is this Seif about?

This Seif deals with a very common situation: a Yid owns tools, a car, professional equipment. May he rent them out or lend them to a non-Jew, knowing the non-Jew will use them on Shabbat?

The answer depends on what the Yid knows at the time of the lending or rental. If the Yid knows the kelim will be used on Shabbat — it is forbidden. If he does not know — it is permitted.

💡
Why is it forbidden?
The main reason is sekhar Shabbat — the Yid receives rental money for a day when his object "worked" on Shabbat. Even though the Yid himself did nothing, benefiting from the melakha done on Shabbat is forbidden.
📜
The Rema's ruling for Ashkenazim
The Rema goes further than the Shulchan Aruch: not only is it assur to rent out, but also to lend free of charge — even though the Yid receives nothing. Why? Because even a free loan creates the appearance that the Yid is sending his shaliach to do melakha on Shabbat (nireh ki-shlucho).
⏰
When must the kli be handed over?
The issur applies even if the kli is handed over on erev Shabbat (Friday). The fact that the contract was made before Shabbat does not permit the Shabbat use. The key is the Yid's knowledge: did he know the kli would be used on Shabbat?
Section 3
The 3 fundamental concepts
①
שְׁבִיתַת כֵּלִים
Shvitat kelim (resting of kelim)
According to Beit Shammai, a Yid's kelim must "rest" on Shabbat, even in the hands of a non-Jew. Beit Hillel (whom we follow le-halakha) hold there is no chiyuv of shvitat kelim per se — but other issurim still apply.
②
נִרְאֶה כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ
Appearance of agency
If a Yid lends his object to a non-Jew who uses it on Shabbat publicly, people might think the non-Jew is acting as the Yid's shaliach — as if the Yid himself were having someone work in his place. This appearance is forbidden.
③
שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת
Sekhar Shabbat (Shabbat wages)
The issur of taking financial benefit from melakha performed during Shabbat. Even if the rental contract was signed before Shabbat, the Yid receives money for an "active" day of Shabbat — this is problematic.
Section 4
The practical pesak
פסק הלכה — Final ruling
אסור להשכיר או להשאיל כלים לגוי כשיודע שישתמש בהם בשבת
For all (Sephardim and Ashkenazim): It is forbidden to rent out one's kelim to a non-Jew when one knows he will use them on Shabbat.

For Ashkenazim (Rema): It is also forbidden to lend one's kelim, even free of charge, under the same conditions.
Section 5
The key concept: הבלעה (havla'ah)

Why havla'ah permits the rental

Sekhar Shabbat is assur because it "appears" as income directly tied to melakha done on Shabbat. But when the rental fee is bundled into an overall price covering several days, the Shabbat profit is "absorbed" into the mass — it is no longer perceived as a specific gain from Shabbat.

This is analogous to the principle of batel be-rov (בטל ברוב — nullification in the majority): something minor and indistinguishable is neutralized.

✅
Permitted example (successful havla'ah)
Renting out a tool for 7 days for €100 total — the Shabbat profit is bundled into the package, not isolated. Mutar.
❌
Forbidden example (sekhar yom)
Renting out the same tool at €15 per day, which means €15 for Shabbat alone. The Shabbat gain is isolated, perceptible — assur.
⚠
Limit: Shabbat must not be the main element
Renting for Shabbat alone, or for Shabbat + Sunday where Shabbat is the main part, is not considered true havla'ah — the essence of sekhar Shabbat remains dominant.
Section 6
Lending vs renting — the Rema's חידוש

The Rema's distinction: השאלה ≠ שכירות

The Rema clearly distinguishes two situations that do not follow the same rule:

  • Sheilah / השאלה (lending without compensation) — permitted even on erev Shabbat close to nightfall, provided it is not a kli she-melakhto le-issur (a kli whose typical use violates Shabbat).
  • Sekhirut / שכירות (renting with compensation) — always assur at sekhar yom (daily rate); permitted only with havla'ah.
Case Status
Lending (free) before Shabbat — neutral kli✅ Mutar
Lending (free) before Shabbat — כלי מלאכה⚠️ Disputed (Rema stricter)
Lending (free) close to nightfall — neutral kli✅ Mutar
Lending (free) close to nightfall — כלי מלאכה❌ ASSUR (Rema)
Renting at a daily rate (sekhar yom)❌ Always ASSUR
Renting via הבלעה (multi-day bundle)✅ Mutar
Section 7
The final pesak — Sephardi vs Ashkenazi
🕎
Sephardi — Mechaber
Follows Rambam / Rif / Rosh: no chiyuv of שביתת כלים. Thus fewer restrictions on כלי מלאכה. Permits lending close to nightfall even for tools typically "le-issur." Source: the Shulchan Aruch text as is.
❄️
Ashkenazi — Rema
Adds a stricter chiddush (following Tossafot in part): for כלי מלאכה (tools whose typical use violates Shabbat), it is assur to lend close to nightfall, even free of charge. And for renting, he keeps his chiddush on havla'ah.
Section 8
Modern practical cases
Assur
I rent out my car to my non-Jewish neighbor Friday night, knowing he will use it on Shabbat.
I know he will use it on Shabbat → sekhar Shabbat + nireh ki-shlucho. Assur even according to the Shulchan Aruch.
Assur (Ashkenazim)
I lend my tools for free close to nightfall to a non-Jew who uses them on Shabbat.
Even without compensation, assur per the Rema due to nireh ki-shlucho (for כלי מלאכה).
Mutar
I rent out my drill for the week to a contractor (flat 7-day package), and he uses it every day.
Rental via הבלעה — the Shabbat profit is bundled into the overall package. Mutar.
Mutar
I lend my book to a non-Jewish neighbor on Thursday, and he reads it on Shabbat.
Free loan, well before Shabbat, on a neutral object (not a כלי מלאכה). Mutar for all.
Mutar
I rent out my drill to a contractor, without knowing he will work on Shabbat.
If the Yid does not know the kli will be used on Shabbat, it is mutar (אם אינו יודע, מותר).
Assur
I rent out my camera at €30 per day to a non-Jew for Shabbat alone.
Daily rate (סכר יום) isolated on Shabbat → sekhar Shabbat. Assur even without knowing the precise use.
Ask a Rav
I rent an apartment to a non-Jew for several months (including Shabbatot).
Long-term rental — specific halakhot apply (השכרת בית), often mutar but consult a Rav.
Ask a Rav
I give my car to a non-Jew so he can make a paid delivery (which includes Shabbat).
More complex: issues of שליחות + sekhar Shabbat. Depends on the contract and the Yid's knowledge. Ask a Rav.
Section 9
To remember — golden rules
The 4 essential rules
① Lending before Shabbat = OK
No chiyuv of resting the kelim. Lending or renting in advance (Thursday, Wednesday…) is mutar according to Beit Hillel.

② Close to nightfall = ASSUR to rent out
So as not to appear to be sending the non-Jew to do melakha in our place (nireh ki-shlucho).

③ Sekhar yom = ALWAYS ASSUR
Daily rate = sekhar Shabbat. הבלעה (multi-day bundle) solves the problem.

④ The Rema adds a chumra for כלי מלאכה
For kelim whose typical use violates Shabbat, lending free of charge close to nightfall is also assur (Ashkenazi).

Part Two
The Following Seifim — Bet through Yod

What's coming up

After establishing the general rule in Seif Alef (renting/lending to a non-Jew, the distinction between havla'ah and sechar yom), the Siman develops and refines the doctrine across nine more seifim. The reference text reproduced below is from the Shulchan Aruch HaRav (the Alter Rebbe), who summarizes and decides between the positions of the Mechaber, the Rama, and the Rishonim.

The throughline gradually shifts from keilim (utensils) to beheima (working animal) — which introduces a new obligation: shvitas behemto (the rest of one's animal), commanded by the Torah itself.

Seif ב׳
Restriction on professional tools (כלי אומנות)
Siman 246 · Seif Bet — Shulchan Aruch HaRav
יֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁהַכֵּלִים שֶׁעוֹשִׂים בָּהֶם מְלָאכָה, כְּגוֹן רֵחַיִם בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁמֻתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָם לְנָכְרִי, וּמַחֲרֵשָׁה וְכַיּוֹצֵא בָהֶן מִשְּׁאָר כְּלֵי אֻמָּנוּת — אָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָם לְנָכְרִי בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת... הֲרֵי נִרְאֶה כְּאִלּוּ עוֹשֶׂה בִּשְׁלִיחוּת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל. וְכֵן עִקָּר.
Translation
According to one shita (accepted as the iker — the main psak), tools of the trade (klei umnus) — like a millstone, a plow, or any other work-tool — have an additional chumra. When a tool's Shabbos use directly benefits the Jewish owner financially (because the non-Jew pays a higher rental knowing he can use it on Shabbos too), it is asur to rent it out on erev Shabbos. The reason: the Jew is directly benefiting from the tool's working on Shabbos, which makes the non-Jew look like his shaliach.

What is this seif about?

This seif introduces a key chiluk: not all keilim are the same. A klei umnus (work-tool) — which brings in extra money because it runs on Shabbos — creates a special problem. The Shabbos profit is baked into the price, so even a contract signed before Shabbos is suspect.

The logic: if the non-Jew would have paid less to rent the tool excluding Shabbos, then the extra represents exactly the Shabbos work — which revives the issue of nireh k'shlucho.

Seif ג׳
Lending (השאלה) — a softer rule than renting
Siman 246 · Seif Gimel
אֲבָל לְהַשְׁאִיל לוֹ כֵּלִים שֶׁעוֹשִׂים בָּהֶם מְלָאכָה — מֻתָּר אֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁאֵין רֶוַח לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּמַה שֶּׁהַנָּכְרִי רַשַּׁאי לַעֲשׂוֹת בָּהֶם בְּשַׁבָּת — אֵין נִרְאֶה כְּאִלּוּ עוֹשֶׂה בִּשְׁלִיחוּתוֹ.
Translation
However, lending for free (hash'ala) a work-tool to a non-Jew is mutar even on erev Shabbos close to nightfall. Since the Jew gets no financial benefit from the Shabbos use — he doesn't look like he's sending the non-Jew on his behalf. Even if the loan is conditional on a return loan after Shabbos (exchange of favors), since no actual rental money changes hands, Chazal did not gezeira.

The core idea

The decisive criterion for nireh k'shlucho is the Jewish owner's financial benefit. Without rental income, without profit, no observer can suspect the non-Jew is acting as a shaliach. A free loan, even of a work-tool, is thus exempt from the previous seif's restriction.

Seif ד׳
The maris ayin issue when an object leaves on Shabbos
Siman 246 · Seif Dalet
אָסוּר לְהַשְׁאִיל שׁוּם חֵפֶץ לְנָכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת, וַאֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, אִם הוּא סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה כָּל כָּךְ עַד שֶׁאֵין שָׁהוּת לַנָּכְרִי לְהוֹצִיא הַחֵפֶץ מִפֶּתַח בֵּיתוֹ שֶׁל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לִרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם — אָסוּר לְהַשְׁאִילוֹ, מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן.
Translation
It is asur to lend any cheftza to a non-Jew on Shabbos itself, and also on erev Shabbos, if it's so close to nightfall that the non-Jew won't have time to take the object out of the Jew's house into reshus harabim before nightfall. The reason: maris ayin — anyone seeing the non-Jew leaving with the Jew's belongings on Shabbos might suspect the Jew is sending out his things via the non-Jew, an actual aveirah of hotza'ah.

What is this seif about?

Here the problem is no longer sechar Shabbos nor nireh k'shlucho (from Seif Alef), but maris ayin in the narrow sense: the worry that a passerby might wrongly assume the Jew is sending the non-Jew to be his porter on Shabbos — a straight-up violation of hotza'ah.

The rule: for a late-Friday loan to be valid, the non-Jew must have time to leave the house before nightfall, so that no one sees him walking out with the object on Shabbos itself.

Seif ה׳
Town with an eruv — kula and chumrasos
Siman 246 · Seif Hei (extract)
אֲבָל בְּעִיר הַמְעֹרֶבֶת שֶׁמֻּתָּר לְטַלְטֵל בְּתוֹכָהּ — מֻתָּר לְהַשְׁאִילוֹ אֲפִלּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת אִם הַנָּכְרִי דָר בְּמָקוֹם הַמְעֹרָב... וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אִם יָדוּעַ לוֹ שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי יוֹלִיכֶנּוּ הַיּוֹם חוּץ לַתְּחוּם — אָסוּר לְהַשְׁאִילוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת.
Translation
In a town with an eruv (where carrying on Shabbos is mutar), one may lend an object to a non-Jew even on Shabbos itself, provided the non-Jew lives within the eruv area (or the Jew's house is far enough from the eruv's edge that no one will see).

But: if the Jew knows the non-Jew will take the object beyond the techumin (the 2000 amos limit), it is asur to lend it even on Shabbos — because the Jew's keilim acquire their own shvisa (movement limit) at the start of Shabbos.

The practical issue

When an eruv is in place, the maris ayin problem of the previous seif drops out: no one will suspect a hotza'ah aveirah. But a new boundary appears: techumin (the 2000 amos limit ≈ 960m). A Jew's belongings acquire their own shvisa at the start of Shabbos and can't be carried beyond.

The Shulchan Aruch HaRav paskens: one may show the object to the non-Jew and let him take it himself (grama — indirect causation), but one may not hand it over directly if one knows he'll leave the techum.

Seif ו׳
Objects belonging to the non-Jew — no restriction
Siman 246 · Seif Vav
וְכָל זֶה בַּחֲפָצִים שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֲבָל חֲפָצִים שֶׁל נָכְרִי שֶׁהָיוּ מֻפְקָדִים בְּיַד יִשְׂרָאֵל וּבָא לִטְּלוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת — יָכוֹל לִתְּנוֹ וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁיּוֹצִיאֶנּוּ לִרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים.
Translation
Everything said until now applies to items belonging to the Jew. But items belonging to the non-Jew that were just being held in pikadon (safekeeping) by the Jew: if the non-Jew comes to collect them on Shabbos, the Jew may hand them over even though he'll carry them into reshus harabim. The Jew is just a shomer — he's returning a man's property to its owner.

Why the difference?

The whole sugya so far (sechar Shabbos, nireh k'shlucho, maris ayin) presupposes an item belonging to the Jew. When the non-Jew comes to take back his own property that he had left in pikadon, the Jew is simply returning it. No observer can suspect anything funny, and the Jew obviously profits nothing.

Seif ז׳
Major shift — animal (beheima) and the Torah-level issur
Siman 246 · Seif Zayin
אָסוּר לְהַשְׁאִיל בְּהֶמְתּוֹ לְנָכְרִי לַעֲשׂוֹת בָּהּ מְלָאכָה, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה בָּהּ גַּם בְּשַׁבָּת, וְאָדָם מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתַת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר "לְמַעַן יָנוּחַ שׁוֹרְךָ וַחֲמֹרֶךָ". וְאַף לְהַשְׂכִּירָהּ לוֹ אָסוּר מִן הַתּוֹרָה.
Translation
It is asur to lend one's animal to a non-Jew for him to work it, because the non-Jew will also use it on Shabbos. A person is commanded on the rest of his animal, as the passuk says: "In order that your ox and your donkey may rest" (Shemos 23:12).

And it is even asur min haTorah to rent it out — even many days before Shabbos. And even if the Jew stipulates that the animal must not work on Shabbos and the non-Jew promises — this does no good, because the non-Jew is not ne'eman on this.

A fundamental category shift

We move from a derabbanan framework (gezeiros on keilim because of maris ayin and sechar Shabbos) to a d'Oraisa framework. For an animal, the passuk in the Aseres haDibros explicitly mandates rest: shvisas behemto is a positive Torah mitzvah, not just a chazal-level takkana.

Consequence: none of the heterim that worked earlier (havla'ah, free loan, etc.) solve the beheima problem. Both renting and lending are asur, even far in advance.

Seif ח׳
Special case — an animal the Jew rented from the non-Jew
Siman 246 · Seif Ches
בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁגּוּף הַבְּהֵמָה שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֲבָל יִשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁשָּׂכַר בְּהֵמָה מִנָּכְרִי — יָכוֹל לַחֲזוֹר וּלְהַשְׂכִּירָהּ אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִילָהּ לְנָכְרִי אַחֵר... וְיֵשׁ מִי שֶׁאוֹסֵר לְהַשְׁאִילָהּ... וְטוֹב לָחוּשׁ לִדְבָרָיו.
Translation
What we said applies when the guf habeheima (body of the animal) belongs to the Jew. But a Jew who has rented an animal from a non-Jew and wants to sub-rent or lend it out to another non-Jew — this is mutar, because the guf isn't his, so he's not metzuveh on its shvisa.

But: there's a yesh mi she'oser on lending in this case, out of concern that the renting created some form of quasi-ownership for the rental period. It's good to be choshesh for his words (l'chumra).

The key principle

The obligation of shvisas behemto applies only to an animal where the guf belongs to the Jew (kinyan haguf). If the Jew only has rental usage rights, the obligation doesn't apply — the "body" stays with the non-Jewish owner.

This chiluk will be critical for the following seifim: one can sell the animal to the non-Jew, or be mafkir it (declare it ownerless), to escape the obligation.

Seif ט׳
The hefker mechanism
Siman 246 · Seif Tes (extract)
מֻתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּיר אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִיל בְּהֶמְתּוֹ לְנָכְרִי וּלְהַתְנוֹת עִמּוֹ שֶׁיַּחֲזִירֶנָּה לוֹ קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת. וְאִם עָבַר הַנָּכְרִי עַל תְּנָאוֹ וְעִכְּבָהּ — יַפְקִירֶנָּה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בֵּינוֹ לְבֵין עַצְמוֹ קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת.
Translation
It is mutar to rent or lend one's animal to a non-Jew on condition that he return it before Shabbos. If the non-Jew is over the tenai and doesn't return it on time, the Jew can be mafkir it (declare it hefker) even bein adam l'atzmo (privately), before Shabbos — at which point he is no longer chayav on its shvisa, since the animal isn't his anymore.

Although a hefker normally requires three witnesses miderabbanan, here — since the Jew genuinely wants to escape an issur d'Oraisa — we assume he is mafkir b'lev shaleim, and the hefker takes effect even privately.

But no one else can take possession of the animal — because the Jew is only mafkir for the duration of Shabbos, and motzaei Shabbos the animal automatically reverts to him.

An elegant halachic solution

If the situation has gone south (the non-Jew kept the animal past Shabbos despite the tenai), halacha offers an emergency exit: declare the animal hefker for the duration of Shabbos. Since the chiyuv of shvisas behemto applies only to an animal one owns, hefker dissolves the issur.

A subtle point: the hefker is time-bounded (only for Shabbos), and the animal reverts automatically motzaei Shabbos — an application of the rule that hefker can be limited in time (Choshen Mishpat siman 273).

Seif י׳
Alternative — transferring ownership for Shabbos day
Siman 246 · Seif Yod
וְאִם הוּא רוֹצֶה יָכוֹל לְהַקְנוֹת גּוּף הַבְּהֵמָה לְנָכְרִי זֶה לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת, וְהוּא שֶׁיֹּאמַר לוֹ בְּפָנָיו "בְּהֶמְתִּי קְנוּיָה לְךָ", שֶׁאָז מִתְכַּוֵּן הַנָּכְרִי לִקְנוֹתָהּ, וְהִיא נִקְנֵית לוֹ בַּאֲמִירָה זוֹ.
Translation
If he wishes, the Jew can transfer ownership of the guf habeheima to the non-Jew for the day of Shabbos, before Shabbos. For this hakna'ah to take effect, he must say in the non-Jew's presence: "My animal is acquired to you" (behemti kenuya lecha). At that point the non-Jew has da'as to acquire, and the kinyan takes effect through this amira since the animal is already in his reshus. But if the Jew says it not in his presence, no kinyan happens.

Second solution — temporary sale

In addition to hefker (Seif Tes), the Shulchan Aruch HaRav offers a second mehalech: transfer ownership of the animal to the non-Jew just for Shabbos day. The mechanism: oral declaration in the non-Jew's presence (kinyan amira).

This technique will be developed and expanded in seifim 11-18 (partnership in the animal, sale with reservations, etc.), and it's the big chiddush of this siman: building legal structures of partial transfer that allow business activity while preserving the spirit of Shabbos.
