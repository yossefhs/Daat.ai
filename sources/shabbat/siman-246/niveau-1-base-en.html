Siman 246 · 19 Seifim

Siman רמ״ו · Level 1 — Lending and renting objects to a non-Jew

Pedagogical study of the 19 seifim of the Shulchan Aruch HaRav — from lending tools to the temporary transfer of an animal
Section 1 — Seif Alef
The text of the Shulchan Aruch (Seif א׳)
שולחן ערוך, אורח חיים, סימן רמ״ו, סעיף א׳ — הטקסט המלא
מֻתָּר לְהַשְׁאִיל וּלְהַשְׂכִּיר כֵּלִים לְגוֹי, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בָּהֶם מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁאֵין אָנוּ מְצֻוִּים עַל שְׁבִיתַת כֵּלִים. בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁהִשְׁאִילָן וְהִשְׂכִּירָן לוֹ מִקֹּדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת. אֲבָל בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה, אָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָן אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִילָן לוֹ, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כְּאִלּוּ הוּא שְׁלוּחוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת מְלָאכָה. וְאָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָן לוֹ אֲפִלּוּ מִקֹּדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת בִּסְכַר יוֹם, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כְּמַשְׂתַּכֵּר בְּשַׁבָּת, אֲבָל בְּהַבְלָעָה — מֻתָּר.
הגה׃ וְהַשְׁאָלָה מֻתֶּרֶת אֲפִלּוּ עֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה, רַק שֶׁלֹּא יְקַבֵּל שָׂכָר אִם הוּא כְּלִי שֶׁמְּלַאכְתּוֹ לְאִסּוּר. וְכָל זֶה בְּשֶׁאֵין הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מַצְוֵהוּ עַל הַמְּלָאכָה.
Full English translation
It is mutar to lend (sheilah / השאלה) and to rent out (sechiras / השכרה) kelim to a non-Jew, even though he does melacha with them on Shabbos, because we are not commanded on the rest of kelim (shvisas kelim / שביתת כלים).

When does this apply? When they were lent or rented out to him before Shabbos. But on erev Shabbos, close to nightfall (samuch la-chasheicha), it is asur to rent out or lend them, because it looks as if the non-Jew is his shaliach (שלוחו) to do the melacha.

And it is asur to rent them out even before Shabbos at a daily rate (sechar yom / סכר יום), because it looks like earning wages on Shabbos (mistaker be-Shabbos / schar Shabbos). But through havla'ah (הבלעה — bundling several days into one combined fee) — it is mutar.
Hagahah of the Rema (for Ashkenazim): And sheilah (השאלה — lending without compensation) is mutar even on erev Shabbos close to nightfall, provided he does not receive payment, if it is a kli she-melachto le-issur (כלי שמלאכתו לאיסור — a kli whose typical use is something assur on Shabbos). And all of this applies only when the Yid does not instruct the non-Jew to do the melacha.
⚖
Two sources of the issur — must be distinguished
① Samuch la-chasheicha (close to nightfall) — gezeira of nireh ki-shlucho (נראה כשלוחו): it looks like the non-Jew is the Yid's shaliach.
② Sechar yom (daily rate) — gezeira of schar Shabbos: the Yid earns money from melacha done on Shabbos.
Havla'ah (bundling) dissolves the second problem: when the rental fee covers several days and is not broken down day-by-day, the "Shabbos wages" disappear.
Section 2
Understanding the halacha

What is this Seif about?

This Seif deals with a very common situation: a Yid owns tools, a car, professional equipment. May he rent them out or lend them to a non-Jew, knowing the non-Jew will use them on Shabbos?

The answer depends on what the Yid knows at the time of the lending or renting. If the Yid knows the kelim will be used on Shabbos — it is asur. If he does not know — it is mutar.

💡
Why is it asur?
The main reason is schar Shabbos — the Yid receives rental money for a day when his object "worked" on Shabbos. Even though the Yid himself did nothing, benefiting from melacha done on Shabbos is asur.
📜
The Rema's psak for Ashkenazim
The Rema goes further than the Mechaber: not only is it asur to rent out, but also to lend free of charge — even though the Yid receives nothing. Why? Because even a free loan creates the appearance that the Yid is sending his shaliach to do melacha on Shabbos (nireh ki-shlucho).
⏰
When must the kli be handed over?
The issur applies even if the kli is handed over on erev Shabbos (Friday). The fact that the deal was made before Shabbos does not permit the Shabbos use. The key is the Yid's knowledge: did he know the kli would be used on Shabbos?
Section 3
The 3 fundamental concepts
①
שְׁבִיתַת כֵּלִים
Shvisas kelim — rest of the kelim
According to Beis Shammai, a Yid's kelim must "rest" on Shabbos, even in the hands of a non-Jew. Beis Hillel (whom we follow le-halacha) hold there is no chiyuv of shvisas kelim per se — but other issurim still apply.
②
נִרְאֶה כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ
Appearance of agency
If a Yid lends his object to a non-Jew who uses it on Shabbos publicly, people might think the non-Jew is acting as the Yid's shaliach — as if the Yid himself were having someone work in his place. This appearance is asur.
③
שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת
Schar Shabbos
The issur of taking financial benefit from melacha performed during Shabbos. Even if the rental contract was signed before Shabbos, the Yid receives money for an "active" day of Shabbos — this is problematic.
Section 4
The practical pesak
פסק הלכה — Final ruling
אסור להשכיר או להשאיל כלים לגוי כשיודע שישתמש בהם בשבת
For all (Sephardim and Ashkenazim): It is asur to rent out one's kelim to a non-Jew when he knows he will use them on Shabbos.

For Ashkenazim (Rema): It is also asur to lend one's kelim, even free of charge, under the same conditions.
Section 5
The key concept: הבלעה (havla'ah)

Why havla'ah permits the rental

Schar Shabbos is asur because it "looks" like income directly tied to melacha done on Shabbos. But when the rental fee is bundled into an overall price covering several days, the Shabbos profit is "absorbed" into the mass — it is no longer perceived as a specific gain from Shabbos.

This is analogous to the principle of batel be-rov (בטל ברוב): something minor and indistinguishable is neutralized.

✅
Mutar example (successful havla'ah)
Renting out a tool for 7 days for $100 total — the Shabbos profit is bundled into the package, not isolated. Mutar.
❌
Asur example (sechar yom)
Renting out the same tool at $15 per day, which means $15 for Shabbos alone. The Shabbos gain is isolated, perceptible — asur.
⚠
Limit: Shabbos must not be the main element
Renting for Shabbos alone, or for Shabbos + Sunday where Shabbos is the main part, is not considered true havla'ah — the essence of schar Shabbos remains dominant.
Section 6
Lending vs renting — the Rema's חידוש

The Rema's distinction: השאלה ≠ שכירות

The Rema clearly distinguishes two situations that do not follow the same rule:

  • Sheilah / השאלה (lending without compensation) — mutar even on erev Shabbos close to nightfall, provided it is not a kli she-melachto le-issur (a kli whose typical use is melacha that violates Shabbos).
  • Sechiras / שכירות (renting with compensation) — always asur at sechar yom (daily rate); mutar only with havla'ah.

Lending (free) before Shabbos — kli umnus⚠️ Disputed (Rema stricter)
Lending (free) close to nightfall — neutral kli✅ Mutar
Lending (free) close to nightfall — kli umnus❌ ASUR (Rema)

Section 7
The final pesak — Sephardi vs Ashkenazi
🕎
Sephardi — Mechaber
Follows Rambam / Rif / Rosh: no chiyuv of shvisas kelim. Thus fewer restrictions on klei umnus. Permits lending close to nightfall even for tools typically "le-issur." Source: the Shulchan Aruch text as is.
❄️
Ashkenazi — Rema
Adds a stricter chiddush (following Tosafos in part): for klei umnus (tools whose typical use violates Shabbos), it is asur to lend close to nightfall, even free of charge. And for renting, he keeps his chiddush on havla'ah.
Section 8
Modern practical cases
Asur
I rent out my car to my non-Jewish neighbor Friday night, knowing he will use it on Shabbos.
I know he will use it on Shabbos → schar Shabbos + nireh ki-shlucho. Asur even according to the Mechaber.
Asur (Ashkenazim)
I lend my tools for free close to nightfall to a non-Jew who uses them on Shabbos.
Even without compensation, asur per the Rema because of nireh ki-shlucho (for klei umnus).
Mutar
I rent out my drill for the week to a contractor (flat 7-day package), and he uses it every day.
Rental via havla'ah — the Shabbos profit is bundled into the overall package. Mutar.
Mutar

Free loan, well before Shabbos, on a neutral object (not a kli umnus). Mutar for all.
Mutar

Asur
I rent out my camera at $30 per day to a non-Jew for Shabbos alone.
Daily rate (סכר יום) isolated on Shabbos → schar Shabbos. Asur even without knowing the precise use.

Long-term rental — specific halachos apply (השכרת בית), often mutar but consult a Rav.

Section 9
To remember — golden rules
The 4 essential rules
① Lending before Shabbos = OK
No chiyuv on the rest of the kelim. Lending or renting in advance (Thursday, Wednesday…) is mutar according to Beis Hillel.

② Close to nightfall = ASUR to rent out
So as not to appear to be sending the non-Jew to do melacha in our place (nireh ki-shlucho).

③ Sechar yom = ALWAYS ASUR
Daily rate = schar Shabbos. Havla'ah (multi-day bundle) solves the problem.

④ The Rema adds a chumra for klei umnus
For kelim whose typical use violates Shabbos, lending free of charge close to nightfall is also asur (Ashkenazi).

Part II

The 19 Seifim of the Shulchan Aruch HaRav

After Seif Alef which sets the general rule (renting/lending to a non-Jew, the distinction between havla'ah and sechar yom), the Siman develops the doctrine over eighteen more seifim. The reference text is that of the Shulchan Aruch HaRav (the Alter Rebbe), who summarizes and rules between the positions of the Mechaber, the Rema, the Rishonim and the Acharonim.

The throughline moves gradually from kelim (objects) — rabbinic territory — toward the behema (working animal) — a Torah issur by shvisas behemto. The last seven seifim develop sophisticated legal mechanisms (hefker, hakna'ah, apothiki, mashkon) to allow economic cooperation with a non-Jew while preserving the spirit of Shabbos.

2. Seif Beis — The klei umnus (professional tools): asur to rent out on erev Shabbos

Full text of the SHHaRav

יֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁהַכֵּלִים שֶׁעוֹשִׂים בָּהֶם מְלָאכָה, כְּגוֹן רֵחַיִם בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁמֻתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָם לְנָכְרִי, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ג, וּמַחֲרֵשָׁה וְכַיּוֹצֵא בָהֶן מִשְּׁאָר כְּלֵי אֻמָּנוּת — אָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָם לְנָכְרִי בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁיֵּשׁ רֶוַח לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בְּמַה שֶּׁהַכֵּלִים נִשְׂכָּרִים לְנָכְרִי גַּם בְּיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, כִּי יָדוּעַ הוּא אַף שֶׁמַּשְׂכִּירָם לוֹ לְשָׁבוּעַ אוֹ לְחֹדֶשׁ אִם הָיָה מַשְׂכִּיר לוֹ חוּץ מִשַׁבָּתוֹת שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה רַשַּׁאי לַעֲשׂוֹת בָּהֶם מְלָאכָה גַּם בְּשַׁבָּת — הָיָה הַנָּכְרִי פּוֹחֵת לוֹ מְעַט מִשְּׂכִירוּת שֶׁנּוֹתֵן עַכְשָׁו כְּשֶׁמַּשְׂכִּיר לוֹ סְתָם וְיֵשׁ לוֹ רְשׁוּת לַעֲשׂוֹת גַּם בְּשַׁבָּת, לְפִיכָךְ כְּשֶׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת סָמוּךְ לְיוֹם שֶׁהִשְׂכִּירָם לוֹ דְּהַיְנוּ עֶרֶב שַׁבָּת — הֲרֵי נִרְאֶה כְּאִלּוּ עוֹשֶׂה בִּשְׁלִיחוּת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁצִּוָּהוּ לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת כְּשֶׁהִשְׂכִּירָם לוֹ אֶתְמוֹל, כֵּיוָן שֶׁיֵּשׁ רֶוַח לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּמַה שֶּׁהוּא רַשַּׁאי לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת. וְכֵן עִקָּר:

English translation

Yesh omrim: kelim with which one does melacha — like a millstone (in places where it is mutar to rent it out to a non-Jew, as explained in siman רמ"ג), a plow, and other klei umnus (professional tools) — are asur to rent out to a non-Jew on erev Shabbos.

The reason: there is a benefit for the Yid in the fact that the kelim are rented to the non-Jew also on the day of Shabbos. For it is known that if the rental had excluded the Shabbosos, the non-Jew would have lowered the price slightly. The fact that he pays the full price including Shabbos proves that part of the rental corresponds precisely to the Shabbos work.

Therefore, when the non-Jew does melacha with the tools on Shabbos right after receiving them (erev Shabbos), it appears as if he is the Yid's shaliach (k'shlichus haYisrael) — as if the Yid had instructed him yesterday to do the work today on Shabbos. V'chein ikar (וכן עיקר — the Rav's clear ruling).

What is this Seif about?

Seif Alef set down a simple rule: one may rent out one's kelim to a non-Jew (with havla'ah). Seif Beis introduces a first restriction: for a special category — the klei umnus (work-tools) — renting on erev Shabbos itself is asur, even with havla'ah.

כְּלִי אֻמָּנוּת = professional production tools. These are tools whose use in itself generates money (millstone, plow, baker's oven). Their rental price builds in a supplement corresponding to Shabbos use — which makes them, when handed over immediately before Shabbos, equivalent to a mission given by the Yid to the non-Jew.

The halachic question

If Seif Alef permits rental with havla'ah, why does Seif Beis re-prohibit it for klei umnus on erev Shabbos? The Rav's answer: economic analysis here reveals a direct benefit for the Yid linked to Shabbos; handing over the tool just before Shabbos suggests a commission from the Yid → nireh ki-shlucho.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Rishonim (yesh omrim)For work-tools, rental on erev Shabbos is asur: the rental premium reveals the Shabbos use.
Mechaber (246:1)Cites the yesh omrim without explicitly ruling, but the Acharonim gloss as ikar.
RemaFollows the strict position on klei umnus.
Mishnah Berurah (246:4-5)Rules like the yesh omrim; specifies the restriction applies only on erev Shabbos itself (before, mutar).

The Rav's pesak

The rule of klei umnus:
  1. Rentals mid-week (before erev Shabbos): mutar even for work-tools (classic havla'ah).
  2. Rental on erev Shabbos of a work-tool (millstone, plow, production machine): asur — the timing reveals the mission.
  3. The decisive criterion: is there an economic benefit for the Yid linked specifically to the Shabbos work?
  • Renting my cement mixer to a non-Jewish contractor, signed Thursday for 2 weeks: 🟢 early rental → classic havla'ah mutar.
  • Renting him the same mixer Friday afternoon for 2 weeks: 🔴 erev Shabbos + klei umnus → asur.
  • Renting my professional printer to a neighbor Friday noon for the weekend: 🔴 asur (klei umnus on erev Shabbos).
  • Renting my light tool (drill) for a month, signed Wednesday: 🟢 mutar (overall rental).
  • Renting my CNC mill Friday 3pm for a 10-day cycle: 🔴 klei umnus + erev Shabbos → asur even with havla'ah.

Points to remember

  • The klei umnus (work-tools) have a reinforced status: asur to rent on erev Shabbos
  • Reason: the rental premium corresponds to the Shabbos use → nireh ki-shlucho
  • The Rav rules "v'chein ikar" — final decision
  • Before erev Shabbos: classic havla'ah mutar
Comprehension question: If havla'ah was already accepted in Seif Alef, what changes for klei umnus on erev Shabbos? Why does temporal proximity alter the halachic analysis?

3. Seif Gimmel — Lending (sheilah) of a klei umnus for free is mutar even on erev Shabbos

Full text of the SHHaRav

אֲבָל לְהַשְׁאִיל לוֹ כֵּלִים שֶׁעוֹשִׂים בָּהֶם מְלָאכָה — מֻתָּר אֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁאֵין רֶוַח לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּמַה שֶּׁהַנָּכְרִי רַשַּׁאי לַעֲשׂוֹת בָּהֶם בְּשַׁבָּת — אֵין נִרְאֶה כְּאִלּוּ עוֹשֶׂה בִּשְׁלִיחוּתוֹ.

וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם מַשְׁאִילוֹ בִּתְנַאי עַל מְנָת שֶׁיַּשְׁאִילֶנּוּ הַנָּכְרִי כְּלִי אַחֵר לְאַחַר הַשַּׁבָּת, וְנִמְצָא שֶׁיֵּשׁ רֶוַח לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּמַה שֶּׁמַּשְׁאִיל לְהַנָּכְרִי גַּם בְּשַׁבָּת, מִכָּל מָקוֹם כֵּיוָן שֶׁאֵינוֹ נוֹטֵל שְׂכִירוּת מַמָּשׁ בְּעַד זֶה — לֹא גָזְרוּ חֲכָמִים:

English translation

But to lend (sheilah — for free) a kli with which melacha is done to a non-Jew — it is mutar even on erev Shabbos close to nightfall. Since there is no benefit for the Yid from the fact that the non-Jew is permitted to use them on Shabbos, he does not appear as his shaliach.

And even if the loan is conditional on a reciprocal loan of another kli from the non-Jew after Shabbos — and there is therefore a benefit for the Yid in lending to the non-Jew also during Shabbos — nonetheless, since he does not actually take rent for it, Chazal did not gezeira here.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav contrasts: renting a klei umnus on erev Shabbos is asur (Seif Beis), but free lending of the same tool — even on late erev Shabbos — is mutar. The decisive criterion is financial benefit.

הַשְׁאָלָה vs הַשְׂכָּרָה:
  • Sheilah (lending) = without compensation. No benefit → no appearance of agency.
  • Hasecharah / sechiras (renting) = with rent. Rent creates a Shabbos benefit → appearance of agency.

The halachic question

And what if the loan is reciprocal ("I lend you my tool today, you lend me yours next week")? There is an economic advantage. The Rav answers: what matters for the gezeira of nireh ki-shlucho is the actual taking of monetary rent (sechirus mamash), not a barter of services. The gezeira was not extended there.

The opinions

SourcePosition
RishonimSheilah without compensation ≠ hascharah. No benefit → no appearance of agency.
Mechaber (246:1)Permits the loan even on late erev Shabbos for klei umnus.
RemaAgrees, but adds the restriction of Seif Alef (kli she-melachto le-issur).
Mishnah Berurah (246:6-7)Details: reciprocal loan mutar as long as there is no monetary transaction.
SHHaRav (246:3)Rules: loan mutar even on late erev Shabbos, and even if conditional on a return loan, since "eino notel sechirus mamash."

The Rav's pesak

The three levels:
  1. Free loan (sheilah) of a klei umnus even on late erev Shabbos → mutar.
  2. Reciprocal loan (exchange of services) → mutar (no monetary rent).
  3. Rental (hascharah) on erev Shabbos → asur (cf. Seif Beis).
  • I lend my circular saw Friday 4pm to my non-Jewish neighbor: 🟢 free loan even late → mutar.
  • I lend my professional ladder to a non-Jewish painter for the weekend: 🟢 sheilah mutar even on erev Shabbos.
  • "I lend you my drill today, lend me your grinder Monday": 🟢 service barter, no monetary rent → mutar.
  • "I lend it to you, but in exchange you'll pay me $20": 🔴 disguised rental → asur on erev Shabbos.
  • Lending my ladder to the non-Jewish neighbor Friday 2pm for known Shabbos use: 🟢 mutar (Mechaber/Sephardim). Rema: depending on tool type (cf. Seif Alef for kli she-melachto le-issur).

Points to remember

  • Loan ≠ rental: the gezeira of nireh ki-shlucho applies only to monetary rental
  • Even on late erev Shabbos, the free lending of a work-tool is mutar
  • Reciprocal lending (service exchange) remains mutar: no sechirus mamash
  • The Rav's criterion: "netilas sechirus mamash" — taking actual rent
Comprehension question: Why does a reciprocal exchange of loans (with an obvious economic advantage for the Yid) not fall under the gezeira of nireh ki-shlucho, while a monetary rental — where the advantage is sometimes no greater — is asur?

4. Seif Daled — Maris ayin: the non-Jew leaves my house with my object on Shabbos

Full text of the SHHaRav

אָסוּר לְהַשְׁאִיל שׁוּם חֵפֶץ לְנָכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת, וַאֲפִלּוּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, אִם הוּא סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה כָּל כָּךְ עַד שֶׁאֵין שָׁהוּת לַנָּכְרִי לְהוֹצִיא הַחֵפֶץ מִפֶּתַח בֵּיתוֹ שֶׁל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לִרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם — אָסוּר לְהַשְׁאִילוֹ, מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, שֶׁהָרוֹאֶה אֶת הַנָּכְרִי יוֹצֵא מִבֵּית הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל וְחֵפֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּיָדוֹ יַחְשׁוֹד אֶת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁהוּא שׁוֹלֵחַ חֲפָצָיו בְּשַׁבָּת עַל יְדֵי נָכְרִי זֶה, שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲלֶה עַל דַּעְתּוֹ שֶׁהִשְׁאִיל לוֹ וְהוּא מוֹצִיאוֹ לְצֹרֶךְ עַצְמוֹ.

וַאֲפִלּוּ בְּעִיר שֶׁאֵין בָּהּ רְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים אֶלָּא כַּרְמְלִית, שֶׁאֵין אִסּוּר שֶׁל תּוֹרָה אֲפִלּוּ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל לְהוֹצִיא לְתוֹכָהּ מֵרְשׁוּת הַיָּחִיד, אַף עַל פִּי כֵן כֵּיוָן שֶׁמִּדִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים אָסוּר לְהוֹצִיא אֲפִלּוּ עַל יְדֵי נָכְרִי שֶׁלֹּא לְצֹרֶךְ מִצְוָה כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן ש"ז, אִם כֵּן יַחְשְׁדוּהוּ שֶׁהוּא עוֹבֵר עַל דִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים:

English translation

It is asur to lend any object to a non-Jew on Shabbos itself, and even on erev Shabbos — if one is so close to nightfall that the non-Jew does not have time to take the object out of the Yid's door into reshus harabim before nightfall. The reason: maris ayin — whoever sees the non-Jew leaving the Yid's house with the Yid's object in his hand will suspect the Yid of sending out his things on Shabbos via this non-Jew (it will not occur to him that it is a mere loan and the non-Jew is carrying it for his own needs).

And even in a town that has no reshus harabim mid'oraisa but only a karmelis (where carrying from a reshus hayachid is asur only mid'rabbanan), it is asur. For Chazal forbade carrying out even via a non-Jew without a need of mitzvah (siman ש"ז) — so people will suspect the Yid of transgressing a word of the Sofrim.

What is this Seif about?

New register: we leave the problem of schar Shabbos / nireh ki-shlucho (tied to the rental of Seif Alef-Beis) for an entirely different problem: maris ayin — the appearance of a transgressor of the issur of hotza'ah (transfer between domains).

מַרְאִית הָעַיִן = "what the eye sees". Even if the action itself is mutar, if it resembles a transgression to an observer, it is asur. Here: seeing a non-Jew leave my house on Shabbos with my belongings makes it look like I sent him.

The halachic question

If there is time before nightfall for the non-Jew to leave with the object, the loan is mutar (the passerby will simply see a non-Jew going home with his object on Friday). But if nightfall comes while the object is still at my house, any Shabbos passerby will suspect. This applies even in a karmelis (semi-public area), because even there carrying via a non-Jew is asur mid'rabbanan for Yidden.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Talmud / RishonimLending on Shabbos or very close to nightfall is asur because of maris ayin.
Mechaber (246:1-2)Cites the rule; restriction even for karmelis.
RemaAccepts; recalls the distinction between town with eiruv and without.
Mishnah Berurah (246:8-10)Specifies: "shahus k'dei l'hotzi" = time to actually exit the house into the street.
SHHaRav (246:4)Specifies the scope of the gezeira: applies even in a karmelis, since even there carrying via a non-Jew is asur mid'rabbanan → identical suspicion.

The Rav's pesak

The rule of an object leaving on Shabbos:
  1. Lending on Shabbos: asur (maris ayin).
  2. Lending on erev Shabbos if nightfall comes before the non-Jew leaves: asur.
  3. Lending on erev Shabbos if the non-Jew has time to leave before nightfall: mutar.
  4. Applies even in a karmelis (semi-public area) — since carrying there via a non-Jew is also asur mid'rabbanan.
  • My non-Jewish neighbor rings at 6:45 pm Friday (nightfall 7 pm) to borrow a tool: 🔴 he will not have time to leave → asur.
  • He comes at 2 pm Friday for the same tool: 🟢 plenty of time to leave → mutar.
  • He rings Shabbos noon to borrow my lawnmower: 🔴 Shabbos loan → asur (maris ayin).
  • My non-Jewish colleague wants to return a sefer to me on Shabbos (he brings me his object): 🟢 he is bringing it, I am not taking anything out.
  • I live in a town without an eiruv; Friday 4 pm, my neighbor borrows an iron: 🟢 he has time to get home before nightfall.

Points to remember

  • New register: maris ayin (appearance of transgressing hotza'ah), not schar Shabbos
  • The test: "does the non-Jew have time to leave my house into the street before nightfall?"
  • Applies even in a karmelis (semi-public area) — the rabbinic issur is enough to create suspicion
  • Lending during Shabbos night itself is always asur
Comprehension question: Why does the halacha distinguish "before nightfall" (object out = mutar) vs "after" (object still at my house = asur) — when in both cases the loan itself is before Shabbos?

5. Seif Heh — Town with eiruv, and the new limit of techumin

Full text of the SHHaRav

אֲבָל בְּעִיר הַמְעֹרֶבֶת שֶׁמֻּתָּר לְטַלְטֵל בְּתוֹכָהּ — מֻתָּר לְהַשְׁאִילוֹ אֲפִלּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת אִם הַנָּכְרִי דָר בְּמָקוֹם הַמְעֹרָב שֶׁמֻּתָּר לְטַלְטֵל בּוֹ. אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ דָר בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁאֵינוֹ מְעֹרָב, אֶלָּא בְּעִנְיָן שֶׁאֵין לָחוּשׁ לְמַרְאִית הָעַיִן שֶׁל הָרוֹאֶה אוֹתוֹ יוֹצֵא מִבֵּיתוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אִם יָדוּעַ לוֹ שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי יוֹלִיכֶנּוּ הַיּוֹם חוּץ לַתְּחוּם — אָסוּר לְהַשְׁאִילוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, לְפִי שֶׁחֶפְצוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁהָיוּ בִּרְשׁוּתוֹ בְּבֵין הַשְּׁמָשׁוֹת קָנוּ אֶצְלוֹ שְׁבִיתָה בִּשְׁבִיתָתוֹ, דְּהַיְנוּ אַלְפַּיִם אַמָּה לְכָל רוּחַ וְלֹא יוֹתֵר.

וְיֵשׁ מַתִּירִין אֲפִלּוּ לִמְסוֹר לוֹ בְּיָדַיִם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי הוּא הַמּוֹצִיאָן חוּץ לַתְּחוּם מִדַּעְתּוֹ לְצֹרֶךְ עַצְמוֹ, וְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל אֵינוֹ אֶלָּא גּוֹרֵם בְּעָלְמָא. וְכֵן עִקָּר.

וּלְעִנְיַן הֲלָכָה, יֵשׁ לְהָקֵל בִּשְׁאֵלָה בְּעֵת הַצֹּרֶךְ כִּסְבָרָא הָרִאשׁוֹנָה.

English translation

But in a town covered by an eiruv (where carrying on Shabbos is mutar), it is mutar to lend even on Shabbos — provided the non-Jew lives within the eiruv area. Or even if he lives outside the eiruv, as long as there is no concern of maris ayin (e.g., if the Yid's house is far enough from the edge of the eiruv that no one will see the non-Jew crossing from the eiruv area to the non-eiruv area).

Nevertheless, if the Yid knows the non-Jew will take the object beyond the techumin (the permitted 2000 amos), it is asur to lend on Shabbos: the Yid's belongings present in his domain at bein hashmashos acquired their shvisa (movement limit) — 2000 amos in every direction, no more.

And yesh matirin even to hand it over directly, since it is the non-Jew who takes the object out beyond the techum of his own accord for his own needs — the Yid is only a cause (gorem). V'chein ikar (וכן עיקר). For practical halacha, one may be lenient with sheilah in case of need according to the first opinion.

What is this Seif about?

Seif Daled set the issur of lending on Shabbos because of maris ayin. Seif Heh relaxes this rule in two cases — and introduces a new restriction: techumin.

תְּחוּמִין = movement limits. The Yid's belongings acquire at bein hashmashos (twilight, start of Shabbos) a "place of rest" (shvisa) from which they may be moved no more than 2000 amos (~960m / about half a mile) in each direction. Beyond that, asur — even via a non-Jew.

The halachic question

If the Yid knows the non-Jew will leave with his object beyond the techumin: (1) there is an intrinsic issur techumin (the Yid's object cannot leave the 2000 amos); (2) does the Yid have the right to hand the object over directly, knowing this? Machlokes: according to the 1st opinion, only show, not hand over; according to the second, one may hand over because the non-Jew is the agent of himself, and the Yid is only a cause (gorem b'alma). The Rav rules like the second (v'chein ikar), with leniency in practice for need.

The opinions

SourcePosition
RambamNo obligation of techumin for the Yid's kelim when the non-Jew takes them out on his own.
Rashba / TosafosThe Yid's objects acquire shvisa; even via non-Jew, one must avoid taking them beyond 2000 amos.
Mechaber (246:3)Permits lending in an eiruv town if the non-Jew lives there; restriction of techumin.
Mishnah Berurah (246:11-15)Permits showing the object without handing it over; but b'eis hatzorech one may hand over.
SHHaRav (246:5)"V'chein ikar": one may hand over — the non-Jew is the agent of himself, the Yid is gorem b'alma. In practice: leniency for need.

The Rav's pesak

Four situations:
  1. Eiruv town + non-Jew lives in the area: Shabbos loan mutar.
  2. Eiruv town + non-Jew outside the area, but Yid's house far from the edge: mutar (no maris ayin).
  3. Knows the non-Jew will go beyond the techumin: the 1st opinion forbids handing over, only show. But the Rav rules: mutar to hand over (gorem b'alma) — v'chein ikar.
  4. In practice ("eis hatzorech"): leniency according to the 1st opinion too.
  • Town with eiruv, non-Jewish neighbor asks for a sefer on Shabbos: 🟢 mutar.
  • No eiruv, neighbor rings Shabbos noon for a tool: 🔴 maris ayin → asur.
  • Eiruv town, but I know the non-Jew will leave town (beyond 2000 amos) with my object: 🟡 Rashba: asur to hand over. SHHaRav rules: mutar (the non-Jew is gorem b'alma).
  • Case of need (urgent medical object): 🟢 leniency according to both opinions.
  • The non-Jew comes with his taxi to take a package on Shabbos to drive it far away: 🟢 per SHHaRav, one may hand over — the non-Jew acts on his own.

Points to remember

  • The eiruv in town relaxes the Shabbos loan (no more local maris ayin)
  • New limit: techumin — 2000 amos for the Yid's objects
  • Machlokes on hand-over when one knows it will be exceeded → SHHaRav "v'chein ikar": mutar
  • In practice: leniency in eis hatzorech (pressing need)
Comprehension question: How does the concept of "gorem b'alma" (the Yid is only a cause, the non-Jew is the direct agent) allow the Rav to rule for permission, even when the Yid knows the techumin will be exceeded?

6. Seif Vav — The non-Jew's objects deposited with the Yid: return mutar on Shabbos

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְכָל זֶה בַּחֲפָצִים שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֲבָל חֲפָצִים שֶׁל נָכְרִי שֶׁהָיוּ מֻפְקָדִים בְּיַד יִשְׂרָאֵל וּבָא לִטְּלוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת — יָכוֹל לִתְּנוֹ וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁיּוֹצִיאֶנּוּ לִרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן שכ"ה, עַיֵּן שָׁם תַּשְׁלוּם דִּינִים אֵלּוּ:

English translation

All this (the preceding seifim) concerns only the Yid's objects. But objects of the non-Jew that were simply held in pikadon (safekeeping) with a Yid, and the non-Jew comes to retrieve them on Shabbos: the Yid may hand them over to him, even though he will carry them into reshus harabim — as explained in siman שכ"ה where these halachos are detailed.

What is this Seif about?

A special but important case: the non-Jew has left his own object at my place (in pikadon). He comes on Shabbos to retrieve it. The entire problem of the preceding seifim (schar Shabbos, nireh ki-shlucho, maris ayin) does not apply, because this is no longer my object — it is his.

פִּקָּדוֹן (pikadon, deposit) = an object belonging to someone else, simply held in trust. The Yid is only a shomer — not an owner, not a lender. No appearance of agency can arise.

The halachic question

Why this permission, even though the non-Jew will carry the object into reshus harabim during Shabbos? The Rav's answer: the gezeiros of the preceding seifim protect the image of the Yid as owner of the object. When the object belongs to the non-Jew, no confusion is possible — the passerby understands that the non-Jew is simply retrieving his own property.

The rule of pikadon:

  2. He comes on Shabbos to retrieve it.
  3. I may hand it over to him — even though he will go out into reshus harabim.
  4. No maris ayin: any passerby understands it is his own property.

  • A non-Jewish delivery person forgot his package in my yard on Shabbos, comes to retrieve it on Shabbos: 🟢 return mutar.
  • My colleague entrusted his laptop to me on Thursday, comes Shabbos to pick it up: 🟢 mutar (subject: asur to turn it on, etc.).

  • A non-Jewish customer comes to pick up his shoes left at the Jewish shoemaker's on Shabbos (repair finished before Shabbos): 🟢 mutar.

  • The non-Jew's property deposited with the Yid may be returned on Shabbos — even into reshus harabim
  • Logic: no maris ayin possible (the passerby understands the return)
  • Additional details in siman שכ"ה (muktzeh cases, etc.)

7. Seif Zayin — Major turning point: the behema (animal) — shvisas behemto, Torah issur

Full text of the SHHaRav

אָסוּר לְהַשְׁאִיל בְּהֶמְתּוֹ לְנָכְרִי לַעֲשׂוֹת בָּהּ מְלָאכָה, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה בָּהּ גַּם בְּשַׁבָּת, וְאָדָם מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתַת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר "לְמַעַן יָנוּחַ שׁוֹרְךָ וַחֲמֹרֶךָ".

וְאַף לְהַשְׂכִּירָהּ לוֹ אָסוּר מִן הַתּוֹרָה, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמַּשְׂכִּירָהּ הַרְבֵּה יָמִים קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת, וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם מַתְנֶה עִמּוֹ שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשֶׂה בָּהּ כְּלוּם בְּשַׁבָּת וְהִבְטִיחַ לוֹ — אֵין זֶה מוֹעִיל, כִּי אֵין הַנָּכְרִי נֶאֱמָן עַל כָּךְ, מִטַּעַם שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן ש"ה, עַיֵּן שָׁם:

English translation

It is asur to lend one's behema (animal) to a non-Jew to do melacha with it — because the non-Jew will use it also on Shabbos, and a person is metzuveh on the rest of his animal, as the passuk says (Shemos 23:12): "In order that your ox and your donkey may rest" (לְמַעַן יָנוּחַ שׁוֹרְךָ וַחֲמֹרֶךָ).

And even renting it to a non-Jew is asur mid'Oraisa — even if rented many days before Shabbos, and even if one stipulates that it not be used on Shabbos and the non-Jew has promised: this is of no avail, because the non-Jew is not ne'eman (trustworthy) on this point (see siman ש"ה).

What is this Seif about?

Major turning point in the Siman. Until now, all issurim were rabbinic (gezeiros on kelim). From Seif Zayin to the end, we enter a biblical domain: shvisas behemto — the positive Torah commandment to give one's animal rest.

שְׁבִיתַת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ = rest of one's animal. Inscribed in the Aseres haDibros: "In order that your ox and your donkey may rest." It is a positive Torah commandment — a genuine issur Torah, not a mere rabbinic gezeira. This reinforced status changes everything: no "simple" solution (havla'ah, free loan) suffices anymore.

The halachic question

Why is even rental made many days in advance asur mid'Oraisa, when for kelim it was mutar? The Rav's answer: the chiyuv is on the actual rest of the animal on Shabbos. As long as the animal belongs to the Yid (guf habehema shelo), it does not matter when the contract is signed: if the non-Jew uses it on Shabbos, the Yid transgresses. And the non-Jew's promise is not trustworthy.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Torah (Shemos 23:12, Aseres haDibros)"In order that your ox and your donkey may rest" — explicit obligation.
Talmud (Shabbos 5a, AZ 15a)Shvisas behemto = issur Torah; cannot be circumvented by rental.
Mechaber (246:3)Forbids the rental or loan of an animal, mid'Oraisa.
RemaAgrees; adds the unreliability of the non-Jew's promise.
Mishnah Berurah (246:18-20)The whole difficulty of the Siman stems from this point: for animals, the solution must be structural (sale, hefker).
SHHaRav (246:7)Clearly lays down the rabbinic → biblical transition; refers to siman ש"ה for the non-reliability of the non-Jew on the promise not to use.

The Rav's pesak

The biblical issur of the behema:
  1. Lending an animal that will work on Shabbos → asur mid'Oraisa.
  2. Renting the same animal, even far in advance → asur mid'Oraisa.
  3. Stipulating not to use it on Shabbos + non-Jew's promise → does not suffice (ein hanochri ne'eman).
  4. The only valid solutions are structural: hefker (Seif Tes), sale (Seif Yod, Yod-Beis, Tes-Vav), shutfus with conditions (Seif Yod-Gimmel).
  • Renting my draft horse to a non-Jewish farmer for the season: 🔴 he will work on Shabbos → issur Torah.
  • Lending my donkey to the neighbor for a month with a promise "not Shabbos": 🔴 promise not trustworthy → asur.
  • My guide dog is used by my non-Jewish friend on Shabbos: 🟡 complex question — for a dog, the chiyuv shvisa is debated; consult a Rav.
  • Selling my donkey Friday night to no longer be its owner on Shabbos: 🟢 this is the path opened by the next seifim (Seif Yod).
  • My cows are at the communal barn managed by a non-Jew who will take them out on Shabbos: 🔴 guf habehema shelo → asur.

Points to remember

  • Turning point: shift from rabbinic to biblical (issur Torah)
  • Shvisas behemto = explicit commandment of the Aseres haDibros
  • No "simple" solution: not havla'ah, not free loan, not promise, not advance signing
  • Only structural solutions (Seifim Tes–Tes-Vav) solve the problem

8. Seif Ches — Sub-renting an animal that the Yid rented from a non-Jew

Full text of the SHHaRav

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁגּוּף הַבְּהֵמָה שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֲבָל יִשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁשָּׂכַר בְּהֵמָה מִנָּכְרִי — יָכוֹל לַחֲזוֹר וּלְהַשְׂכִּירָהּ אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִילָהּ לְנָכְרִי אַחֵר, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁאֵין גּוּף הַבְּהֵמָה שֶׁלּוֹ — אֵינוֹ מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתָתָהּ.

וְיֵשׁ מִי שֶׁאוֹסֵר לְהַשְׁאִילָהּ, לְפִי שֶׁיֵּשׁ לְהִסְתַּפֵּק שֶׁמָּא עַל יְדֵי הַשְּׂכִירוּת שֶׁשְּׂכָרָהּ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, שֶׁנּוֹתֵן דָּמִים לְהַנָּכְרִי בַּעֲדָהּ כְּדֵי שֶׁיְּהֵא רַשַּׁאי לַעֲשׂוֹת בָּהּ מְלָאכָה, נִקְנֶה לוֹ עַל יְדֵי כָךְ הַבְּהֵמָה לְיָמִים אֵלּוּ שֶׁשְּׂכָרָהּ לוֹ מֵהַנָּכְרִי. וְטוֹב לָחוּשׁ לִדְבָרָיו:

English translation

When does all this apply (the issur of Seif Zayin)? When the guf habehema (body of the animal) belongs to the Yid. But a Yid who rented an animal from a non-Jew may sub-rent or lend it to another non-Jew: since the body of the animal is not his, he is not metzuveh on its rest.

And yesh mi she-oser the loan (sheilah), because there is a sefek: perhaps through the rental, the Yid (who pays for the right to use it) has acquired the animal for those days. And it is good to take account of this opinion (b'tov lachush — by way of stringency).

What is this Seif about?

Reverse case: it is not the Yid who owns the animal, but who has rented it from a non-Jew. May he sub-rent (or lend) it to another non-Jew who will use it on Shabbos? The Rav distinguishes two opinions according to the legal conception of rental (sechirus).

גּוּף הַבְּהֵמָה = "body of the animal", real ownership. The question: does renting an animal transfer only a usage right (the "body" stays with the owner), or does it acquire the animal for the duration of the lease ("sechirus l'yoma mamkar")? This talmudic machlokes (BM 56b) determines here whether the issur of shvisas behemto applies to the renter.

The halachic question

If rental transfers only a usage right, the Yid is not the owner and has no obligation of shvisas behemto → he may sub-rent or lend. If rental creates acquisition (sechirus l'yoma mamkar), then during the lease the Yid is the de facto owner → the obligation applies. The Rav brings both: the first (Mechaber) permits, the second (yesh mi she-oser) forbids the loan — and advises to take account of this stringency.

The opinions

SourcePosition
1st opinion (Rishonim)Sechirus is not mamkar. The Yid renter may sub-rent or lend — no obligation of shvisa.
Yesh mi she-oserSechirus l'yoma mamkar; the Yid is acquirer for the duration → asur to lend (sheilah).
Mechaber (246:4)Permits sub-rental and loan according to the 1st opinion.
RemaMentions the machlokes; advises stringency for the loan.
Mishnah Berurah (246:21-23)"Tov lachush": do not lend; sub-rental remains mutar.
SHHaRav (246:8)Permits sub-rental (1st opinion); "tov lachush" to forbid the loan (sheilah) by stringency.

The Rav's pesak

An animal rented by the Yid from a non-Jew:
  1. Sub-rental to another non-Jew: mutar per the Rav (1st opinion; no obligation).
  2. Loan to another non-Jew: good to refrain by stringency (tov lachush).
  3. Reason for the stringency: sefek whether sechirus is equivalent to a temporary mamkar.
  • I rent a horse from my non-Jewish colleague for the year; I sub-rent it to another non-Jew: 🟢 sub-rental mutar (the animal is not mine).
  • Same situation, but I lend it to another non-Jew: 🟡 tov lachush — preferable to refrain.
  • My colleague rented me his tractor (treated as a motorized behema) and I sub-rent it: 🟡 machines have no chiyuv shvisa, but see Seif Beis for klei umnus.
  • I manage a non-Jew's animal on his behalf — pure management, no lease: 🟢 case of Seif Vav adapted — the animal stays the non-Jew's.
  • Rental of a vacation rental animal for the duration of the trip, sub-lent to a non-Jewish friend: 🟡 stringency recommended.

Points to remember

  • The decisive criterion is guf habehema — real ownership, not usage right
  • Sub-rental to another non-Jew: mutar (the Yid is not the owner)
  • Loan: tov lachush — preferable to refrain by stringency (sefek sechirus = mamkar)
  • This chiluk lays the groundwork for the next seifim's solutions (partial transfer, temporary sale)

9. Seif Tes — The hefker mechanism (declaration of ownerless property)

Full text of the SHHaRav

מֻתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּיר אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִיל בְּהֶמְתּוֹ לְנָכְרִי וּלְהַתְנוֹת עִמּוֹ שֶׁיַּחֲזִירֶנָּה לוֹ קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת. וְאִם עָבַר הַנָּכְרִי עַל תְּנָאוֹ וְעִכְּבָהּ וְלֹא הֶחֱזִירָהּ קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת — יַפְקִירֶנָּה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בֵּינוֹ לְבֵין עַצְמוֹ קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁאָז אֵינוֹ מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתָתָהּ, כֵּיוָן שֶׁאֵינָהּ שֶׁלּוֹ, שֶׁכְּבָר הִפְקִירָהּ.

וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמִּדִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים כָּל הֶפְקֵר שֶׁאֵינוֹ בִּפְנֵי שְׁלֹשָׁה אֵינוֹ הֶפְקֵר כְּלָל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמַּפְקִיר בֵּינוֹ לְבֵין עַצְמוֹ — בְּוַדַּאי מַפְקִיר בְּלֵב שָׁלֵם, כְּדֵי שֶׁיִּנָּצֵל מֵאִסּוּר שֶׁל תּוֹרָה.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אֵין אָדָם אַחֵר יָכוֹל לִזְכּוֹת בָּהּ אֲפִלּוּ אִם מַפְקִירָהּ בִּפְנֵי שְׁלֹשָׁה כִּשְׁאָר הֶפְקֵר, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁאֵינָהּ מַפְקִירָהּ אֶלָּא כְּדֵי לְהַפְקִיעַ מֵעָלָיו אִסּוּר שַׁבָּת בִּלְבָד, אִם כֵּן מִן הַסְּתָם אֵינוֹ מַפְקִירָהּ אֶלָּא לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בִּלְבָד. וּבְמוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת מִיָּד הִיא חוֹזֶרֶת לִהְיוֹת שֶׁלּוֹ:

English translation

It is mutar to rent or lend one's animal to a non-Jew on condition that he return it before Shabbos. If the non-Jew is over the tenai and keeps it without returning it before Shabbos, the Yid declares the animal hefker bein adam l'atzmo (between himself and himself) before Shabbos. At that point he is no longer metzuveh on its shvisa, since the animal is no longer his.

Although, according to the Chachamim, any hefker not made in front of three witnesses is not valid as hefker; nevertheless, even if he declares between himself and himself — he certainly declares b'lev shaleim (with a complete heart), in order to escape an issur Torah.

Nevertheless, no other person can "win" (lizkos) this animal — even if it was declared in front of three witnesses like a normal hefker. For since the purpose is only to escape the Shabbos issur, the hefker is limited to the day of Shabbos alone. And at motzaei Shabbos, the animal immediately reverts to the Yid's ownership.

What is this Seif about?

First structural solution to the biblical problem of Seif Zayin. The Yid rented his animal on condition of return before Shabbos; the non-Jew did not keep his promise → the animal is still in his possession and he will use it on Shabbos. The Rav offers a sophisticated halachic exit: the temporary and private hefker.

הֶפְקֵר = declaration of property as ownerless. Normally, hefker requires being made in front of 3 witnesses (otherwise: invalid per the Chachamim). But here, exception: (1) it is done to escape an issur Torah → the intent is sincere; (2) it is limited to the time of Shabbos; (3) no one else may acquire the animal — it reverts to the Yid at motzaei Shabbos.

The halachic question

Several problems: (1) Is hefker in front of one (oneself) valid? Answer: yes here, since sincerity is guaranteed by the issur Torah being escaped. (2) May a third party take possession of the animal? Answer: no, because the hefker is time-limited (only Shabbos). (3) How can a temporary hefker be valid? Answer: siman רע"ג of Choshen Mishpat permits time-limited hefker.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Talmud (BM 30b, AZ 15a)Hefker saves from the issur of shvisas behemto; sincerity assumed when issur Torah.
RishonimFor emergency, accept private hefker.
Mechaber (246:3)Permits hefker bein adam l'atzmo to escape the issur.
RemaAgrees; specifies it is b'di'eved (as a last resort after failure of the condition).
Mishnah Berurah (246:25-28)Details the time limitation: hefker only for Shabbos; automatic return at motzaei Shabbos.
SHHaRav (246:9)Architecture of the temporary hefker: (a) initial condition, (b) private hefker valid in emergency, (c) time limitation, (d) cross-reference to Choshen Mishpat רע"ג for the principle of time-limited hefker.

The Rav's pesak

The sequence of Seif Tes:
  1. Always rent/lend on condition of return before Shabbos.
  2. If the non-Jew is over: declare hefker bein adam l'atzmo before Shabbos.
  3. Validity of private hefker: assured by sincerity (issur Torah being escaped).
  4. Limitation: hefker only for the time of Shabbos.
  5. At motzaei Shabbos: the animal automatically reverts to the Yid automatically.
  6. No third party may acquire the animal in the meantime.
  • I rented my horse for the week on condition of return Friday 5 pm; it is still not back at 6:45 pm: 🟢 declare hefker bein adam l'atzmo before nightfall.
  • I am traveling, without 3 witnesses; same situation: 🟢 private hefker valid (issur Torah).
  • Someone wants to acquire my animal during Shabbos: 🚫 impossible — the hefker is valid only for Shabbos.
  • At motzaei Shabbos, I want to repurchase the animal: 🚫 unnecessary — it is automatically mine.
  • For an agricultural drone rented to my non-Jewish partner who does not return it before Shabbos: 🟢 same mechanism — declare hefker before nightfall to avoid schar Shabbos (for a drone, not shvisas behemto).

Points to remember

  • First structural solution to the biblical problem: temporary hefker
  • Validity of private hefker guaranteed by sincerity (issur Torah)
  • Limitation: only for Shabbos; no third party may acquire
  • Automatic return at motzaei Shabbos
  • Always begin with the initial condition (return before Shabbos)

10. Seif Yod — Temporary sale by declaration (kinyan amira)

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְאִם הוּא רוֹצֶה יָכוֹל לְהַקְנוֹת גּוּף הַבְּהֵמָה לְנָכְרִי זֶה לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת, וְהוּא שֶׁיֹּאמַר לוֹ בְּפָנָיו "בְּהֶמְתִּי קְנוּיָה לְךָ", שֶׁאָז מִתְכַּוֵּן הַנָּכְרִי לִקְנוֹתָהּ, וְהִיא נִקְנֵית לוֹ בַּאֲמִירָה זוֹ כֵּיוָן שֶׁהִיא בִּרְשׁוּתוֹ, אֲבָל אִם אוֹמֵר כֵּן שֶׁלֹּא בְּפָנָיו — לֹא קְנָאָהּ הַנָּכְרִי כְּלָל:

English translation

And if he wishes, he can transfer the guf habehema of the animal to this non-Jew for the day of Shabbos — before Shabbos. And that is by saying to him in his presence: "My animal is acquired to you" (behemti kenuya lecha). At that point the non-Jew has da'as to acquire, and the animal is acquired to him by this very declaration, since it is already in his possession.

But if he says this not in his presence — the non-Jew has acquired nothing at all.

What is this Seif about?

Second structural solution (alternative to the hefker of Seif Tes). Instead of declaring the animal ownerless, the Yid sells it to the non-Jew for the day of Shabbos alone. This is a kinyan amira (acquisition by simple declaration), valid because the animal is already physically with the non-Jew.

קִנְיָן אֲמִירָה = acquisition by verbal declaration. Normally kinyanim (acquisitions) require a concrete act (meshicha, kesef, shtar…). But when the property is already in the physical possession of the acquirer, a simple declaration suffices (cf. Bava Basra 53). Here: the non-Jew possesses the animal → the declaration of transfer makes him owner in the halachic sense.

The halachic question

Why is the declaration in person indispensable? The Rav's answer: every kinyan requires the acquirer's intent (da'as makneh ve-da'as koneh). If the Yid says "behemti kenuya lecha" out of the non-Jew's presence, the latter has no intent to acquire — the acquisition does not take effect.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Talmud (BB 53a)Property already in possession may be acquired by simple declaration in person.
RishonimKinyan amira valid for temporary sale; da'as koneh requires presence.
Mechaber (246:3)Mentions temporary sale; the Rema specifies the presence condition.
RemaInsists: in his presence is essential to validate the acquisition.
Mishnah Berurah (246:29-32)Details the acceptable forms; alternative to hefker; preferable since the animal is never "ownerless."
SHHaRav (246:10)Lays down the presence rule: without the non-Jew, no da'as koneh, no acquisition. The mechanism foreshadows the solutions of seifim Yod-Alef–Yod-Ches.

The Rav's pesak

The temporary sale by declaration:
  1. Before Shabbos, declare in the non-Jew's presence: "behemti kenuya lecha" ("my animal is acquired to you").
  2. The animal must already be in his physical possession (he has already rented it).
  3. Out of presence: the kinyan does not take effect (no da'as koneh).
  4. The acquisition applies for the day of Shabbos alone.
  5. Preferable alternative to hefker (Seif Tes): the animal always has an owner (thus more legal protection).
  • My horse remained at a non-Jewish farmer's who is keeping it; I phone him Friday to say "behemti kenuya lecha": 🟡 by phone, some permit (he "hears" → it is b'fanav). Check with a Rav.
  • I go in person to tell him: 🟢 kinyan amira valid.
  • I send him a letter saying "behemti kenuya lecha": 🔴 out of presence → no da'as koneh → invalid.
  • My animal is not already at the non-Jew's (I want to sell it to him for Shabbos without his having received it): 🔴 kinyan amira does not work — a kinyan with meshicha is needed.
  • Is hefker or temporary sale preferred? → sale: the animal always has an owner, so less maris ayin and no risk of third-party claim.

Points to remember

  • Second structural solution: temporary sale by kinyan amira
  • Formula: "behemti kenuya lecha" in the non-Jew's presence
  • Validity: requires the animal to be already in the non-Jew's physical possession
  • Preferable alternative to hefker: no risk of third-party claim
  • Foreshadows the more sophisticated mechanisms of seifim Yod-Alef–Yod-Ches
Comprehension question: Why does the mere presence of the non-Jew suffice to constitute his intent to acquire (da'as koneh), without his having to say "I accept"? What is the halachic logic of kinyan amira?

11. Seif Yod-Alef — Lechatchila with hefker before three and complete acquisition

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְכָל זֶה בְּדִיעֲבַד, שֶׁכְּבָר הִשְׂכִּירָהּ אוֹ הִשְׁאִילָהּ לְנָכְרִי בְּהֶתֵּר, שֶׁהִתְנָה עִמּוֹ שֶׁיַּחֲזִירֶנָּה לוֹ קֹדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת, אֲבָל לְכַתְּחִלָּה אָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָהּ אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִילָהּ סְתָם עַל סְמַךְ שֶׁכְּשֶׁיַּגִּיעַ הַשַּׁבָּת יַפְקִירֶנָּה אוֹ יַקְנֶה אוֹתָהּ לְהַנָּכְרִי, לְפִי שֶׁמְּפֻרְסָם לָרַבִּים שֶׁהִיא בֶּהֱמַת יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְהַהֶפְקֵר וְהַהַקְנָאָה אֵינָן מְפֻרְסָמִים כְּלָל, וְיֵשׁ כַּאן חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית הָעַיִן.

וְיֵשׁ מַתִּירִין לְהַשְׂכִּיר אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִיל לְכַתְּחִלָּה עַל סְמַךְ שֶׁכְּשֶׁיַּגִּיעַ הַשַּׁבָּת יַפְקִירֶנָּה אוֹ יַקְנֶה אוֹתָהּ לְהַנָּכְרִי בִּפְנֵי שְׁלֹשָׁה יִשְׂרְאֵלִים. וְיֵשׁ לִסְמוֹךְ עַל דִּבְרֵיהֶם בִּשְׁעַת הַדְּחַק.

וְכֵן יֵשׁ לְהַתִּיר לְצֹרֶךְ מִצְוָה, כְּגוֹן לִשְׁלוֹחַ אֶתְרוֹגִים עַל בְּהֶמְתּוֹ עַל יְדֵי נָכְרִי, עַל דֶּרֶךְ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן תרנ"ה.

English translation

All this (the solutions of Seifim Tes-Yod) applies only b'di'eved — when one has already rented or lent the animal in a permitted way (with the condition of return). But lechatchila (a priori), it is asur to rent or lend without condition, relying on the fact that at the arrival of Shabbos one will declare hefker or transfer to the non-Jew. Reason: it is publicly known that the animal is the Yid's, and the hefker or transfer is not publicly known — there is here a concern of maris ayin.

And yesh matirin lechatchila, provided the transfer or hefker is done in front of three Yidden (who will publicize it). One may rely on this opinion b'sha'as ha-dechak (time of pressing need).

Likewise, one may permit for a tzorech mitzvah — like sending esrogim on one's animal via a non-Jew (cf. siman תרנ"ה).

What is this Seif about?

Practical question: may one plan in advance to use the mechanism of hefker or transfer (Seifim Tes-Yod)? No — lechatchila it is asur, because the "Yiddish" character of the animal is public, while the hefker is not → maris ayin persists. Except: in front of three witnesses (who publicize), or b'sha'as ha-dechak, or for a mitzvah.

לְכַתְּחִלָּה vs בְּדִיעֲבַד:
  • Lechatchila = action a priori, planned in advance. Stricter.
  • B'di'eved = action after the fact, handling a situation that has already occurred. More flexible.
Seifim Tes-Yod are b'di'eved solutions. To use them lechatchila, adequate publication (3 witnesses) is needed.

The halachic question

Why require 3 witnesses lechatchila? Because the publicity of the hefker counterbalances the prior publicity of the Yiddish ownership. Without the 3 witnesses, passersby will see "the Yid's animal working on Shabbos" and will suspect — maris ayin. With 3 witnesses, the news spreads: "he did derech heter" (an act of permission).

The opinions

SourcePosition
1st opinion (Rashba)Asur lechatchila even with 3 witnesses: the Yiddish ownership remains more known.
2nd opinion (Ran, Tosafos)Permits lechatchila in front of 3 witnesses (publication sufficient).
Mechaber (246:3)Asur lechatchila except in exceptional cases.
RemaPermits b'sha'as ha-dechak / mitzvah in front of 3 witnesses.
Mishnah Berurah (246:33-37)Three cases where leniency: (1) economic sha'as ha-dechak, (2) tzorech mitzvah, (3) dayanei ha-ir to publicize.
SHHaRav (246:11)Practical synthesis: (a) lechatchila asur, (b) with 3 witnesses b'sha'as ha-dechak mutar, (c) for mitzvah even without 3 witnesses (sending esrogim via non-Jew).

The Rav's pesak

Four levels of permission:
  1. Classic lechatchila: asur. Hefker/transfer only b'di'eved.
  2. Sha'as ha-dechak (e.g., vital economic necessity): mutar lechatchila in front of 3 Yidden.
  3. Ideally in front of the dayanei ha-ir (city court judges) who will formally publicize.
  4. Tzorech mitzvah (sending esrogim, etc.): mutar even bein adam l'atzmo (no 3 witnesses required).
  • From the outset I want to rent my horse to a non-Jew for the season, relying on the Shabbos hefker: 🔴 asur lechatchila.
  • My business will close if I do not rent (sha'as ha-dechak); I make hefker in front of 3 Jewish neighbors: 🟢 mutar.
  • Ideally, do it in front of the city beis din: 🟢 preferable (formal publication).
  • Sending my esrogim for Sukkos via a non-Jew on my donkey: 🟢 mitzvah → mutar even without 3 witnesses.
  • Wanting to avoid having an employee on Shabbos by putting the business in the non-Jew's name for the duration: 🟡 economic sha'as ha-dechak — formalization required (3 witnesses / published contract).

Points to remember

  • Distinction lechatchila / b'di'eved: Seifim Tes-Yod are recovery solutions
  • To plan the transfer lechatchila: 3 Jewish witnesses (publication)
  • Cases where the Rav permits lechatchila: sha'as ha-dechak (with 3 witnesses) and tzorech mitzvah (even alone)
  • Ideal: go through the city beis din for formal publication
Comprehension question: Why does publicity (in front of 3 witnesses) suffice to neutralize maris ayin? And why does a mitzvah case exempt one even from this requirement?

12. Seif Yod-Beis — Conditional transfer "if you use on Shabbos" + notarized deed

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְכֵן יָכוֹל לְהַשְׂכִּירָהּ אוֹ לְהַשְׁאִילָהּ לְנָכְרִי עַל יְדֵי שֶׁיַּזְהִיר אוֹתוֹ שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשֶׂה בָּהּ בְּשַׁבָּת, וּבְאִם שֶׁיַּעֲבוֹר עַל תְּנָאוֹ וְיַעֲשֶׂה אֲזַי הִיא קְנוּיָה לוֹ לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת וְיִתְחַיֵּב בְּאֹנָסִין שֶׁיֶּאֶרְעוּ לָהּ בְּשַׁבָּת, וְיִכְתּוֹב כֵּן בְּעַרְכָּאוֹתֵיהֶם, שֶׁנִּמְצָא כְּשֶׁבָּא הַנָּכְרִי לַעֲשׂוֹת בָּהּ בְּשַׁבָּת אֵינָהּ בֶּהֱמַת יִשְׂרָאֵל כְּלָל, שֶׁהֲרֵי קְנָאָהּ הַנָּכְרִי לְהִתְחַיֵּב בְּאֹנָסֶיהָ. וְאַף שֶׁאֵינָהּ קְנוּיָה לוֹ אֶלָּא בְּשַׁבָּת בִּלְבָד — אֵין לָחוּשׁ לְמַרְאִית הָעַיִן, כֵּיוָן שֶׁכָּתַב כֵּן בְּעַרְכָּאוֹתֵיהֶם — יֵשׁ לַדָּבָר קוֹל וּפִרְסוּם:

English translation

Likewise, he may rent or lend his animal to a non-Jew by warning him not to use it on Shabbos, and that if he transgresses this condition and uses it, the animal is acquired to him for the day of Shabbos, and he is responsible for accidents that may befall it on Shabbos.

And let him write this in their courts (b'arka'oseihem — non-Jewish notarized deeds). Thus, when the non-Jew comes to use the animal on Shabbos, it is no longer a Yiddish animal at all, since the non-Jew has acquired it (through the triggering of the condition) and is responsible for accidents.

And even though it is only acquired to him for Shabbos alone — there is no concern of maris ayin, since this is written in their notarized deeds: there is official publication (kol u-firsum).

What is this Seif about?

Third structural solution. The Rav develops the doctrine into a sophisticated legal mechanism: the automatic conditional transfer by notarized deed. The contract says: "you may use the animal, but not on Shabbos; if you do anyway, then ipso facto the animal becomes yours for that day." And official publication (civil notarized deed) solves the problem of maris ayin.

עַרְכָּאוֹתֵיהֶם = non-Jewish courts. Normally, recourse to civil courts is very limited in halacha (cf. Choshen Mishpat). But for registering a deed and giving it official publication, one may do so — here, that is the key that eliminates the risk of maris ayin because the contract is public and binding on all.

The halachic question

How can a simple condition automatically transfer ownership? Answer: this is a tenai kaful (double-faceted condition): the rental contract expressly stipulates that in the case of Shabbos use, the legal status of the animal changes by operation of law. The validity of this mechanism rests on the sincerity of the contract and its public registration.

The opinions

SourcePosition
AcharonimThis solution combines condition and publicity: best protection against maris ayin.
Mechaber (cf. Beis Yosef)Mentions the possibility of the automatic condition.
RemaInsists on the necessity of the notarized deed for publicity.
Mishnah Berurah (246:38-40)Praises this solution as the most complete: (a) legal protection, (b) civil liability transferred, (c) official publication.
SHHaRav (246:12)Combined architecture: usage condition + automatic transfer of ownership + civil liability + notarized deed = complete and lechatchila solution.

The Rav's pesak

The complete solution of Seif Yod-Beis:
  1. Rental/loan contract with an express clause: "no Shabbos use."
  2. Additional clause: "if you use on Shabbos, the animal becomes yours for that day, with your civil liability."
  3. The contract is registered at a notary or civil court (arka'oseihem).
  4. Result: if the non-Jew violates the condition, ownership transfers automatically → no longer a Yiddish animal → no violation of shvisas behemto.
  5. Official publication dissolves the maris ayin.
  • I rent my tractor to a non-Jewish farmer via a notarized contract containing the automatic transfer clause: 🟢 complete solution.
  • Same contract, but the notary records only the rental without the transfer clause: 🔴 missing the publication of the transfer → maris ayin persists.
  • My neighbor rents my horse; we sign a private agreement with the clause: 🟡 legally valid but less publicity — preferable to go to the notary.
  • Commercial lease of a workshop to a non-Jew who might use it on Shabbos: automatic clause + notarized deed: 🟢 analogous solution for kelim too.
  • Software or SaaS platform that my non-Jewish subscriber uses on Shabbos? 🟡 modern question — a license contract with a similar clause may be considered.

Points to remember

  • The most refined structural solution: combines condition + automatic transfer + notarized deed
  • The clause "if you transgress, the animal becomes yours" operates automatically (tenai kaful)
  • Official registration (arka'oseihem) creates the publicity that dissolves maris ayin
  • Solution valid lechatchila thanks to legal publicity
Comprehension question: Why is recourse to a civil notarized deed (arka'oseihem) here positive when it is usually restricted in halacha? What halachic function does this publication serve?

13. Seif Yod-Gimmel — Shutfus (partnership) with initial Shabbos / week split

Full text of the SHHaRav

יִשְׂרָאֵל וְנָכְרִי שֶׁהֵם שֻׁתָּפִים בִּבְהֵמָה לִטְחוֹן בָּרֵחַיִם וְכַיּוֹצֵא — יֵשׁ הֶתֵּר שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בָּהּ הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת עַל יְדֵי שֶׁיַּתְנֶה עִם הַנָּכְרִי בַּתְּחִלָּה בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁקְּנָאוּהָ שֶׁיִּטּוֹל הַנָּכְרִי כָּל שְׂכַר הַשַּׁבָּת בִּלְבַדּוֹ וְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל יִטּוֹל כְּנֶגֶד זֶה כָּל שְׂכַר יוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל בִּלְבַדּוֹ, שֶׁכְּשֶׁמַּתְנֶה עִמּוֹ כֵּן בִּתְחִלַּת הַשֻּׁתָּפוּת — הֲרֵי זֶה כְּמַקְנֶה לוֹ בִּלְבַדּוֹ כָּל גּוּף הַבְּהֵמָה לְשַׁבָּתוֹת וּלְיִשְׂרָאֵל בִּלְבַדּוֹ בְּיוֹם אַחֵר, וְאֵין לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל חֵלֶק בָּהּ בְּשַׁבָּתוֹת, וְאֵינוֹ מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתָתָהּ.

English translation

A Yid and a non-Jew who are shutfim (partners) in an animal to grind in the mill or do similar work — there is a heter for the non-Jew to use it on Shabbos. How? By stipulating with him at the very beginning, at the time of jointly purchasing the animal, that the non-Jew will take for himself alone all the Shabbos profit, and the Yid will take in return all the profit of one weekday for himself alone.

This initial stipulation is equivalent to transferring to the non-Jew alone the entire guf of the animal for the Shabbosos, and to the Yid alone for one weekday. Thus, the Yid has no part in the animal on the Shabbosos, and is not metzuveh on its shvisa.

(Even if the responsibility for the animal remains shared on the other days — no matter; what counts is that for Shabbos, exclusive ownership goes to the non-Jew.)

What is this Seif about?

Case of shutfus (partnership) between a Yid and a non-Jew over an animal. The Rav proposes a structure allowing the partnership to operate on Shabbos: an initial clause of exclusive Shabbos attribution to the non-Jew (and one weekday to the Yid). This stipulation, made at the very purchase, is equivalent to a permanent transfer of the "Shabbos day" to the non-Jew.

שֻׁתָּפוּת (shutfus) = legal partnership. Different from a mere co-ownership: it is an economic agreement on management and profit-sharing. An initial clause that distributes the days (not percentages) creates a temporal exclusive ownership: Shabbos = the non-Jew's, day X = the Yid's. This neutralizes the problem of shvisas behemto.

The halachic question

How can a simple sharing clause create exclusive ownership? Answer: because it is initial. At the time of purchase, the parties together decide that the days are attributed individually. This is not a post-hoc exchange (which would be ma'amid po'el bimkomo, cf. siman רמ"ה), but a structuring ab initio of the ownership — as if one had purchased in common while distributing the days from the start.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Talmud (AZ 22a)Shutfus with initial stipulation = exclusive transfer of Shabbos to the non-Jew.
RishonimThe b'tchila / le-achar mikan distinction is crucial: only the initial clause works.
Mechaber (245:1, 246:5)Initial stipulation permits; after the fact asur (cf. siman רמ"ה).
RemaAgrees; specifies the details of the stipulation.
Mishnah Berurah (246:41-43)This stipulation must be explicit on the attribution of days, not implicit.
SHHaRav (246:13)"B'tchilas ha-shutfus": the initial stipulation is equivalent to a transfer ab initio of the individual days. The Yid has no part on the Shabbosos.

The Rav's pesak

The mechanism of Seif Yod-Gimmel:
  1. At the time of joint purchase of the animal, explicitly stipulate:
    • "The non-Jew takes all the Shabbos profit, for him alone."
    • "The Yid takes all the profit of one weekday, for him alone."
  2. This initial clause = exclusive transfer of the days → Yid has no part on Shabbos → no obligation of shvisa.
  3. Shared responsibility on other days does not affect the Shabbos status (cf. discussion siman רמ"ה).
  4. If the clause was not made at the outset: do not make it later — see the following seifim for recovery solutions.
  • Purchase of a tractor in common (50/50) with a non-Jew, with an initial clause "Shabbos yours, Monday mine": 🟢 mutar.
  • Agricultural joint-venture: we buy the cattle together while distributing usage days from the contract: 🟢 valid halachic structure.
  • Commercial 50/50 partnership without initial clause; my partner works on Shabbos: 🔴 without initial stipulation, case of siman רמ"ה — asur as is.
  • Professional practice co-founded with initial clause attributing call shifts by day: 🟢 clean structure.
  • I buy a share in a racehorse with my non-Jewish partner without specific clause: 🔴 fix via seifim Yod-Daled–Tes-Vav.

Points to remember

  • Operative mechanism for partnerships: initial daily attribution clause
  • The key moment: b'tchilas ha-shutfus (at the time of joint purchase)
  • After the fact: does not work (cf. siman רמ"ה — ma'amid po'el bimkomo)
  • Shared responsibility on other days does not affect the Shabbos status
Comprehension question: Why does the initial stipulation (at the time of purchase) work as a permanent transfer, while a simple post-hoc agreement ("I will give you that day in exchange for my Shabbos day") is asur (siman רמ"ה)?

14. Seif Yod-Daled — Partnership without initial clause: havla'ah as recovery

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְאִם לֹא הִתְנָה עִמּוֹ כֵּן בִּתְחִלַּת הַשֻּׁתָּפוּת — לֹא יַתְנֶה עִמּוֹ אַחַר כָּךְ, שֶׁאַף אִם עַכְשָׁו אֵין כַּאן אִסּוּר מִשּׁוּם שְׁבִיתַת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ, שֶׁהֲרֵי עַכְשָׁו אֵין הַנָּכְרִי נִמְנָע מִלַּעֲשׂוֹת בָּהּ מְלָאכָה בְּעַל כָּרְחוֹ שֶׁל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם יֵשׁ אִסּוּר בְּמַה שֶּׁנּוֹטֵל רֶוַח יוֹם אֶחָד בְּחֹל כְּנֶגֶד יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁהֲרֵי נוֹטֵל שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה.

וּבִכְגוֹן זֶה שֶׁלֹּא הִתְנוּ מִתְּחִלָּה — יֵשׁ הֶתֵּר כְּשֶׁלֹּא יֹאמַר לוֹ כְּלָל "טֹל אַתָּה בְּשַׁבָּת וַאֲנִי בְּחֹל", אֶלָּא יַחֲלוֹק עִמּוֹ כָּל הָרֶוַח שֶׁל כָּל יְמוֹת הַשָּׁבוּעַ בְּשָׁוֶה, שֶׁאָז הוּא נוֹטֵל שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת בְּהַבְלָעָה.

אֶלָּא שֶׁגַּם זֶה אֵין לְהַתִּיר אֶלָּא בִּמְקוֹם הֶפְסֵד גָּדוֹל.

English translation

If the initial stipulation (of Seif Yod-Gimmel) was not made, one must not make it after the fact. For even if there is now no issur of shvisas behemto (since the non-Jew is already a partner and the Yid cannot stop him), there remains an issur in taking the profit of one weekday in exchange for the Shabbos day — this is schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah (naming the Shabbos in the account).

In that case (no initial stipulation), there is a heter: do not say to him at all "take Shabbos, I take a weekday," but rather split equally all the profits of the entire week. Then the Yid takes the Shabbos profit b'havla'ah (bundled in the total).

But even this should only be permitted in a case of hefsed gadol (great loss).

What is this Seif about?

Recovery solution if the initial stipulation of Seif Yod-Gimmel was not made. Two options: (a) do not make a post-hoc stipulation naming the Shabbos (shelo b'havla'ah); (b) split the profits b'havla'ah (without naming the Shabbos) — but only in case of hefsed gadol.

The halachic question

Why is the post-hoc stipulation asur, even when the non-Jew is already a partner and does his work? Because as soon as one names the Shabbos in the account ("your Shabbos day against my weekday"), one creates a schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah. Unless one splits globally all the profits, without detailing the days — then the Shabbos profit is bundled.

The opinions

SourcePosition
Mechaber (245:1)Without initial stipulation: asur to mention Shabbos in the account.
RemaPermits global havla'ah in case of hefsed gadol.
Mishnah Berurah (246:44-46)Details hefsed gadol: e.g., if dissolving the partnership would entail significant loss.

Three levels of severity:
  1. Post-hoc stipulation naming the Shabbos ("you Shabbos, me weekday"): asur (schar Shabbos shelo b'havla'ah).
  2. Global split without naming the Shabbos (50/50 on everything): mutar only in hefsed gadol.
  3. Ideal case: go back to Seif Yod-Gimmel and include the stipulation from the purchase.
  • Existing partnership without initial clause; I propose to my partner "you take Shabbos, I take Tuesday": 🔴 asur (shelo b'havla'ah).
  • Same partnership; we decide to split annual profits 50/50 without detailing: 🟢 global havla'ah — mutar if dissolution would cost dearly.
  • Flourishing partnership with no economic difficulty; global split: 🟡 hefsed gadol not established → better to restructure (cf. Seif Tes-Vav).
  • Small family business co-founded that would go bankrupt if dissolved: 🟢 clear hefsed gadol → havla'ah mutar.
  • Accountant who details the Yid-days and non-Jew-days Shabbos included: 🔴 incompatible with havla'ah.

Points to remember

  • Without initial clause (Seif Yod-Gimmel): never name the Shabbos in the accounts

15. Seif Tes-Vav — Restructuring: apothiki, mashkon, and sale with debt

Full text of the SHHaRav

וְגַם בִּמְקוֹם הֶפְסֵד גָּדוֹל, אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ אֵינוֹ רוֹצֶה לִטּוֹל כְּלָל בְּחֹל כְּנֶגֶד הַשַּׁבָּת, אַף שֶׁאֵין אִסּוּר מִשּׁוּם שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת, וְלֹא מִשּׁוּם שְׁבִיתַת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ — מִכָּל מָקוֹם יֵשׁ אִסּוּר לְמַפְרֵעַ בְּמַה שֶּׁנִּשְׁתַּתֵּף עִם הַנָּכְרִי בִּבְהֵמָה.

וְיֵשׁ תִּקּוּן לְזֶה עַל יְדֵי שֶׁיַּתְנֶה עִמּוֹ שֶׁאִם יַעֲשֶׂה בָּהּ מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת — אֲזַי הִיא קְנוּיָה לוֹ לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, וְיִכְתּוֹב כֵּן. אוֹ עַל יְדֵי שֶׁיַּקְנֶה לוֹ גַּם חֶלְקוֹ לְעִנְיַן יֹקֶר וְזוֹל וּמִיתָה בְּאֹנֶס.

אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ יִמְכּוֹר לוֹ חֶלְקוֹ בִּמְכִירָה גְמוּרָה, וְאַף עַל פִּי כֵן לֹא יוּכַל הַנָּכְרִי לְמָכְרָהּ שֶׁלֹּא מִדַּעְתּוֹ, כְּגוֹן שֶׁיִּזְקוֹף עָלָיו הַדָּמִים בְּמִלְוָה שֶׁיִּפְרָעֵם לוֹ כְּשֶׁיִּתְבָּעֶנּוּ, וְיַחֲזוֹר הַנָּכְרִי וְיַעֲשֶׂה לוֹ בְּהֵמָה זוֹ אַפּוֹתִּיקִי לְחוֹבוֹ.

English translation

Even in a case of hefsed gadol, or even if the Yid does not want at all to take a weekday in exchange for the Shabbos — although there is no issur of schar Shabbos nor of shvisas behemto, there remains a retroactive issur in the very fact of having partnered with a non-Jew over an animal (which will work on Shabbos of its own will).

There is a tikkun (correction) for this: (1) by condition that if he works on Shabbos, the animal is acquired to him for that day (cf. Seif Yod-Beis, in writing); (2) or by transferring also his share for inflation/deflation and accident liability.

Or even by selling him his share in a complete sale — while ensuring that the non-Jew cannot resell it without his consent. For this: convert the price into a debt (zekifas milveh) payable on demand, then make the animal an apothiki (specific collateral) for that debt.

What is this Seif about?

The most sophisticated of the seifim. For cases where the havla'ah of Seif Yod-Daled is not enough (or if one does not even want to take profit), the Rav offers a series of legal restructurings: (a) automatic transfer condition (cf. Seif Yod-Beis), (b) transfer of market risks, (c) complete sale with debt + apothiki (specific collateral).

אַפּוֹתִּיקִי (apothiki) = specific collateral. A property is designated as guarantee for a precise debt: as long as the debt is not repaid, the creditor cannot accept any other payment (only that property). It is stronger than a mortgage: it is an exclusive affectation.

מַשְׁכּוֹן (mashkon) = classic collateral handed over in person.

זְקִיפַת מִלְוָה = conversion into a debt. The sale price is not paid in cash but transformed into a deferred debt — which creates a permanent legal link between the parties.

The halachic question

How does one sell one's share while keeping control? Solution: (1) formal complete sale to the non-Jew, (2) price converted into debt (zekifas milveh), (3) specific collateral on the animal (apothiki). Result: the non-Jew is legally the owner (no obligation of shvisa for the Yid), but cannot sell the animal without repaying the debt → control preserved.

RishonimApplicability to the Shabbos case debated; majority accepts.

The Rav's pesak

The three restructuring options:
  1. Automatic condition (cf. Seif Yod-Beis): the contract stipulates ipso facto transfer in case of Shabbos use. Written in a notarized deed.
  2. Transfer of market risks: one transfers to the non-Jew the fluctuations of value and responsibility for accidents → the animal is economically his even if legally still shared.
  3. Formal complete sale: (a) sale, (b) price converted into debt (zekifas milveh), (c) specific collateral on the animal (apothiki). The non-Jew is the owner; the Yid has a collateral right that prevents resale.
  • Agricultural partnership: I sell my share of the livestock to my non-Jewish partner, price converted into debt, animals as apothiki: 🟢 sophisticated valid solution.
  • I want to keep control: I officially transfer responsibility for accidents and fluctuations: 🟢 economic transfer mutar.
  • Kosher restaurant run with a non-Jewish partner on Shabbos: formal sale + debt + collateral: 🟢 complex but clean structure.
  • Informal solution ("I'll let you do it, we'll see later"): 🔴 without formalization, the animal remains half-mine → asur.
  • For modern business: company structure (LLC/Corp) with shares attributed so as to neutralize Shabbos exposure: 🟡 requires halachic + civil engineering — consult a specialist Rav.

Points to remember

  • Apothiki (specific collateral) allows selling while keeping control
  • Zekifas milveh (conversion of price into debt) maintains the legal link
  • Solutions valid lechatchila with notarized deed and publicity (cf. Seif Tes-Zayin)
  • Advanced halachic engineering: always consult a Rav for the exact structure

16. Seif Tes-Zayin — Publicity (pirsum) remains required in every case

Full text of the SHHaRav

וּבֵין שֶׁעֲשָׂאָה אַפּוֹתִּיקִי וּבֵין שֶׁלֹּא עֲשָׂאָהּ, וּבֵין שֶׁנְּטָלָהּ לְמַשְׁכּוֹן וּבֵין שֶׁלֹּא נְטָלָהּ, אֶלָּא מְכָרָהּ לוֹ בִּמְכִירָה גְמוּרָה שֶׁיְּהֵא רְשׁוּת בְּיָדוֹ לְמָכְרָהּ שֶׁלֹּא מִדַּעְתּוֹ, כֵּיוָן שֶׁכָּל זְמַן שֶׁלֹּא מְכָרָהּ הֵם שֻׁתָּפִים בָּהּ וְיֵשׁ חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית עַיִן, לְפִיכָךְ יֵשׁ לוֹ לְפַרְסֵם לָרַבִּים שֶׁעָשָׂה דֶּרֶךְ הֶתֵּר:

English translation

Whether one made an apothiki or not, whether one took a mashkon or not, or even if one made a complete sale giving the non-Jew the right to resell without the Yid's consent — as long as the sale is not actual, the two are considered shutfim, and there is a concern of maris ayin.

Therefore one must publicize publicly that one has acted by a derech heter (way of permission).

What is this Seif about?

Conclusion on publicity. Whatever the structures of the preceding seifim (apothiki, mashkon, complete sale), the risk of maris ayin persists as long as the public does not know the procedure. The active publication of the "derech heter" is therefore essential.

דֶּרֶךְ הֶתֵּר = "way of permission", the formal halachic procedure one has used. Publicizing that one followed such a procedure (initial transfer, sale with apothiki, etc.) serves to inform passersby: "do not suspect — here is how it is legitimized."

The halachic question

Why is publicity still required even after a complete sale? Because as long as outward appearances suggest a partnership (even temporary), the public can suspect — hence the obligation to announce that one has done derech heter.

The obligation of publication:

  • Big city: how to publicize? 🟡 notarized deed + informing the local beis din is ideal.

Points to remember

18. Seif Yod-Ches — The non-Jew's work for the Yid (vs for himself)

Full text of the SHHaRav

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁמַּשְׂכִּירָהּ אוֹ מַשְׁאִילָהּ לוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת מְלֶאכֶת עַצְמוֹ, אֲבָל לַעֲשׂוֹת מְלֶאכֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּשַׁבָּת אֵין הֶתֵּר כְּלָל אֲפִלּוּ אִם מְכָרָהּ לוֹ בִּמְכִירָה גְמוּרָה, שֶׁמִּכָּל מָקוֹם הַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת בִּשְׁבִיל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל אִם לֹא קָצַץ לוֹ שָׂכָר עַל זֶה.

אֲבָל אִם אֵינוֹ שָׂכוּר לוֹ אֶלָּא לִמְלָאכָה זוֹ בִּלְבָד... כְּגוֹן מִי שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ סוּסִים וַעֲגָלָה וְשָׂכַר לוֹ נָכְרִי לְשָׁנָה שֶׁיִּסַּע עִם סוּסִים וַעֲגָלָה שֶׁלּוֹ בְּכָל עֵת שֶׁיִּצְטָרֵךְ לְהוֹלִיךְ בָּהֶם סְחוֹרָה בְּתוֹךְ שָׁנָה זוֹ, וְהַנָּכְרִי נוֹסֵעַ גַּם בְּשַׁבָּת מֵעַצְמוֹ בְּלֹא אֲמִירַת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל — אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר לוֹ שֶׁלֹּא יִסַּע בְּשַׁבָּת אִם נוֹסֵעַ מִבֵּית הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם. רַק שֶׁיַּקְנֶה לוֹ הַסּוּסִים בְּאֶחָד מֵהַדְּרָכִים שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֲרוּ, וִיפַרְסֵם שֶׁעָשָׂה דֶּרֶךְ הֶתֵּר.

English translation

When does all this apply? When one rents/lends the animal to him to do his own work (the non-Jew's). But to do the Yid's work on Shabbos — no heter at all, even if one sold it to him in a complete sale: because in any case the non-Jew is working on Shabbos for the Yid (if no specific salary was fixed for that work).

But if the non-Jew is hired only for a specific task (cf. siman רמ"ד for kablanus) — for example, if he has horses and a wagon to transport merchandise during the year, and the non-Jew travels also on Shabbos of his own without amira from the Yid: there is no need to tell him not to travel on Shabbos, provided he leaves the Yid's house before nightfall. He just needs to transfer the horses by one of the methods (seen earlier), and publicize that he has done derech heter.

What is this Seif about?

Crucial distinction: everything before concerns the case where the non-Jew uses the animal for his own work. If the non-Jew is the Yid's employee (the work benefits the Yid), the transfer does not suffice: this amounts to amira. The Rav here joins siman רמ"ד (kablanus): only a precise contract by task can permit it.

מְלֶאכֶת עַצְמוֹ vs מְלֶאכֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל:
  • Melechet atzmo = the non-Jew does his own work with the animal. The transfer suffices.
  • Melechet Yisrael = the non-Jew does the Yid's work (delivery driver, worker). The transfer does not suffice; a kablanus by task is needed (cf. siman רמ"ד).

The halachic question

The Rav illustrates with the delivery driver case: if the Yid has a wagon and hires a non-Jew to deliver his merchandise on Shabbos — asur by default (the non-Jew works for him). Solution: (a) transfer the horses by one of the methods (sale + debt + apothiki), (b) the contract must be by task (kablanus per delivery), not an annual salary for all deliveries, (c) publicize.

  1. Transfer the animals by one of the methods (cf. Seifim Yod-Beis, Tes-Vav).
  2. Hire by task (kablanus — cf. siman רמ"ד), not at a global salary.

  4. Publicize the derech heter.
  5. If any of these 4 elements is missing: asur.
  • My non-Jewish driver delivers my packages on Shabbos with my truck: 🔴 default → transfer + kablanus per delivery + Friday departure + publication.
  • My annual non-Jewish employee runs all my errands on Shabbos: 🔴 annual salary ≠ kablanus → asur.
  • Non-Jewish delivery company paid per delivery, with my vehicles transferred + debt/collateral: 🟢 correct structure.

  • Automated platform (customer order → direct delivery) without human Yiddish intervention on Shabbos: 🟡 modern case — questions of grama and automation: contemporary Rav required.

Points to remember

  • Work for the Yid: the transfer does not suffice — kablanus per task is also required (siman רמ"ד)

  • Typical modern cases: delivery drivers, drivers, managers — always consult a Rav

19. Seif Yod-Tes — Application to Yom Tov: shvisas behema applies on Yom Tov as well

Full text of the SHHaRav

כְּשֵׁם שֶׁאָדָם מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתַת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת — כָּךְ הוּא מְצֻוֶּה עַל שְׁבִיתָתָהּ בְּיוֹם טוֹב. לְפִיכָךְ, כָּל פְּרָטֵי הַהֲלָכוֹת שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֲרוּ לְעִנְיַן שְׁאֵלָה וּשְׂכִירוּת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ לְנָכְרִי וְשֻׁתָּפוּת עִמּוֹ גַּבֵּי שַׁבָּת, הֵן נוֹהֲגִים גַּם כֵּן גַּבֵּי יוֹם טוֹב, שֶׁאֵין בֵּין יוֹם טוֹב לְשַׁבָּת אֶלָּא מְלָאכוֹת שֶׁהֻתְּרוּ בִּגְלַל אֹכֶל נֶפֶשׁ.

וְיֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁבְּיוֹם טוֹב אֵין אָדָם מְצֻוֶּה מִן הַתּוֹרָה עַל שְׁבִיתַת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ, לְפִי שֶׁלֹּא נִצְטַוִּינוּ בְּיוֹם טוֹב אֶלָּא שֶׁלֹּא נַעֲשֶׂה מְלָאכָה, וּמַה שֶּׁבְּהֶמְתֵּנוּ עוֹשָׂה אֵין זוֹ נִקְרֵאת מְלָאכָה אֶצְלֵנוּ אֶלָּא אֵצֶל הַבְּהֵמָה.

וּלְעִנְיַן הֲלָכָה, יֵשׁ לְהַחֲמִיר בְּשֶׁל תּוֹרָה כִּסְבָרָא הָרִאשׁוֹנָה (עַיֵּן סִמָּן תצ"ה):

English translation

Just as a person is metzuveh on the rest of his animal on Shabbos, so he is metzuveh on its rest on Yom Tov. All the preceding halachic details (loan, rental, partnership) apply also to Yom Tov — the only difference with Shabbos being the melachos permitted for ochel nefesh (food preparation).

And yesh omrim that on Yom Tov, a person is not metzuveh mid'Oraisa on the rest of his animal — for on Yom Tov we were forbidden only our own melachos, and what the animal does is not our melacha but its own. (Same for the slave — see "yanuach avdecha.")

In practice, one must be stringent in matters of Torah according to the first opinion (cf. siman תצ"ה).

What is this Seif about?

Final seif: extension of the entire siman to Yom Tov. The Rav brings a machlokes: (1) shvisas behemto applies also to Yom Tov (1st opinion), (2) only on Shabbos (2nd opinion). In practice, one rules stringent per the 1st opinion (asur mid'Oraisa).

יוֹם טוֹב = biblical festival (Pesach, Shavuos, Sukkos, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Shemini Atzeres). More permissive than Shabbos (food preparation permitted) but retains the essential issurim.

אֹכֶל נֶפֶשׁ = immediate food needs — the specific "license" of Yom Tov, which permits cooking and preparing the food of the day.

The halachic question

Does the passuk "in order that your ox and your donkey may rest" target Yom Tov too or only Shabbos? Per the 1st opinion: yes, the spirit is the same. Per the 2nd: no, only Shabbos explicitly has this obligation; on Yom Tov, only our melachos are forbidden, not the animal's. Ruling: 1st opinion — this is a rule of stringency for Torah matters.

The Rav's pesak

Application to Yom Tov:
  1. Shvisas behemto applies also to Yom Tov, mid'Oraisa (stringent — 1st opinion).
  2. All seifim of the Siman carry over to Yom Tov.
  3. Only difference: the melachos permitted for ochel nefesh (food preparation) remain permitted.
  4. For Yom Tov-specific details: see siman תצ"ה.
  • My horse goes to plow for my non-Jewish partner on Pesach: 🔴 same issur as on Shabbos.
  • My cow is used on Shavuos by a non-Jewish neighbor (transport of material for his business): 🔴 asur mid'Oraisa.
  • My donkey carries food for my family on Yom Tov: 🟢 ochel nefesh permitted.
  • My animal draws a wagon for a non-Jewish neighbor on Sukkos (not connected to ochel nefesh): 🔴 asur.
  • Transfer mechanisms (Seifim Yod-Beis–Tes-Vav) on Yom Tov: 🟢 also work (same structures).

Points to remember

  • Shvisas behemto applies also to Yom Tov (stringent — 1st opinion)
  • All seifim of the Siman carry over to Yom Tov
  • Only difference: ochel nefesh (food preparation) remains permitted

Part III

General synthesis of Siman רמ״ו

After the detailed study of the 19 seifim, here is a structured overview and general comprehension questions covering the entire Siman.

Overview — the three major parts of the Siman
א׳–ו׳
כֵּלִים
Kelim — rabbinic domain
Rental and loan of objects to a non-Jew. Rabbinic gezeiros: schar Shabbos, nireh ki-shlucho, maris ayin. Solutions: havla'ah, free loan, eiruv, return of a pikadon.
ז׳–י׳
בְּהֵמָה
Behema — Torah issur
Shvisas behemto = issur Torah. First structural b'di'eved solutions: private hefker (Seif Tes), temporary sale by kinyan amira (Seif Yod).
י"א–י"ט
תיקונים
Halachic engineering lechatchila
Sophisticated mechanisms: transfer in front of 3 witnesses, contract with automatic condition (notarized deed), shutfus with initial split, apothiki + debt, and extension to Yom Tov.
The five big ideas to remember
Halachic synthesis
① Distinction loan / rental
The gezeira of nireh ki-shlucho aims only at monetary rental (Seif Gimmel). Free lending, even of a klei umnus on late erev Shabbos, is mutar.

② Havla'ah solves schar Shabbos
Rent bundled in a multi-day package = no isolated Shabbos gain. Sechar yom, by contrast, isolates the Shabbos → asur (Seif Alef).

③ Biblical turning point at Seif Zayin
The behema introduces shvisas behemto = issur Torah. No simple solution: structural arrangements are needed (hefker, temporary sale, apothiki).

④ Lechatchila vs b'di'eved
The hefker solutions (Seif Tes) are b'di'eved recoveries. To use these mechanisms lechatchila: 3 witnesses (Seif Yod-Alef) or notarized deed (Seif Yod-Beis).

⑤ The vital role of publicity
Whatever legal structures are used, publication (in front of 3 witnesses, shul, notarized deed) remains essential to dissolve maris ayin (Seif Tes-Zayin).
General comprehension questions
Question 1 — Architecture of the Siman: Why does the Siman begin with kelim (Seifim Alef–Vav) before moving to behema (Seifim Zayin–Yod-Tes)? What is the pedagogical logic of the rabbinic → biblical progression?
Question 2 — The economic criterion: How does the Rav use the rental analysis ("the non-Jew would have paid less without Shabbos") to ground the restriction of Seif Beis on klei umnus? What general halachic principle do we derive from this reasoning?
Question 3 — The two registers of maris ayin: Distinguish (a) the maris ayin of Seif Beis (appearance of agency for renting a tool) and (b) the maris ayin of Seif Daled (appearance of hotza'ah transgression when the non-Jew leaves with my object). Why are they different?
Question 4 — The hefker mechanism: Why is a hefker made privately valid here, when Chazal normally require 3 witnesses? And why can a third party not acquire the animal during the hefker period?
Question 5 — The conditional sale: How does the mechanism "if you work on Shabbos, the animal becomes yours" (Seif Yod-Beis) operate automatically? What is the halachic nature of this condition (tenai) and why does it require a notarized deed?
Question 6 — Initial partnership vs after-the-fact: Why is the stipulation "you Shabbos, me weekday" mutar at the time of purchase (Seif Yod-Gimmel) but asur if made after (Seif Yod-Daled)? What is the connection with siman רמ"ה?
Question 7 — Apothiki and legal control: How does the combination "complete sale + debt + specific collateral (apothiki)" allow the Yid to transfer full ownership while keeping effective control over the animal? Why is it preferable to a simple partnership?
Question 8 — Work for himself vs for the Yid: Why does the transfer of the animal (Seif Yod-Ches) not suffice when the work is for the Yid? What additional principle intervenes and what siman develops it?
Question 9 — Synthesis practical case: You co-found a dairy operation with a non-Jewish partner. How should the partnership be structured from the start to allow milking on Shabbos without violating any seif? List the required legal components.
Question 10 — Yom Tov: Why does the Rav rule stringently in applying shvisas behemto to Yom Tov (Seif Yod-Tes)? And what is the only melacha the animal could do on Yom Tov without violating this issur?
