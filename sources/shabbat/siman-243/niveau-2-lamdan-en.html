📜 The Text of the Shulchan Aruch — Se'ifim 1-2

סעיף א. לֹא יַשְׂכִּיר אָדָם מֶרְחָץ שֶׁלּוֹ לְגוֹי, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנִּקְרֵאת עַל שְׁמוֹ וְגוֹי זֶה עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, דִּסְתַם מֶרְחָץ לָאו לַאֲרִיסוּתָא.

סעיף ב. שָׂדֶה מֻתָּר לְקַבֵּל בַּאֲרִיסוּת, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁיּוֹדְעִים שֶׁהִיא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְנִמְצָא שֶׁהַגּוֹי עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בִּשְׁלִיחוּתוֹ, דַּאֲדַעְתֵּיהּ דְּנַפְשֵׁיהּ עָבִיד.

הגה (רמ״א): וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁלֹּא לְקָחָהּ הַגּוֹי רַק לְשָׁלִישׁ אוֹ לְרָבִיעַ... שָׁרֵי, דְּגוֹי אַדַּעְתָּא דְּנַפְשֵׁיהּ עָבִיד. וְתַנּוּר דִּינוֹ כְּמֶרְחָץ וְרֵחַיִים דִּינָם כְּשָׂדֶה.

Se'if Alef. One does not rent out one's bathhouse to a non-Jew, because it is called by his name and this non-Jew performs melakha in it on Shabbos, since the standard practice of a bathhouse is not arisus (sharecropping).

Se'if Beis. A field — it is permitted to give it out in arisus, even though it is known that it belongs to a Jew, since the non-Jew works for his own benefit (adata de-nafshei).

Hagahas haRema. Even if the non-Jew takes only a third or a quarter of the fruits, it is permitted, since the non-Jew works adata de-nafshei. A tanur (oven) follows the din of the bathhouse; a rechayim (mill) follows the din of the field.

1. Introduction to the Sugya — Issur of Amira le-Akum on Shabbos

The Basic Source

The Mishnah (Shabbos 121a) makes clear that a melakha which a Jew is forbidden to perform on Shabbos — it is forbidden to tell a non-Jew to perform it on his behalf. And the Gemara in several places (Shabbos 150a, Eruvin 67b, Gittin 8b, Bava Metzia 90a) states that amira le-akum is a shevus.

And several Acharonim wrote that from the implication of the Poskim it appears that they held the issur of amira le-akum is derabanan.
In what did the Rishonim disagree regarding the taam of the issur of amira le-akum? The Poskim give three taamim:
  1. Sheiltos — because of "mimtzo cheftzecha ve-daber davar"
  2. Rashi — because of shelichus (agency)
  3. Rambam — so that Shabbos should not become light in his eyes

2. Shittas haSheiltos — "Mimtzo Cheftzecha ve-Daber Davar"

The Sheiltos de-Rav Achai (Vayera She'ilta 10, Shemos She'ilta 37):
Wrote that the issur is because of "mimtzo cheftzecha ve-daber davar" (Yeshayahu 58), from which they derived (Shabbos 113) that it is forbidden to speak about the performance of melakhos prohibited on Shabbos.
And so wrote Rashi (Avodah Zarah 15a, s.v. keivan).
According to this shittah, the issur of amira le-akum is essentially an issur upon the Jew himself — that he is forbidden to speak about melakha on Shabbos. The non-Jew is merely the "instrument" through which the issur passes.

Nafka mina : If the Jew did not speak with the non-Jew on Shabbos, but only on erev Shabbos, or only hinted to him, or the non-Jew acts on his own initiative — there is no essential issur of amira here.

3. Shittas Rashi — Because of Shelichus

Rashi (Shabbos 153a, s.v. mai ta'ama; Avodah Zarah 21b, s.v. mipnei; Gittin 21b, s.v. lo):
That the taam of the issur is because of shelichus, since "shelucho shel adam ke-moso" (a person's agent is like himself) — this is as if the Jew himself were performing the act.
Hagahos Maimoniyos (Perek 6, os 2): Although there is no shelichus for a non-Jew (Gittin 23b), we nevertheless find (Bava Metzia 71b) that le-chumra he does have shelichus.
The Meiri (Avodah Zarah 21b, s.v. klal): Although there is no shaliach for a devar aveirah, nevertheless the Gemara (Bava Metzia 10b) makes clear that if the shaliach is not himself obligated in that mitzvah, there is shelichus.
The chakira here : How can it be said that there is shelichus for a non-Jew, when the rule is "ein shelichus le-akum" (Gittin 23b)?

Three answers :
  1. Le-chumra there is shelichus (Hagahos Maimoniyos)
  2. The shaliach is not obligated in this mitzvah → there is shelichus (Meiri)
  3. This is merely "ke-ein shelichus" derabanan (an alternative pshat in Rashi)

4. Shittas haRambam — Kalus Shabbos be-Einav

The Rambam (Hilkhos Shabbos perek 6, halakhah 1):
Wrote that Chazal forbade amira le-akum so that Shabbos should not become light in his eyes and he come to perform melakha himself.
According to the Rambam's shittah, the issur is a pedagogical seyag. There is no objective transgression here, but rather a concern that if we permit a Jew to tell a non-Jew to act, he will come to be lenient and do it himself.

Nafka mina :
  • The Rambam does not require shelichus
  • Even indirectly it is forbidden — any avenue that may lead to "kalus Shabbos"
  • But also — more lenient in certain situations (e.g., pikuach nefesh)

The yesod : Le-halakha — we are concerned for all the te'amim le-chumra, and even when only one of them applies, it is forbidden to tell a non-Jew to act on one's behalf (Biur Halakha).

6. The Sugya in Shabbos 121a — The Source

The Lashon of the Mishnah

The Mishnah (Shabbos 121a) :
"A non-Jew who comes to extinguish — we do not say to him 'extinguish' nor 'do not extinguish,' because their shevisah is not upon us."

The Limmud of the Gemara in Eruvin

The Gemara (Eruvin 67b) :
"Amira le-oved kochavim shevus" — it is merely a derabanan issur.

The Source in the Mekhilta

The Mekhilta (Bo Masechta de-Pis'cha perek 9) :
"You say so, or is it not rather 'any melakha shall not be done — neither by you nor by a non-Jew on your behalf'? Talmud lomar 'sheshes yamim ta'aseh melakhtekha' — you shall not do it, and a non-Jew shall not do your melakha — divrei Rabbi Yoshiyah. Rabbi Yonasan says: it is not necessary, since it is a kal vachomer from Yom Tov — that you are not warned about your friend's work as you are about your own; whence Shabbos? Kal vachomer..."
The Mekhilta makes clear that the lashon of the pasuk "lo ye'aseh" — which implies "by others" — proves somewhat that this is a complete derashah, and it is forbidden mi-deOraisa to tell a non-Jew to perform melakha for a Jew.

However, the Semag wrote that the lashon of the pasuk "lo ye'aseh bahem" that Rabbi Yoshiyah cited in the Mekhilta does not appear to be a complete derashah.

7. The Four Scenarios — General Overview

The chakira : In our siman and in Siman 244 it is explained the din of a Jew's land — such as a field or bathhouse — where the non-Jew performs melakha and the Jew profits from it.
And there are four scenarios in which the non-Jew performs melakha and the Jew profits from it :

Scenario A — Sechir Yom (Day-laborer)

The Jew hires the non-Jew to work for him, and all profits of his labor belong to the Jew, and the Jew pays him a fixed sum per day.
This scenario is asur min ha-din, and this is included in the amira le-akum that Chazal forbade.

Scenario B — Kablanus (Contracting)

Here too the Jew hires the non-Jew to work for him, and all profits from his labor belong to the Jew, except that they stipulate that the Jew will not pay him by the day but a fixed wage for his work — for example, that he stipulated he would do all the necessary work in the field, and when he produces the fruits that grew, the Jew would give him so much money as wages, whether he had to toil much or little.

In this case, if it is a matter that the non-Jew can perform his work on weekdays alone, and what he does on Shabbos is only for his own convenience so that he may rest during the week — and such is common in agricultural work — it is permitted min ha-din, since the non-Jew works for the Jew only for his own wages, and the Jew has no profit from what the non-Jew does on Shabbos.

However, if it is a matter where if he does not work on Shabbos the Jew loses — such as a bathhouse, where those who come to bathe on Shabbos would not bathe there on weekdays — there are those who say it is asur min ha-din, since the Jew benefits from what the non-Jew does on Shabbos, in that he cannot make up this profit afterwards.

Scenario C — Renting the Land

The Jew rents the land to the non-Jew for a fixed rent, and the non-Jew works the land and takes all the profits.
In this scenario it is permitted min ha-din, since the Jew does not profit at all from the non-Jew's labor — for even if he does not work, he must pay the Jew his land rent — and the non-Jew works for himself, since he takes all the field's profits.

Scenario D — Arisus (Sharecropping)

The non-Jew receives the land to work in it on condition that he take half, a third, or a quarter of the profits. This too is permitted, as in the previous scenario, since the non-Jew works for his portion. Although the Jew also benefits incidentally, there is no issur min ha-din here.

8. Gader of Maaras Ayin — When Do We Decree

Fundamental kashya : If kablanus, haskarah and arisus are permitted from the standpoint of the essential din of amira le-akum, why did Chazal forbid some of these cases?
Terutz : All this is from the standpoint of the essential din of amira le-akum, but Chazal forbade two permitted matters because of maaras ayin — since it appears that one is acting in an issur :
  1. Something which is customarily done by a sechir yom — it is forbidden also to rent it out or give it in arisus, since the onlookers would suspect that the non-Jew is a sechir yom
  2. Even something customarily done but where the Jew loses on Shabbos if it is not worked — it is forbidden, since the Jew has hana'ah from what the non-Jew does on Shabbos
The yesod of maaras ayin here :
  • This is not an intrinsic issur
  • Rather a gezeirah Chazal because the public may err
  • The guf of the din — fixed wages, permitted. But maaras ayin — forbidden

9. Shittos of the Poskim on Kablanus — Mahloket

The Shittah of the Bach

The Bach (Siman 244, sof se'if katan 4) :
Wrote that one should not rely on the non-Jew, and what the Gemara said — that the non-Jew complies — that is according to the view of Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar there, but according to what the halakha follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, there is no proof to this; and one may say that even a non-Jew does not necessarily comply with what the Jew told him at the time of the rental.

And in Sha'arei Torah (sof se'if katan 5) he explained the taam — that even though the non-Jew has no quarrel with the Jew, and he finds another way, nevertheless it is forbidden.

The Shittah of Rabbi Akiva Eiger

Rabbi Akiva Eiger (s"k 3) :
Wrote that the taam of the issur is because the Jew is suspected — namely, that the non-Jew is a sechir yom — and it emerges that it is not clear to him to be machmir in this; and it appears that even in kablanus people might say that this is so.

The Central Mahloket — Is Kablanus Always Permitted?

Posek Shittah on a bathhouse in kablanus
The Rashba In kablanus it is permitted even for a bathhouse, since the non-Jew works for himself
The Rambam, Mahari Abuhav They forbade in a bathhouse even in kablanus because of maaras ayin
The Beis Yosef, Shulchan Aruch They brought the view of Mahari Abuhav as the ikar ha-halakha
The Rema in his Hagahah Depends on the minhag ha-makom and the intention
The Bach In kablanus, if the non-Jew did not take only a quarter or a third — permitted

10. The Shittos of the Magen Avraham and the Bach

The Kashya of the Magen Avraham on the Bach

The Bach wrote that for a bathhouse, kablanus is forbidden even if the non-Jew did not take only a small portion, since it still appears to be shelichus.
Kashya : But in kablanus the non-Jew takes for himself! If so, what is the difference between a bathhouse and a field?
The Magen Avraham terutzed (s"k 2) :
That in a bathhouse "it is explained in scenarios where amira le-akum is forbidden, etc., that in a scenario where the non-Jew need not work at all, it is permitted, but when a day is lost from the acquisition, he loses." That is — if the bathhouse is closed, the Jew loses.
The Magen Avraham is medayek : In every place where there is some concern from the standpoint of the land — that it is the Jew's — it is forbidden even in kablanus. But if the land is not the Jew's, it is permitted.

This is the terutz, that the bathhouse is considered as the Jew's land, and therefore there is maaras ayin because of the field and the house, and in kablanus it is forbidden as long as the land is still the Jew's.

11. The Sugya of Bathhouse and Oven

Why Specifically Bathhouse and Oven Are Forbidden?

Chakiras ha-yesod : What is common to a bathhouse and an oven, to distinguish them from a field and a mill?

  1. A bathhouse — it is the way of the Jew to bathe on Shabbos, and this counts as direct hana'as Shabbos
  2. An oven — it is the way to bake on Shabbos, and this is hana'as ma'akhal
By contrast :
  1. A field — there is no direct hana'ah on Shabbos, only general profit
  2. A mill — there is no direct hana'ah on Shabbos for the specific Jew
The yesod : In a bathhouse and an oven there is immediate personal hana'ah for the Jew on Shabbos, and therefore maaras ayin is stronger. The public sees a Jew entering the bathhouse or buying bread — and they will think the non-Jew is working as his shaliach.

The Connection with Mills — the Shittah of the Rema

The Rema in his Hagahah :
"And an oven follows the din of a bathhouse, and a mill follows the din of a field."

Explanation : With a mill, the flour is not needed immediately — one can grind from erev Shabbos. There is no maaras ayin that the non-Jew is grinding for me. By contrast with an oven — the public sees the hot bread and will think it was baked on Shabbos.

12. Se'if Beis — The Five Scenarios in Which Renting a Bathhouse Was Permitted

Introduction — From the General to the Particular

The first se'if forbade renting a bathhouse or oven because of maaras ayin (sesam mirchatz lav le-arisusa, and according to the minhag it is by sechir yom).

The second se'if comes to particularize five scenarios in which the maaras ayin is removed, and therefore renting the bathhouse or oven becomes permitted.

The analysis of the scenarios is based on the three layers of perush in this edition : biurei Shu"a, biurei Taz, and biurei Magen Avraham.

Scenario (A) — Established Practice and Publicity of the Matter

The lashon of the Shulchan Aruch : "If he has already rented them out to others year after year two and three times and the matter has become publicized thereby that it is not the way of this person to keep them in his possession and hire workers, but to rent them out — in such a case there is no issur of amira le-akum."

Chakira : What does the publicity solve? Maaras ayin is measured by the public. If the public knows that Reuven does not operate the bathhouse but rents it out, there is nothing to suspect him of, even if other townspeople conduct themselves otherwise. The public recognizes the specific reality of Reuven.

Nafka mina : A person for whom this is the first year of renting out — this scenario will not avail him. Continuity (2-3 years) and active publicity are needed.

Scenario (B) — Kablanus / Behavla'ah (the Girsa of the Tur)

Two nuschaos in the words of the Tur :
• Girsa "be-kablanus" — the non-Jew takes all the profit for a fixed wage
• Girsa "behavla'ah" — the wages for Shabbos and Yom Tov are absorbed within the weekday wages

The Beis Yosef corrected the Tur's girsa from "be-kablanus" to "behavla'ah" (what my master my father z"l said). But the Taz defends both girsaos : "And he wrote further that if we read 'behavla'ah,' this is its pshat... and if we read 'be-kablanus,' this is its pshat... it is one matter."

Chakiras ha-yesod : What is the conceptual difference between the two?
  • In kablanus — the non-Jew is the ba'al ha-inyan of the melakha (he assumed achrayus on the profit)
  • In havla'ah — the Shabbos wage is not distinguishable in the Jew's obligation to pay
And according to the Taz, if the non-Jew is "adata de-nafshei avid" — both scenarios are mattir. And according to Mahari Abuhav — only kablanus is mattir.

Scenario (C) — Bathhouse in Residential Quarters (Hagahas haRema)

The lashon of the Rema : "And likewise, if there is a bathhouse in residential dwellings and only the residents of the houses bathe in it."

The chakira of the Magen Avraham : Which scenario precisely did the Rema permit?
  1. For his household members alone — even if it stands in the reshus ha-rabbim, permitted
    (taam : because the firing is not recognizable to passersby outside)
  2. In a separate domain by itself — his neighbors know it is the Jew's, but it is known to them that he has rented it out
    (taam : the neighbors know)
The kashya of the Bach on the shittah of Mahari Abuhav : "If so, regarding mills — where it is the practice be-heter to rent them out, since they stand for arisus (Shu"a se'if 1) — yet if the non-Jew did not perform the work of the mill on Shabbos, the Jew would lose the profit of that day, and nevertheless, certainly here too we say that the non-Jew adata de-nafshei avid, and it is permitted le-khulei alma min ha-din."

Scenario (D) — Bought from a Non-Jew and Has Not Yet Settled In

Sugya : If the Jew purchased a bathhouse from a non-Jew but has not yet settled in (= not entered into ownership use) — is it permitted to rent it out to a non-Jew to operate on Shabbos?

The shittah of the Rema (according to the pshat) : The heter applies only if he rented them from a non-Jew, not if he bought it.

The shittah of the Beis Yosef + Magen Avraham + Bach : "Nevertheless, it appears to me to permit when the Jew has not settled in, even in the case of purchase, since it is not publicized that it is the Jew's and they will not come to suspect him."

Source of these matters : The Beis Yosef brought in the name of Rav Natronai Gaon (Toraso shel Rishonim Horowitz vol. 2 p. 58) that if one bought from a non-Jew and has not yet settled in — permitted. And we do not find that the Rema speaks of this. Dispute among the Acharonim whether the Rema agreed to this or was machmir.

Chakira : "Settling in" here = public consensus that he is the owner, not merely a legal change. Until the Jew has settled in, his name is not upon it in the public's eyes.

Scenario (E) — Only Rented from a Non-Jew / Explanation of the Shittah of Mahari Abuhav

The lashon of the Rema : "If the bathhouse does not belong to a Jew but he rented it from a non-Jew and re-rented it to another non-Jew — it is permitted."

Chakira : Why did the Rema specifically take "rented from a non-Jew" and not "bought from a non-Jew"?
• According to Mahari Abuhav : Even in kablanus it is forbidden because of maaras ayin — therefore only when ownership is not the Jew's, it was permitted.
• According to the Bach and the Taz : Even in kablanus it was permitted if the non-Jew is "adata de-nafshei avid" — and the Rema took rental because it was the easiest example, not because purchase is forbidden.

Nafka mina le-ma'aseh : A person who rented a place from a non-Jew for maintenance and again rented it to a third non-Jew — permitted le-khulei alma. A person who bought from a non-Jew and has already settled in — forbidden le-khulei alma. A person who bought and has not yet settled in = mahloket, and the ikar ha-din is permissive (like the view of the Magen Avraham).

Be-Diavad Scenario — Transgressed and Rented Out (Se'if 5 of biurei Magen Avraham)

The kashya : If he transgressed and rented out in a place where it is forbidden — is the wage itself permitted for the Jew to take?

Shittos :
  1. Asur min ha-din (scenarios that are asur min ha-din as sechir yom) — Shabbos wages are mublaim within the issur and are thus forbidden
  2. Forbidden because of maaras ayin alone — mahloket :
    • The Beis Yosef + the Bach (in the name of Rav Natronai Gaon) — its wages are permitted be-diavad
    • The Pri Megadim (Eshel Avraham s"k 3) — its wages are forbidden even be-diavad
Summary of the Beis Yosef : "If he transgressed and rented it out in a place forbidden because of maaras ayin — its wages are permitted. And the ikar taam of the heter : there is no essential issur here except because others should not suspect him."

Note on the sugya : This is not merely a monetary question — but a theoretical question about the character of the issur. If the issur is a social ordinance (maaras ayin), then be-diavad it does not contaminate the wages. If the issur is in the essence of the act (like the stringent view of Mahari Abuhav), the wages are contaminated.
Summary of the sugya of the five scenarios : Se'if Beis teaches a foundational principle — the issur of Se'if Alef is a social issur, not an intrinsic one. Whenever the social yesod in it (maaras ayin of the public) is removed — whether through publicity (Scenario A), through the structure of the contract (Scenario B), through the private context (Scenario C), or through the non-ownership of the Jew (Scenarios D-E) — the issur itself falls. This is a conception of an issur gezeirah, not of an intrinsic issur.

13. Attribution of the Melakha

The Sugya of "Adata de-Nafshei Avid"

Explanation of the Shulchan Aruch :
"De-sesam mirchatz lav le-arisusa — meaning, he does not receive it to perform melakha in it for a quarter or a third as his wages, but rather he gives him a fixed coin at the outset, and afterwards the non-Jew adata de-nafshei avid."
Two perushim :
  1. Simple pshat — the non-Jew works for himself, because he is a salaried worker with a fixed wage, and desires to finish quickly
  2. The Magen Avraham's pshat — even if he is a true sechir yom, since he is not acting for the Jew's benefit but for his wages, all that he works more is to his own benefit
The chakira : What is the criterion for "adata de-nafshei avid"?

  1. Fixed wage — the non-Jew takes a fixed wage and has an interest in finishing quickly
  2. Arisus / kablanus — the non-Jew is a partner in the profits, he has a true interest
  3. Rental — the land is essentially the non-Jew's, he works it as his own
And in all of these — the non-Jew is essentially "adata de-nafshei avid."

14. Nafka Mina le-Ma'aseh — In Modern Times

Modern situation Halakhah
Jewish-owned store with a non-Jewish manager If on kablanus (% of sales) — permitted. If sechir yom — forbidden.
Restaurant with a non-Jewish cook Like an oven — forbidden even in kablanus (maaras ayin).
Jewish-owned 24/7 factory Solution : sale of the factory to a non-Jew for Shabbos (special heter iska), or establishment of a non-Jewish corporation.
Agricultural field Arisus permitted in any case.
Gas station / mini-mart Depends on maaras ayin — usually full rental to a non-Jew is required.
Hi-tech company with a non-Jewish director If the non-Jew is a partner in the profits — permitted. As an employee — forbidden.
