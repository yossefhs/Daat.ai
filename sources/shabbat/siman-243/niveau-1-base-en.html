1. General context

What is this Siman about?

Siman רמ״ג deals with a fundamental practical question: may one entrust real property to a non-Jew (akum) who will work in it during Shabbos?

4 types of property are addressed:
  • מרחץ (merchatz) — a public bathhouse
  • שדה (sadeh) — an agricultural field
  • תנור (tanur) — an oven (bakery)
  • רחיים (reichayim) — a mill

The fundamental question

If I am a Yid and I own a public bathhouse, may I rent it out to a non-Jew who will work in it on Shabbos as well?

Short answer: it depends on the type of rental and the type of property.

Connection to other Simanim

Important — Place in the Shulchan Aruch:
  • 📚 Siman רמ״ב (242) — Honoring Shabbos (oneg)
  • 📚 Siman רמ״ג (243) — Our Siman: Field and bathhouse
  • 📚 Siman רמ״ד (244) — Having a non-Jew do a melachah
  • 📚 Siman רמ״ו (246) — Lending and renting objects to a non-Jew
Siman רמ״ג and Siman רמ״ו are complementary — they deal with the same topic (renting to an akum), but one with real estate and the other with movable objects.

2. Seif Alef — The bathhouse (מרחץ)

Original text

לֹא יַשְׂכִּיר אָדָם מֶרְחָץ שֶׁלּוֹ לְגוֹי, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנִּקְרֵאת עַל שְׁמוֹ וְגוֹי זֶה עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, דִּסְתַם מֶרְחָץ לָאו לַאֲרִיסוּתָא.

English translation

A person should not rent out his bathhouse to a non-Jew, because it is called by his name (= the Yid's) and this akum does melachah in it on Shabbos — since a bathhouse is generally not taken in arisus (sharecropping).

Why is it assur?

3 combined reasons:
  1. נקראת על שמו — The bathhouse is known as belonging to the Yid. Passersby will think the Yid is having the akum work for him on Shabbos.
  2. גוי עושה מלאכה בו בשבת — The akum is doing melachah there (heating water, cleaning) during Shabbos.
  3. סתם מרחץ לאו לאריסותא — The customary arrangement for a bathhouse is NOT arisus, but a fixed wage (sechir yom or sechar katzuv). Therefore there is marit ayin = people will think the akum is the Yid's hired employee.
🔴 Conclusion of Seif Alef: Renting out a public bathhouse to a non-Jew who will work in it on Shabbos = FORBIDDEN, even if rented for the entire week, on account of marit ayin (מראית עין).

3. Seif Beis — When a bathhouse (מרחץ) may be rented

The central idea of Seif Beis

Seif Alef established a general rule: the bathhouse is forbidden to be rented to an akum (because of marit ayin).

Seif Beis enumerates 5 specific situations where the marit ayin disappears, and where the bathhouse (and oven) become permitted again.

Original text (Mechaber)

אֲפִלּוּ מֶרְחָץ אוֹ תַנּוּר שֶׁהִתְבָּאֵר בַּסָּעִיף הַקּוֹדֵם שֶׁאָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָם — אִם כְּבָר הִשְׂכִּירָם לַאֲחֵרִים שָׁנָה אַחַר שָׁנָה ב׳ פְּעָמִים וְשָׁלֹשׁ וְנִתְפַּרְסֵם הַדָּבָר עַל יְדֵי כָּךְ שֶׁאֵין דַּרְכּוֹ שֶׁל אָדָם זֶה לְהַשְׁאִיר אוֹתָם בְּחֶזְקָתוֹ וְלִשְׂכּוֹר פּוֹעֲלִים, אֶלָּא לְהַשְׂכִּירָם — שֶׁבָּזֶה אֵין אִסּוּר אֲמִירָה לְגוֹי, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהַגּוֹי עוֹשֶׂה לְדַעַת עַצְמוֹ.

English translation

Even a bathhouse or an oven — which in the previous seif were declared forbidden to rent out because they are usually worked by daily wage — if he has already rented them out to others two or three years consecutively, and the matter has become publicized to the point that everyone knows it is not his way to keep them in his possession and hire workers but rather to rent them out — then it is permitted, since there is no issur of amira l'akum: the non-Jew is working on his own behalf.

Text of the Hagahah of the Rema (on Seif Beis)

וְכֵן אִם יֵשׁ מֶרְחָץ בְּבָתֵּי דִירָה וְאֵין רוֹחֲצִין בַּמֶּרְחָץ רַק אוֹמֵן שֶׁבָּתִּים — מֻתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּירוֹ לַגּוֹי, דְּמֵאַחַר שֶׁאֵין שְׁכֵנֵי הַדִּירָה בּוֹ אֶלָּא יוֹדְעִים לָהֶם שֶׁהִשְׂכִּירוּ לַגּוֹי, אֵין מָקוֹם לַחֲשָׁד.
Similarly, if there is a bathhouse within a residential dwelling and only the residents of the dwelling bathe in it — it is permitted to rent it out to the akum, because the neighbors know that it has been rented and there is no basis for chashad.

The 5 sub-topics of Seif Beis

The traditional analysis (according to the be'urim on the Shulchan Aruch / Taz / Magen Avraham accompanying the text) extracts 5 distinct cases where rental becomes permitted:

(1) רגילות להשכיר — The practice of rental is publicized
If the owner has rented his bathhouse/oven publicly for 2-3 consecutive years and the practice is known → the community recognizes that it is rental and not sechir yom.
🟢 Permitted, since the publicized minhag nullifies the marit ayin.
(2) בהבלעה / קבלנות — Lump-sum contract absorbing Shabbos
The contract is a global lump sum (the Shabbos wage is absorbed into the weekday wage, or else the akum himself takes all the profits).
Classical discussion: the Tur writes "b'kablanus" and the Beis Yosef emends to "b'havla'ah" — the author of the be'urim on the Taz defends both: "קבלנות בזה והבלעה — חד מילתא היא" (in this context they are one thing).
🟢 Permitted, since the akum is paid for the entire contract, not for Shabbos specifically.
(3) מרחץ בבתי דירה — Bathhouse in a residential dwelling (Hagahos of the Rema)
If the bathhouse is private, within a dwelling, and only the residents enter → the neighbors know that it is rented to the akum.
🟢 Permitted, since marit ayin vis-à-vis the broader public does not exist.
(4) רק שכרם מגוי — The bathhouse was only rented from a non-Jew (Hagahos of the Rema)
If the Yid does not own the bathhouse (he himself rented it from a non-Jew) and sublets it to another akum → the bathhouse is not "called by his name" ("שמו עליו").
🟢 Permitted, since the Yid is not the owner — so there is no appearance of "his" bathhouse working on Shabbos.
(5) קנה מגוי ועדיין לא ישב בה — Bought from a non-Jew and not yet occupied
A discussed case: the Yid has purchased the bathhouse from a non-Jew but has not yet settled in.
Position of the Rema: mentions only the sublet (case 4).
Position of the B"H + be'urim of the Magen Avraham + Beis Yosef in the name of Rav Natronai Gaon: also permitted in this case, since the bathhouse is not yet "אצלו" (associated with him in the public mind).
🟢 Permitted according to the majority of Acharonim.

And the שדה (field)? — a pedagogical contrast

To properly understand why the bathhouse needs these 5 conditions while the field is permitted directly, here is the reason:

For a שדה (field), the customary arrangement IS arisus (sharecropping):
  • People recognize that it is in arisus — no marit ayin of sechir yom
  • The akum has a personal interest in completing the work (he will take a share of the produce)
  • Therefore אדעתיה דנפשיה עביד — he works for himself, not for the Yid
→ Permitted without any additional condition.
🟢 Conclusion of Seif Beis: Seif Alef laid down a general rule (bathhouse forbidden). Seif Beis shows that this rule is not absolute: as soon as marit ayin disappears (through publication, type of contract, private context, or non-ownership), the rental becomes permitted again. The שדה remains the archetype of "permitted without condition" because arisus is its natural minhag.

4. The Hagahah of the Rema — Oven and mill

Text of the Rema

הַגָּהּ — וַאֲפִלּוּ עַל פִּי שֶׁלֹּא לְקָחָהּ הַגּוֹי רַק לִשְׁלִישׁ אוֹ לִרְבִיעַ וְיֵשׁ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל הֲנָאָה בְּמַה שֶּׁהַגּוֹי עוֹבֵד בְּשַׁבָּת, שָׁרֵי דְּגוֹי אַדַּעְתָּא דְנַפְשֵׁיהּ עָבִיד. וְתַנּוּר דִּינוֹ כַּמֶּרְחָץ, וְרֵחַיִם דִּינָם כְּשָׂדֶה.

English translation

Hagahah of the Rema: And even if the akum has only taken it for a third or a quarter, and the Yid has hana'ah from what the akum does on Shabbos — it is permitted, since the akum is working on his own behalf.

And an oven (תנור) — its din is like the bathhouse (forbidden);
and a mill (רחיים) — its din is like the field (permitted).
The Rema's extension:
  • Oven (תנור) = like the bathhouse → 🔴 Forbidden (the minhag is sechir yom)
  • Mill (רחיים) = like the field → 🟢 Permitted (the minhag is kablanus)
The fundamental rule is the minhag of the trade in the region.

5. The 4 types of rental

To properly understand this Siman, one must know the 4 ways of having an akum do work:

Type 1 — שכיר יום (Sechir yom / daily wage)

The akum is the direct employee of the Yid. All revenue from the work belongs to the Yid, who pays a fixed daily wage.

Example: "I give you 100 shekels per day to work in my field."
🔴 ALWAYS FORBIDDEN because this is the typical case of amira l'akum (instruction to a non-Jew) — the Yid explicitly tells him what to do.

Type 2 — קבלנות (Kablanus / lump-sum contract)

The akum is an independent contractor. He commits to completing a job in exchange for a fixed sum, without being told when to work.

Example: "I pay you 5000 shekels to plow my entire field — do it whenever you want."
🟢 PERMITTED in principle, since the akum is working for his own interest (אדעתיה דנפשיה).
🔴 EXCEPT if the minhag of the trade is sechir yom (case of the bathhouse and oven).

Type 3 — השכרת קרקע (Pure rental of land)

The Yid rents out the land to the akum for a fixed price. The akum works there and keeps all the revenue.

Example: "I rent you my field for 10,000 shekels per year. Work it and keep the entire harvest."
🟢 PERMITTED since the Yid has no benefit from the Shabbos work — he has already been paid.
🔴 EXCEPT for marit ayin (case of the bathhouse).

Type 4 — אריסות (Arisus / sharecropping)

The akum receives the land in order to share the revenues with the Yid (1/3, 1/4 or 1/2).

Example: "Work my field, and we will share the harvest: 2/3 for you, 1/3 for me."
🟢 PERMITTED since the akum has a personal interest in doing the work well (he will take his share). This is explicitly the case authorized by our Seif Beis.

6. The 3 key concepts

Concept 1 — אמירה לנכרי (Amira l'akum / instruction to a non-Jew)

General rule (Mishnah Shabbos 121a): any melachah forbidden to a Yid on Shabbos cannot be told to an akum to do in his place.

This is what is called a shevus (rabbinic prohibition).
Why is it assur? 3 reasons according to the Rishonim:
  1. Sheiltos: "ממצוא חפצך ודבר דבר" (Yeshayahu) — one should not speak of melachah on Shabbos
  2. Rashi: shelichus — the akum is like the Yid's agent
  3. Rambam: so that Shabbos should not be light in the eyes of the Yid (who would end up working himself)

Concept 2 — מראית עין (Marit ayin / appearance)

Appearance. Even when something is permitted on the pure halachic level, Chazal forbade it when people might THINK there is a transgression.
Application in our Siman:
  • The bathhouse is forbidden in kablanus / arisus ONLY because of marit ayin
  • Why? Because people know the minhag — a bathhouse is usually leased through sechir yom
  • So seeing an akum working in a Yid's bathhouse, they will think he is the Yid's employee

Concept 3 — אדעתיה דנפשיה עביד

"He acts on his own behalf" (in Aramaic).

This is the criterion that permits arisus and kablanus: we consider that the akum is not working on a mission for the Yid, but for his own interest.
Application: When the akum has a personal interest in finishing quickly (because he takes a share of the produce, or because he has a lump-sum contract to complete), we say that he "works for himself" — so the Yid is not responsible.

8. Modern practical cases

📍 Case 1 — A Jewish merchant and his non-Jewish employee

A Yid owns a store and has hired a non-Jew as an employee.
→ If the akum is sechir yom (fixed daily wage) = 🔴 Forbidden for him to work on Shabbos
→ If the akum is on kablanus (% of sales or commissions) = possibly permitted depending on the minhag of the trade

📍 Case 2 — A Jewish-owned factory and a non-Jewish manager

A Yid owns a factory and his non-Jewish manager decides when the factory operates.
→ If the factory is known as belonging to the Yid = problem of marit ayin
→ Solution: rent the factory on kablanus to the manager (who becomes owner of the profits)

📍 Case 3 — A bakery and a non-Jewish baker

A Yid owns a bakery. The non-Jewish baker wants to work on Shabbos.
→ תנור = like מרחץ = 🔴 Forbidden even on kablanus
→ Solution: rent the bakery completely to the baker for the week

📍 Case 4 — A gas station and a non-Jewish operator

A Yid owns a gas station. How does it operate on Shabbos?
→ Common solution: rent the entire station to a non-Jew for Shabbos (specific heter mechirah)
→ Always consult a Rav for practical implementation!

Part II — The 16 seifim of the SHHaRav

Whereas the Mechaber handles the subject in only 2 seifim (the forbidden bathhouse, then 5 cases of heter), the Shulchan Aruch of the Admur HaZakein (שולחן ערוך הרב) unfolds Siman רמ״ג into 16 seifim. This more detailed structure allows the Rav to present each practical case and each halachic nuance with its own logic — from the foundation of the issur of amira l'akum (Seif Alef) to the fine casuistry of the private bathhouse in a courtyard of non-Jews (Seif Tes-Zayin). What follows is a pedagogical study of each seif, in the shitah of the Rav.

Logical plan of the 16 seifim of the Rav:
  • Seifim א-ב: The theoretical foundation — the issur of amira and the obligation to protest
  • Seifim ג-ה: Forbidden contractual structures (sechir shanah, sechir yamim) and their kenas
  • Seifim ו-י: Types of property and the distinction of minhag hamakom (מרחץ vs שדה vs רחיים vs תנור)
  • Seifim יא-יב: Schar Shabbos and the rule of havla'ah
  • Seifim יג-טז: Particular cases — bediavad, erev Shabbos, unidentified bathhouse, courtyard of non-Jews

9. Seif Alef (SHHaRav) — The foundation: אמירה לנכרי and shelichus mi-deRabbanan

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif א)

דִּין הַמַּשְׂכִּיר שָׂדֶה וּמֶרְחָץ לְנָכְרִי וּבוֹ ט"ז סְעִיפִים:

אָסְרוּ חֲכָמִים לוֹמַר לְנָכְרִי לַעֲשׂוֹת לָנוּ מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת, בֵּין בְּחִנָּם בֵּין בְּשָׂכָר, וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאוֹמֵר לוֹ מִקֹּדֶם הַשַּׁבָּת שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת, וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לְאוֹתָהּ מְלָאכָה עַד לְאַחַר הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁכְּשֶׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת הוּא עוֹשֶׂה בִּשְׁלִיחוּת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל. וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵין אוֹמְרִים "שְׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל אָדָם כְּמוֹתוֹ" מִן הַתּוֹרָה אֶלָּא בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל הַנַּעֲשֶׂה שָׁלִיחַ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר בִּתְרוּמָה "כֵּן תָּרִימוּ גַם אַתֶּם תְּרוּמַת ה'", וְדָרְשׁוּ חֲכָמִים "גַּם" לְרַבּוֹת שְׁלוּחֲכֶם, מִכַּאן שֶׁ"שְּׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל אָדָם כְּמוֹתוֹ", וּמָה "אַתֶּם" בְּנֵי בְרִית אַף שְׁלוּחֲכֶם בְּנֵי בְרִית, אֲבָל הַנָּכְרִי אֵינוֹ בְּתוֹרַת שְׁלִיחוּת מִן הַתּוֹרָה, מִכָּל מָקוֹם מִדִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים יֵשׁ שְׁלִיחוּת לְנָכְרִי לְחוּמְרָא.

וְיֵשׁ רֶמֶז לְאִסּוּר זֶה בְּשַׁבָּת וְיוֹם טוֹב מִן הַתּוֹרָה, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר בְּיוֹם טוֹב "כָּל מְלָאכָה לֹא יֵעָשֶׂה בָהֶם", מַשְׁמָע אֲפִלּוּ עַל יְדֵי אֲחֵרִים שֶׁאֵין מְצֻוִּים עַל הַשְּׁבִיתָה בְּיוֹם טוֹב, וְקַל וָחֹמֶר לְשַׁבָּת.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אֵין זוֹ אֶלָּא אַסְמַכְתָּא בְּעָלְמָא. וְעִקַּר הָאִסּוּר אֲמִירָה לְנָכְרִי בֵּין בְּשַׁבָּת בֵּין בְּיוֹם טוֹב אֵינוֹ אֶלָּא מִדִּבְרֵיהֶם, כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא תְהֵא שַׁבָּת קַלָּה בְּעֵינֵי הָעָם וְיָבֹאוּ לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּעַצְמָן, וְכֵן בְּיוֹם טוֹב, וַאֲפִלּוּ בְּחֻלּוֹ שֶׁל מוֹעֵד, וְכֵן בִּשְׁאָר כָּל אִסּוּרִין שֶׁבַּתּוֹרָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֲרוּ בִּמְקוֹמָן.

English translation

Halachos of one who rents out a field and a bathhouse to a non-Jew — contains 16 seifim:

Chazal forbade telling a non-Jew to do melachah for us on Shabbos — whether for free or for pay, and even when one tells him before Shabbos to do it on Shabbos, and even when one does not need the work until after Shabbos; for when the akum acts on Shabbos, he acts as the Yid's shaliach (shelichus).

And although the rule "a man's shaliach is like himself" only applies on the level of the Torah to a Yid appointed as shaliach by another Yid — for it is said regarding terumah: "You too (גַּם) shall set apart the terumah of Hashem", and Chazal interpreted "גַם" to include your shaliach, whence the rule "shelucho shel adam k'moso"; and from the comparison "you" — bnei bris, so too your shaliach must be bnei bris — therefore the non-Jew is not in the category of shelichus min haTorah — nevertheless, miDivrei Sofrim, the non-Jew is treated as a shaliach l'chumrah (מדברי סופרים יש שליחות לנכרי לחומרא).

There is also a remez (allusion) for this issur in the Torah, both for Shabbos and Yom Tov — for it is said regarding Yom Tov: "no melachah shall be done in them" — which suggests "even by others" (who are not commanded in the resting of Yom Tov), and kal vachomer for Shabbos.

But this is only an asmachta (mere scriptural support). And the essence of the issur of amira l'akum, on Shabbos as on Yom Tov, is only miDerabbanan — so that Shabbos should not be light in the eyes of the people, lest they come to do melachah themselves. And likewise for Yom Tov, even for Chol HaMoed, and so for all the issurim of the Torah, as will be explained in their places.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav opens the Siman with the theoretical foundation of the entire edifice: why is it forbidden to tell an akum to work for us on Shabbos? This seif is unique — whereas the Mechaber begins directly with the case of the bathhouse, the Rav takes the time to demonstrate the root of the issur. Three key elements:

The three pillars of the Rav's Seif Alef:
  1. The rule of shelichus (שליחות) — is the akum the Yid's shaliach? Nuanced answer: not on the biblical level, yes miDerabbanan (l'chumrah).
  2. The scriptural asmachta — there is an allusion in the pasuk "כל מלאכה לא יעשה בהם" for Yom Tov, and kal vachomer for Shabbos — but this is only support (אסמכתא), not biblical proof.
  3. The ta'am (טעם) of the Rabbanan — the effective reason for the issur is miDerabbanan: so that Shabbos should not become light in the people's minds.

The halachic question

Why does the Rav take the trouble, at the opening of the Siman, to establish whether the issur is min haTorah or miDivrei Sofrim? Because all the lifting of particular cases that will follow (kablanus, arisus, hasakharah, chutz l'techum, etc.) depend on the exact nature of the issur: a derabbanan issur tolerates allowances (heteirim) that the Torah would never accept.

The opinions

Source Position
Rashi (Avodah Zarah 15a, Shabbos 153a) The issur of amira is grounded in the principle of shelichus — the akum is the Yid's agent.
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 6:1) Explicitly gives the ta'am: "so that Shabbos should not be light in the eyes of the people." No mention of shelichus.
Mechaber (OC 243:1) Does not discuss the nature of the issur — passes directly to the case of the bathhouse.
Mishnah Berurah (243:1) Clarifies that the issur is derabbanan and that its reason is the kalus rosh (lightening of Shabbos).
SHHaRav (243:1) Masterful synthesis: (1) takes up Rashi (shelichus derabbanan); (2) adds the biblical asmachta; (3) returns to the Rambam's ta'am as the operative cause. Triple architecture — each of the poskim finds his place.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's conceptual construction on 4 levels:
  1. Level 1 — The logic of shelichus: the akum is almost like the Yid's shaliach, but not quite — yes derabbanan, no min haTorah.
  2. Level 2 — The biblical allusion: "כל מלאכה לא יעשה בהם" for Yom Tov contains a remez suggesting "even by others."
  3. Level 3 — The actual nature: only asmachta — the issur remains derabbanan.
  4. Level 4 — The ta'am: שלא תהא שבת קלה בעיני העם. It is this reasoning that will justify the later allowances.

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — Asking your non-Jewish colleague to send an email on Shabbos: 🔴 Forbidden, even by anticipation on Friday. This is the archetype of amira l'akum, since the work benefits the Yid.
  • Case 2 — The non-Jewish doorman of a Jewish building: if he is sechir yom and the Yid tells him "take out the trash on Shabbos," 🔴 forbidden. If he acts on his own initiative without instruction, see Seif Beis.
  • Case 3 — Asking a non-Jewish delivery person to deliver a package during Shabbos: 🔴 Forbidden, since delivery constitutes melachah (transport, all the more so if it crosses a public domain).
  • Case 4 — A Jewish developer who runs a SaaS platform: asking a non-Jewish subcontractor to push a deployment on Shabbos = 🔴 forbidden. Solution: schedule an automated deployment in advance (see Level 2 on modern application).
  • Case 5 — A car breaks down on Shabbos and a non-Jewish mechanic: one cannot ask the mechanic to come repair it on Shabbos — even for payment, even if the car is needed to go to shul. Except for pikuach nefesh, the issur of amira remains.

Points to remember

  • The issur of amira l'akum is derabbanan (not min haTorah)
  • The mechanism: shelichus l'chumrah (יש שליחות לנכרי לחומרא)
  • The operative ta'am: שלא תהא שבת קלה בעיני העם
  • The issur applies even to an instruction given before Shabbos
  • The issur applies even if the result is only used after Shabbos
  • This theoretical base grounds all the heteirim that will follow in the Siman
Comprehension question: Why does the Rav take care, in Seif Alef, to affirm both that "יש שליחות לנכרי לחומרא" (shelichus l'chumrah) and that the operative cause is "שלא תהא שבת קלה בעיני העם"? Isn't this redundant?

10. Seif Beis (SHHaRav) — The non-Jew acting on his own: when must one protest?

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ב)

וְהִפְלִיגוּ חֲכָמִים בְּאִסּוּר זֶה וְגָזְרוּ בוֹ גְּזֵרוֹת, כְּגוֹן אִם עָשָׂה הַנָּכְרִי מֵעַצְמוֹ בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל — אָסְרוּ לֵהָנוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת מִמְּלַאכְתּוֹ וּלְמוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת עַד בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשֶׂה, מִשּׁוּם גְּזֵרָה שֶׁמָּא יֹאמְרוּ לוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת.

וְכֵן אִם רוֹאֵהוּ עוֹשֶׂה מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת בִּשְׁבִילוֹ — פְּעָמִים שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת, כְּגוֹן אִם יַגִּיעַ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל רֶוַח מִמֶּנָּה אֲפִלּוּ לְאַחַר הַשַּׁבָּת (וְאִם גַּם לוֹ מַגִּיעַ רֶוַח מִמֶּנָּה — יִתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ד וְרנ"ב).

English translation

Chazal extended this issur by adding decrees — for example, if the akum acts on his own for the benefit of a Yid, they forbade benefiting from his work on Shabbos and even at the conclusion of Shabbos, for the time it would have taken to redo it — this is the gezeirah "lest one come to tell him to do melachah on Shabbos."

And likewise: if one sees the akum doing melachah on Shabbos for one's benefit — one must sometimes protest, for example if the Yid derives benefit even after Shabbos. (If the akum also benefits — will be explained at simanim רמ"ד and רנ"ב.)

What is this Seif about?

After establishing the root of the issur (Seif Alef), the Rav moves to the rabbinic extension: Chazal forbade not only direct instruction but also declared the benefits of the akum's spontaneous work forbidden. The Rav distinguishes two situations:

Two scenarios:
  1. Passive benefit (the akum did spontaneously, I discover afterwards) — forbidden to benefit on Shabbos and at the conclusion during the time "k'dei she-ye'aseh."
  2. Active vision (I see him acting on Shabbos for me) — one must sometimes protest (mecha'ah) — not always, only when the Yid derives benefit even post-Shabbos.

The halachic question

Does the Yid's silence amount to consent? The Rav answers yes, under certain conditions. The mecha'ah (protest) is not just a social convention — it is a halachic act that releases the Yid from complicity.

The opinions

Source Position
Talmud Shabbos 122a "גוי שעשה מלאכה מעצמו לישראל אסור לישראל ליהנות ממנה" — lays down the principle.
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 6:2) Clarifies: forbidden to benefit on Shabbos and at the conclusion, "k'dei she-ye'aseh."
Mechaber (OC 252:2) Takes up the rule "k'dei she-ye'aseh" without nuance on the protest.
Mishnah Berurah (252:9) Discusses the case of mecha'ah: one must protest if one derives lasting benefit.
SHHaRav (243:2) Two-stage synthesis: (a) prohibition of benefit = absolute rule; (b) obligation to protest = conditional rule (only if post-Shabbos benefit). The cross-reference to simanim רמ"ד and רנ"ב shows the systemic coherence of the Rav.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's algorithm for the spontaneous non-Jew:
  1. Is the work for the Yid? If yes → forbidden to benefit on Shabbos + motza"sh "k'dei she-ye'aseh"
  2. Does the Yid see the work in progress? If yes →
  3. Is there a post-Shabbos benefit for the Yid? If yes → obligation to protest
  4. Does the akum also derive a benefit? Case reserved for simanim רמ"ד and רנ"ב (complex mechanism).

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — The non-Jewish neighbor turns on the light in the stairwell by mistake: if I can take another route, I do not benefit. If the light is explicitly for me (he turns it on at my arrival), I should not linger there.
  • Case 2 — My non-Jewish employee decides to finish an important report on Shabbos without being asked: I must protest (a message after Shabbos suffices), since I derive lasting benefit. The report cannot be used immediately.
  • Case 3 — The doorman vacuums the lobby on Shabbos on his own initiative: if I live in the building, the clean lobby benefits me durably → I must protest (to the management or directly).
  • Case 4 — The non-Jewish cleaning lady comes on a different day and it falls on Shabbos: I must inform her on arrival that she should not work — not after the fact, but in real time when I notice.
  • Case 5 — The Amazon driver drops off my package at my door on Shabbos: if the package is personally addressed to me and I scheduled the delivery, I have lasting benefit → ideally, schedule delivery for after Shabbos at the time of order.

Points to remember

  • The akum's spontaneous work for the Yid is forbidden to benefit from on Shabbos and "k'dei she-ye'aseh" afterwards
  • The obligation to protest (mecha'ah) is conditional on lasting benefit
  • Prolonged silence becomes halachic complicity
  • Systemic cross-reference to Siman רמ"ד (chutz l'techum) and רנ"ב (erev Shabbos)
  • The "k'dei she-ye'aseh" rule prevents indirect profit — base of the entire doctrine of the akum on Shabbos
Comprehension question: Why is mecha'ah obligatory only sometimes and not systematically? What is the logic of "post-Shabbos benefit" as the trigger?

11. Seif Gimmel (SHHaRav) — Bathhouse, oven, mill rented to a yearly non-Jewish worker

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ג)

לְפִיכָךְ מִי שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ מֶרְחָץ שֶׁרוֹחֲצִים בָּהּ הַכֹּל וְנוֹתְנִים לוֹ שָׂכָר, אוֹ שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ תַּנּוּר שֶׁאוֹפִין בּוֹ הַכֹּל וְנוֹתְנִים לוֹ שָׂכָר, אוֹ שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ רֵחַיִם שֶׁטּוֹחֲנִין בָּהֶן הַכֹּל וְנוֹתְנִין לוֹ שָׂכָר, וְרוֹצֶה לִשְׂכּוֹר לוֹ נָכְרִי לְכָל הַשָּׁנָה שֶׁיִּתְעַסֵּק בְּכָל הַמְּלָאכוֹת הַצְּרִיכוֹת בַּמֶּרְחָץ וְרֵחַיִם וְתַנּוּר, וִיקַבֵּל הַשָּׂכָר מִן הָרוֹחֲצִים וְהַטּוֹחֲנִים וְהָאוֹפִים וִיבִיאֵהוּ לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל — צָרִיךְ הוּא לִמְחוֹת בְּהַנָּכְרִי וְלֹא יַנִּיחֶנּוּ שֶׁיִּתְעַסֵּק בָּהֶם בְּשַׁבָּת בִּשְׁבִיל נָכְרִים שֶׁיִּרְחֲצוּ וְיִטְחֲנוּ וְיֹאפוּ שָׁם, לְפִי שֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מַרְוִיחַ בַּעֲבוֹדַת הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁמַּגִּיעַ לוֹ שָׂכָר מִזֶּה.

(וַאֲפִלּוּ אִם הַנָּכְרִי אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לַעֲבוֹד כְּלוּם, שֶׁכְּבָר הֵכִין הַכֹּל מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, מִכָּל מָקוֹם מַה שֶּׁהַנָּכְרִי הַזֶּה מְקַבֵּל הַשָּׂכָר מֵהַנָּכְרִים הוּא עוֹשֶׂה זֶה בִּשְׁבִיל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וּלְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל עַצְמוֹ אָסוּר לוֹ לְקַבֵּל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁל יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, לָכֵן אָסוּר גַּם כֵּן שֶׁיְּקַבֵּל הַנָּכְרִי בִּשְׁבִילוֹ).

וְאֵין לְהַתִּיר לְפִי שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי טוֹרֵחַ לְעַצְמוֹ בִּשְׁבִיל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁקָּצַץ לוֹ, כֵּיוָן שֶׁאִם הָיָה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מוֹנֵעַ אֶת עַצְמוֹ מֵרֶוַח הַשַּׁבָּת וְהָיָה אוֹמֵר לַנָּכְרִי שֶׁלֹּא יִהְיֶה כְלָל בְּשַׁבָּת אֵצֶל הַמֶּרְחָץ וְרֵחַיִם וְתַנּוּר לֹא הָיָה מְנַכֶּה לוֹ כְּלוּם מִשְּׂכָרוֹ, שֶׁהֲרֵי לֹא שְׂכָרוֹ לְכָל יוֹם וְיוֹם בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ אֶלָּא לְשָׁנָה אוֹ חֹדֶשׁ בְּבַת אַחַת.

English translation

Therefore: one who owns a bathhouse where everyone bathes and pays him a wage, or an oven where everyone bakes and pays him, or a mill where everyone grinds and pays him, and who wants to hire a non-Jew for the entire year to handle all the work needed (heating, operation, etc.), receive payment from the customers and bring it to the Yid — must protest to the akum and not let him operate on Shabbos for non-Jews who come to bathe, grind, or bake — because the Yid profits from the akum's work on Shabbos, since he receives the wages from it.

(And even if the akum has no work to do — everything having been prepared before Shabbos — nevertheless the fact that he receives the payment from the other non-Jews, he does this for the Yid; and the Yid himself has no right to receive the wage of Shabbos — therefore it is also forbidden that the akum receive it for him.)

And one cannot permit based on "the akum is working for himself" (since he gets his fixed wage) — because if the Yid renounced the Shabbos profit and told the akum "do not be at the bathhouse on Shabbos at all," the Yid would deduct nothing from his wage — since he did not hire him day by day but in bulk for the year or month.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav moves to the most complex concrete case: a Yid owns a bathhouse (or oven, or mill) and wants to hire an annual non-Jew to manage it. The question is subtle: the akum has a global annual wage, not by the day — may he operate on Shabbos? Answer: no, because it is the Yid who pockets the Shabbos receipts.

The halachic question

The paradox: one might think this case is permitted since the akum acts for himself (אדעתיה דנפשיה) — he gets his annual wage whether he operates or not. The Rav refutes this reasoning: precisely because the akum is paid "in bulk," if he doesn't work on Shabbos the Yid deducts nothing — so the Shabbos work serves only the Yid (who pockets the receipts). This is a case where "אדעתיה דנפשיה" does not work.

The opinions

Source Position
Talmud Avodah Zarah 21a-22a Case of the bathhouse (merchatz): forbidden to rent it to an akum (since called by the Yid's name).
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 6:12-13) If the Yid receives the receipts on Shabbos → forbidden, it is "schar Shabbos."
Mechaber (OC 243:1) The bathhouse is not rented — "סתם מרחץ לאו לאריסותא."
Tosafos Shabbos 122a Discusses the case of the akum who acts for himself: criterion = marginal benefit.
SHHaRav (243:3) Masterful analysis: demolishes the false reasoning "אדעתיה דנפשיה" by pointing to the structure of the contract: if the wage is in bulk, the akum has no personal interest in working on Shabbos — so he works for the Yid.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's test to distinguish "for himself" / "for the Yid":
  1. Hypothesis: if the Yid told the akum "do not work on Shabbos"
  2. Would the wage decrease?
  3. If YES (the akum loses something) → he works for himself = permitted (subject to marit ayin)
  4. If NO (the akum gets his wage anyway) → he works for the Yid = forbidden

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A Yid owns a laundromat and hires a non-Jewish manager at a fixed annual salary: 🔴 Forbidden for the laundromat to operate on Shabbos — the Yid pockets the receipts, the manager has no marginal interest in working on Shabbos.
  • Case 2 — A Jewish hotel with a non-Jewish receptionist paid monthly: 🔴 Forbidden for the desk to operate on Shabbos (check-in payments) — solution: rent the front desk to the manager or suspend payments on Shabbos.
  • Case 3 — A Jewish bakery with a non-Jewish baker on annual salary: 🔴 Forbidden for him to operate on Shabbos — this is the very archetype of the Seif. Solution: rent the oven on kablanus (see following seifim).
  • Case 4 — A Jewish parking lot managed by a monthly-paid non-Jewish agent: 🔴 Forbidden to collect on Shabbos — solution: barrier raised, or free parking on Shabbos, or rent the lot to the manager.
  • Case 5 — A Jewish e-commerce website with a non-Jewish sysadmin on monthly salary: 🔴 Forbidden for him to maintain the site on Shabbos if the Yid collects the orders — solution: suspension of payments on Shabbos (technical setting).

Points to remember

  • A bulk wage (annual/monthly) does not free the Yid from responsibility
  • The criterion: would the akum lose something if he did not work on Shabbos?
  • If no → the work is actually for the Yid → forbidden
  • Even if the akum believes he is acting for himself, halachic objectivity prevails
  • The Yid may not receive schar Shabbos via a non-Jewish intermediary (see Seif Yod-Alef)
Comprehension question: If the akum is paid monthly and, in his mind, firmly believes he is working for his own profit — why does the Rav refuse to retain this subjective intention?

12. Seif Daled (SHHaRav) — שכיר לימים: the sechir yom is forbidden a fortiori

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ד)

וְכָל שֶׁכֵּן שֶׁאִם שָׂכַר הַנָּכְרִי לְיָמִים, שֶׁאָמַר לוֹ בְּעַד כָּל יוֹם וְיוֹם שֶׁתִּתְעַסֵּק בַּמֶּרְחָץ אוֹ תַּנּוּר אוֹ רֵחַיִם אֶתֵּן לְךָ כָּךְ וְכָךְ, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁלֹּא אָמַר לוֹ שֶׁיִּתְעַסֵּק בְּשַׁבָּת, וְהַנָּכְרִי מֵעַצְמוֹ הוֹלֵךְ וּמִתְעַסֵּק גַּם בְּשַׁבָּת — צָרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדוֹ וְלֹא יַנִּיחֶנּוּ לְהִתְעַסֵּק בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁאַף שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי מִתְכַּוֵּן לְטוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ כְּדֵי לִטּוֹל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁקָּצַץ לוֹ בְּעַד כָּל יוֹם, מִכָּל מָקוֹם כֵּיוָן שֶׁהוּא עוֹשֶׂה כֵּן מֵחֲמַת דִּבּוּרוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁשְּׂכָרוֹ לְהִתְעַסֵּק אֵיזֶה יָמִים — הֲרֵי זֶה כְּעוֹסֵק בִּשְׁלִיחוּת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, שֶׁאַף שֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לֹא אָמַר לוֹ לְהִתְעַסֵּק בְּשַׁבָּת אֶלָּא שְׂכָרוֹ לְאֵיזֶה יָמִים סְתָם, מִכָּל מָקוֹם הַנָּכְרִי שֶׁמִּתְעַסֵּק בְּשַׁבָּת מִתְעַסֵּק הוּא מֵחֲמַת הַשְּׂכִירוּת שֶׁשְּׂכָרוֹ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְאֵיזֶה יָמִים, וַהֲרֵי בְּדַעְתּוֹ הַיּוֹם שֶׁהוּא שְׂכִירוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל הַיּוֹם וְהוּא עוֹשֶׂה שְׁלִיחוּת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, לְפִיכָךְ אַף שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה בִּשְׁבִיל טוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ — אֵין זֶה מוֹעִיל כְּלוּם, שֶׁלְּעִנְיַן שְׁלִיחוּת אֵין לְחַלֵּק בֵּין עוֹשֶׂה לְטוֹבַת הַמְשַׁלֵּחַ בֵּין עוֹשֶׂה לְטוֹבַת הַשָּׁלִיחַ, בֵּין כָּךְ וּבֵין כָּךְ שְׁלוּחוֹ הוּא.

English translation

And kal vachomer (כל שכן): if the Yid has hired the akum by the day — telling him "for each day you operate the bathhouse or oven or mill, I will pay you such-and-such" — even if he did not explicitly tell him to work on Shabbos, and the akum goes on his own on Shabbos as well — one must protest and not let him operate on Shabbos.

And although the akum aims at his own benefit (to collect the daily wage), nevertheless, since he acts on the basis of the Yid's instruction which hired him for several days — he is like a shaliach of the Yid. For even though the Yid did not tell him "work on Shabbos" but hired him "for several days" in general, the akum who works on Shabbos does so by virtue of the hire which hired him for several days; and he has in mind "today I am the Yid's hired worker and I am doing the Yid's mission." Therefore, even though he acts for his own profit, this is of no avail: regarding shelichus, there is no distinction between "acting for the benefit of the meshalei'ach" and "acting for the benefit of the shaliach" — in both cases, he is his shaliach.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav moves to the case even clearer: no longer the annual "bulk" wage (Seif Gimmel), but the daily wage (sechir yom / sechir l'yamim). Here, the akum is explicitly paid for each day worked — so Shabbos is literally a paid day. The Rav clarifies that even if the Yid did not say "do it on Shabbos," the very structure of the "by-the-day" contract makes the Shabbos work a mission for the Yid.

The halachic question

The argument refuted: one might say "the akum wants the wage — he therefore acts for himself." The Rav answers with a general rule of shelichus:
"לעניין שליחות אין לחלק בין עושה לטובת המשלח בין עושה לטובת השליח"
→ regarding shelichus, intention is irrelevant. As long as the meshalei'ach has given a mandate, the shaliach is his agent — his personal intention changes nothing.

The opinions

Source Position
Talmud Avodah Zarah 21b Distinguishes sechir yom (forbidden) and aris (permitted) — base of the sugya.
Rashi & Tosafos Discussion: the sechir yom is forbidden because people would say "שלוחו של ישראל הוא."
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 6:12) Absolute issur for sechir yom.
Mechaber (OC 243:1 and 244:1) Sechir yom always forbidden.
SHHaRav (243:4) Decisive principle: shelichus is objective, not subjective. The intention of the day worker does not remove him from his status of shaliach.

The pesak of the Rav

The principle in 3 propositions:
  1. Sechir yom = sechir yamim — same problem: paid per day worked
  2. Subjective intention does not erase the halachic objectivity of shelichus
  3. The Yid must therefore protest and prevent the akum from operating on Shabbos

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A non-Jewish freelancer hired by the day to manage a Jewish website: 🔴 Forbidden for him to intervene on Shabbos, even if he says "I bill by the day, I have an interest in working." He is the Yid's shaliach.
  • Case 2 — A non-Jewish real estate agent paid by the day to show properties of a Jewish owner: 🔴 Forbidden for him to do showings on Shabbos — he acts as a shaliach for the Yid.
  • Case 3 — A non-Jewish Uber driver under contract with a Jewish company paid by the day: 🔴 Forbidden for him to drive on Shabbos under this contract — even if he wants to earn his day's pay.
  • Case 4 — A non-Jewish consultant billed at a daily rate: 🔴 Forbidden to work on a Jewish project on Shabbos — the very structure of the contract (by day) makes the Shabbos work shelichus.
  • Case 5 — A non-Jewish babysitter paid by the hour / by the day to watch Jewish children: 🔴 Forbidden on Shabbos — this is the classic case where one cannot rely on "he wants the wage."

Points to remember

  • Sechir yom / l'yamim = same subjective intention, halachic objectivity prevails
  • General rule of shelichus: whose benefit the shaliach acts for does not matter
  • The akum's intention "I am working for my wage" does not save him
  • The Yid must protest (mecha'ah) and prevent the Shabbos work
  • This seif reinforces the principle: it is the structure of the contract that determines, not the psychology
Comprehension question: If shelichus is so objective that it ignores intentions, how then can kablanus (lump-sum contract) be permitted? Isn't this also shelichus by contractual structure?

13. Seif Heh (SHHaRav) — The kenas (קנס): forbidden to benefit from Shabbos earnings

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ה)

אֲפִלּוּ אִם שָׂכַר הַנָּכְרִי לְשָׁנָה, אִם עָבַר וְלֹא מִחָה בְּיָדוֹ וְהִנִּיחוֹ לְהִתְעַסֵּק בְּשַׁבָּת וְהֵבִיא הַנָּכְרִי אֶת הַשָּׂכָר לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁלֹּא הֵבִיא לוֹ שָׂכָר שֶׁקִּבֵּל בְּיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ אֶלָּא הֱבִיאוֹ בְּהַבְלָעָה עִם שְׁאָר הַיָּמִים, כְּגוֹן שֶׁהֵבִיא לוֹ בְּבַת אַחַת שְׂכַר אַרְבָּעָה וַחֲמִשָּׁה יָמִים וְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בֵּינֵיהֶם, שֶׁאֵין בָּזֶה אִסּוּר מִשּׁוּם שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בִּסְעִיף י"א — אַף עַל פִּי כֵן אָסוּר לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לֵהָנוֹת מִן הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁקִּבֵּל הַנָּכְרִי בְּיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁקְּנָסוּהוּ חֲכָמִים.

English translation

Even if the Yid hired the akum for the year (case of Seif Gimmel) — if he transgressed by not protesting and let him operate on Shabbos, and the akum brought the wage to the Yid — even if the Shabbos wage is not separated but was brought "in havla'ah" with the other days (for example: he brings the wage of 4-5 days at once with Shabbos in between) — although in this case there is no issur of "schar Shabbos" as explained in Seif Yod-Alef — nevertheless it is forbidden for the Yid to benefit from the wage received on Shabbos — because Chazal imposed a kenas (קנסוהו חכמים).

What is this Seif about?

Bediavad case: what happens if the Yid transgressed (did not protest, let the akum operate on Shabbos)? The Rav's answer: a special kenas (special rabbinic penalty) — even if technically the schar Shabbos would be permitted through havla'ah, here Chazal refuse any benefit from the Shabbos earnings — it is a punishment for not having protested.

The halachic question

The pedagogical principle of the kenas: Chazal use the instrument of the kenas (halachic fine) to sanction one who has circumvented a rabbinic issur. Without the kenas, the Yid could say to himself: "I let the akum work on Shabbos, then collect through havla'ah — no loss." The kenas nullifies this strategy.

The opinions

Source Position
Talmud Avodah Zarah 22a Lays down the principle of the kenas for one who let his bathhouse operate on Shabbos.
Beis Yosef & Rema Extend the kenas to the case of the sechir shanah who does not protest.
Magen Avraham (OC 243:3) Clarifies the conditions for applying the kenas.
SHHaRav (243:5) Lays down a general rule: the kenas applies even when the issur would have been circumvented by havla'ah — non-protest is an autonomous fault.
Kuntres Acharon (the Rav) Discusses whether the kenas also applies when one transgressed only marit ayin (see Seif Yod-Gimmel).

The pesak of the Rav

The logic of the kenas:
  1. There is an obligation to protest (Seif Gimmel-Daled)
  2. If one neglects this obligation and collects the earnings → kenas miDerabbanan
  3. The kenas forbids all benefit from the Shabbos earnings, even on havla'ah
  4. Pedagogical goal: one cannot calculate "I gain and get away with it through havla'ah"

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — The Yid neglected his duty and his non-Jewish employee operated the bathhouse on Shabbos: the Shabbos receipts are forbidden to benefit from — he must give them to tzedakah or destroy them (according to the poskim).
  • Case 2 — A Jewish e-commerce site where Shabbos sales were not blocked: the Shabbos receipts (revenue collected) must be identified and subtracted from personal profit — give to Torah causes.
  • Case 3 — A Jewish parking lot where Shabbos receipts continued to flow in: the Shabbos receipts are forbidden for personal consumption.
  • Case 4 — A Jewish investor in a fund that trades on Shabbos: the portion of gains made on Shabbos is subject to the kenas if the Yid could have disabled the orders and did not.
  • Case 5 — A Jewish AirBnB host whose platform collects on Shabbos: must configure the platform to suspend Shabbos collections — otherwise, penalized portion.

Points to remember

  • The kenas is a rabbinic tool to prevent circumvention
  • Even if havla'ah would technically save it, the kenas forbids benefit
  • The obligation to protest is structural — its violation triggers the kenas
  • The penalized gain must be excluded from personal patrimony (tzedakah, public uses)
  • The kenas creates accountability — it transforms a passive fault into a concrete sanction
Comprehension question: Why does the Rav clarify that even havla'ah — which suffices in the case of Seif Yod-Alef — does not save the Yid who neglected to protest? What is the pedagogical logic of the kenas?

14. Seif Vav (SHHaRav) — Renting out the bathhouse (השכרה): permitted by din, forbidden by marit ayin

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ו)

אֲבָל אִם רוֹצֶה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל לְהַשְׂכִּיר הַמֶּרְחָץ לַנָּכְרִי שֶׁיַּעֲבוֹד בּוֹ הַנָּכְרִי בְּכָל אֵימַת שֶׁיִּרְצֶה וְלֹא יִהְיֶה מֻכְרָח בַּעֲבוֹדָתוֹ, וְיִטּוֹל כָּל הַשָּׂכָר לְעַצְמוֹ, רַק שֶׁיִּתֵּן לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל דָּבָר קָצוּב בְּכָל שָׁנָה אוֹ בְּכָל חֹדֶשׁ, בֵּין שֶׁיַּסִּיקֶנּוּ אוֹ לֹא יַסִּיקֶנּוּ — מִן הַדִּין הָיָה מֻתָּר, שֶׁאַף שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי יַעֲבוֹד בּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, הֲרֵי אֵינוֹ מִתְכַּוֵּן בַּעֲבוֹדָתוֹ לְטוֹבַת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל אֶלָּא לְטוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ, שֶׁהֲרֵי אַף אִם לֹא יַסִּיקֶנּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת לֹא יְנַכֶּה לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל כְּלוּם מִשְּׂכָרוֹ.

אַף עַל פִּי כֵן אָסְרוּ חֲכָמִים לַעֲשׂוֹת כֵּן, מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, לְפִי שֶׁשֵּׁם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל נִקְרָא עַל הַמֶּרְחָץ, שֶׁהַכֹּל יוֹדְעִים שֶׁמֶּרְחָץ זֶה הוּא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, וּכְשֶׁיִּרְאוּ שֶׁנָּכְרִים רוֹחֲצִים בְּמֶרְחָצוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּשַׁבָּת, וְנָכְרִי מִתְעַסֵּק בַּמֶּרְחָץ — יֹאמְרוּ שֶׁנָּכְרִי זֶה הוּא שְׂכִיר יוֹם אֵצֶל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְיַחְשְׁדוּהוּ שֶׁעוֹבֵר עַל דִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים בְּמַה שֶּׁמַּנִּיחַ אֶת הַנָּכְרִי לְהִתְעַסֵּק בְּשַׁבָּת.

וְאַף שֶׁגַּם הַיִּשְׂרְאֵלִים רָחֲצוּ בְּתוֹכוֹ לִפְעָמִים בְּחֹל וְרָאוּ שֶׁנָּכְרִי זֶה הוּא הַמִּתְעַסֵּק גַּם בְּחֹל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם לֹא יַעֲלֶה עַל דַּעְתָּם שֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל הִשְׂכִּיר הַמֶּרְחָץ לְנָכְרִי זֶה שֶׁיִּטּוֹל כָּל הַשָּׂכָר לְעַצְמוֹ — לְפִי שֶׁאֵין דֶּרֶךְ רֹב הָעוֹלָם לִשְׂכּוֹר הַמֶּרְחָץ, לְפִי שֶׁהוֹצָאוֹתָיו מְרֻבּוֹת וּשְׂכָרוֹ מוּעָט.

English translation

But if the Yid wants to rent out the bathhouse to the akum in temporary full ownership — the akum works there whenever he wants without obligation, collects all the revenue for himself, and pays the Yid only a fixed amount per year or per month (whether he heats it or not) — according to the pure din, this would be permitted. For even if the akum works on Shabbos, he does not aim at the Yid's profit but his own: if he does not heat on Shabbos, he deducts nothing from the Yid.

Nevertheless, Chazal forbade it because of marit ayin: for the Yid's name is attached to the bathhouse — everyone knows this bathhouse belongs to such-and-such a Yid. And when non-Jews are seen bathing there on Shabbos with an akum operating it — people will say "this akum is a daily worker for the Yid," and they will suspect the Yid of transgressing the words of the Sofrim by letting the akum operate on Shabbos.

And even if Yidden sometimes see this akum operating during the week (and should therefore understand he is a tenant), it would not occur to them that the Yid has rented out the bathhouse — since the customary practice is not to rent a bathhouse (its expenses are heavy and its revenue meager — it is therefore managed by an employee).

What is this Seif about?

This seif is the pivot of the siman: it introduces the structural distinction between pure din and the gezeirah of marit ayin. Pure rental (hasakharah) of the bathhouse to the akum should be permitted (the akum acts for himself) — but Chazal forbade it by decree on account of appearance. The ta'am of the decree: the customary practice is sechir yom, so passersby will assume sechir yom.

The halachic question

Two concepts in tension:
  • אדעתיה דנפשיה עביד — the renting akum acts for himself = permitted by din
  • מראית עין — the appearance deceives = forbidden by decree
The Rav explains precisely why: it is not the custom of "real" tradespeople to rent out a bathhouse → so the public does not guess that this is a rental → suspicion of sechir yom.

The opinions

Source Position
Avodah Zarah 21a-b "מרחץ לאו לאריסותא" — the bathhouse is not taken in arisus: a rule of minhag.
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 6:13) Forbidden to rent a bathhouse on account of marit ayin.
Mechaber (OC 243:1) Takes up the rule: bathhouse forbidden, no rental.
Magen Avraham (OC 243:1-2) Clarifies the ta'amim — minhag of the trade as a factor.
SHHaRav (243:6) Two-stage analysis: (1) by pure din it is permitted; (2) forbidden only by decree of marit ayin. This distinction announces the allowances to come (Seifim Tes and Yod).

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's masterful decomposition:
  1. Layer 1 — Pure din: hasakharah of the bathhouse = permitted, since the akum acts for himself (אדעתיה דנפשיה)
  2. Layer 2 — The gezeirah of marit ayin: forbidden by decree, on account of misleading appearance
  3. Layer 3 — Sociological perspective: the decree holds because the minhag of the trade is sechir yom (not rental)
  4. Layer 4 — Implication: if the minhag changes, the decree may fall (Seif Yod)

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — Renting a bakery to a non-Jew for Shabbos only (heter mechirah): 🔴 Problem of marit ayin in a city where the practice is salaried employment — solution: clearly display the rental contract.
  • Case 2 — Renting an entire gas station to a non-Jewish operator for the week: 🔴 Problem if the sign bears a Jewish name — arisus is rare in the trade. Solution: neutral signage + official publication of the contract.
  • Case 3 — A Jewish AirBnB rented to a non-Jew who sublets it nightly on Shabbos: potential problem of marit ayin depending on local minhag; should be discussed with a Rav.
  • Case 4 — A Jewish textile factory with a non-Jewish "tenant" manager: 🔴 Problem if the decree of marit ayin applies (standard industry). Solution: clearly contractualized and publicized structure.
  • Case 5 — A laundromat rented to a non-Jew: in modern urban areas where rental is the customary practice, the decree may fall (see Seif Yod on minhag hamedinah).

Points to remember

  • Pure hasakharah of the bathhouse = permitted by din (the akum acts for himself)
  • Forbidden only by gezeiras marit ayin
  • The decree is relative to the minhag of the trade in the region
  • If the minhag changes → the decree may fall (Seif Yod)
  • The din / decree distinction is essential to understand the rest of the siman

15. Seif Zayin (SHHaRav) — The שדה (field): the minhag of arisus and its heter

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ז)

אֲבָל מֻתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּיר שָׂדֶה לְנָכְרִי שֶׁיַּעֲבוֹד עֲבוֹדָתָהּ כָּל אֵימַת שֶׁיִּרְצֶה וְלֹא יִהְיֶה מֻכְרָח בַּעֲבוֹדָתוֹ וְיִטּוֹל כָּל הַפֵּרוֹת לְעַצְמוֹ, רַק שֶׁיִּתֵּן לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל דָּבָר קָצוּב בְּכָל שָׁנָה, בֵּין שֶׁיִּתֵּן לוֹ מָעוֹת בֵּין שֶׁיִּתֵּן לוֹ מִפֵּרוֹת שָׂדֶה זוֹ, וְהַנָּכְרִי עוֹבֵד אֲפִלּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת.

וְאֵין לָחוּשׁ לְמַרְאִית הָעַיִן, שֶׁיֹּאמְרוּ שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי שְׂכִיר יוֹם הוּא אֵצֶל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, אוֹ שֶׁשְּׂכָרוֹ לַעֲבוֹד אַדְמָתוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, לְפִי שֶׁדֶּרֶךְ רֹב הָעוֹלָם הוּא לִתֵּן הַשָּׂדֶה בַּאֲרִיסוּת לְאָרִיס, דְּהַיְנוּ אָדָם הַמְקַבֵּל הַשָּׂדוֹת לְחָרְשָׁן וּלְזָרְעָן וְלַעֲבוֹד כָּל עֲבוֹדָתָן, וְנוֹטֵל בִּשְׂכַר עֲבוֹדָתוֹ מֶחֱצָה אוֹ שְׁלִישׁ אוֹ רְבִיעַ הַפֵּרוֹת וְהַשְּׁאָר לְבַעַל הַשָּׂדֶה, לָכֵן מִן הַסְּתָם כְּשֶׁיִּרְאוּ נָכְרִי עוֹבֵד בְּשַׁבָּת בְּשָׂדֶה שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל יֹאמְרוּ שֶׁזֶּהוּ אֲרִיסוֹ שֶׁל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְלִתֵּן הַשָּׂדֶה בַּאֲרִיסוּת לְנָכְרִי אֵין בּוֹ שׁוּם אִסּוּר כְּלָל.

אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁיֵּשׁ לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל הֲנָאָה בַּמְּלָאכָה שֶׁהָאָרִיס עוֹשֶׂה בְּשָׂדֵהוּ בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁהֲרֵי רֹב הַפֵּרוֹת שֶׁל הַשָּׂדֶה הֵם שֶׁל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם כֵּיוָן שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי אֵינוֹ מִתְכַּוֵּן בִּמְלַאכְתּוֹ בִּשְׁבִיל טוֹבַת הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, אֶלָּא בִּשְׁבִיל טוֹבַת עַצְמוֹ, שֶׁיִּטּוֹל חֵלֶק הַמַּגִּיעַ לוֹ מִפֵּרוֹת הַשָּׂדֶה, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמֵּאֵלָיו נֶהֱנֶה הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל — אֵין בְּכָךְ כְּלוּם. רַק שֶׁיִּזָּהֵר שֶׁלֹּא יֹאמַר לוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה בְּשַׁבָּת.

English translation

But it is permitted to rent out a field to an akum who will work it whenever he wants, without obligation, and who will take all the produce for himself — paying the Yid only a fixed amount annually (in money or in produce); and the akum works there even on Shabbos.

And there is no concern of marit ayin — that people would say "the akum is the Yid's sechir yom" or "the Yid hired him to work his land on Shabbos" — because the practice of the vast majority is to give the field in arisus to an aris, that is, a man who takes fields to plow, sow, and do all the work, and who receives as wages half, a third, or a quarter of the produce, the rest going to the owner. Therefore spontaneously, when an akum is seen working on Shabbos in a Yid's field, they will say "this is his aris" — and giving a field in arisus to an akum contains no issur whatsoever.

And although the Yid derives benefit from the work the aris does on Shabbos — since most of the field's produce goes to him — nevertheless, since the akum does not aim at the Yid's benefit but his own (his share of the produce), even though the Yid enjoys it incidentally, there is nothing in it. One must only be careful not to tell him to work on Shabbos.

What is this Seif about?

The inverse of Seif Vav: the field (שדה) is the archetype of the permitted. Why? Because the universal minhag of the trade is arisus. So:

  • Hasakharah and arisus of the field are permitted
  • No problem of marit ayin: passersby will assume arisus (the norm)
  • Even if the Yid benefits from the Shabbos work (enriched harvest), this is only a permitted indirect benefit

The halachic question

The paradox resolved: in the case of the bathhouse (Seif Vav), pure hasakharah is forbidden by marit ayin even though the din permits it. In the case of the field, hasakharah (and arisus) are permitted — why? Because the minhag here is consistent with the appearance. The sociology of the minhag is what distinguishes the bathhouse from the field.

The opinions

Source Position
Avodah Zarah 21b-22a "שדה לאריסותא" — the field is given in arisus: recognized practice.
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 6:11) Permitted to give a field in arisus to an akum.
Mechaber (OC 244:1) Takes up the rule of the permitted field.
Tosafos Avodah Zarah 21b Clarifies: the criterion is the minhag of the trade; what is habitually given in arisus is permitted.
SHHaRav (243:7) Clarifies the modes: (1) pure rental (fixed sum) or (2) proportional arisus; both permitted. And adds: even if the Yid benefits, the akum's own intention suffices. However, one must avoid explicit instruction ("שלא יאמר לו שיעשה בשבת").

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's 4 propositions on the field:
  1. Hasakharah (pure rental) to the akum = permitted, even if he works on Shabbos
  2. Arisus (sharecropping) to the akum = permitted (same mode)
  3. Incidental benefit for the Yid = no problem (the intention remains the akum's)
  4. Limit: never say "work on Shabbos" — amira remains forbidden

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A Jewish owner in Eretz Yisroel who rents his orchard to a non-Jewish fellah for harvesting: 🟢 Permitted under the contract (rental or arisus). The Yid does not tell him when to work.
  • Case 2 — A Jewish vineyard managed by a non-Jew on a harvest-sharing basis: 🟢 Permitted, even if the akum works on Shabbos (worldwide viticulture minhag is partnership).
  • Case 3 — A Jewish olive grove in Italy, non-Jewish management for 30% of the oil: 🟢 Permitted — classic arisus, universally accepted.
  • Case 4 — A Jewish coffee plantation with permanent workers: 🔴 If sechir yom = problem. 🟢 If arisus / tenancy = permitted.
  • Case 5 — Jewish agricultural land rented for market gardening: 🟢 Permitted in pure rental — passersby understand the agricultural structure.

Points to remember

  • The field (שדה) is the archetype of the permitted, as opposed to the bathhouse
  • Reason: the universal minhag is arisus
  • Both modes — hasakharah and arisus — are equally permitted
  • The Yid's indirect benefit does not invalidate the heter
  • Sole absolute limit: do not say "work on Shabbos"
  • This seif grounds the extension to the mill (Seif Tes) and the analysis of minhag (Seif Yod)

16. Seif Ches (SHHaRav) — שכיר שנה for a field: marit ayin and chutz l'techum

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ח)

אֲבָל מִי שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ נָכְרִי שָׂכִיר לְשָׁנָה שֶׁיַּעֲשֶׂה כָּל הַמְּלָאכוֹת הַצְּרִיכוֹת לְשָׂדֵהוּ בְּאוֹתָהּ שָׁנָה, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמִּן הַדִּין הָיָה מֻתָּר לְהַנִּיחוֹ לַעֲבוֹד שָׂדֵהוּ בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁהֲרֵי הַנָּכְרִי לַהֲנָאַת עַצְמוֹ הוּא מִתְכַּוֵּן, כְּדֵי שֶׁיִּטּוֹל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁקָּצַץ לוֹ, שֶׁאַף שֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל יְשַׁלֵּם לוֹ אַף אִם לֹא יַעֲשֶׂה אוֹתָהּ מְלָאכָה בְּשַׁבָּת, מִכָּל מָקוֹם הֲרֵי יִצְטָרֵךְ לַעֲשׂוֹתָהּ בְּיוֹם אַחֵר, שֶׁהֲרֵי שְׂכָרוֹ לְכָל הַמְּלָאכוֹת הַצְּרִיכוֹת לְשָׂדֵהוּ כָּל הַשָּׁנָה, לְכָךְ הוּא עוֹשֶׂה אוֹתָהּ בְּשַׁבָּת, כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יִצְטָרֵךְ לַעֲשׂוֹתָהּ בְּיוֹם אַחֵר (וְגַם אֵינוֹ נִרְאֶה כִּשְׁלוּחוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל כְּמוֹ שְׂכִיר יוֹם, מִטַּעַם שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ד), מִכָּל מָקוֹם מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן צָרִיךְ הוּא לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדוֹ, שֶׁהָרוֹאֶה אֶת הַנָּכְרִי עוֹבֵד בְּשַׁבָּת בְּשָׂדֶה שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל לֹא יַעֲלֶה עַל דַּעְתּוֹ שֶׁהוּא שְׂכִיר שָׁנָה, אֶלָּא יִדְמֶה לוֹ שֶׁהוּא שְׂכִיר יוֹם.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אִם הַשָּׂדֶה הִיא חוּץ לַתְּחוּם — אֵין לֶאֱסוֹר מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ד, עַיֵּן שָׁם.

English translation

But one who has a non-Jew hired yearly to do all the work needed for his field — although by din it would be permitted to let him work on Shabbos (the akum aiming at his own benefit: finish the work today so he won't have to redo it tomorrow — and moreover, he is not seen as a shaliach of the Yid as a sechir yom is, for a reason explained at siman רמ"ד) — nevertheless, because of marit ayin, one must protest. Because one who sees the akum working on Shabbos in a Jewish field will not think he is a sechir shanah — he will believe he is a sechir yom.

And it was not said that for the rental of a field there is no marit ayin (since the minhag is arisus) — except when this is really the case: the akum who works actually has a right to the produce like an aris — and even more than an aris, since the field is rented to him and all the produce belongs to him.

And nevertheless, if the field is chutz l'techum (outside the Shabbos zone) — there is no issur for marit ayin, as will be explained at siman רמ"ד; see there.

What is this Seif about?

A counterintuitive case: even for a field (which, as seen in Seif Zayin, is the archetype of the permitted), if the akum is sechir shanah (yearly worker on a fixed wage, not an aris) — it is forbidden to let him work on Shabbos. Why? Because passersby will not see the difference between sechir shanah and sechir yom — so marit ayin takes over.

The Rav's criterion:
  • Renter/aris of the field: visible by direct benefit (produce) → no marit ayin
  • Annual employee: invisible to passersby (who confuse him with a sechir yom) → marit ayin
Exception: chutz l'techum — if the field is outside the Shabbos zone, no one sees → no marit ayin → permitted.

The halachic question

The Rav distinguishes with precision the halachic reality (yes, this is sechir shanah, so permitted by din) and public perception (which only sees "akum at work on Shabbos in a Jewish field"). The sociology of perception drives the halachic decision.

17. Seif Tes (SHHaRav) — Mill (רחיים) like שדה; oven (תנור) like the bathhouse

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif ט)

הָרֵחַיִם דִּינָם כְּשָׂדֶה, שֶׁמֻּתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָם לְנָכְרִי לְשָׁנָה אוֹ לְחֹדֶשׁ שֶׁיַּעֲבוֹד וְיִתְעַסֵּק בָּהֶם כָּל אֵימַת שֶׁיִּרְצֶה וְיִטּוֹל כָּל הַשָּׂכָר לְעַצְמוֹ, רַק שֶׁיִּתֵּן לְהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל דָּבָר קָצוּב, וְהַנָּכְרִי עוֹבֵד אֲפִלּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת. וְאֵין לָחוּשׁ לְמַרְאִית הָעַיִן, לְפִי שֶׁגַּם הָרֵחַיִם דֶּרֶךְ רֹב הָעוֹלָם לִתֵּן בַּאֲרִיסוּת כְּמוֹ שָׂדוֹת.

אֲבָל הַתַּנּוּר דִּינוֹ כְּמֶרְחָץ, שֶׁאָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירוֹ לְנָכְרִי יוֹתֵר מִלְּשֵׁשֶׁת יְמֵי הַחֹל, לְפִי שֶׁכְּשֶׁיַּעֲסוֹק בּוֹ הַנָּכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת — יֵשׁ בּוֹ מִשּׁוּם מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, שֶׁיֹּאמְרוּ שֶׁשְּׂכִיר יוֹם הוּא, שֶׁכֵּן הוּא דֶּרֶךְ הַתַּנּוּר לַעֲסוֹק בּוֹ עַל יְדֵי שְׂכִירֵי יוֹם וְלֹא לְהַשְׂכִּירוֹ וְלֹא לִתְּנוֹ בַּאֲרִיסוּת, לְפִי שֶׁיְּצִיאוֹתָיו מְרֻבּוֹת וּשְׂכָרוֹ מוּעָט כְּמוֹ מֶרְחָץ.

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, הַכֹּל לְפִי מִנְהַג הַמְּדִינָה, שֶׁבִּמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁדֶּרֶךְ רֹב אַנְשֵׁי הָעִיר לְהַשְׂכִּיר הַתַּנּוּר אוֹ הַמֶּרְחָץ אוֹ לִתְּנָם בַּאֲרִיסוּת וְלֹא לַעֲסוֹק בָּהֶם עַל יְדֵי שְׂכִירֵי יוֹם — מֻתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָם שָׁם לְנָכְרִי אוֹ לִתְּנָם לוֹ בַּאֲרִיסוּת. וּבִמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁאֵין דֶּרֶךְ רֹב אַנְשֵׁי הָעִיר לְהַשְׂכִּיר רֵחַיִם וְלֹא לִתְּנָם בַּאֲרִיסוּת אֶלָּא עוֹסְקִים בָּהֶם עַל יְדֵי שְׂכִירֵי יוֹם — אָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּירָם שָׁם לְנָכְרִי אוֹ לִתְּנָם לוֹ בַּאֲרִיסוּת.

English translation

The mill (רחיים): its din is like the field — it is permitted to rent it to an akum for the year or the month (the akum works in it whenever he wants and keeps all the revenue, paying the Yid only a fixed amount), and the akum works even on Shabbos. No concern of marit ayin, since the mill is also universally given in arisus like fields.

But the oven (תנור): its din is like the bathhouse — forbidden to rent it for more than the six weekdays. Because when the akum works on Shabbos, there is marit ayin: people will say "this is a sechir yom" — because the minhag of the oven is to be operated by sechirei yom and not to be rented out or given in arisus (since its expenses are heavy and its revenue meager, as with the bathhouse).

And nevertheless, all is according to minhag hamedinah (the local custom): in places where the minhag is to rent out the oven or bathhouse (and not to hire sechirei yom) — it is permitted to rent them to an akum or to give them to him in arisus. And in places where the minhag is not to rent out the mill but to hire sechirei yom — it is forbidden to rent / give in arisus.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav extends the logic of Seif Zayin (field permitted) and Seif Vav (bathhouse forbidden) to two new types of property:

Extension by analogy:
  • רחיים (mill) = like שדה (field) — permitted, since the minhag is arisus
  • תנור (oven) = like מרחץ (bathhouse) — forbidden, since the minhag is sechirei yom
But the decisive criterion remains the local minhag (מנהג המדינה) — which can reverse the rule.

The halachic question

The Rav introduces here explicitly the concept of minhag hamedinah as a halachic variable: the law of the bathhouse and the mill changes with the local minhag. This is not a caprice — it is the very nature of the marit ayin decree which depends on public perception, which itself depends on common practice.

18. Seif Yod (SHHaRav) — The local precedent of an individual

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif י)

וְאַף בִּמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁאֵין דֶּרֶךְ רֹב אַנְשֵׁי הָעִיר לְהַשְׂכִּיר מֶרְחָץ אוֹ תַּנּוּר אוֹ רֵחַיִם אוֹ לִתְּנָם בַּאֲרִיסוּת, מִכָּל מָקוֹם אִם יֵשׁ שָׁם אָדָם שֶׁהִשְׂכִּירָם אוֹ נְתָנָם בַּאֲרִיסוּת שָׁנָה אַחַר שָׁנָה עַד שֶׁנִּתְפַּרְסֵם לָרַבִּים שֶׁאֵין דַּרְכּוֹ לִשְׂכּוֹר פּוֹעֲלִים אֶלָּא לְהַשְׂכִּירָם אוֹ לִתְּנָם בַּאֲרִיסוּת — מֻתָּר לוֹ לְהַשְׂכִּירָם לְנָכְרִי אוֹ לִתְּנָם לוֹ בַּאֲרִיסוּת.

English translation

And even in places where the majority minhag is not to rent the bathhouse, oven or mill (or give them in arisus) — nevertheless, if there is someone who has rented or given them in arisus year after year, until it becomes public that his personal practice is not to hire workers but rather to rent or give in arisus — it is permitted to him to rent or give them in arisus to an akum.

What is this Seif about?

An important nuance to the previous seif: if the general minhag is unfavorable (sechir yom), but an individual particular person has established his own precedent by systematically renting, then the marit ayin falls for him — the public knows that by him, it is in rental.

The principle: marit ayin is relative to the perception of the concerned public. If the practice of this individual is known and public, the public will not be mistaken.

The halachic question

The publicity of practice (פרסום) is a halachic category. At what point does individual behavior become publicly known to the point of neutralizing marit ayin? The Rav answers: "שנה אחר שנה" — annual repetition, until public notoriety.

The opinions

Source Position
Mechaber (OC 243:2) Takes up the principle: 2 or 3 times → publicity.
Magen Avraham (OC 243:3) Discusses the repetition threshold.
Mishnah Berurah (243:11) Clarifies the conditions of notoriety.
SHHaRav (243:10) Emphasizes publicity: "עד שנתפרסם לרבים" — not just 2 or 3 times, but up to attested notoriety.

The pesak of the Rav

The algorithm of individual precedent:
  1. The general minhag is sechir yom (so hasakharah forbidden)
  2. The individual practices hasakharah year after year
  3. The practice becomes publicly known
  4. From notoriety onwards → marit ayin falls for him → hasakharah permitted

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A Yid who rents his bathhouse to a non-Jew for 5 consecutive years with public announcement: 🟢 Permitted from acquired notoriety onwards — the neighbors know it is in rental.
  • Case 2 — A Yid who announces on social networks that his business is under autonomous non-Jewish management: 🟢 If the announcement is widely known, marit ayin falls.
  • Case 3 — A Jewish fast-food chain franchised to a non-Jewish operator: 🟢 If the franchise is officially declared and public, it is permitted (the public knows it is franchised).
  • Case 4 — A Yid who suddenly changes model (employees → rental): 🔴 At first, marit ayin remains. 🟢 After a few years of public practice, the model becomes legitimate.
  • Case 5 — A Jewish website subcontracted to a non-Jewish operator with public mention: public transparency allows application of the Seif Yod rule.

Points to remember

  • Repeated individual precedent can neutralize marit ayin
  • Criterion: public notoriety (פרסום) — not just 1-2 occurrences
  • Explicit announcement (sign, plaque, advertisements) accelerates notoriety
  • Modernity (web, social media) facilitates publicity
  • Flexibility of the halachic system: sociological reality is integrated

19. Seif Yod-Alef (SHHaRav) — שכר שבת: the rule of havla'ah

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif יא)

אַף בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁמֻּתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּיר לְנָכְרִי מֶרְחָץ וְרֵחַיִם וְתַנּוּר לְיוֹתֵר מִלְּשֵׁשֶׁת יְמֵי הַחֹל — לֹא הִתִּירוּ לוֹ לִטּוֹל הַשָּׂכָר הַמַּגִּיעַ לוֹ בְּעַד יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁהִשְׂכִּיר לוֹ אֶת יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בְּהַבְלָעָה עִם שְׁאָר הַיָּמִים, כְּגוֹן שֶׁהִשְׂכִּיר לוֹ לְשָׁנָה אוֹ לְחֹדֶשׁ, שֶׁאָמַר לוֹ בְּעַד כָּל שָׁבוּעַ תִּתֶּן לִי כָּךְ וְכָךְ, אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ בְּעַד כָּל ג' יָמִים אוֹ כָּל ב' יָמִים תִּתֶּן לִי כָּךְ וְכָךְ, שֶׁנִּמְצָא שֶׁלֹּא הִשְׂכִּיר לוֹ לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ אֶלָּא בְּהַבְלָעָה עִם יָמִים אֲחֵרִים, וְאַחַר כָּךְ כְּשֶׁהַנָּכְרִי מְשַׁלֵּם לוֹ הַשָּׂכָר מְשַׁלֵּם בְּעַד כָּל שָׁבוּעַ כָּךְ וְכָךְ, אוֹ בְּעַד כָּל ג' יָמִים אוֹ בְּעַד כָּל ב' יָמִים כָּךְ וְכָךְ, נִמְצָא שֶׁאֵין מְשַׁלֵּם לוֹ שָׂכָר שֶׁל יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ אֶלָּא בְּהַבְלָעָה עִם יוֹם אַחֵר.

English translation

Even in places where it is permitted to rent to an akum a bathhouse, mill, or oven for more than the six weekdays — they did not permit taking the wage allotted to the day of Shabbos, except where one rented the day of Shabbos in havla'ah (absorption) with the other days. For example: he rented it out for the year or the month saying "for each week you will pay me such-and-such" — or even "for each 3-day or 2-day span you will pay me such-and-such" — so that he did not rent the day of Shabbos in isolation, but in absorption with other days. And later, when the akum pays the wage — he pays "for each week such-and-such" or "for each 2 or 3-day span such-and-such" — so that he does not pay the wage of the day of Shabbos in isolation, but in absorption.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav introduces here a central halachic principle: schar Shabbos (the wage of Shabbos). Receiving payment specifically for Shabbos is forbidden by rabbinic decree (גזירה משום מקח וממכר). The solution: havla'ah = absorption. If the Shabbos wage is melted into a larger period (week, 2 days, 3 days), it is permitted.

The mechanism of havla'ah:
  • Contract for the week (X per week) → permitted
  • Contract by spans of 2-3 days (X per span) → permitted
  • Contract day by day (X per day, Shabbos isolated) → 🔴 forbidden (Seif Yod-Beis)

The halachic question

The decree of schar Shabbos bears on pricing, not on the material possibility of including Shabbos. As long as Shabbos is hidden within a period, one is not in the case of the decree. Havla'ah is not a trick — it is a recognized halachic category.

The opinions

Source Position
Talmud Bava Metzia 58a "שכר שבת — שאין משתכרין בשבת, גזירה משום מקח וממכר".
Rambam (Hilchos Shabbos 6:25) Codifies havla'ah as the solution.
Mechaber (OC 306:4) Takes up the rule of havla'ah.
Mishnah Berurah (243:23) Clarifies the valid modes of havla'ah.
SHHaRav (243:11) Details the valid pricings: by week, by 2-3 day spans. Rigorously distinguishes the structure of the contract.

The pesak of the Rav

The conditions of havla'ah:
  1. The contractual period must encompass Shabbos with other days
  2. The pricing must be by period, not by day
  3. The actual payment must reflect the pricing (by period)
  4. Havla'ah by the week is ideal; by 2-3 day spans, still valid

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A Jewish hotel rented to a non-Jewish operator: monthly or weekly pricing → 🟢 valid havla'ah. If daily pricing with a line for Shabbos → 🔴 forbidden.
  • Case 2 — A Jewish AirBnB managed by a non-Jew: contract by the week or month → 🟢 permitted. Day-by-day contract with Shabbos isolated → 🔴 problematic.
  • Case 3 — A Jewish parking lot managed by a non-Jew: monthly or weekly subscription → 🟢 havla'ah. Hourly/daily rate → case-by-case analysis.
  • Case 4 — A Jewish-owned car rented to a non-Jewish company: monthly rental → 🟢. By the day including Shabbos → 🔴 unless structured havla'ah.
  • Case 5 — A Jewish venue rented for events (wedding, seminar): weekend package including Shabbos → 🟢 valid havla'ah (Friday + Saturday + Sunday in one price).

Points to remember

  • Schar Shabbos is forbidden by rabbinic decree (gezeiras mekach umemkar)
  • Havla'ah (absorption in a period) is the canonical solution
  • Valid modes: by week, by 2-3 day spans
  • The structure of the contract must reflect havla'ah (pricing, payment)
  • Wide application in modern contracts (rentals, subscriptions)
Comprehension question: Why is havla'ah not considered a halachic trick? Substantively, the Yid does receive a wage for Shabbos — why is it permitted?

20. Seif Yod-Beis (SHHaRav) — Daily wage paid separately: forbidden even retroactively

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif יב)

אֲבָל אִם הִשְׂכִּיר לוֹ לְיָמִים נִפְרָדִים, שֶׁאָמַר לוֹ אֲנִי מַשְׂכִּיר לְךָ לְשָׁנָה אוֹ לְחֹדֶשׁ וּבְעַד כָּל יוֹם וְיוֹם תִּתֶּן לִי כָּךְ וְכָךְ, נִמְצָא שֶׁיּוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת מֻשְׂכָּר לְהַנָּכְרִי בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ, שֶׁהֲרֵי כָּל יוֹם וְיוֹם הוּא שְׂכִירוּת בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ (וְיוּכַל לַחֲזוֹר בּוֹ בְּאֶמְצַע שָׁנָה אוֹ חֹדֶשׁ), לְפִיכָךְ אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאַחַר כָּךְ מְשַׁלֵּם לוֹ הַנָּכְרִי בְּעַד כָּל הַיָּמִים בְּבַת אַחַת — אָסוּר לוֹ לִטּוֹל שָׂכָר הַמַּגִּיעַ לִימֵי הַשַּׁבָּתוֹת, שֶׁהֲרֵי הַנָּכְרִי מְחַשֵּׁב עִמּוֹ סְכוּם יָמִים נִפְרָדִים, וּמְשַׁלֵּם לוֹ בְּעַד כָּל יוֹם וְיוֹם, נִמְצָא שֶׁמְּשַׁלֵּם בְּעַד יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה, וְאָסוּר לִטּוֹל שְׂכַר שַׁבָּת שֶׁלֹּא בְהַבְלָעָה, שֶׁאֵין מִשְׂתַּכְּרִים בְּשַׁבָּת, מִשּׁוּם גְּזֵרַת מִקָּח וּמִמְכָּר.

English translation

But if he rented for separate days — saying "I rent to you for the year or month, but for each day you will pay me such-and-such" — then the day of Shabbos is rented in isolation, since each day is a rental in itself (and the akum could withdraw in mid-period). Consequently, even if the payment comes in bulk at the end for all days together, it is forbidden for the Yid to take the wage of the Shabbos days — because the akum calculates with him day by day and pays him for each day, so he pays for Shabbos in isolation, without havla'ah. And it is forbidden to take schar Shabbos without havla'ah, because one does not earn on Shabbos — decree of mekach umemkar.

What is this Seif about?

The Rav completes Seif Yod-Alef by laying out the opposite case: pricing day by day, even if paid in bulk, is forbidden. The criterion is not the timing of payment but the pricing structure. If the contract identifies a price per day, Shabbos is priced in isolation — so schar Shabbos forbidden.

The halachic question

The decisive criterion: the structure of the contract, not the mode of payment.
  • Price by period (week, 2-3 days) → havla'ah ✅
  • Price by day + bulk payment → not havla'ah 🔴
The proof: the akum "could withdraw mid-period" — so each day is an autonomous contractual unit.

The opinions

Source Position
Tosafos Bava Metzia 58a Clarifies the pricing structure as the criterion.
Magen Avraham (OC 306:7) Insists on the distinction between "structure of the contract" and "mode of payment."
Mishnah Berurah (243:26) Takes up the rule and illustrates it.
SHHaRav (243:12) Brings the criterion of "possible withdrawal mid-period": if the akum can terminate mid-period, each day is an autonomous rental — so havla'ah invalid.

The pesak of the Rav

Test to verify havla'ah:
  1. How is the contract priced? By day or by period?
  2. Could the akum withdraw mid-period without proportional penalty?
  3. If yes to either → each day is autonomous → havla'ah invalid
  4. If genuine contractual period with exit penalty → havla'ah valid

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A Jewish AirBnB with rate "$60/night, total $420 for 7 days": 🔴 Daily pricing identified → no valid havla'ah for Shabbos.
  • Case 2 — A Jewish AirBnB with "weekly package $420" (no daily breakdown): 🟢 Valid havla'ah.
  • Case 3 — A Jewish driver with a non-Jewish company — "daily rate $200, total $1400": 🔴 Daily structure → forbidden for Shabbos.
  • Case 4 — A Jewish parking with hourly rate but monthly flat subscription: 🟢 If the subscription is flat (not calculated in hours), havla'ah valid.
  • Case 5 — A Jewish freelancing platform paid per mission (not per day): 🟢 If the mission naturally encompasses Shabbos, havla'ah valid.

Points to remember

  • The criterion is the pricing structure, not the timing of payment
  • Day-by-day pricing = each day autonomous = no valid havla'ah
  • Test: can the akum withdraw mid-period without penalty?
  • Schar Shabbos outside havla'ah is forbidden by gezeiras mekach umemkar
  • Major implications for modern contracts (by day, by hour)
Comprehension question: Why does the Rav clarify "ויוכל לחזור בו באמצע שנה או חודש"? What is the link between the possibility of withdrawal and the validity of havla'ah?

21. Seif Yod-Gimmel (SHHaRav) — Bediavad: no kenas if the issur was only marit ayin

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif יג)

בִּמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁאָסוּר לְהַשְׂכִּיר לְנָכְרִי מֶרְחָץ אוֹ תַּנּוּר וְרֵחַיִם מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, אִם עָבַר וְהִשְׂכִּיר — מֻתָּר לוֹ לְקַבֵּל כָּל הַשָּׂכָר מֵהַנָּכְרִי, דְּכֵיוָן שֶׁלֹּא עָשָׂה אִסּוּר מֵעִקַּר הַדִּין אֶלָּא מִפְּנֵי מַרְאִית הָעַיִן — אֵין קוֹנְסִין אוֹתוֹ.

English translation

In places where it is forbidden to rent out a bathhouse, oven or mill to an akum on account of marit ayin, if the Yid transgressed and nevertheless rented out — he is permitted to receive the entire wage from the akum. Because, since he did not commit an issur grounded in pure din but only on account of marit ayin — he is not penalized.

What is this Seif about?

A fine halachic distinction: the kenas (penalty) of Seif Heh does not apply here. Why? Because the issur was only marit ayin, not a pure-din issur. The Rav distinguishes the two types of issurim:

Hierarchy of issurim:
  • Issur me-ikkar hadin (Seif Heh: sechir shanah without mecha'ah) → kenas
  • Issur mipnei marit ayin alone (this seif) → no kenas

The halachic question

Why this distinction? The kenas is an educational penalty. When the issur from the din is serious, one punishes to prevent repetition. But when the issur is merely precautionary (marit ayin), the bediavad is tolerable — the Yid has not actually benefited from illegitimate Shabbos work.

The opinions

Source Position
Beis Yosef (OC 243) Discusses the nuance between the two types of issurim.
Magen Avraham (OC 243:4) Clarifies the conditions of bediavad.
Taz Discusses whether the kenas also applies for marit ayin.
SHHaRav (243:13) Rules clearly: no kenas if only marit ayin. The Kuntres Acharon discusses the nuance with the Beis Yosef.

The pesak of the Rav

The Rav's distinction:
  1. If the transgression is on the pure din → kenas miDerabbanan
  2. If the transgression is on the marit ayin alone → no kenas
  3. Logic: precaution does not have the same weight as substantive issur
  4. Practical implication: if one transgressed marit ayin, one can keep the earnings

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A Yid rented his hammam to a non-Jew (forbidden by marit ayin) — bediavad: 🟢 He can keep the entire rental.
  • Case 2 — A Yid rented his bakery without public procedure (forbidden by marit ayin): 🟢 No kenas on the receipts.
  • Case 3 — A Yid neglected to protest against his employee (din issur): 🔴 Kenas — no benefit from the Shabbos receipts.
  • Case 4 — The Yid realizes after the fact that he violated the marit ayin of Seif Tes: 🟢 Permitted to keep the earnings; but must correct for the future.
  • Case 5 — Jewish e-commerce site with marit ayin problem (appearance of a non-Jewish employee): bediavad, earnings retainable; for the future, restructure.

Points to remember

  • Hierarchy: pure din > marit ayin in terms of severity
  • The kenas only applies to pure-din transgressions
  • Bediavad on marit ayin: no penalty on the earnings
  • An equilibrated distinction: one does not oppress by excessive zeal
  • Importance of correcting for the future (lechatchilah remains forbidden)
Comprehension question: Why is the issur of marit ayin "lesser" than the pure-din issur? Isn't it also a devar Chachamim?

22. Seif Yod-Daled (SHHaRav) — Not to rent erev Shabbos

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif יד)

אַף בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁמֻּתָּר לְהַשְׂכִּיר לְנָכְרִי מֶרְחָץ תַּנּוּר וְרֵחַיִם — לֹא יַשְׂכִּירֵם לוֹ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת אֶלָּא קֹדֶם לְכֵן, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִמָּן רמ"ו, עַיֵּן שָׁם הַטַּעַם.

English translation

Even in places where it is permitted to rent a bathhouse, oven or mill to an akum — one should not rent them to him on erev Shabbos but earlier, as will be explained at siman רמ"ו — see there for the ta'am.

What is this Seif about?

A short but important seif: even when the rental is permitted, one should not conclude it erev Shabbos. The ta'am is referred to siman רמ"ו (on the rental of objects): amira on erev Shabbos for Shabbos — giving the instruction "do it on Shabbos" in mid-erev Shabbos resembles too much a direct amira l'akum.

The timing of the contract:
  • Before erev Shabbos (Thursday, Monday, etc.) → 🟢 permitted
  • Erev Shabbos (Friday) → 🔴 forbidden
  • Shabbos itself → 🔴 a fortiori forbidden

The halachic question

Why is erev Shabbos a sensitive moment? Because any action done just before Shabbos is perceived as an immediate preparation for Shabbos work — so as a disguised amira. The Rav's decree is preventive.

The opinions

Source Position
Talmud Shabbos 19a Discussion on erev Shabbos contracts.
Mechaber (OC 246:1) Codifies the rule for objects and property.
Rema (OC 246:1) Clarifies the nuances.
SHHaRav (243:14) Synthetic reminder: the רמ"ו rule applies also to the property of our siman. Systemic coherence.

The pesak of the Rav

General rule:
  1. Any rental contract to an akum must be concluded several days before Shabbos
  2. Concluding erev Shabbos = problem of disguised amira
  3. Systemic cross-reference to siman רמ"ו for the complete doctrine
  4. Application to all types of property: bathhouse, oven, mill, field, etc.

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — Wanting to rent a property to a non-Jew on a Friday afternoon: 🔴 Push to Sunday or earlier in the week.
  • Case 2 — Renewing a rental contract with a non-Jew: 🟢 Do it early in the week, not just before Shabbos.
  • Case 3 — A Jewish AirBnB with Friday-night check-in: contract signed in advance — 🟢. Contract concluded on-site Friday PM → 🔴.
  • Case 4 — A commercial lease signed on a Friday: 🔴 Push to Sunday or another day.
  • Case 5 — An operating license to a non-Jewish operator: 🟢 Conclude several days before Shabbos to avoid the problem.

Points to remember

  • No rental to an akum on erev Shabbos
  • Logic: disguised amira — resembles too much a direct instruction
  • Conclude contracts early in the week
  • Systemic rule: see also siman רמ"ו for the ta'am
  • Wide application: bathhouses, ovens, AirBnBs, commercial leases
Comprehension question: How does this rule apply to an automatic contract (tacit renewal) that triggers on a Friday evening? Must one anticipate the cancellation?

23. Seif Tes-Vav (SHHaRav) — The bathhouse not yet identified with the Jew

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif טו)

אַף בִּמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁאָסְרוּ לְהַשְׂכִּיר מֶרְחָץ תַּנּוּר וְרֵחַיִם לְנָכְרִי — לֹא אָסְרוּ אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁנִּקְרָא שֵׁם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל עֲלֵיהֶם, שֶׁיָּדוּעַ וּמְפֻרְסָם לָרַבִּים שֶׁהֵם שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, שֶׁרָאוּ אוֹתוֹ מִתְעַסֵּק בָּהֶם כַּמָּה פְעָמִים בִּימוֹת הַחֹל. אֲבָל יִשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁקָּנָה מִנָּכְרִי מֶרְחָץ תַּנּוּר וְרֵחַיִם וְלֹא הִתְעַסֵּק בָּהֶם כְּלָל, אֶלָּא חָזַר וְהִשְׂכִּירוֹ מִיָּד לְנָכְרִי אַחֵר — הֲרֵי זֶה מֻתָּר, לְפִי שֶׁעֲדַיִן לֹא נִקְרָא עֲלֵיהֶם שֵׁם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, וְאֵין כַּאן חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית הָעַיִן.

English translation

Even in places where one has forbidden renting a bathhouse, oven or mill to an akum — one has only forbidden when the Yid's name is attached to them, that is, known and public that these properties belong to him (because he was seen operating them several times during the week).

But a Yid who purchased from a non-Jew a bathhouse, oven or mill and did not work in them at all, but immediately re-rented to another non-Jew — this is permitted, because the Yid's name is not yet attached and there is no concern of marit ayin.

What is this Seif about?

A magnificent exception to the bathhouse issur: if the Yid was never identified as the owner of the bathhouse (he bought it and immediately re-rented), there is no marit ayin — the public does not even know that this is "his" bathhouse.

The Rav's criterion: nikra shemo (public identification)
  • If the Yid never operated the property personally → no "attached name"
  • If the Yid already operated during the week → "attached name" → forbidden
  • Acquisition + immediate re-rental = no public identification

The halachic question

The Rav refines the concept of marit ayin: it is not enough that the property is juridically the property of a Yid — it must be perceived as such by the public. Social perception precedes the halachic issur.

The opinions

Source Position
Avodah Zarah 21b Discussion of the bathhouse "called by the Yid's name."
Tosafos & Ramban Define the criterion of "publicity of the name."
Beis Yosef & Rema (OC 243:2) Extend the exception to the case of purchase-immediate rental.
SHHaRav (243:15) Synthesizes and clarifies: the criterion is public identification through personal operation. Without operation → no identification → no issur.

The pesak of the Rav

The rule of the attached name:
  1. The Yid operated the property several times during the week → "attached name" → forbidden to re-rent
  2. The Yid operated only a few times → case-by-case analysis
  3. The Yid never operated personally → no identification → permitted
  4. Purchase + immediate re-rental to another non-Jew → 🟢 permitted

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A Yid buys back a bakery from a non-Jew and immediately re-rents it to another non-Jew: 🟢 Permitted, his name is not attached.
  • Case 2 — A Jewish investor buys commercial assets and rents them to non-Jewish operators (private equity): 🟢 Permitted if no direct operation.
  • Case 3 — A Yid inherits a bathhouse and re-rents it immediately: Case-by-case analysis: family transmission can be public.
  • Case 4 — A Yid buys a hotel chain and franchises it immediately: 🟢 Permitted — the franchisee is the public operator.
  • Case 5 — A Yid buys a website and immediately sells it to a non-Jewish operator: 🟢 No public identification with the Yid → no marit ayin.

Points to remember

  • The criterion is not juridical ownership but public perception
  • "Attached name" = visible and repeated personal operation
  • Acquisition + immediate re-rental = no attachment of the name → permitted
  • Important for modern investment structures (PE, REIT, etc.)
  • Social perception precedes pure halachic analysis

24. Seif Tes-Zayin (SHHaRav) — Private bathhouse in a courtyard inhabited by non-Jews

Full text of the SHHaRav (Seif טז)

וְכֵן אִם יֵשׁ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בֵּית דִּירָה בְּחָצֵר אַחֶרֶת שֶׁהוּא אֵינוֹ דָר בְּתוֹכָהּ, וְשָׁם יֵשׁ מֶרְחָץ בְּאוֹתָהּ הַדִּירָה, אִם כָּל יְמוֹת הַחֹל אֵין דֶּרֶךְ שׁוּם יִשְׂרָאֵל לִרְחוֹץ בְּאוֹתָהּ מֶרְחָץ רַק שְׁכֵנֵי הַדִּירָה — מֻתָּר לוֹ לְהַשְׂכִּירוֹ לְנָכְרִי אוֹ לִתְּנוֹ לוֹ בַּאֲרִיסוּת.

וְאֵין כַּאן חֲשַׁשׁ מַרְאִית הָעַיִן, לְפִי שֶׁכָּל מִי שֶׁאֵינוֹ רוֹחֵץ בְּאוֹתוֹ מֶרְחָץ כְּלָל בִּימוֹת הַחֹל אֵינוֹ יוֹדֵעַ שֶׁמֶּרְחָץ זֶה הוּא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, שֶׁכֵּיוָן שֶׁהוּא עוֹמֵד בְּחָצֵר בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ וְאֵין שׁוּם יִשְׂרָאֵל דָּר בְּאוֹתָהּ חָצֵר, וּשְׁכֵנֵי הַמֶּרְחָץ שֶׁרוֹחֲצִין בָּהּ בְּחֹל יוֹדְעִין הֵן שֶׁאֵין הַנָּכְרִי שְׂכִיר יוֹם אֵצֶל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל וְכָל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁנּוֹתְנִים בְּעַד הָרְחִיצָה נוֹתְנוֹ הַנָּכְרִי לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶלָּא הִשְׂכִּירוֹ לוֹ אוֹ נְתָנוֹ לוֹ בַּאֲרִיסוּת וְכָל הַשָּׂכָר שֶׁנּוֹתְנִין בְּעַד הָרְחִיצָה הוּא לְהַנָּכְרִי.

אֲבָל אִם דֶּרֶךְ שׁוּם יִשְׂרָאֵל שֶׁאֵינוֹ מִשְּׁכֵנֵי הַמֶּרְחָץ לִרְחוֹץ בּוֹ בְּחֹל, מִתּוֹךְ שֶׁהוּא יוֹצֵא וְנִכְנָס בּוֹ יוֹדֵעַ שֶׁהוּא שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְאַחַר כָּךְ כְּשֶׁיַּעֲבוֹר שָׁם לִפְעָמִים בְּשַׁבָּת וְיִרְאֶה נָכְרִים רוֹחֲצִין בּוֹ — יַחְשׁוֹד אֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל בַּעַל הַמֶּרְחָץ לוֹמַר שֶׁנָּכְרִי הַמִּתְעַסֵּק בַּמֶּרְחָץ בְּשַׁבָּת הוּא שְׂכִיר יוֹם אֵצֶל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל.

English translation

Similarly, if a Yid owns a dwelling in another courtyard (where he does not live himself) and there is a bathhouse in that dwelling — if during the week no Yid bathes there except the immediate neighbors of the dwelling — it is permitted to him to rent it out to an akum or to give it in arisus.

And there is no concern of marit ayin here: because whoever does not bathe in this bathhouse during the week does not even know that it is Jewish — it is in a separate courtyard and no Yid lives there. The neighbors (non-Jews) who bathe there during the week know that the akum is not sechir yom of the Yid and that all the wages of the bathers do not go to the Yid, but that he rented out the bathhouse and the wage goes to the akum.

But if it is the practice of another Yid (who is not a neighbor of the bathhouse) to go there during the week — then, in entering and leaving, he knows that the bathhouse belongs to a Yid. And when he passes there on Shabbos and sees non-Jews bathing there, he will suspect the Jewish owner of having a sechir yom working on Shabbos.

What is this Seif about?

The final seif of the siman, which closes the discussion with a very specific and refined case: a Jewish bathhouse in a courtyard inhabited by non-Jews. The Rav's logic:

The "eyes that see" test:
  • No one knows that it is Jewish → no marit ayin → permitted
  • Another Yid frequents the bathhouse during the week → he knows → marit ayin returns
The criterion is the public Jewish circulation around the property.

The halachic question

The Rav refines ultimately the notion of marit ayin: not only the perception of the general public, but specifically that of Yidden who could suspect. The decree is contextual to the observant Jewish community.

The opinions

Source Position
Avodah Zarah 21b Discussion of the bathhouse in a courtyard of non-Jews.
Rema (OC 243:2) Mentions the case of the bathhouse in a private non-Jewish dwelling.
Magen Avraham (OC 243:5) Clarifies the criterion of "Jewish passage."
SHHaRav (243:16) Final clarification: the criterion is Jewish passage during the week. If a Yid circulates around, he identifies — so marit ayin is reborn.

The pesak of the Rav

The algorithm of Seif Tes-Zayin:
  1. Is the bathhouse in a courtyard of non-Jews (no Jewish dwelling)?
  2. Are the bathers during the week exclusively the neighbors (non-Jews)?
  3. If yes to both → permitted (no marit ayin)
  4. If an outside Yid frequents during the week → marit ayin returns → forbidden

Modern practical cases

  • Case 1 — A Yid owns a rental property in a non-Jewish area (no Jewish dwelling): 🟢 If no Yid circulates, marit ayin neutralized.
  • Case 2 — A Jewish AirBnB in a non-Jewish neighborhood where no Yid lodges: 🟢 Permitted.
  • Case 3 — A Yid owns a small store in a non-Jewish area, managed by a non-Jew: 🟢 If the clientele is exclusively non-Jewish and no Yidden frequent.
  • Case 4 — A Yid owns a laundromat in a mixed area: 🔴 If a few Yidden go there during the week — marit ayin returns → forbidden for it to operate on Shabbos.
  • Case 5 — A Yid owns real estate in a foreign country, outside Jewish areas: 🟢 If truly outside the Jewish circuit, marit ayin absent → permitted.

Points to remember

  • The bathhouse in a courtyard of non-Jews without Jewish circulation = permitted
  • The criterion is visibility by other Yidden (not the general public)
  • Modern application: geographic areas without a Jewish community
  • The occasional passage of a Yid is enough to restore marit ayin
  • This seif closes the siman on a note of maximum refinement
Comprehension question: If the criterion is Jewish passage, how does one evaluate a borderline situation (rare, occasional Jewish passage)? Is marit ayin binary or by threshold?

25. Practical synthesis of Siman רמ״ג

The 5 pillars of the Siman in 5 sentences:
  1. Amira l'akum is forbidden miDerabbanan — grounded on shelichus l'chumrah and the concern that Shabbos not be light
  2. Bathhouse and oven = customary sechir yom → forbidden; field and mill = customary arisus → permitted
  3. The decisive criterion is the minhag of the trade (minhag hamedinah), which can reverse the rule
  4. Schar Shabbos is forbidden outside havla'ah (absorption in a larger period)
  5. The kenas sanctions transgression of pure din (Seif Heh), not mere marit ayin (Seif Yod-Gimmel)
The unique contributions of the SHHaRav (Seifim Beis to Tes-Zayin):
  1. Seif Alef — Theoretical construction: shelichus derabbanan + biblical asmachta + rabbinic ta'am
  2. Seif Beis — The spontaneous non-Jew: forbidden to benefit + conditional obligation to protest
  3. Seif Gimmel — "Marginal benefit" test: if the akum would lose nothing by not working on Shabbos → it is actually for the Yid
  4. Seif Daled — Shelichus is objective: the subjective intention of the day worker does not save him
  5. Seif Heh — The kenas for one who did not protest: forbidden to benefit from Shabbos earnings, even in havla'ah
  6. Seif Vav — Pure din / marit ayin distinction: pure hasakharah permitted by din, forbidden by decree
  7. Seif Zayin — The field: double permission (rental + arisus), indirect benefit allowed
  8. Seif Ches — Sechir shanah for field: forbidden by marit ayin (except chutz l'techum)
  9. Seif Tes — Extension by analogy: mill/oven; minhag hamedinah clause as variable
  10. Seif Yod — Individual precedent: personal notoriety can neutralize marit ayin
  11. Seif Yod-Alef — Havla'ah: pricing by period, not by day
  12. Seif Yod-Beis — Day-by-day pricing = no valid havla'ah (even bulk payment)
  13. Seif Yod-Gimmel — No kenas if only marit ayin (educational balance)
  14. Seif Yod-Daled — No rental erev Shabbos (preventive vs disguised amira)
  15. Seif Tes-Vav — Attached name: public identification precedes the issur
  16. Seif Tes-Zayin — Bathhouse in courtyard of non-Jews: contextual neutralization
