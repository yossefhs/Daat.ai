1. The central axiom

Hatmana: to wrap, never to add heat — and always for the mitzvah of the chamim.
Siman רנ״ז codifies the 3rd question of the Shabbat cooking triptych (simanim 253-254-257): wrapping. The rule is built on two crossed axes: when (Shabbat / twilight / Friday) and with what (mosif hevel which adds / she-eino mosif hevel which preserves). The halakhic structure is binary; but the purpose is existential — according to the Rama, eating chamim on Shabbat is a mitzvah, refusal indicates epikoros. The correct hatmana allows the Shabbat table to retain its festive dimension.

2. The central dichotomy (mosif hevel vs she-eino mosif hevel)

mosif hevel — Adds heat

  • Criterion: material that generates its own heat (fermentation, reaction, accumulation)
  • Examples: olive pulp, manure, salt, lime, sand, straw, grape marc
  • Rule: forbidden for hatmana even on Friday; sanction if transgressed

she-eino mosif hevel — Preserves only

  • Criterion: passive material (insulator)
  • Examples: garment, fruit, feathers, tow, sawdust
  • Rule: permitted Friday for hatmana; forbidden Shabbat (except cases of Seif Daled / Hé / Vav)
Watch out for Seif Het: a she-eino mosif hevel material becomes equivalent to mosif hevel if placed on a kedeirah with a heat source below. Cover on kedeirah + plata = hatmana with mosif = forbidden.

4. Mnemonic — M-A-T

M — Moussif Hevel (mosif hevel): material that adds heat (manure, salt, sand, straw). Always forbidden for hatmana.

A — Aino moussif (she-eino mosif hevel): passive material (garment, feathers, aluminum). Permitted Friday, forbidden Shabbat (except exceptions).

T — Tcholent (chamim): the central mitzvah. Eating hot on Shabbat = honor of Shabbat. Refusal = sign of epikoros (Rama).

To memorize: "M-A-T for siman 257: Moussif forbidden, Aino permitted Friday, Tcholent is the mitzvah."

5. Decision tree

Question 1: Do you want to wrap or simply protect (classical lid)?
↓
If protect → shemira — permitted whenever
↓
Question 2: With which material?
↓
If mosif hevel (salt, straw, sand, electric) → ALWAYS FORBIDDEN
↓
If she-eino mosif hevel (towel, aluminum, feathers, duvet) → continue
↓
Question 3: When?
↓
If Friday / twilight → permitted
↓
If Shabbat itself → only Seifim 4-5-6 (cover/transfer/cold); otherwise forbidden
↓
Question 4: Is there a heat source BELOW the pot?
↓
If yes → the cover must not touch the pot (Seif Het)
↓
✓ Successful hatmana → mitzvah of the chamim fulfilled!

7. 5 pitfalls to avoid

Pitfall 1: Wrapping a blanket around the Crock-Pot to preserve heat. Forbidden — the Crock's heat + the insulator = hatmana with mosif hevel. Leave the Crock-Pot uncovered.
Pitfall 2: Covering the pot on the plata with a thick blanket that touches the pot. Forbidden (Seif Het) — the heat of the plata transforms the blanket into mosif hevel. Solution: interpose a large object (inverted dish, blech).
Pitfall 3: Thinking that hatmana is forbidden outright. False — it is a mitzvah according to the Rama. Friday with she-eino mosif hevel = perfectly permitted and even recommended.
Pitfall 4: Forgetting to wrap on Friday, and wanting to do it on Shabbat. Forbidden — hatmana on Shabbat is forbidden (gezera). Solutions: cover if already partially wrapped (Seif Daled), transfer then wrap (Seif Hé).
Pitfall 5: Using an electric heating pad to preserve heat. Forbidden even Friday — it is the modern equivalent of mosif hevel. Use a classical plata (= diffuse heat, status of sim. 253).

10. The 5 practical commandments of Siman רנ״ז

For daily conduct

  1. Distinguish mosif hevel / she-eino mosif hevel — straw/salt/sand = add; towel/aluminum/duvet = preserve. Never mosif hevel in hatmana.
  2. Friday = wrapping with she-eino mosif hevel permitted (and recommended). Shabbat = no new hatmana (except Seifim 4-5-6).
  3. Blankets on pot + plata = major pitfall. If they touch the pot, it is hatmana with mosif. Solution: interpose an object or avoid.
  4. Lid / aluminum = shemira (protection) as long as the intention is not purely thermal. Permitted even on Shabbat.
  5. Mitzvah of the chamim on Shabbat — it is the halakhic identity of the Jewish people (anti-Karaism). Making tcholent / chamim = honoring Shabbat.
