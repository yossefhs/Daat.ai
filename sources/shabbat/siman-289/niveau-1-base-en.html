1. The Text of the Shulchan Aruch

Siman רפ״ט contains 2 seifim of the Mechaber (Rabbi Yosef Karo) which codify the halachos relating to the order of the Shabbos morning seudah.

Seif א — Structure of the Shabbos morning seudah and Kiddusha Rabba

סדר סעודת שחרית של שבת. ובו ב סעיפים:
יהיה שלחנו ערוך ומטה מוצעת יפה ומפה פרוסה כמו בסעודת הלילה ויברך על היין בורא פרי הגפן והוא נקרא קידושא רבה ואח״כ יטול ידיו (וע״ל סי' רע״א סעיף י״ב בהג״ה) ויבצע על לחם משנה כמו בלילה ויסעוד וגם זה הקידוש צריך שיהיה במקום סעודה ושלא יטעום קודם לו כלום כמו בקידוש הלילה ומיהו לשתות מים בבקר קודם תפלה מותר מפני שעדיין לא חל עליו חובת קידוש: הגה וע״ל כל דיני קידוש סי' רע״א רע״ב רע״ג:
Translation: "The table should be set, the bed neatly made, and the tablecloth spread — just as for the night seudah. He recites the brachah on wine 'Borei pri ha-gefen' — and this is called Kiddusha Rabba ('the Great Kiddush' — by way of euphemism). Then he washes his hands [for bread], makes hamotzi on lechem mishneh (two loaves) as at night, and eats. This Kiddush too must be at the 'makom seudah' (place of the seudah), and he must not taste anything before it, just like the night Kiddush. However, drinking water in the morning before tefillah is permitted, because the chiyuv of Kiddush has not yet taken effect. Rema: See also all the halachos of Kiddush in simanim 271-273."
Structure of the Shabbos morning seudah:
  1. Set table = kavod Shabbos.
  2. Kiddusha Rabba on wine (just Borei pri ha-gefen, no long brachah like at night).
  3. Netilas yadayim (ritual hand-washing).
  4. Lechem mishneh = 2 whole loaves (zecher to the double mon of erev Shabbos).
  5. Full seudah with fish/meat, zemiros, divrei Torah.
Why "Kiddusha Rabba" ("the Great Kiddush")? Paradoxically, it is the shortest of the 2 Kiddushim (just Borei pri ha-gefen, without the long brachah of "Va-yechulu" of the night). The name "Rabba" comes — according to Pesachim 106a — from euphemistic usage: this is how a short thing is named, just as a blind person is called "sagi nahor" ("full of light"). According to others: "Rabba" because it is the obligatory daytime Kiddush (the master of the daytime aspect).

Seif ב — In the absence of wine: "chamar medinah" and alternatives

במקום שאין יין מצוי הוי שכר ושאר משקין חוץ מהמים חמר מדינה ומקדשין עליו ואם אין לו אפילו שכר ושאר משקין אוכל בלא קידוש:
Translation: "In a place where wine is not available, beer (shechar) and other beverages (except water) constitute chamar medinah ('wine of the land'), and Kiddush may be made on them. And if he does not even have beer or other beverages, he eats without Kiddush."
Hierarchy of beverages for Kiddusha Rabba:
  1. Wine (ideal, Borei pri ha-gefen)
  2. Chamar medinah (beer, other honorable beverages besides water) — recite "she-ha-kol nihiyah bi-dvaro"
  3. Nothing — eat without Kiddush (exceptional case)
"Chamar medinah": "the drink of the land" — meaning the dignified beverage that people in a given region consume in place of wine. In Talmudic times = beer (shechar). Today: whiskey, cognac, grape juice (according to the poskim). Water is excluded because it is not a specific "honorable" beverage.
Full text: these 2 seifim constitute the entirety of the Mechaber's codification on this topic. Each specifies a case, a condition, or an exception.

2. General Context

What is this siman about?

Our siman codifies the second of the 3 seudos of Shabbos — the morning meal, after shacharis and Mussaf. It is the principal seudah after the night. It revolves around Kiddusha Rabba ("the Great Kiddush") — paradoxically the shortest of the Kiddushim — and the classical structure: set table, wine, lechem mishneh, meal, zemiros, divrei Torah.

The 3 seudos of Shabbos (foundational mitzvah):
  • Seudah 1 — Friday night: after Kabbalas Shabbos (simanim 271-273)
  • Seudah 2 — Shabbos morning: after shacharis + Mussaf (siman 289)
  • Seudah 3 — Seudah Shelishis: between Mincha and the end of Shabbos (siman 291)
Source: Shabbos 117b — "Three seudos a person is obligated to eat on Shabbos."

Position in Hilchos Shabbos

Siman רפ״ט fits within the sequence of meals/tefillos of Shabbos:

  • Siman 286 — Mussaf of Shabbos
  • Siman 287-288 — bikkur cholim, fasting
  • Siman 289 — Seudas shacharis (our siman)
  • Siman 290 — completing the 100 brachos with fruit
  • Siman 291 — Seudah Shelishis

3. Key Halachic Concepts

1. קידושא רבא (Kiddusha Rabba): Kiddush of the day. Short (just Borei pri ha-gefen); called "Rabba" by euphemism (like "sagi nahor" for a blind person). Source: Pesachim 106a.
2. קידוש במקום סעודה (Kiddush bi-Mkom Seudah): the Kiddush must be followed by the seudah in the same place. If one says Kiddush and then eats elsewhere without eating anything on the spot — the Kiddush is invalid.
3. חמר מדינה (Chamar Medinah): "wine of the land" — local honorable beverage that may be used in the absence of wine. Beer (shechar) classically; whiskey, grape juice according to contemporary poskim. Water is excluded.
4. לחם משנה (Lechem Mishneh): two whole loaves (challos) on which hamotzi is made — zecher to the double mon that fell on erev Shabbos (Shemos 16:22). At every Shabbos seudah.
5. מטה מוצעת ושלחן ערוך: bed made, table set, tablecloth spread — visible kavod of Shabbos. Not just eating, but receiving Shabbos with dignity.
6. לשתות מים לפני התפלה: drinking water in the morning before shacharis is permitted because the chiyuv of Kiddush only takes effect from the moment one begins to eat or drink "significantly". Water does not trigger the chiyuv.

6. The Rema's Position

Where the Rema (Rabbi Moshe Isserles) adds a הגהה (gloss), he generally specifies the Ashkenazi nuances relative to the Sephardi Mechaber. Carefully check the Hebrew text above to spot the passages introduced by הגה.

The Rema's position on this siman: the Rema only refers back to simanim 271-273 (general halachos of Kiddush). No substantial Sephardi/Ashkenazi addition here — the minhag is common.

7. Modern Practical Cases

SituationPractical halachah
Early morning: coffee before tefillahWater permitted (seif א). Coffee/tea: machlokes — many are matir (before tefillah, to help concentrate). Wine/whiskey: forbidden before Kiddush.
No wine availableBeer, whiskey, cognac, grape juice = chamar medinah. Recite Borei pri ha-gefen on wine if there is even a little; otherwise she-ha-kol on chamar medinah.
Drinking a little before KiddushForbidden. Kiddush must be the first thing drunk/eaten in the morning (after tefillah).
Kiddush in shul ("Kiddush club")If one really tastes (pas ka-beitzah of mezonos, ≈ 56g of cake) — that is makom seudah and is mekayem the Kiddush. Otherwise, redo Kiddush at home before the real meal.
Lechem mishneh: only one whole loaf?Lechatchilah 2 whole loaves. Otherwise, 1 whole + 1 piece (bedieved). Cf. siman 274.
Woman who must make Kiddush for herselfPermitted and obligatory (nashim are mechuyavos in Kiddush — kol she-yeshno bi-shemirah yeshno bi-zechirah). Borei pri ha-gefen + drink / eat.
Diabetic who cannot drink wineGrape juice (kosher, alcohol-free) = real wine halachically. Otherwise, chamar medinah.
Without tablecloth / simple tableLechatchilah: spread a tablecloth. Bedieved: valid. The ideal is the visible kavod ("kavod Shabbos").
Practical rule: Shabbos morning = set table + Kiddush + Netilah + Lechem mishneh + seudah + zemiros + Birkas Ha-Mazon. This is the principal seudah of the day.

8. Practical Synthesis of the Siman

The 5 key teachings of Siman רפ״ט:
  1. Kavod of Shabbos morning: set table, made bed, tablecloth — visible kavod.
  2. Kiddusha Rabba: short (just Borei pri ha-gefen) — the "Great Kiddush" by euphemism.
  3. Makom seudah: Kiddush must be at the place of the seudah — otherwise void.
  4. Don't taste before: as at night, wait for Kiddush. Exception: water alone, permitted before shacharis.
  5. Chamar medinah: if no wine, beer or other honorable beverage; last resort: eat without Kiddush.

9. Comprehension Questions

Check your understanding:
  1. What is the etymology of "Kiddusha Rabba" and why is it actually short?
  2. What does "Kiddush bi-mkom seudah" mean and what is its practical implication?
  3. Is drinking water in the morning before Kiddush permitted? Why?
  4. What is "chamar medinah" and when may it be used?
  5. Can water be chamar medinah? Why not?
  6. What is the place of the "set table" in the mitzvah (kavod Shabbos)?
  7. If one has neither wine nor chamar medinah, what does he do?
