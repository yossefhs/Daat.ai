דעת הרב

Daas HaRav — The Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe on Siman שכ"ה

The shitah of the Alter Rebbe on the 22 seifim codifying spontaneous melachah by a non-Jew for a Jew on Shabbos — when may one benefit, distinctions, and modern application (non-Jewish employee of a company).

Why a dedicated Level 4 for the Alter Rebbe? The Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe is not a commentary on the Mechaber — it is a standalone and complete Shulchan Aruch, authored by the Alter Rebbe. Its singularity: it combines halachah + טעמי המצוות + inner dimension in one work, and rules with unique Talmudic rigor.

For Chabad, the Alter Rebbe is the final posek. This page gathers, for siman שכ"ה, the full text of the Rebbe, his originality, and the divrei haRebbe that illuminate the topic.

שלחן ערוך הרב — סימן שכ"ה

The full text of the Shulchan Aruch of the Alter Rebbe (שולחן ערוך הרב)

סימן שכ"ה — נָכְרִי שֶׁעָשָׂה מְלָאכָה בְּעַד יִשְׂרָאֵל — וּבוֹ כב סְעִיפִים
Source: Kehot edition, as reproduced on Sefaria. 22 seifim + 0 Kuntres Acharon entries.

סעיף א נָכְרִי שֶׁעָשָׂה מְלָאכָה בְּעַד יִשְׂרָאֵל וּבוֹ כ"ב סְעִיפִים: מֻתָּר לְזַמֵּן נָכְרִי לְבֵיתוֹ לֶאֱכוֹל…

נָכְרִי שֶׁעָשָׂה מְלָאכָה בְּעַד יִשְׂרָאֵל וּבוֹ כ"ב סְעִיפִים:

מֻתָּר לְזַמֵּן נָכְרִי לְבֵיתוֹ לֶאֱכוֹל עִמּוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאָסוּר בְּיוֹם טוֹב מִשּׁוּם גְּזֵרָה שֶׁמָּא יַרְבֶּה לְבַשֵּׁל בִּשְׁבִילוֹ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁיִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן תקי"ב, אֲבָל בְּשַׁבָּת אֵין לָחוּשׁ לָזֶה. וּמֻתָּר אֲפִלּוּ לִתֵּן מְזוֹנוֹת לִפְנֵי נָכְרִי בִּלְבַדּוֹ, וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵין מְזוֹנוֹתָיו עָלֶיךָ, מִכָּל מָקוֹם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁמְּפַרְנְסִים עֲנִיֵּי נָכְרִים מִפְּנֵי דַּרְכֵי שָׁלוֹם — חָשׁוּב כִּמְזוֹנוֹתָיו עָלֶיךָ:

Non-Jew who performed melachah on behalf of a Jew — containing 22 seifim:

It is permitted to invite a non-Jew to one's home to eat together with him on Shabbos. Although it is forbidden on Yom Tov by decree lest one cook more for his sake — as will be explained in siman 512 — on Shabbos there is no concern for this. And it is even permitted to place food before a non-Jew alone. Even though his sustenance is not upon you, since we sustain poor non-Jews on account of darkei shalom (the ways of peace) — it is considered as though his sustenance is upon you.

סעיף ב וּמֻתָּר לִתֵּן לְפָנָיו מְזוֹנוֹת בְּחָצֵר, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁיּוֹדֵעַ שֶׁיּוֹצִיאֵן לַחוּץ. וְהוּא…

וּמֻתָּר לִתֵּן לְפָנָיו מְזוֹנוֹת בְּחָצֵר, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁיּוֹדֵעַ שֶׁיּוֹצִיאֵן לַחוּץ. וְהוּא שֶׁיִּהְיֶה לוֹ רְשׁוּת לְאָכְלָן שָׁם אִם יִרְצֶה. וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, לֹא יִתֵּן הוּא לְיָדוֹ מַמָּשׁ, כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשֶׂה הוּא הָעֲקִירָה מֵרְשׁוּת הַיָּחִיד, אֶלָּא הַנָּכְרִי יִטּוֹל מִיָּדוֹ, שֶׁאָז הַנָּכְרִי הוּא הָעוֹשֶׂה הָעֲקִירָה. וְאֵין צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר שֶׁמֻּתָּר אִם מַנִּיחַ עַל גַּבֵּי קַרְקַע אוֹ עַל גַּבֵּי שֻׁלְחָן וְהַנָּכְרִי נוֹטֵל מִשָּׁם.

אֲבָל אִם אֵין הָרְשׁוּת בְּיָדוֹ לְאָכְלָן שָׁם, אוֹ שֶׁהֵם מְזוֹנוֹת מְרֻבִּים שֶׁאִי אֶפְשָׁר לוֹ לְאָכְלָן שָׁם וְנִכָּר הַדָּבָר שֶׁיּוֹצִיאֵן לַחוּץ, וְכֵן אִם הַנָּכְרִי עוֹמֵד בַּחוּץ וּפָשַׁט יָדוֹ לִפְנִים שֶׁיָּדוּעַ וְנִכָּר הוּא שֶׁיּוֹצִיא — אָסוּר לִתֵּן לְפָנָיו, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כְּנוֹתֵן לוֹ עַל מְנָת לְהוֹצִיא. וְאַף שֶׁמּוֹצִיא לַהֲנָאָתוֹ וְלֹא בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל, מִכָּל מָקוֹם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁדָּבָר זֶה שֶׁהוּא מוֹצִיא נוֹתְנוֹ לוֹ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל מִשֶּׁלּוֹ — אָסוּר מִטַּעַם שֶׁנִּתְבָּאֵר בְּסִימָן ש"ז:

And it is permitted to place food before him in the courtyard, even though one knows he will carry it outside — provided that he have permission to eat it there if he wishes. In any case, one shall not place it directly into his hand, in order that he himself not perform the akirah (uprooting) from the reshus hayachid (private domain); rather, the non-Jew takes from his hand, so that the non-Jew is the one performing the akirah. Needless to say, it is permitted to place it on the ground or on the table and have the non-Jew take it from there.

But if he does not have permission to eat it there, or it is so much food that he cannot eat it there and it is apparent that he will carry it outside, and likewise if the non-Jew stands outside and stretches his hand inside such that it is known and evident that he will carry it out — it is forbidden to place it before him, since it appears as though one is giving it to him on condition that he carry it out. And although he carries it for his own benefit and not for the Jew, nevertheless, since this item that he is carrying is given to him by the Jew from his own property — it is forbidden for the reason explained in siman 307.

סעיף ג וַאֲפִלּוּ כְּשֶׁעוֹמֵד בִּפְנִים, לֹא הִתִּירוּ לִתֵּן לְפָנָיו אֶלָּא מְזוֹנוֹת…

וַאֲפִלּוּ כְּשֶׁעוֹמֵד בִּפְנִים, לֹא הִתִּירוּ לִתֵּן לְפָנָיו אֶלָּא מְזוֹנוֹת, שֶׁיִּתָּכֵן לְאָכְלָם בֶּחָצֵר, אֲבָל לֹא שְׁאָר חֲפָצִים שֶׁדֶּרֶךְ לְהוֹצִיאָן, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כְּנוֹתֵן לוֹ עַל מְנָת לְהוֹצִיא.

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? בַּחֲפָצִים שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל. אֲבָל חֲפָצִים שֶׁל נָכְרִי מֻתָּר לִתְּנָם לוֹ, שֶׁאַף שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כְּנוֹתֵן לוֹ עַל מְנָת לְהוֹצִיא — אֵין בְּכָךְ כְּלוּם, כֵּיוָן שֶׁאֵינוֹ מוֹצִיא בִּשְׁבִיל הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל אֶלָּא בִּשְׁבִיל עַצְמוֹ, וְהַחֵפֶץ אֵינוֹ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל...

Even when he stands inside, they only permitted placing food before him — which can possibly be eaten in the courtyard. But not other items that one customarily carries out, since it appears as though one is giving them to him on condition that he carry them out.

In what does this apply? With items belonging to the Jew. But items belonging to the non-Jew — it is permitted to give them to him. For although it appears as if one is giving on condition that he carry, there is no concern, since he is not carrying for the Jew but for himself, and the item does not belong to the Jew. And even saying explicitly, "Do your work with your own items," is permitted, as explained there.

And nevertheless, if the non-Jew stands outside and stretches his hand inside — it is forbidden to place even his own items before him, on account of mar'is ha'ayin (the appearance of impropriety): for whoever sees the Jew giving to him assumes it is the Jew's item. This is not the case when he is given items in the courtyard, where there are no onlookers, and whoever sees him leaving the Jew's domain with an item in hand assumes he is carrying his own and not the Jew's.

(Nevertheless, if the truth is that it is the Jew's item, it is forbidden, since perhaps the truth will become known to onlookers who will suspect that he gave it to him in order that he carry it out for him; this is not so with a non-Jew's item — if the truth becomes known, there is no suspicion at all.)

סעיף ד וַאֲפִלּוּ הוּא חֵפֶץ שֶׁל נָכְרִי אֶלָּא שֶׁהוּא מְמֻשְׁכָּן לְיִשְׂרָאֵל — דִּינוֹ כְּחֵפֶץ שֶׁל…

וַאֲפִלּוּ הוּא חֵפֶץ שֶׁל נָכְרִי אֶלָּא שֶׁהוּא מְמֻשְׁכָּן לְיִשְׂרָאֵל — דִּינוֹ כְּחֵפֶץ שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל... אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי הוּא אַלָּם, שֶׁאָז יֵשׁ לְהָקֵל אֲפִלּוּ לֹא יִחֵד לוֹ מָקוֹם. וְכֵן בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁיֵּשׁ בּוֹ מִשּׁוּם דַּרְכֵי שָׁלוֹם, כְּגוֹן נָכְרִי שֶׁחָלָה וְשָׁלַח אַחַר מַאֲכַל יִשְׂרָאֵל — מֻתָּר לָתֵת לוֹ אוֹ אֲפִלּו לִשְׁלוֹחַ לוֹ עַל יְדֵי נָכְרִי. וְהוּא הַדִּין לְצֹרֶךְ מִצְוָה...

And even if it is the non-Jew's item that is pledged to the Jew (mashkon) — its status is like that of a Jew's item, even if the non-Jew redeemed it (so it appears in siman 252 regarding a sale) before sunset on Friday. And even if one designated a place in his domain for the non-Jew before Shabbos to deposit his pledge until the next day when he would come and take it from there — it is forbidden to let him take it on Shabbos, on account of mar'is ha'ayin.

Unless the non-Jew is forceful (alim), in which case one may be lenient even if he did not designate a place. And likewise in a case of darkei shalom — for example a non-Jew who fell ill and sent for a Jew's food: it is permitted to give to him or even to send to him via a non-Jew. The same applies to a need of a mitzvah — for example to remove chametz from one's home on Pesach, as will be explained in siman 444, since the agency through a non-Jew is a shevus; and now that some say we have no reshus harabim mid'oraisa but only karmelis, as will be explained in siman 345, it is a shevus di-shevus, which was permitted for the sake of a mitzvah, as explained in siman 307. The same for darkei shalom; the same for a forceful non-Jew.

And nevertheless, one must take care not to place it directly into the non-Jew's hand, since this can be avoided. And even with the non-Jew's own items, one must take this precaution.

סעיף ה מֻתָּר לְהַחֲלִיף מַשְׁכּוֹן לְנָכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת אִם הוּא מַלְבּוּשׁ…

מֻתָּר לְהַחֲלִיף מַשְׁכּוֹן לְנָכְרִי בְּשַׁבָּת אִם הוּא מַלְבּוּשׁ וְיוֹצִיאֶנּוּ הַנָּכְרִי דֶּרֶךְ מַלְבּוּשׁ, כִּי אֵין זֶה מַשָּׂא וּמַתָּן. וְגַם בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל מֻתָּר בְּעִנְיָן זֶה, אִם הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל צָרִיךְ לְלָבְשׁוֹ בְּשַׁבָּת. וְאִם הַנָּכְרִי מֵבִיא הַמָּעוֹת וְנוֹטֵל הַמַּשְׁכּוֹן — מֻתָּר גַּם כֵּן... וְטוֹב שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי יִקַּח הַמַּשְׁכּוֹן בְּעַצְמוֹ וְלֹא יִגַּע בּוֹ הַיִּשְׂרָאֵל, כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יְהֵא נִרְאֶה כְּנוֹשֵׂא וְנוֹתֵן.

It is permitted to exchange a pledge to a non-Jew on Shabbos if it is a garment and the non-Jew takes it out by wearing it, since this is not buying-and-selling. Also for a Jew it is permitted in this manner, if the Jew needs to wear it on Shabbos. And if the non-Jew brings the money and takes the pledge — it is also permitted, provided one does not reckon accounts with him. It is preferable that the non-Jew take the pledge himself and that the Jew not touch it, so that it not appear as if engaged in trade. And if he brings another pledge in its place — he shall not touch that either.

סעיף ו בִּמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁנּוֹהֲגִים לֶאֱכוֹל פַּת שֶׁל פַּלְטֵר נָכְרִי — יֵשׁ מַתִּירִין לִקַּח מִמֶּנּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת…

בִּמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁנּוֹהֲגִים לֶאֱכוֹל פַּת שֶׁל פַּלְטֵר נָכְרִי — יֵשׁ מַתִּירִין לִקַּח מִמֶּנּוּ בְּשַׁבָּת אֲפִלּוּ פַּת שֶׁנֶּאֱפֵית הַיּוֹם... וְיֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים... וּלְעִנְיַן הֲלָכָה — יֵשׁ לְהַחֲמִיר כַּסְבָרָא הָאַחֲרוֹנָה. אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן הוּא שְׁעַת הַדְּחָק... וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, אָסוּר לִתֵּן מָעוֹת לְהַפַּלְטֵר מֵעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת שֶׁיִּתֵּן לוֹ הַפַּת בְּשַׁבָּת, שֶׁאָז הוּא אוֹפֶה לְצֹרֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְאָסוּר לֶאֱכוֹל מֵהַפַּת עַד לְמוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשׂוּ:

In places where the custom is to eat bread of a non-Jewish baker (palter) — some permit taking from him on Shabbos even bread baked that day, if it is a city the majority of whose residents are non-Jews, since presumably it is baked for the non-Jews and not for the Jews. If so, there is no reason to forbid it on grounds of a non-Jew performing melachah for a Jew. And there is also no basis to forbid it as muktzeh, even if it was flour or dough at bein hashemashos, which are muktzin, as explained in siman 308 — we do not say of it "since it was set aside at bein hashemashos, it is set aside for all of Shabbos." For we only say this of something that becomes fit on its own after nightfall — such as a candle that went out by itself after nightfall. But any matter that is completed by human hand — that is, whose preparation is completed on Shabbos and made fit through human action — is not muktzeh at all, even at bein hashemashos while not yet fit, since the person does not entirely set his mind aside from it at bein hashemashos, given that it is in his power to prepare it and make it fit that day. Thus the flour in a non-Jew's possession has no status of muktzeh whatsoever on Shabbos, since it is in his power to bake it and make it fit that day; and being prepared for its non-Jewish owner — it is prepared for all.

And some say that we only said "everything completed by human hand is not muktzeh" regarding something whose work-of-preparation to render it fit had begun before Shabbos and was completed on Shabbos — like a non-Jew who began making a vessel before Yom Tov and finished it on Yom Tov: there is no nolad in it, since it was completed by human hand, as explained in siman 252. (Likewise bread that one began to bake — that is, placed in the oven before sunset — though it was not fit at bein hashemashos, has no muktzeh status.) But bread whose work-of-preparation to render it fit — namely the act of baking — was begun on Shabbos itself, has muktzeh if it was flour or dough at bein hashemashos. Whereas if it was wheat at bein hashemashos, and was ground and baked on Shabbos — there is no muktzeh, nor nolad on Yom Tov, as will be explained in siman 517.

For the halachah — one should be stringent according to the latter view. Except in a case of pressing need (sha'as hadechak), such as one who dwells alone in a village without anyone to lend him bread; or for a mitzvah need, such as a bris milah meal, or for birkas hamotzi — then one may rely on the lenient view.

Nevertheless, it is forbidden to give money to the baker before Shabbos so that he give the bread on Shabbos, since then he bakes for the Jew. And it is forbidden to eat from that bread until Motzaei Shabbos plus bichdei she-yei'asu (the time it would take to be made), as will be explained.

סעיף ז (וְכָל זֶה בְּפַת. אֲבָל נָכְרִי שֶׁבִּשֵּׁל לְעַצְמוֹ…

(וְכָל זֶה בְּפַת. אֲבָל נָכְרִי שֶׁבִּשֵּׁל לְעַצְמוֹ, מִדְּבָרִים שֶׁאֵין בָּהֶם מִשּׁוּם בִּשּׁוּלֵי נָכְרִים — אֵין בָּהֶם מִשּׁוּם מֻקְצֶה לְדִבְרֵי הַכֹּל, אֲפִלּוּ הֵן פֵּרוֹת שֶׁאֵינָן נֶאֱכָלִים חַיִּין וְלֹא הָיוּ רְאוּיִים כְּלָל בֵּין הַשְּׁמָשׁוֹת...):

(And all this pertains to bread. But a non-Jew who cooked for himself items not subject to bishul akum — there is no muktzeh issue at all, according to all, even if they are fruits not eaten raw and were not fit at all at bein hashemashos. For "since it was set aside at bein hashemashos, it is set aside for all of Shabbos" is said only of a thing that a person sets aside with intent and rejects with his hands — such as wheat ground into flour. But not of a thing that is muktzeh against one's will, as explained in siman 310.)

סעיף ח וְכָל זֶה בַּאֲפִיָּה (וּבִשּׁוּל) וְכַיּוֹצֵא בָהֶן מִשְּׁאָר מְלָאכוֹת…

וְכָל זֶה בַּאֲפִיָּה (וּבִשּׁוּל) וְכַיּוֹצֵא בָהֶן מִשְּׁאָר מְלָאכוֹת הַנַּעֲשׂוֹת בְּתָלוּשׁ, חוּץ מִסְּחִיטַת פֵּרוֹת... וְכֵן נָכְרִי שֶׁלִּקֵּט פֵּרוֹת מֵהַמְחֻבָּר לְעַצְמוֹ... אֲסוּרִים לְיִשְׂרָאֵל עַד לָעֶרֶב, לְפִי שֶׁהֵם בִּכְלַל הַגְּזֵרָה שֶׁגָּזְרוּ עַל פֵּרוֹת הַנּוֹשְׁרִים מֵאֲלֵיהֶן... וְכֵן נָכְרִי שֶׁצָּד דָּגִים אוֹ חַיָּה אוֹ עוֹף לְעַצְמוֹ — אֲסוּרִים לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בְּיוֹם טוֹב:

And all this concerns baking (and cooking) and the like — the other melachos performed on a detached item (talush) — except squeezing fruit: for if a non-Jew squeezed them even for himself, the juices are forbidden to the Jew until evening, in a case in which they would have been forbidden had they flowed of themselves, as explained in siman 320. For these juices that come to the Jew via the non-Jew's squeezing are also included in the decree against juices that flow of themselves, since they too come to the Jew incidentally (mimeila) like the ones flowing on their own.

Likewise a non-Jew who picked fruit from the attached for himself, even in a case where there is no muktzeh — for example he was heard saying on erev Shabbos "tomorrow I will pick these fruits," and they are fully ripened fruits, for which preparation is effective even while attached, as explained in siman 318 — they are still forbidden to a Jew until evening, since they are included in the decree against fruits that fell of themselves, as explained in siman 322; these fruits too come to the Jew incidentally like the fallen ones.

And not fruit only — even any attached thing that becomes detached on its own or by a non-Jew, even if not human food, is included in this decree. For example a non-Jew who picked attached grasses, even when there is no muktzeh issue (the Jew has animals that could graze on these grasses, so they are prepared for him) — they are still forbidden because of the decree of fallen fruits. Therefore it is forbidden for the Jew to use them in any way, just as it is forbidden to use the fallen fruits. They are therefore also forbidden in moving (tiltul), since they are not fit for any use that day: they are muktzin, just as fallen fruits have the status of muktzeh for the same reason.

And even wood that the non-Jew detached for himself, or that fell of itself on Yom Tov — is included in this decree, as will be explained in siman 507.

Likewise a non-Jew who captured fish or animals or fowl for himself — they are forbidden to a Jew on Yom Tov, as will be explained in siman 515.

סעיף ט אִם לִקֵּט אוֹ צָד אוֹ אָפָה וּבִשֵּׁל אוֹ שֶׁעָשָׂה אַחַת מִשְּׁאָר מְלָאכוֹת בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל…

אִם לִקֵּט אוֹ צָד אוֹ אָפָה וּבִשֵּׁל אוֹ שֶׁעָשָׂה אַחַת מִשְּׁאָר מְלָאכוֹת בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל, אוֹ בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל וְנָכְרִים — צָרִיךְ לְהַמְתִּין לָעֶרֶב בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשׂוּ, גְּזֵרָה שֶׁמָּא יֹאמַר לַנָּכְרִי לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּשַׁבָּת... אֲבָל אִם נַעֲשָׂה אִסּוּר שֶׁל דִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל — מֻתָּר לַאֲחֵרִים מִיָּד:

If he picked or trapped or baked or cooked or did any other melachah for a Jew, or for a Jew and non-Jews together — one must wait until evening bichdei she-yei'asu, by decree lest one say to the non-Jew to do it on Shabbos so it will be ready for him at the conclusion of Shabbos immediately. And even others for whom the melachah was not done must wait that interval, since whenever a melachah forbidden mid'oraisa was performed for a Jew, the Sages were stringent not to distinguish between the one for whom it was done and others. But if a rabbinic prohibition was transgressed for a Jew — it is permitted to others immediately, as will be explained. And the measure of bichdei she-yei'asu will be explained in siman 515.

סעיף י סָפֵק אִם לִקְּטָן בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל…

סָפֵק אִם לִקְּטָן בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל, אוֹ שֶׁיָּדוּעַ שֶׁלִּקְּטָן בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאֵין יָדוּעַ אִם נִלְקְטוּ הַיּוֹם אוֹ לָאו — אֲסוּרוֹת בּוֹ בַיּוֹם וְגַם לָעֶרֶב בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשׂוּ...

If there is a doubt whether he picked them for a Jew, or it is known that he picked them for a Jew but unknown whether they were picked today or not — they are forbidden on the day itself and also in the evening for bichdei she-yei'asu. And although this is a doubt regarding a rabbinic matter, we do not rule leniently, since it is a "davar she-yesh lo matirin" (a thing that has a permitting outcome). And some say that in the evening it is immediately permitted, since this prohibition is merely a kind of penalty so that one not come to say to the non-Jew to do it on Shabbos — they did not impose stringency in its doubt, even though it has a permitting outcome. And the primary view is according to the first opinion.

And even according to the latter opinion, they were only lenient in a case where the non-Jew said to the Jew on erev Shabbos to give him fruits on Shabbos, and he went to the non-Jew's house and gave him — for then there is room for doubt: perhaps he picked these fruits on Shabbos for the Jew (intending from erev Shabbos to pick these particular fruits for him on Shabbos), or perhaps he intended to pick other fruits for him and picked these for himself or for another non-Jew, then reconsidered and gave them to the Jew. And likewise in all similar cases. But a non-Jew who brought a gift (doron) to a Jew, or brought to sell in a city the majority of whose residents are Jews — one does not presume leniently that he picked them originally for himself and then reconsidered; rather, presumably he picked them with this end in view from the start. Likewise one does not presume leniently that he picked them on erev Shabbos, since a non-Jew bringing a gift or coming to sell presumably intends to bring the finest, and brings the ones picked that day. So too regarding trapping on Yom Tov.

סעיף יא דָּבָר שֶׁאֵין בּוֹ חֲשַׁשׁ צֵידָה וּמְחֻבָּר אֶלָּא שֶׁהוּבָא מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם…

דָּבָר שֶׁאֵין בּוֹ חֲשַׁשׁ צֵידָה וּמְחֻבָּר אֶלָּא שֶׁהוּבָא מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם, אִם הֱבִיאוֹ הַנָּכְרִי לְעַצְמוֹ אוֹ לְנָכְרִי אַחֵר — מֻתָּר לְיִשְׂרָאֵל אֲפִלּוּ בּוֹ בַיּוֹם. וְאִם הֱבִיאוֹ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל — אָסוּר בַּאֲכִילָה בּוֹ בַיּוֹם לְמִי שֶׁהוּבָא בִּשְׁבִילוֹ וְלָעֶרֶב בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשׂוּ... אֲבָל לַאֲחֵרִים מֻתָּר אֲפִלּוּ בּוֹ בַיּוֹם, לְפִי שֶׁאִסּוּר הַתְּחוּמִין הוּא מִדִּבְרֵי סוֹפְרִים...

An item that has no concern of trapping nor of attached, but only was brought from outside the techum (Shabbos boundary) — if the non-Jew brought it for himself or for another non-Jew, it is permitted to a Jew even on the same day. And if he brought it for a Jew — it is forbidden in eating on the day itself to the one for whom it was brought, and in the evening for bichdei she-yei'asu, by decree lest he say to the non-Jew to bring for him from outside the techum on Shabbos in order to eat from it that day or that evening immediately. But it is permitted to others even on the day itself, since the prohibition of techumin is rabbinic, and they did not impose stringency to such an extent on a rabbinic matter as to forbid even to one for whom it was not brought.

Therefore even the one for whom it was brought may carry it (no muktzeh), since it is fit for consumption to others on the same day. He must only take care not to carry it beyond four cubits from the place where the non-Jew set it down, if he placed it in a place not enclosed for habitation, such as in a field or in a non-eruv city — meaning if one person carried it two cubits, a second cannot carry it more than two further cubits, as will be explained in siman 401 for anything carried out of the techum by a non-Jew.

And if it was brought in a ship from outside the techum — it is permitted for eating even to the one for whom it was brought, if the ship traveled above ten tefachim (handbreadths), since there are no techumin above ten tefachim, as will be explained in siman 404. He must only take care not to carry it beyond four cubits, unless it was in the ship from erev Shabbos and was not below ten tefachim from the entry of Shabbos until it arrived within the techum of the present location, as will be explained there.

סעיף יב אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהַבָּא מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם מֻתָּר לְמִי שֶׁלֹּא הוּבָא בִּשְׁבִילוֹ…

אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהַבָּא מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם מֻתָּר לְמִי שֶׁלֹּא הוּבָא בִּשְׁבִילוֹ, מִכָּל מָקוֹם אִם הֵבִיאוּ שְׁנֵי דְבָרִים לִשְׁנֵי בְּנֵי אָדָם — אֲסוּרִים לְהַחֲלִיף זֶה עִם זֶה, שֶׁהֲרֵי עַל כָּל פָּנִים נֶהֱנֶה הוּא בְּמַה שֶּׁהוּבָא לוֹ מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם:

Although what comes from outside the techum is permitted to one for whom it was not brought, nevertheless if they brought two items for two people — they are forbidden to exchange one with the other, since in any case each person benefits from what was brought to him from outside the techum, for it is by means of it that his fellow gives him the exchange.

סעיף יג כָּל מָקוֹם שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְהַמְתִּין בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשׂוּ…

כָּל מָקוֹם שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְהַמְתִּין בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשׂוּ — אֵין צָרִיךְ לְהַמְתִּין אֶלָּא בִּכְדֵי שֶׁשָּׁהָה הַמֵּבִיא בְּיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת... וְיֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁבְּכָל מָקוֹם שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְהַמְתִּין בִּכְדֵי הֲבָאָה מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם... אֵין לֵיל מוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת עוֹלֶה מִן הַחֶשְׁבּוֹן... וְיֵשׁ לָחוּשׁ לְדִבְרֵיהֶם לְעִנְיַן שַׁבָּת:

Wherever one must wait bichdei she-yei'asu, one need only wait the amount of time the porter actually spent on Shabbos day. For example, if he brought it from a distant place in three hours of Shabbos because he rode a horse and so hastened his coming — one need not wait, at Motzaei Shabbos, the time it takes to walk there on foot; it suffices to wait three hours. And one need not be concerned that perhaps he did not gallop all that quickly, but rather rode much of the Shabbos night, since presumably it is not customary to ride at night.

In what does this apply? When one knows from where he brought it. But if one has no knowledge at all and it is possible he brought it from a nearby place — it suffices to wait at Motzaei Shabbos the time of bringing it from outside the techum, beyond what is needed to remain there for picking or trapping if it is something whose species exists attached or requires trapping, as will be explained in siman 515.

And some say that wherever one must wait the time of bringing from outside the techum — and certainly where one must wait the time of bringing from a more distant place — the night of Motzaei Shabbos does not count in the reckoning, and one must wait that measure the next day, Sunday. Since it is not customary to bring at night from a distant place outside the techum, there is concern that one might therefore say to a non-Jew to bring on Shabbos so it be ready for him on Motzaei Shabbos night after waiting that measure.

One should heed their words for Shabbos. But for Yom Tov one may be lenient according to the first view. And even for Shabbos one may be lenient for the sake of guests or another meal of mitzvah.

But where one must wait the time of bringing from within the techum — for example a non-Jew who picked fruits or trapped fish within the techum, and one must wait the time of picking or trapping and bringing from the place where he picked or trapped within the techum — there the night does count in the reckoning, according to all.

סעיף יד אִם הוּא סָפֵק אִם הוּבָא מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם — אָסוּר לְמִי שֶׁהוּבָא בִּשְׁבִילוֹ…

אִם הוּא סָפֵק אִם הוּבָא מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם — אָסוּר לְמִי שֶׁהוּבָא בִּשְׁבִילוֹ עַד לְמוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת בִּכְדֵי הֲבָאָה מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהוּא "דָּבָר שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ מַתִּירִין". וּלְהָאוֹמְרִים שֶׁבְּאִסּוּר כָּזֶה... לֹא הֶחֱמִירוּ בִּסְפֵקוֹ... אַף כָּאן מֻתָּר... וּכְבָר נִתְבָּאֵר שֶׁהָעִקָּר כַּסְבָרָא הָרִאשׁוֹנָה:

If it is doubtful whether it was brought from outside the techum — it is forbidden to the one for whom it was brought until Motzaei Shabbos plus the time of bringing from outside the techum, since it is a "davar she-yesh lo matirin."

And according to those who say that in this kind of prohibition — which is only a sort of penalty so that one not come to ask a non-Jew to do work on Shabbos — they did not impose stringency in its doubt even though it has a permitting outcome, as explained above — here too it is permitted for eating even to the one for whom it was brought, even on the same day. And it has already been explained that the primary view is according to the first opinion.

סעיף טו וְכָל זֶה בְּנָכְרִי שֶׁאֵינוֹ שָׁרוּי עִמּוֹ בָּעִיר…

וְכָל זֶה בְּנָכְרִי שֶׁאֵינוֹ שָׁרוּי עִמּוֹ בָּעִיר, אֲבָל נָכְרִי הַשָּׁרוּי עִמּוֹ בָּעִיר וּפֵרוֹת הַמְּצוּיִים עִמּוֹ בָּעִיר — אֵין מַחֲזִיקִים מִסָּפֵק לוֹמַר שֶׁמָּא הֱבִיאָן מִחוּץ לַתְּחוּם, כִּי אַדְּרַבָּה אָנוּ אוֹמְרִים "כָּאן נִמְצְאוּ וְכָאן הָיוּ"... אֲבָל אִם יֵשׁ לוֹ שְׁנֵי בָתִּים חוּץ לַתְּחוּם וְאֶחָד בְּתוֹךְ הַתְּחוּם — יֵשׁ לָחוּשׁ לְרֹב הַבָּתִּים שֶׁהֵם חוּץ לַתְּחוּם:

And all this concerns a non-Jew who does not reside with him in the city. But a non-Jew who resides with him in the city and the fruits found with him in the city — we do not presume out of doubt that he brought them from outside the techum, for on the contrary we say "here they are found and here they were" (kan nimtze'u ve-kan hayu).

And even if the non-Jew has two houses, one outside the techum and one within — we presume leniently that he brought it from within the techum, since presumably he does not leave the nearby house and go to fetch from the far house.

But if he has two houses outside the techum and one inside — there is reason to follow the majority of houses, which are outside the techum.

סעיף טז נָכְרִי שֶׁמִּלֵּא מַיִם לִבְהֶמְתּוֹ מִבּוֹר…

נָכְרִי שֶׁמִּלֵּא מַיִם לִבְהֶמְתּוֹ מִבּוֹר שֶׁהוּא עָמֹק עֲשָׂרָה טְפָחִים וְרָחָב אַרְבָּעָה טְפָחִים הָעוֹמֵד בִּרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים... מֻתָּר לְיִשְׂרָאֵל לְהַשְׁקוֹת מֵהֶם בְּהֶמְתּוֹ, כֵּיוָן שֶׁהַנָּכְרִי לֹא נִתְכַּוֵּן כְּלָל בִּשְׁבִילוֹ. וְהוּא שֶׁאֵין הַנָּכְרִי מַכִּירוֹ, אֲבָל אִם הוּא מַכִּירוֹ — אָסוּר, גְּזֵרָה שֶׁמָּא יַרְבֶּה הַנָּכְרִי לִדְלוֹת גַּם בִּשְׁבִילוֹ... וְעַכְשָׁו נָהֲגוּ הָעוֹלָם הֶתֵּר אֲפִלּוּ לְכַתְּחִלָּה לוֹמַר לְנָכְרִי לְהָבִיא שֵׁכָר אוֹ שְׁאָר דְּבָרִים שֶׁהֵם צֹרֶךְ הַשַּׁבָּת דֶּרֶךְ כַּרְמְלִית...

A non-Jew who drew water for his animal from a pit ten tefachim deep and four tefachim wide situated in reshus harabim, and is found to have transferred from reshus hayachid to reshus harabim — it is permitted to the Jew to water his animal therefrom, since the non-Jew had no intent at all for his sake. And this is when the non-Jew does not recognize him; but if he recognizes him, it is forbidden, by decree lest the non-Jew increase the drawing also for the Jew's sake if he too will water his animal therefrom.

And even if he does not recognize him, if he drew for the sake of a Jew's animal — it is forbidden even for others to use this water, even for another use such as washing or rinsing dishes, since whatever was done in a Torah prohibition by a non-Jew for the sake of a Jew — the Sages did not distinguish between the one for whom it was done and others.

But if he drew from a pit standing in karmelis, or brought water into reshus harabim from a river that is karmelis — it is permitted to another for whom it was not brought, since the prohibition of karmelis is rabbinic.

And now the world has the custom to permit even lechatchilah to tell a non-Jew to bring beer or other things needed for Shabbos via a karmelis. Some have justified them, saying that the needs of Shabbos are considered as a mitzvah, for which they permitted shevus di-shevus, as explained in siman 307. They must take care not to place the vessel into the non-Jew's hand, and likewise not to take from his hand, as explained there; and also not to give money to a non-Jew, as will be explained in siman 517.

(Nevertheless, one should be lenient only with beer and the like — things truly needed on Shabbos, without which one can manage only with some difficulty. But things not so necessary, such as fruits and extra dishes and the like — are not called "matters of mitzvah," as explained in siman 261.)

סעיף יז לִקֵּט נָכְרִי עֲשָׂבִים מֵהַמְחֻבָּר לְצֹרֶךְ בְּהֶמְתּוֹ…

לִקֵּט נָכְרִי עֲשָׂבִים מֵהַמְחֻבָּר לְצֹרֶךְ בְּהֶמְתּוֹ — מַאֲכִיל אַחֲרָיו יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת בְּהֶמְתּוֹ... וְהוּא שֶׁאֵין הַנָּכְרִי מַכִּירוֹ, אֲבָל אִם מַכִּירוֹ — אָסוּר... אֲבָל לְמוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת אֵין צָרִיךְ לְהַמְתִּין בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשׂוּ:

A non-Jew who picked attached grasses for the needs of his animal — a Jew may feed his animal after him, meaning by standing in its way so that it can only turn in that direction, as explained in siman 324 regarding anything forbidden in tiltul (these grasses too are forbidden in tiltul, as explained above).

And this is when the non-Jew does not recognize him; but if he recognizes him — even standing in the way for the animal to eat is forbidden on Shabbos, by decree lest the non-Jew gather more for the animal if the Jew lets it eat from them. But at Motzaei Shabbos one need not wait bichdei she-yei'asu, since the non-Jew picked for his own needs and the Shabbos prohibition is only the decree lest he add for the Jew's sake.

סעיף יח וְכֵן הַדִּין בְּכָל דָּבָר שֶׁיֵּשׁ לָחוּשׁ בּוֹ שֶׁמָּא יַרְבֶּה בִּשְׁבִילוֹ…

וְכֵן הַדִּין בְּכָל דָּבָר שֶׁיֵּשׁ לָחוּשׁ בּוֹ שֶׁמָּא יַרְבֶּה בִּשְׁבִילוֹ אִם הוּא מַכִּירוֹ, כְּגוֹן נָכְרִי שֶׁצָּלָה אוֹ בִּשֵּׁל לְעַצְמוֹ דְּבָרִים שֶׁאֵין בָּהֶם מִשּׁוּם בִּשּׁוּלֵי נָכְרִים... אֲבָל בְּדָבָר שֶׁאֵין לָחוּשׁ בּוֹ שֶׁמָּא יַרְבֶּה בִּשְׁבִילוֹ, כְּגוֹן שֶׁהִדְלִיק נֵר לְעַצְמוֹ, אוֹ שֶׁעָשָׂה כֶּבֶשׁ לֵירֵד בּוֹ מֵהַסְּפִינָה, שֶׁבְּנֵר אֶחָד וְכֶבֶשׁ אֶחָד יַסְפִּיק לַכֹּל — אֲפִלּוּ מַכִּירוֹ מֻתָּר:

And so too the law for every matter where there is concern lest he add for the Jew's sake if he recognizes him — for example a non-Jew who roasted or cooked for himself items not subject to bishul akum, and all similar cases that require adding more for two than for one.

But for something where there is no concern that he add for him — such as he kindled a lamp for himself, or made a ramp to descend from a ship, where one lamp and one ramp suffice for everyone — even if he recognizes him, it is permitted.

סעיף יט אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵינוֹ מַכִּירוֹ, אִם אוֹמֵר בְּפֵרוּשׁ שֶׁלְּצֹרֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל הוּא עוֹשֶׂה…

אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵינוֹ מַכִּירוֹ, אִם אוֹמֵר בְּפֵרוּשׁ שֶׁלְּצֹרֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל הוּא עוֹשֶׂה, אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ אֵינוֹ אוֹמֵר כֵּן, אִם מַעֲשָׂיו מוֹכִיחִים שֶׁלְּצֹרֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל הוּא עוֹשֶׂה, כְּגוֹן שֶׁהִדְלִיק נֵר בַּבַּיִת שֶׁהַיִּשְׂרָאֵל בּוֹ וְהָלַךְ לוֹ הַנָּכְרִי וְלֹא נֶהֱנָה מִמֶּנּוּ כְּלוּם — אָסוּר לְהִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ לְאוֹרוֹ:

Even if he does not recognize him, if he says explicitly that he is doing it for the sake of a Jew, or even if he does not say so but his actions prove that he is doing it for the sake of the Jew — for example he kindled a lamp in a house in which the Jew is and the non-Jew left without benefiting from it at all — it is forbidden to make use of its light.

סעיף כ אִם לִקֵּט נָכְרִי עֲשָׂבִים (לִבְהֶמְתּוֹ) וְהֶאֱכִיל (גַּם) לְבֶהֱמַת יִשְׂרָאֵל…

אִם לִקֵּט נָכְרִי עֲשָׂבִים (לִבְהֶמְתּוֹ) וְהֶאֱכִיל (גַּם) לְבֶהֱמַת יִשְׂרָאֵל — אֵין צָרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדוֹ... בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁהַנָּכְרִי עוֹשֶׂה כֵּן דֶּרֶךְ מִקְרֶה. אֲבָל אִם רָגִיל לַעֲשׂוֹת כֵּן — צָרִיךְ לִמְחוֹת בְּיָדוֹ:

If a non-Jew picked grasses (for his animal) and fed (also) the Jew's animal — one need not protest against him. And even though presumably he also gathered for the Jew, this gathering carries no prohibition, since any non-Jew who acts on his own surely intends his own benefit, knowing that he will not lose, as explained in siman 252. Provided that the grasses do not belong to the Jew but to the non-Jew or are hefker, for the reason explained there.

(There is also no prohibition in the fact that the non-Jew feeds the Jew's animal with grasses he gathered for him: for although it is forbidden for the Jew to benefit from what the non-Jew did for him even if he intended his own benefit, as explained there, nevertheless here the Jew's body itself does not benefit from this — the Sages only decreed when the Jew himself stands in the way for the animal to eat, but not over what the non-Jew himself feeds it, since in this he intends his own benefit.)

In what does this apply? When the non-Jew does so occasionally (when it is not clearly known that he gathered also for a Jew, since perhaps he gathered them for himself and then reconsidered and gave to the Jew's animal). But if he habitually does so, one must protest (since he certainly gathered them for the Jew, the Sages decreed even on what the non-Jew himself feeds to the Jew's animal).

סעיף כא נָכְרִי שֶׁעָשָׂה בְּשַׁבָּת אָרוֹן אוֹ קֶבֶר לִקְבּוֹר בּוֹ מֵתוֹ אוֹ לִמְכּוֹר…

נָכְרִי שֶׁעָשָׂה בְּשַׁבָּת אָרוֹן אוֹ קֶבֶר לִקְבּוֹר בּוֹ מֵתוֹ אוֹ לִמְכּוֹר (בְּעִיר שֶׁרֻבָּהּ נָכְרִים) — מֻתָּר לְיִשְׂרָאֵל לִקָּבֵר בְּמוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת מִיָּד. וְאִם עֲשָׂאוֹ בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל — צָרִיךְ לְהַמְתִּין בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיֵּעָשׂוּ... אֲבָל קֶבֶר הָעוֹמֵד בְּפַרְהֶסְיָא... אָסוּר לִקְבּוֹר בּוֹ אוֹתוֹ יִשְׂרָאֵל עוֹלָמִית, מִשּׁוּם קְנַס:

A non-Jew who made on Shabbos a casket or a grave to bury his deceased therein, or to sell (in a city the majority of whose residents are non-Jews) — it is permitted to a Jew to be buried in it on Motzaei Shabbos immediately. And if he made it for a Jew — one must wait bichdei she-yei'asu, whether to bury in it this Jew for whom it was made or to bury in it another Jew.

In what does this apply? When the grave or casket stands in a concealed place, where the matter is not publicized to all that it was made on Shabbos for a particular Jew. But a grave standing in public (befarhesia), and likewise a casket made on Shabbos near a grave standing in public, in such a way that everyone knows that this grave or this casket was made on Shabbos for so-and-so the Jew — it is forbidden to bury that Jew in it forever, even after bichdei she-yei'asu, as a penalty (kenas) for the desecration of Shabbos that occurred publicly through a non-Jew on his behalf. But it is permitted to bury another Jew in it after bichdei she-yei'asu. The same applies if Shabbos was publicly desecrated by a non-Jew for the sake of a living Jew — it is forbidden to him forever, as a penalty, even though he did not command the non-Jew to do it.

And some say that they did not forbid forever regarding the dead only as a penalty, but because it is a disgrace to the deceased to be buried in a grave publicly known to have been made in desecration of Shabbos for him. According to this view, regarding a living person, it is permitted even for him after bichdei she-yei'asu. In a time of need, one may rely on their view to be lenient in rabbinic matters.

And even according to the first view, the Sages only imposed stringency when the non-Jew acted on his own. But if he undertook before Shabbos to do this and the Jew set him a wage (katzatz), and did not set a particular time when he should do it, and the non-Jew did it on Shabbos of his own accord — there is no prohibition mid'in (by strict law) after waiting bichdei she-yei'asu, even though he did it publicly. And even if the non-Jew was a daily hired worker (sechir yom), but the Jew did not assign him a particular day, and he did it on his own on Shabbos.

And even regarding the deceased, it is permitted in this situation mid'in. Nevertheless, it is proper to be stringent even regarding a living person, according to all, that no Jew ever benefit from it, whenever Shabbos was publicly desecrated by a non-Jew at the bidding of a Jew, even if the non-Jew did it in kabbalanus (contract work), where there is no prohibition by strict law (as will be explained in siman 664) — unless he protested with the non-Jew, who did not heed for the sake of his own benefit, as explained in siman 244.

סעיף כב נָכְרִי שֶׁהֵבִיא בְּשַׁבָּת דֶּרֶךְ רְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים חֲלִילִין לְהַסְפִּיד בָּהֶם יִשְׂרָאֵל…

נָכְרִי שֶׁהֵבִיא בְּשַׁבָּת דֶּרֶךְ רְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים חֲלִילִין לְהַסְפִּיד בָּהֶם יִשְׂרָאֵל, כֵּיוָן שֶׁנַּעֲשָׂה בָהֶם אִסּוּר שֶׁל תּוֹרָה, לֹא יַסְפִּידוּ בָּהֶם לֹא אוֹתוֹ יִשְׂרָאֵל וְלֹא יִשְׂרָאֵל אַחֵר, עַד שֶׁיַּמְתִּינוּ בִּכְדֵי שֶׁיְּכוֹלִים לַהֲבִיאָם בְּמוֹצָאֵי שַׁבָּת... וְכָל זֶה בִּרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים גְּמוּרָה, אֲבָל אִם הֱבִיאָם דֶּרֶךְ כַּרְמְלִית, אִם הֱבִיאָם מִתּוֹךְ הַתְּחוּם — אֵין צָרִיךְ לְהַמְתִּין כְּלָל אֲפִלּוּ לְמִי שֶׁהוּבְאוּ בִּשְׁבִילוֹ:

A non-Jew who brought on Shabbos, through reshus harabim, flutes for the Jew to lament with them — since a Torah prohibition was performed with them, neither that Jew nor another shall lament with them, until they wait, at Motzaei Shabbos, the time it would take to bring them from the place from which the non-Jew brought them on Shabbos — whether that place is outside the techum or within it. And if it is not known from where he brought them, it suffices to wait the time it would take to bring them from outside the techum.

In what does this apply? When he brought them discreetly (betzin'a). But if he brought them publicly (befarhesia), in such a way that all know they were brought in reshus harabim on Shabbos for so-and-so the Jew — it is forbidden for that Jew to lament with them forever.

And all this is in a full reshus harabim. But if he brought them through a karmelis: if he brought them from within the techum — there is no need to wait at all, even for the one for whom they were brought. For the Sages were only stringent on the one for whom it was brought in karmelis that he not benefit from it on Shabbos itself, but not that he wait bichdei she-yei'asu on Motzaei Shabbos — unless he brought them from outside the techum, in which case the bringing on Shabbos is effective; but not so within the techum, where the bringing on Shabbos is not so effective (since the Jew could have walked there himself on Shabbos and brought them at Motzaei Shabbos immediately, and over the short measure of his return at Motzaei Shabbos there is no concern that he say to a non-Jew to go there on Shabbos).

And if it is doubtful whether he brought them from outside the techum or from within it — it is forbidden to the one for whom it was brought, but to others it is permitted, even with certainty from outside the techum.

And if he brought them from outside the techum through karmelis, and then carried them through reshus harabim — one need only wait the time it would have taken to bring them from the beginning of the reshus harabim at Motzaei Shabbos; thereafter it is permitted to others, or even to the one for whom they were brought if they were brought from within the techum.
