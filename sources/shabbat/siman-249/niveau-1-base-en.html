1. The text of the Shulchan Aruch

Siman רמ״ט of the Shulchan Aruch contains 4 seifim that codify the conducts to adopt on Friday in order to prepare for Shabbat with dignity. The Shulchan Aruch HaRav (Admur HaZakein) expands the material into 13 seifim — see Section 1b for the additions.

Seif Alef — The 3 parsaos limit

אֵין הוֹלְכִין בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת יוֹתֵר מִג' פַּרְסָאוֹת, כְּדֵי שֶׁיַּגִּיעַ לְבֵיתוֹ בְּעוֹד הַיּוֹם גָּדוֹל וְיוּכַל לְהָכִין צָרְכֵי סְעוּדָה לְשַׁבָּת. בֵּין שֶׁהוֹלֵךְ לְבֵית אֲחֵרִים בֵּין שֶׁהוֹלֵךְ לְבֵיתוֹ. וְהָנֵי מִילֵי כְּשֶׁהוּא בְּיִשּׁוּב בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁיּוּכַל לְהָכִין צָרְכֵי שַׁבָּת. אֲבָל אִם בְּמָקוֹם שֶׁהוּא שָׁם אִי אֶפְשָׁר לוֹ לְהָכִין צָרְכֵי שַׁבָּת, אוֹ שֶׁאֵינוֹ מְקוֹם יִשּׁוּב בָּטוּחַ — מֻתָּר לֵילֵךְ אֲפִלּוּ כַּמָּה פַּרְסָאוֹת. וְאִם שָׁלַח לְהוֹדִיעָם שֶׁהוּא הוֹלֵךְ שָׁם לְשַׁבָּת — מֻתָּר לוֹ לֵילֵךְ כַּמָּה פַּרְסָאוֹת בְּכָל גַּוְנָא.
One does not walk more than 3 parsaos on Friday, in order to arrive home while the day is still long, so as to be able to prepare the needs of the Shabbat seudah. Whether one goes to one's own house or to others. This applies when one is in a settled place where one can prepare the needs of Shabbat. But if one is in a place where one cannot prepare (or an unsettled, unsafe place) — it is permitted to walk even several parsaos. And if one sent word ahead to inform them that one is coming for Shabbat — it is permitted in all cases.
One parsa = 4 mil = about 4 km. So 3 parsaos ≈ 12 km. Beyond that, the traveler risks arriving late and not having time to prepare the needs of the seudah. The halakha protects a dignified preparation for Shabbat.

Seif Beis — The issur of a large seudah on Friday

אָסוּר לִקְבֹּעַ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סְעוּדָה וּמִשְׁתֶּה שֶׁאֵינוֹ רָגִיל בִּימֵי הַחוֹל. וַאֲפִלּוּ הוּא סְעוּדַת אֵרוּסִין — מִפְּנֵי כְּבוֹד הַשַּׁבָּת שֶׁיִּכָּנֵס לְשַׁבָּת כְּשֶׁהוּא תָּאֵב לֶאֱכוֹל. וְכָל הַיּוֹם בִּכְלָל הָאִסּוּר. הגה: וּסְעוּדָה שֶׁזְּמַנָּהּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, כְּגוֹן בְּרִית מִילָה אוֹ פִּדְיוֹן הַבֵּן — מֻתָּר. וְכֵן הַמִּנְהָג פָּשׁוּט. וְלֶאֱכוֹל וְלִשְׁתּוֹת בְּלִי קְבִיעוּת סְעוּדָה אֲפִלּוּ סְעוּדָה שֶׁרָגִיל בָּהּ בְּחוֹל — כָּל הַיּוֹם מֻתָּר לְהַתְחִיל מִן הַדִּין. אֲבָל מִצְוָה לְהִמָּנַע מִלִּקְבֹּעַ סְעוּדָה שֶׁנָּהוּג בָּהּ בְּחוֹל מִתֵּשַׁע שָׁעוֹת וּלְמַעְלָה.
It is assur to set on Friday a seudah/mishteh to which one is not accustomed on weekdays — even a seudas eirusin (engagement meal), because of kavod Shabbat, so that one enters Shabbat with an appetite. And the whole day is included in the issur. Hagaha (Rama): But a seudah whose time is erev Shabbat (e.g., bris milah or pidyon haben) — is permitted. And so is the clear minhag. And to eat/drink without a fixed seudah — even one's regular weekday seudah — the whole day is permitted from the strict din. But it is a mitzvah to refrain from a regular weekday seudah from the 9th hour onwards.
Three levels of conduct on Friday:
  1. All day: assur to set a new, unusual seudah (except bris/pidyon haben)
  2. From the 9th halakhic hour (≈3 PM): mitzvah to refrain even from a regular seudah
  3. Eating/drinking lightly without a fixed seudah: permitted all day
The goal: to enter Shabbat with an appetite to honor Shabbat with a real seudah.

Seif Gimel — Minhag of anshei maaseh to fast

דֶּרֶךְ אַנְשֵׁי מַעֲשֶׂה לְהִתְעַנּוֹת בְּכָל עֶרֶב שַׁבָּת.
The way of anshei maaseh (pious/men of action) is to fast every erev Shabbat.
This minhag is mentioned in several Rishonim. The goal: to enter Shabbat with tefillah (spiritual preparation), to do teshuvah, and to increase the desire for the Shabbat seudah (thus honoring it). This is not an obligation for all — it is the practice of the chassidim of the era. The Chabad practice and some poskim recommend a partial fast rather than a true taanis.

Seif Daled — Until when to fast

אִם קִבֵּל עָלָיו לְהִתְעַנּוֹת בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת — צָרִיךְ לְהִתְעַנּוֹת עַד צֵאת הַכּוֹכָבִים, אִם לֹא שֶׁפֵּרֵשׁ בִּשְׁעַת קַבָּלַת הַתַּעֲנִית עַד שֶׁיַּשְׁלִימוּ הַצִּבּוּר תְּפִלָּתָן. הגה: וְיֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים דְּלֹא יַשְׁלִים אֶלָּא מִיָּד שֶׁיּוֹצְאִים מִבֵּית הַכְּנֶסֶת יֹאכַל. וְלָכֵן בְּתַעֲנִית יָחִיד לֹא יַשְׁלִים, וְטוֹב לְפָרֵשׁ כֵּן בִּשְׁעַת קַבָּלַת הַתַּעֲנִית. וּבְתַעֲנִית צִבּוּר יַשְׁלִים, וְהָכִי נָהוּג. וְאִם הוּא תַּעֲנִית חֲלוֹם — צָרִיךְ לְהִתְעַנּוֹת עַד צֵאת הַכּוֹכָבִים.
If one accepted a taanis on erev Shabbat — one must fast until tzeis hakochavim (emergence of the stars), unless one specified at the time of accepting that one would only fast until the tzibbur finishes their tefillah. Hagaha (Rama): Some say one should not complete the fast, but rather eat as soon as leaving the beis haknesses. Therefore, in a private taanis (yachid) one does not complete; and it is good to specify this at the time of accepting. In a taanis tzibbur (public) one does complete — such is the practice. For a taanis chalom (fast after a bad dream) — one must fast until tzeis hakochavim.
3 distinct cases for the end of the fast:
  • Taanis yachid (private): per Rama, eat as soon as leaving shul. Per the strict Mechaber, until tzeis hakochavim
  • Taanis tzibbur (public — 17 Tammuz, etc.) falling on a Friday: until tzeis hakochavim (standard practice)
  • Taanis chalom: until tzeis hakochavim even on Friday

1b. The additional seifim (SHHaRav · seifim 5 to 13)

The Shulchan Aruch of the Mechaber has 4 seifim, but the Shulchan Aruch HaRav (Admur HaZakein) develops the material into 13 seifim, adding essential clarifications. Below are the additional seifim (5 to 13) with their Hebrew text, translation and pedagogical explanation.

Seif Heh — Issur of a great seudah of mishteh

אָסוּר לִקְבּוֹעַ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת סְעֻדָּה גְדוֹלָה שֶׁל מִשְׁתֶּה שֶׁאֵינוֹ רָגִיל בָּהּ בִּימֵי הַחֹל, מִפְּנֵי כְּבוֹד הַשַּׁבָּת, שֶׁעַל יְדֵי שֶׁמַּרְבֶּה בַּאֲכִילָה וּשְׁתִיָּה בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת אֲפִלּוּ בַּבֹּקֶר יוֹתֵר מִדַּרְכּוֹ בִּשְׁאָר יְמוֹת הַחֹל — לֹא יוּכַל לְקַיֵּם סְעֻדַּת שַׁבָּת שֶׁבַּלַּיְלָה כָּרָאוּי.
It is assur to set on Friday a great seudah/mishteh to which one is not accustomed during weekdays, because of kavod Shabbat: for by increasing one's eating and drinking on Friday — even in the morning, beyond one's usual conduct on weekdays — one will not be able to fulfill properly the Friday-night seudah.
Key clarification of the SHHaRav: the rule applies from the morning onwards and concerns any excess relative to the weekday norm. The criterion is not only temporal (the 9th hour) but also qualitative: any seudah exceeding one's usual weekday rhythm is included.
The Admur HaZakein emphasizes the spiritual mechanism: morning excess already undermines the evening seudah. This is a protective halakha for the central seudah of Shabbat. This differs slightly from the Mechaber, who speaks mainly of "setting" an unusual seudah.

Seif Vav — When the meal has its set time on erev Shabbat

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? בִּסְעֻדַּת הָרְשׁוּת, אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ סְעֻדַּת מִצְוָה אֶלָּא שֶׁאֵין זְמַנָּהּ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, שֶׁיָּכוֹל לִדְחוֹתָהּ לְיוֹם אַחֵר. אֲבָל אִם זְמַנָּהּ הַיּוֹם, כְּגוֹן סְעֻדַּת מִילָה וּסְעֻדַּת אֵרוּסִין וְנִשּׂוּאִין... מֻתָּר לַעֲשׂוֹת הַסְּעֻדָּה גַם כֵּן בּוֹ בַיּוֹם.
To what does this refer? To a seudas reshus (optional meal), or even a seudas mitzvah whose time is not erev Shabbat (which can be postponed to another day). But if its time is today, like the seudas milah, eirusin (engagement) or nesuin (marriage) — which cannot be deferred (eirusin, since someone else might precede in espousing her; nesuin, so as not to nullify periya u'reviya; milah, since it is assur to leave the child uncircumcised even one day) — it is permitted to make the seudah that day as well.
SHHaRav's criterion: a seudas mitzvah is permitted only if it has its זמן קבוע (set time) on erev Shabbat — and therefore cannot be deferred without compromising the mitzvah. The 3 paradigm cases: milah, eirusin, nesuin.
The Admur HaZakein adds a logical refinement: if one performs the mitzvah that day, one may not have an appetite for the Shabbat seudah — and that is not a problem, since it is also a seudas mitzvah, and one will fulfill the 3 Shabbat seudahs the next day (see Siman רע״ד).

Seif Zayin — Begin the meal before the 10th hour

וּמִכָּל מָקוֹם, מִצְוָה לְהַתְחִיל הַסְּעֻדָּה לְכַתְּחִלָּה קֹדֶם שָׁעָה עֲשִׂירִית, שֶׁהֲרֵי מִשָּׁעָה עֲשִׂירִית וְאֵילַךְ מִצְוָה לְהִמָּנַע מִלִּקְבּוֹעַ אֲפִלּוּ סְעֻדָּה קְטַנָּה. וְטוֹב שֶׁלֹּא לֶאֱכוֹל בִּסְעֻדָּה זוֹ אֶלָּא מִנְיָן מְצֻמְצָם.
Nevertheless, it is a mitzvah to begin this seudas mitzvah (milah, eirusin, etc.) l'chatchilah before the 10th hour, since from the 10th hour onwards it is a mitzvah to refrain even from a small habitual seudah. And it is good not to have more than the strict minimal minyan — except for those directly connected to the seudah (close relatives, witnesses, mohel, sandak).
SHHaRav's chiddush: for a permitted seudas mitzvah on erev Shabbat, he prescribes:
  1. Begin it before the 10th hour (≈4 PM in halakhic hours)
  2. Limit guests to the strict minimum (the minyan + direct actors)
This dual restriction preserves the Shabbat seudah without nullifying the seudas mitzvah.

Seif Ches — Pidyon haben out of zman and deferred eirusin

סְעֻדַּת פִּדְיוֹן הַבֵּן בִּזְמַנּוֹ, דְּהַיְנוּ בְּיוֹם ל"א לְלֵדָתוֹ — דִּינָהּ כִּסְעֻדַּת מִילָה. אֲבָל אִם אֵרַע אֵיזֶה אֹנֶס שֶׁלֹּא פְדָאוֹ בְּיוֹם ל"א — אֵין לִפְדּוֹתוֹ בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת וְלַעֲשׂוֹת אָז סְעֻדָּה שֶׁל מִשְׁתֶּה.
The seudas pidyon haben in its time (the 31st day) — its law is like that of the seudas milah. But if an ones (unavoidable circumstance) prevented redeeming on the 31st day — one does not perform the pidyon on a Friday with a large seudah (one redeems without a seudah, or defers the seudah). Same for an eirusin that already took place earlier and whose seudah was deferred due to ones: no seudah on a Friday.
SHHaRav's principle: "once already deferred from its set time — it is also deferred from erev Shabbat". If the mitzvah has lost its original "zmanah", it no longer has the right to the kavod Shabbat exemption. Concrete case: a pidyon haben falling on a Friday will be done without a seudah.
SHHaRav exception (in parentheses): nesuin has a zman extended over 7 days after the chuppah, so the seudah can be made throughout the week — including a Friday (see Siman תר״מ).

Seif Tes — The habitual seudah (סְעֻדָּה קְטַנָּה)

סְעֻדָּה שֶׁרָגִיל בָּהּ בִּימוֹת הַחֹל, דְּהַיְנוּ סְעֻדָּה קְטַנָּה שֶׁאֵינָהּ שֶׁל מִשְׁתֶּה — מִן הַדִּין מֻתָּר לְאָכְלָהּ בְּכָל הַיּוֹם אֲפִלּוּ סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁכָה. אֲבָל מִצְוָה לְהִמָּנַע מִלְּקָבְעָהּ מִתְּחִלַּת שָׁעָה עֲשִׂירִית וְאֵילַךְ.
The regular weekday meal — i.e., a small seudah that is not a mishteh — is, from strict halakha, permitted to be eaten the whole day, even close to nightfall. But it is a mitzvah to refrain from setting it from the 10th hour onwards, so as to enter Shabbat with an appetite. However, eating and drinking without fixing a seudah — permitted until בין השמשות (except erev Pesach).
SHHaRav's distinction: he clearly distinguishes 3 levels:
  • סעודה גדולה של משתה (great mishteh): assur all day
  • סעודה קטנה רגילה (small habitual meal): permitted from the din all day; mitzvah to refrain from the 10th hour
  • אכילה ושתיה בלי קביעות (eating/drinking without setting a seudah): permitted until בין השמשות

Seif Yud — After accepting Shabbat (קבלת שבת מבעוד יום)

אֲבָל לְאַחַר שֶׁחֲשֵׁכָה, אוֹ לְאַחַר שֶׁקִּבֵּל עָלָיו שַׁבָּת אֲפִלּוּ מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם — מִצְוָה לְהִמָּנַע מִלֶּאֱכוֹל שׁוּם דָּבָר אֲפִלּוּ בְּלֹא קְבִיעוּת סְעֻדָּה, אֲפִלּוּ לְאַחַר שֶׁקִּדֵּשׁ עַל הַכּוֹס קֹדֶם אֲכִילַת עִקַּר סְעֻדַּת שַׁבָּת.
But after nightfall, or after accepting Shabbat even by day (if he intends to start the seudah before nightfall) — it is a mitzvah to refrain from eating anything at all, even without setting a seudah, even after making kiddush on the cup — before the main meal (the "hamotzi" on the bread). For if one ate something else before the bread, one would not eat the bread with appetite. However, one does not protest against those who are lenient, since it is not a strict obligation.
Practical halakha: from the moment one has accepted Shabbat (Kabbalas Shabbat or candle-lighting), one refrains even from small snacks until the "hamotzi" of the main seudah. The bread is the heart of the Shabbat seudah — without it, one does not fulfill the 3 seudahs.

Seif Yud-Alef — Intention for the meah berachos

בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? כְּשֶׁאוֹכֵל אוֹתוֹ מַאֲכָל קֹדֶם הַסְּעֻדָּה מֵחֲמַת שֶׁחָפֵץ וּמִתְאַוֶּה לְאָכְלוֹ, אֲבָל אִם מַה שֶּׁאוֹכְלוֹ קֹדֶם הַסְּעֻדָּה הוּא מֵחֲמַת שֶׁרוֹצֶה לְהַשְׁלִים מֵאָה בְרָכוֹת — יֵשׁ בְּזֶה אִסּוּר.
To what does this refer? When one eats that food before the seudah out of desire (taavah). But if one eats it before the seudah in order to complete the 100 berachos (by adding a berachah acharonah not covered by birkas hamazon) — there is in this an issur gamur (strict prohibition): one causes a berachah-obligation that could have been avoided (Siman רט״ו), and one precedes the berachah of that food before the berachah of bread (which has priority — Siman רי״א).
SHHaRav's halakhic subtlety: the custom of eating fruits before kiddush to complete the 100 berachos of Shabbat is a good intention, but it contradicts two halakhos: (1) not to create a berachah obligation when one can avoid it, (2) priority of the bread berachah. Solution: if one eats a food that one will not eat during the meal, one can do so — since it is then not covered by hamotzi.

Seif Yud-Beis — Taanis on erev Shabbat (detailed law)

אֵין קוֹבְעִים תַּעֲנִית לְכַתְּחִלָּה בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת, אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן שֶׁיִּטְעוֹם מְעַט קֹדֶם קַבָּלַת שַׁבָּת. בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים? בְּתַעֲנִית יָחִיד שֶׁאֵין קָבוּעַ לוֹ זְמַן. אֲבָל אִם זְמַנּוֹ הוּא בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת... אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לִדְחוֹת... וְרַשַּׁאי לְהַשְׁלִים עַד צֵאת הַכּוֹכָבִים.
One does not set a taanis l'chatchilah on Friday, unless one tastes a little before Kabbalas Shabbat — except for an istenis (delicate person) for whom even a small taste during the day prevents a real appetite at night. To what does this refer? To a private taanis without a set time, which can be deferred. But if its time is erev Shabbat (yahrzeit of tzaddikim, erev Rosh Chodesh, etc.) — no need to defer. One can complete until tzeis hakochavim. For taanis chalom — according to all opinions, until tzeis hakochavim.
SHHaRav's practical hierarchy:
  1. Private taanis without fixed date: do not set it on Friday, or taste a little before Shabbat
  2. Private taanis with fixed date (yahrzeit, etc.): may be done on Friday; better to specify upon acceptance that it ends at shul departure
  3. Taanis tzibbur: obligation to complete until tzeis hakochavim, even with contrary stipulation
  4. Taanis chalom: until tzeis hakochavim for all opinions
SHHaRav's posek-of-record on private taanis: if one has already davened Maariv of Shabbat, one must eat immediately — since one does not fast on Shabbat. However, since one has already passed the Kabbalas Shabbat moment, one is considered to have already completed one's taanis.

Seif Yud-Gimel — Taanis become custom (status of neder)

הַמִּתְעַנֶּה בְּכָל עֶרֶב חֹדֶשׁ, אוֹ בְּכָל עֲשֶׂרֶת יְמֵי תְשׁוּבָה... אִם בַּפַּעַם הָרִאשׁוֹן שֶׁאֵרַע הַתַּעֲנִית בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת הִשְׁלִימוֹ עַד צֵאת הַכּוֹכָבִים — צָרִיךְ לִנְהוֹג כֵּן לְעוֹלָם, מִשּׁוּם שֶׁהוּא כְּמוֹ נֶדֶר.
One who fasts regularly on every erev Rosh Chodesh, or during the 10 days of teshuvah, or on 20 Sivan, or on the yahrzeits of tzaddikim, or on the yahrzeit of his parents — if the first time the taanis fell on a Friday he completed it until tzeis hakochavim, he must continue thus forever, since it counts as a neder (vow) — and 3 people would be needed to annul it. Provided that on that first occasion, he intended to continue thus forever, or he did it 3 times without a "bli neder" condition.
Fundamental principle: in halakha, 3 repetitions without "bli neder" condition establish a custom with the value of a neder. This applies specifically to repeated private taaniyos. To release oneself, one needs hataras nedarim before 3 people.
SHHaRav's practical advice: for those who fast regularly (erev Rosh Chodesh, etc.), always specify "bli neder" each time — that way one is not caught by the custom becoming an obligation. And the 1st time such a fast falls on a Friday, do not complete it — to avoid the creation of a permanent neder. However, one who is lenient when needed, following the first opinion — has not lost out.

2. General context

What does this siman discuss?

Siman רמ״ט deals with halakhic conducts specific to Friday (erev Shabbat): travel limits, conduct at meals, recommended fasts. It is a halakhic pause before the simanim that describe the very entry of Shabbat.

The structuring question: how does one prepare with dignity for Shabbat? Which conducts must be adopted (material hakhanah), avoided (excessive feasts), or recommended (the fast of the pious)?

3. Key concept 1 — the 3 parsaos

1 parsa = 4 mil = about 4 km (per traditional calculations).
3 parsaos ≈ 12 km. This is the maximum walking limit on Friday outside of emergency or prior notice.

Why this limit?

The goal is to arrive home early enough so that the day is still long (בעוד היום גדול) — i.e., ideally before the 6th hour (midday). This enables one to:

  • Buy the needs of the seudah (fish, meat, wine)
  • Cook
  • Light the fire / set up the hot-plate (blech)
  • Wash and dress in honor of Shabbat
  • Arrive at shul with calm

The 3 exceptions of the Mechaber

ExceptionConduct
Place where one cannot prepareOne may walk several parsaos
Unsettled, unsafe placeOne may walk several parsaos (safety takes priority)
Sent word ahead that one is comingOne may walk several parsaos in all cases

4. Key concept 2 — the issur of a large seudah (kavod Shabbat)

The principle: do not fix (likboa) an unusual seudah/mishteh on Friday, in order to enter Shabbat with an appetite. The big seudah of Friday night must have a contrast with the day, to express kavod Shabbat.

Three levels of strictness

TimeRegular seudahUnusual seudahBris/Pidyon Haben
All dayPermitted❌ Assur✓ Permitted (Rama)
From the 9th halakhic hour (≈3 PM)Mitzvah to refrain❌ Assur✓ Permitted
Practice today:
  • Normal light meal during the day until 2–3 PM ✓
  • No large buffet on Friday at noon that would spoil the appetite ✗
  • Bris, pidyon haben, seudas mitzvah → permitted (even as a feast)
  • From ~3 PM in winter (the 9th hour), refrain from a real seudah

5. Key concept 3 — taanis erev Shabbat (anshei maaseh)

אנשי מעשה (anshei maaseh) = "men of action / pious" — in the classical sense: those who engage in deep spiritual avodah. The minhag mentioned by the Mechaber is to fast every erev Shabbat in order to enter Shabbat with prepared teshuvah.

Position of the poskim

PositionRecommendation
MechaberMentions the minhag of anshei maaseh — not an obligation
Sephardic AcharonimHistorical minhag, little followed today
Ashkenazic AcharonimSame — practiced by Chassidim and certain individual pious people
Chassidus (Chabad in particular)Prefers tefillah b'minyan and study of Tanya rather than a fast. The taanis is not a recommended Chabad practice

6. Detail of the 4 seifim

Seif Alef — Distance and preparation

Practical test: can I get home before midday to prepare for Shabbat? If yes → the 3 parsaos limit does not concern me. If no → I must either send word ahead or change plans.

Seif Beis — Daytime seudah

Rule: eating normally is OK. A new feast = assur. 9th hour and after = mitzvah to refrain even from a regular seudah. Except bris or pidyon haben.

Seif Gimel — Minhag of the taanis

Minhag of the pious mentioned. Not an obligation. Today, little followed. Chabad prefers spiritual hakhanah (Tanya, Mishneh Torah) rather than a fast.

Seif Daled — End of the fast

Three cases:
  • Taanis yachid: Rama → eat as soon as leaving shul; Mechaber → tzeis hakochavim
  • Taanis tzibbur (public): tzeis hakochavim
  • Taanis chalom: tzeis hakochavim

8. Modern practical cases

Case 1 — Train travel on Friday

Case: I am returning home by train on Friday for Shabbat. The trip takes 4 hours; I arrive at 2 PM.
Analysis: The 3 parsaos apply to walking. The train is not restricted — the spirit is that one arrives with enough time. If I arrive at 2 PM and Shabbat is at 6 PM, I have 4 hours to prepare → OK.
Conduct: permitted. But for a trip that would extend into Shabbat, see siman רמ״ח.

Case 2 — Business brunch on Friday at noon

Case: I am invited to a business brunch on Friday at 12 PM.
Analysis:
  • If it is a new festive seudah that one does not have during the week → ❌ assur per seif beis
  • If it is a regular seudah (regular business lunch) → permitted before the 9th halakhic hour, mitzvah to refrain after
  • If it is a light cocktail without kevius seudah → permitted
Conduct: depends on the type. When in doubt, prefer a light brunch.

Case 3 — Bris milah falling on a Friday

Case: Bris milah at 11 AM Friday with the seudas bris afterwards.
Conduct: permitted (Rama) — such is the practice. The seudas bris is explicitly mutar even on a Friday because its time (zemanah) is erev Shabbat.

Case 4 — 17 Tammuz fast falling on a Friday

Case: 17 Tammuz falls on a Friday.
Conduct: public taanis → fast until tzeis hakochavim. The Shabbat seudah begins after. Such is the general practice (Rama).

Case 5 — A Chabad chassid — minhag of taanis erev Shabbat?

Case: should one fast erev Shabbat per Chabad?
Conduct: No. The Chabad practice prefers:
  • Study of Tanya (chapters 41–44 specifically directed towards Shabbat preparation)
  • Study of the day's Shulchan Aruch HaRav
  • Mikveh (purifying preparation)
  • Dignified material hakhanah
The taanis is not the Chabad chassidic way. The Alter Rebbe gives priority to simcha shel mitzvah.

9. Practical summary

The 4 teachings of Siman רמ״ט:
  1. Limit of 3 parsaos (≈12 km) — arrive home in time to prepare for Shabbat
  2. Do not make an unusual feast on Friday — enter Shabbat with an appetite (תאב לאכול)
  3. Minhag of the pious to fast erev Shabbat — not an obligation, rarely followed
  4. If one accepts a taanis — rules vary by yachid/tzibbur/chalom
